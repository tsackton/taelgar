# Embodied Gods

The divinities known as the Embodied Gods are the direct creations of the [[High Gods]], given physical form, permanence and consciousness, and the power to create. Unlike the [[Incorporeal Gods]], the Embodied Gods do not depend upon belief for their power. 

The most well known of the Embodied Gods are the deities created by the [[Divine Presence]], and often known as the Firstborn, who themselves created new species in their images. These are: [[Aldanor]] and [[Elmerca]], the Living Tree and the Wild Star of the elves; [[Entamba]], the Elder Mountain of the stoneborn; [[Hazkunde]], [[Bero]], [[Udazkena]], and [[Negu]], sometimes known as Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter, the four [[Tharzen Anzinakoa]] of the lizardfolk; the [[Uzdan|Ruler]], [[Karthel|Runecrafter]], [[Fanrukel|Priest]], [[Azar|Warrior]], [[Maganna|Traveler]], [[Tarbaka|Stoneworker]], and [[Oshkir|Farmer]], the seven [[Bahrazel]] of the dwarves; and [[Nwana]], called the Stranger-Child, [[Obito]], called the Merchant-Musician, and [[Jemghari]], called the Traveler-Storyteller, the two-faced [[First Ones]] of the halflings. 

[[Thark]], the God of War Without End and the divine creator of the [[orcs]], is often counted among the Firstborn, though unlike the other Firstborn, he is an avatar of the [[Void Mind]], created under mysterious circumstances, by something, but not the [[Divine Presence]]. Thark is the divine force that, through his compulsion, drives orcs to violence for the sake of violence. 

Less well understood among the Embodied Gods are the other avatars, echoes, and deities associated with the [[Void Mind]], created for inscrutable, destructive ends, and not known to be the creators of any identified species. Elven lore records rumors of these gods: [[Jinnik]], the Mad One, associated with lycanthropy and [[Pandemonium]]; and [[Umidzka]], associated with undeath.
