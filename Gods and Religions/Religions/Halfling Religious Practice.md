# Halfling Religious Practice

Halflings, at least to outsiders, do not have deep traditions of prayer, or organized religious practice. For many halflings, the [[First Ones]] are simply the backdrop to life, the gods on whom their shared ancestral mind and memory depend. Every experience, every memory of a halfling’s life will eventually contribute in some small way to the greater halfling consciousness, and for many halflings simply acknowledging and paying attention to their experience as they go through life is the extent of their worship practice.  

Nonetheless, some religious traditions are common among halfling communities.
## Prayers and Worship

Prayer and worship traditions in halfling culture typically serve to call the attention of the [[First Ones]], to ask them to attend with purpose, to help create and preserve strong memories. Prayers for beginnings, and for endings, are often said, and especially prayers to remember those who have passed and ensure their memories are strong.

History-bearers, those halflings who through practice or divine inspiration have a deeper, and stronger, connection to the ancestral memory, bear witness to these beginnings and endings, and many halfling prayers are also stories of the past. In this way, history-bearers serve as a priesthood and mark occasions.
## Sacred Places

Halflings rarely build temples, in part because they do not often settle in one place for long enough to be concerned with the construction of physical monuments to the gods. But halfling shrines dot the landscape, often in natural places that capture particular experiences, that evoke the feelings of an aesthetic that embraces life-as-it-is, not life as something artificially made to be perfect. A small, out of the way path off a main trade road, that leads to a particular ravine or grove of trees or waterfall may turn out to be a sacred halfling shrine, though often not obvious to anyone who does not share access to the halfling ancestral mind. 

>[!important] Memories and Shrines
>Some halflings also believe that particular memories become, in a way, shrines themselves. While some memories fade, even in the ancestral mind, others, those that capture a particular religious feeling, are experienced and re-experienced over and over again. As new generations of halflings re-experience the same memory, layers grow and accrete over time, so a halfling can experience not only the original memory, but the memory of a particularly famous storyteller experiencing the original memory for the first time.Thus, a shrine to a calm day at sea becomes more than a place, it becomes instead a feeling and an experience and a memory. Though there may still be places, perhaps a high cliff overlooking a sheltered bay, where this memory shrine might feel particularly strong, halflings may experience religious monuments primarily alongside and in the context of these ancestral memories.

## Afterlife

*One possible account of the afterlife, not established canon. The ancestral mind’s dependence on the First Ones does not establish that memories and souls are the same thing, or that the ancestral mind is specifically located in the divine realm or represents the divine realm.*
 
Halflings, when they die, do not long linger as individual, distinct souls. Instead, they slowly fade into the halfling soulmind, their memories and personalities becoming diffuse and indistinct, a part of the great ocean of halfling souls. In this way, the Divine Realm of the First Ones is less a place than a memory, a superposition of all the experiences, hopes, and dreams of halflings since the dawn of time.
