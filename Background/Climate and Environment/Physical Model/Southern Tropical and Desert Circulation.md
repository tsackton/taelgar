# Southern Tropical and Desert Circulation

**Scope:** The Nayan–Shrev tropical transition, the southern Garamjala margin and lower Istaros, the mainland jungle coast near Drankor, and the separate Great Desert and Orekatu regions. [[Taelgar Climate Model]] controls the continental framework. This page explains how a dry plateau and interior can coexist with humid tropical lowlands; it does not join these places into one rainfall regime.

**Standing:** K04, K29 and K30 in [[Climate and Weather Constraints]] require tropical forest compatibility along and south of the Shrev, persistent Great Desert interior aridity, and tropical warmth and moisture in the separate mainland and Orekatu jungles. Drankor's humid heat and heavy rain are retained observations. Rainfall quantities, detailed seasons and most local mechanisms remain open. The explanations below are qualitative model development under those bounds.

## Shared tropical framework

The tropical rain belt moves north during northern summer and south during northern winter. Its movement changes where moist air converges and rises, while easterly trade winds remain the broad background outside the monsoon districts. Near-equatorial districts can receive recurring rain as the belt moves, and sea-facing terrain can collect moisture even when the strongest convergence lies elsewhere. Farther into the subtropical interior, dry air and mountain shelter sharply reduce that access.

This supplies a physical route from seasonal savanna to wetter tropical forest without placing a precise rainfall boundary at a river or latitude. A longer rain-bearing season, more frequent showers outside its main peak, or both can lessen the moisture deficit toward the wetter coast. The model leaves their relative contributions open. The [NOAA description of tropical convergence](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/tropical/convergence-zone) supports the moving rain-belt mechanism; its African rainfall dates and totals are not Taelgar parameters.

Temperature and moisture are separate gradients. Winter cooling weakens toward the tropical lowlands, but a warm place can remain arid if it lacks moist inflow. Conversely, a river valley can remain green within dry country because its water comes from a larger catchment.

## Nayan Floodplains and the Shrev margin

[[Dunmar and Nevos Circulation]] owns the summer Nevos inflow and its approach to the western Yuvanti. The [[Nayan Floodplains]] lie on this exposed side of the range, retaining strong summer rainfall and a drier, gentler cool season. The [[Shrev]] floods during the summer monsoon and waters the southern savanna. These floods reinforce the seasonal landscape without measuring local rainfall at every point along the river.

Southward, weaker winter cooling and greater access to tropical maritime moisture support the transition to the accepted jungle and tropical hardwoods. The working explanation combines the Nevos wet season with more persistent moisture near the tropical rain belt. It does not require every forest south of the Shrev to be wet in every month. Wetter sheltered valleys and river margins can grade into more seasonal open country within this transition.

K04 fixes a landscape outcome rather than a rainfall calendar. The river marks a familiar geographic boundary; it is not modeled as a climatic wall. The width and seasonal character of the savanna–forest transition remain open, as do the relative contributions of coastal showers, rain-belt passages and water stored through drier intervals.

## Garamjala plateau to the mainland jungle coast

### Why the plateau stays dry

The [[Garamjala Plateau]] lies behind the Yuvanti barrier to Nevos monsoon moisture. The [[Garamjala Desert]] also receives less dependable rain farther from the more accessible northwestern margins. This western rain shadow is maintained in [[Dunmar and Nevos Circulation]]; it is not sufficient by itself to explain every coast south of the plateau.

The adopted continental model treats Garamjala as a hot, moderately elevated desert. Elevation can reduce its temperature relative to Hara without supplying rain. The [[Istaros]] carries water from a much larger watershed through this dry country, sustaining riparian life independently of the local rain regime. Neither a perennial river nor an upstream flood turns the plateau into a humid climate.

### Why the southern lowlands can be wet

The lower Istaros descends from the plateau through waterfalls into increasingly lush country; the [[Awakened Jungle]] occupies the coast south of Garamjala and west of [[Drankor]]. These established geographic contrasts provide a second maritime approach: moisture can reach the southern lowlands from the adjoining tropical sea without first crossing the Yuvanti and the arid plateau.

The working mechanism is recurrent humid inflow and tropical convergence over the lower coast, with rainfall enhanced where moist air rises toward the plateau margin. During favorable episodes, coastal breezes can bring this air inland and hills can help initiate showers. When the moist layer is shallow or the interior remains dry and stable, much of that moisture rains out near the exposed slopes or stays below the plateau rim. This permits a wetter coast beside an arid upland without requiring a continuous mountain wall of invented height.

## Drankor and the lower Istaros

Tropical warmth and moisture sufficient for extensive jungle are adopted in the continental account. The local model permits humid, relatively still intervals followed by heavy convective rain when moist air rises. The sequence recorded near Drankor on May 23, 1749—humid still heat followed by heavy rain, E20—is compatible with that mechanism. One day does not establish a May rainfall maximum, daily afternoon storms, or an annual wet-season onset.

Ocean access and converging air maintain the atmospheric moisture supply. River flow and wet ground can preserve humid local conditions between storms but do not, alone, account for a broad rain-fed jungle. Coastal mangroves support a warm wetland setting; they do not establish that every month is rainy across the whole hinterland.

The [[Sea of Storms]], magical disturbances and the Awakened Jungle's extraordinary inhabitants do not determine ordinary storm frequency. The climate model explains the retained warmth and forest moisture without making supernatural weather a recurring physical mechanism. It also does not settle the modern lower Istaros course or the condition of historically altered swamps.

## Great Desert and the southern Green Sea interface

The [[Great Desert]] is a separate broad dry interior in the [[Far South]]. Its aridity follows the continental model's combination of limited moisture access, mountain shelter where relevant, and dry stable air above a strongly heated lower atmosphere. A desert heat low can draw air inward near the ground while deep rain clouds remain difficult to sustain: low surface pressure does not guarantee a wet climate.

During northern summer, the heat low strengthens and its northern flank reinforces easterlies across the southern Green Sea. [[Green Sea Atmospheric Circulation]] owns those coastal winds, the eastern ocean high and their navigation consequences. This page supplies the desert-side boundary: air arriving at exposed coastal mountains can produce rain there, yet the broad interior remains arid. It does not extend a uniformly dry climate to every coast or identify Garamjala and the Great Desert as one pressure center.

In northern winter, reduced heating weakens the heat low while subtropical ridge influence becomes more important toward the southern Green Sea. Some marginal rain-bearing episodes remain possible; a weaker heat low does not create an interior rainy season. Toward the tropical margins, occasional deeper moisture incursions can produce localized storms without changing the desert's long-term moisture deficit. Their reach and frequency are unassigned.

The [Saharan heat-low observations of Engelstaedter and colleagues](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A7c8298bf-5730-47f7-b80d-9a3b26bf766f) provide a mechanism comparison for dry heat-low circulation with episodic moisture transport. Neither the measured pressure structure nor African monsoon pathways are copied onto Taelgar.

## Orekatu and other tropical southern lands

[[Orekatu]] is a separate tropical region in the far east, with accepted jungle and swamp environments. Its broad physical basis is tropical warmth, access to humid maritime air, and recurring uplift through tropical convergence and local terrain. The background easterly trades can supply exposed coasts and hills where their approach crosses warm water; the relevant exposure depends on the local geography.

That explanation gives the forest a moisture source without assigning the mainland Drankor calendar to Orekatu. The model does not yet decide whether its principal inhabited districts have a short dry season, substantial rain throughout the year, or different regimes on opposite slopes. Other tropical southern regions are covered only by this continental framework until their coasts and terrain are developed. The intentionally sparse [[Far South]] geography is preserved.

The current explanation covers the retained landscape contrasts qualitatively; it is not independent evidence that would restrict later revision.
