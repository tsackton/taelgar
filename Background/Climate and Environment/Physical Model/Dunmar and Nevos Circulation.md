# Dunmar and Nevos Circulation

**Scope:** The [[Nevos Sea]] moisture supply, the Dunmari monsoon, Illoria, and the Chataan transition toward Chardon. This note develops the regional mechanisms in [[Taelgar Climate Model]], which controls the continental circulation and temperature framework. [[Western Ocean and Coast Circulation]] owns the shared western ridge and the Apporian coast; [[Southern Tropical and Desert Circulation]] continues the moisture gradient beyond the Shrev and across southern Garamjala to the jungle coast.

**Standing:** The monsoon, the usual arrival order, the wetter western/northern districts, Alta Tonaro's winter rain, and Illoria's warm subtropical character are retained requirements K01–K03, K17 and K18 in [[Climate and Weather Constraints]]. The mechanisms below are a qualitative working model. They explain those outcomes without fixing local rainfall totals, pressure values, or a calendar for every district.

## Atmospheric circulation and monsoon

Seasonal movement of the western subtropical ridge and heating of the mainland organize the contrast between Dunmar and Chardon. Winter ridge influence favors Dunmar's dry season while western disturbances reach Chardon. In summer, stronger ridge influence over Chardon maintains predominantly dry weather there, while a monsoon trough over the northern Nevos and adjoining mainland supports maritime inflow into Dunmar. 

[[Western Ocean and Coast Circulation]] extends this circulation through Apporia and the Coastlands to Mawakel, including the transition between the Chardon ridge and the Nevos monsoon around Emerald Bay.

The Dunmari monsoon draws moisture from the Nevos, with coastal inflow broadly **W–SW → E–NE**. The trough lies north to northwest of the main inflow: its southern flank supports the eastward component, with near-surface air also moving toward lower pressure. This is a regional circulation distinct from the Cymean low and Green Sea inflow. Their wet seasons need not begin together, and the model requires no continuous trough or moisture route between them.

Terrain divides this inflow into two broad routes. Across the [[Nayan Floodplains]], maritime air reaches the western slopes of the [[Yuvanti Mountains]] directly. Farther north, the broad opening between the Yuvanti and [[Chataan Mountains]] admits air across the [[Darba Highlands]] toward the [[Rudhan Heights]]. The Darba gap is a lower route through an upland landscape, not an unobstructed sea-level corridor. Air loses moisture along the way and crossing the height of land, so an open route can coexist with dry country beyond it.

The model assumes a Nevos moisture supply sufficient for the adopted coastal monsoon. It does not yet assign sea-surface temperatures, currents, or a current-driven seasonal trigger. Illorian islands and coastline geometry can redirect local winds without blocking the entire regional inflow.

## Seasonal cycle

The Western Dunmar cycle is **warm dry season → hot premonsoon season → summer rains**. Its local seasonal vocabulary differs from a northern four-season division: [[Dunmari Festivals]] places the beginning of Dunmari summer around April and its end around June.

The hottest part of the year can precede the main rains: clear skies allow strong daytime heating, while the later wet season brings more cloud and evaporation. This does not assign a temperature curve to every Dunmari region.

| Phase                        | Circulation and weather                                                                                           | Regional expression                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Northern winter              | The summer trough weakens; higher pressure and continental or northeasterly flow become more common.              | Warm dry weather in western Dunmar; occasional fog in moist low ground. Exposed interiors retain large day/night contrasts. Winter disturbances can water the Chardon side of the Chataans. |
| Premonsoon hot season        | Mainland heating develops lower pressure, but reliable deep maritime moisture has not yet reached every district. | Hot, generally clear conditions; increasing humidity or isolated clouds near the coast can precede sustained rain inland. Dunmari April–June summer belongs mainly to this phase.           |
| Onset and active rains       | Stronger Nevos inflow and a favorable trough position bring repeated rain-bearing episodes.                       | Rain establishes first in accessible western districts, then reaches farther into Hara. Uplift enhances exposed slopes; sheltered basins receive less.                                      |
| Breaks within the wet season | The trough weakens or shifts and maritime moisture penetrates less effectively.                                   | Sunnier, hotter intervals and scattered storms replace widespread rain. Interior rain can pause while coastal or mountain showers continue.                                                 |
| Retreat and postmonsoon      | Cooling land and renewed ridge influence weaken the sustained inflow.                                             | Dry intervals lengthen, nights cool, and wet soils and rivers carry the previous rains into a productive postmonsoon period. November growth does not require a November monsoon.           |

Onset is the establishment of a regional wind-and-moisture regime, not a single front that rains on every place in order. As inflow deepens, more inland storms become possible; one isolated early storm need not mark the local onset. The usual progression can therefore hold across the region while neighboring valleys differ on a particular day.

Retreat likewise need not retrace the arrival sequence. As a working expectation, the least well-supplied interior districts become unreliable first, while sea-facing slopes can retain showers after broad inland rains end. An individual late storm can interrupt this drying. Generally the monsoon period lasts something like June–September, with rain commonly beginning in June or July; but there is some year-to-year variation and region-to-region variation. 

Active and break phases provide a physical basis for this variability; their duration and number are unassigned. The [Bureau of Meteorology's monsoon explanation](https://www.bom.gov.au/resources/learn-and-explore/climate-knowledge-centre/climate-factors/monsoon) supplies the mechanism comparison, not an Australian timetable or wind direction.

## Terrain and monsoon progression

Rain diminishes across and beyond the Yuvanti and Rudhan Heights. The [[Hara Basin]] has a shorter or less reliable wet season, strongest in its west and north. The terrain produces several connected contrasts:

| Route or district                                         | Moisture access and resulting contrast                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Nevos coast and western Darba Highlands                   | Direct maritime access supplies the wetter end of the gradient. Heavy seasonal recharge, shaded valleys and stored water support seasonal forest and perennial rivers through the drier months.                                                              |
| Rudhan Heights and [[Copper Hills]]                       | The height of land lifts the inflow and reduces the moisture reaching its lee. Dry hills can still receive intense monsoon rain and brief torrents.                                                                                                          |
| Tokra, [[Songara Plains]] and northern Hara               | Access around the northern Yuvanti and uplift near the enclosing foothills permit more recurring rain than in the southern basin. This explains the broad pattern without giving every northern slope the same exposure.                                     |
| [[Sukal Plains]] and [[Lower Hara Valley]] around Askandi | The main Yuvanti barrier shelters these districts from direct western inflow. Rain is unreliable; water arriving down the Hara can precede local rain.                                                                                                       |
| [[Samtal]], Karawa and northwestern Garamjala             | Foothill storms and occasional stronger incursions sustain the more watered northern margins and sparse desert rains. Karawa remains much drier than Samtal and Songara.                                                                                     |
| Deep [[Garamjala Plateau]]                                | Longer inland travel and strong mountain shelter leave only very little rain. Elevation can relieve some basin heat without making the plateau moist. The southern coastal contrast is developed separately in [[Southern Tropical and Desert Circulation]]. |

The rains generally reach western Dunmar and Darba before Tokra and Songara, advancing through the Yuvanti foothills and Copper Hills. The more distant northern peaks, Raven's Hold, Karawa and Gomat follow later; sparse rain reaches Askandi and deep Garamjala later still. [[The Dunmari Monsoon]] records this progression. Its particular intervals are testimony about an unfolding season, not a fixed delay between every pair of places. Height alone does not determine the arrival order: a high slope cannot extract rain from an air mass that has not yet brought enough moisture to it.

> [!tip] How a rain shadow works
> Air forced up a mountain cools; if it contains enough moisture, clouds and rain form on the exposed slope. Air descending on the other side warms, reducing its relative humidity and making further rain less likely. This can leave a dry interior close to a wet coast. The effect depends on wind direction, mountain height, and gaps through the terrain, so it produces a gradient rather than a perfectly dry boundary. See the [Met Office explanation of the foehn effect](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/types-of-weather/wind/foehn-effect).

## Chataan and Alta Tonaro transition

The Chataans separate two sources of seasonal precipitation. During the monsoon, slopes exposed to Nevos air receive summer rain and seasonal waterfalls. Across the ridges, shelter and descending air limit moisture reaching [[Alta Tonaro]]. At the same time, the summer ridge around Chardon discourages widespread rain on the northern side. Rare summer thunderstorms remain possible when a stronger moisture incursion or local uplift overcomes that suppression.

In winter, the western storm belt reaches Chardon and sometimes the northern foothills. Alta Tonaro can therefore receive its important winter rain while Dunmar remains predominantly dry. The transition runs across slopes and valleys rather than following a single latitude: an exposed high slope, a sheltered plateau, and a river canyon can differ within the same province. Cooler high terrain also loses less water than hot low ground, but this does not erase the established aridity of the northern foothills.

Snow is physically possible on the highest cold terrain in this southern range. Its ordinary extent, persistence and contribution to individual rivers remain unassigned here; this page does not extend the Sentinel glacier requirements to all Chataan or Yuvanti summits.

## Illoria and the Nevos islands

[[Illoria]] retains the warm subtropical character required by K18. The surrounding sea limits temperature swings along exposed shores, while elevation cools the interiors. These are the continental model's geographic projections; the retained source does not prescribe an annual rainfall pattern.

In active summer monsoon flow, western or southwestern exposures can receive moist air and uplifted rain, with local shelter on the opposite side. In the cooler season, northeasterly air that has traveled over land may arrive relatively dry, but gains moisture during a longer crossing of open water. The wetter exposure can therefore change with the wind. Island relief, ocean travel distance, and proximity to the northern storm fringe determine how pronounced that change becomes. A wetter windward slope does not establish that the entire archipelago is wet year-round or has Dunmar's rainfall calendar.

The August 1748 voyage through Illoria had monsoon headwinds on westward legs and more variable winds farther north, yet reached Chardon. This is compatible with changing winds across the Nevos–Apporia transition. K18 and E21 do not establish guaranteed sailing winds, storm frequency, a natural canal wind, or an annual voyage duration; the western continuation is maintained in [[Western Ocean and Coast Circulation#Seasonal coastal winds]].

## Weather episodes and retained examples

These are useful local expressions of the circulation, not a schedule to generate every year.

| Episode | Expected contrast and evidence limit |
| --- | --- |
| Clear premonsoon heat | Strong afternoon heating in Hara and Garamjala, followed by marked night cooling; coastal districts can remain more humid. This accommodates E01 without treating early references to cold nights as an absolute temperature requirement. |
| Advancing monsoon surge | Western streams rise and the coast is already wet while the inner basin waits. Tokra's first rain on July 9, 1748 and rain farther west later that month fit E02; these dates do not set annual onset. |
| Active inland storm | A wet hillside or thunderstorm feeds a flood down a dry wash; nearby sheltered ground may receive little rain. River rise and local rainfall need not coincide. |
| Wet-season break | Clearer skies and drying roads can interrupt the rains while isolated foothill storms continue. This is a model prediction, without an assigned recurrence rate. |
| Cooler postmonsoon spell | Moisture from earlier rain sustains grass and cultivation under cooler conditions, as in E03's November Dunmari plains. This does not restore a winter-rain or spring-bloom desert regime. |

Magical Stormcaller weather and supernatural local environments remain outside this ordinary circulation model. The full exclusions and dated source links belong in [[Climate and Weather Constraints]].

## Unspecified parameters

> [!question] Unspecified parameters
> Exact onset and retreat dates, active/break durations, local wind bearings, rain totals, and monsoon reliability remain unassigned. The Western Dunmar temperature sequence does not impose identical winter temperatures on Hara or the islands. Darba's broad temperature range follows the continental model; no additional numerical normals are introduced here. Nevos currents and sea temperatures, Illoria's rainfall calendar, Chataan snow duration, and the width of each terrain transition still require local development.
