# Western Ocean and Coast Circulation

**Scope:** Seasonal atmospheric circulation over the western ocean and its coast, from Apporia and Chardon north through the Coastlands to Mawakel and the Slate Sea, together with Tawir, Valandros, the western forests, and the Fiatara–Voltara–Erbalta interior. The northern margin includes Blackwater and the forests north of the Fiatara; the southern edge includes the northern fringe of the Nevos monsoon.

The western shores face the [[Endless Ocean]] and, farther north, the [[Slate Sea]]. Western Apporia faces the open ocean, while the [[Gulf of Chardon]] lies partly sheltered behind the peninsula. The [[Mawar Mountains]] and [[Fiatara Mountains]] interrupt maritime air routes inland; lower hills and forested basins lead toward the much higher Sentinels. Air can reach the southern interior around the southern Fiatara, while Blackwater has an open western approach north of the range. These routes and barriers determine where ocean air rises and loses moisture, descends into sheltered country, or penetrates inland with much of its moisture intact.

## The circulation framework

### The ridge and the moving storm belt

The western ocean high is a broad area of high pressure, with an elongated northern extension called a **ridge**. Sinking air beneath it suppresses widespread rain. Farther north, temperate westerlies carry moving **lows** and their fronts generally eastward or northeastward. A front is the boundary between different air masses; lifting along it can produce a broad band of rain or snow well away from the low's center. Winds higher in the atmosphere steer these systems along paths that can curve as they cross the region.

The ridge and the main storm belt shift northward toward summer and southward toward winter. Chardon consequently spends much of summer beneath the ridge, while winter fronts reach the city and adjoining country more often. Mawar and the northern coast remain within reach of ocean weather through summer, though lows are generally weaker and their tracks lie farther north. The frequency and position of these passages are major climatic drivers: closely spaced systems bring prolonged unsettled weather, while more distant or infrequent passages leave longer settled intervals.

In winter, cold air also accumulates over the northern interior, where continental high pressure sustains cold, predominantly dry spells. Incoming ocean systems encounter this air and can displace it, lift moist air over it, or leave it in place. The balance changes as the continental high weakens in spring and strengthens again toward winter.

### Winds around passing lows

Wind names describe where air comes from. Winds circulate counterclockwise around lows and clockwise around highs, with some inward flow toward a low near the ground. A place south of a low receives broadly westerly winds; southeast of it, southwesterly winds; southwest of it, northwesterly winds. The low itself can be moving eastward or northeastward throughout.

For a low passing north of a place, this commonly gives southwest winds before passage, westerlies as the center moves past, and northwest winds afterward. A more southerly track can leave that place north of the low, in easterly winds and colder continental air. The fronts and the air on either side of them determine when precipitation arrives and how temperatures change.

The low's track locates its center; the air reaching a district follows its own path around that moving circulation. The shape of the low, nearby high pressure, and the timing of its fronts affect that path. Similar storm tracks can therefore send air over a mountain barrier or around its end, with very different results inland.

### The broad temperature regime

The region becomes cooler northward. Chardon and the lower Coastlands lie near 37°N, the upper Coastlands around 45°N, Mawar and the southern Slate Sea around 50°N, and the northern Slate Sea shores around 55°N. Mild southern coastal winters give way to increasingly frequent northern cold spells; the warm season becomes shorter and cooler along the northern coast.

The sea moderates exposed shores, limiting both winter cooling and summer heat. Inland valleys and plains warm more strongly in summer and cool more readily on clear nights. Mountain shelter increases these contrasts in Voltara, Erbalta, and Highveil; the plains have warm to hot summer days, cooler nights, and cold winters. Blackwater lies several hundred miles inland at roughly 50–55°N, but its open western approach allows maritime air to interrupt continental cold. The higher northern Sentinel forests remain colder than the low fens.

Elevation adds a separate gradient throughout the region. High slopes and summits can hold snow above rainy lowlands, while shaded woodland and sheltered hollows retain cold after exposed ground warms. During a storm, the temperature of the arriving air determines how far down the slopes snow reaches. A sufficiently deep mild layer melts falling snow into rain; snowfall that reaches low ground may still melt quickly afterward. The seasonal accounts below follow these changing air masses across the underlying temperature pattern.

## Winter

The subtropical ridge withdraws south of Chardon, allowing more active ocean systems and their fronts to reach the southern coast. The winter storm belt includes southern passages across the Coastlands and western forests, northeastward passages through Mawar toward Blackwater, and tracks farther north along the Slate Sea. The path of each low and the reach of its fronts determine which parts of the region share a wet spell.

### Storm passages

Southern lows move broadly west to east, with fronts bringing rain to northern Apporia, Chardon, and the Coastlands before continuing into the southern interior. Lows farther north can also reach Chardon with their trailing southern fronts. Exposed Apporian shores and headlands receive direct wind and ocean swell; the Gulf has greater shelter, although winds crossing its waters can still raise rough conditions. Low ground along the southern and upper Coastlands receives predominantly rain, with snow chiefly on higher terrain toward the Mawar Mountains.

Inland, these southern passages carry moisture into Valandros, the Crimson Forest, Elderwood, the Yeraad low country, and the Chasa basin, well south of Voltara and Blackwater. Maritime air reaches Valandros around the southern Fiatara, with partial shelter from the [[Beacon Hills]]; rain near the lake and in the Crimson Forest gives way to snow on higher Sentinel slopes. Elderwood, Chasa, and the Yeraad lowlands have mild rainy winters, sheltered by the high Sentinels from direct eastern continental air. Precipitation increases where air rises toward the mountains and becomes weaker or patchier beyond intervening ridges. Related disturbances higher in the atmosphere can continue across the Sentinels and organize renewed weather farther east; [[Taelgar Climate Model]] follows that continental connection.

A common northern passage approaches Mawar from the west or southwest, then continues northeast across the Slate Sea, over or north of the northern Fiatara and just north of Blackwater. Around Mawar, shifts in this track determine whether mild southwest air reaches the peninsula or colder air persists north of the low. Rain and thaw alternate with snow, with longer-lasting cover in the hills and sheltered interiors around the Sulqat than on exposed shores. Tawir receives wet maritime air across the Coastlands and southern Slate Sea, while uplift brings heavy snow to the high Fiatara.

As the low moves inland north of the range, western maritime air can reach [[Blackwater Fens]] without crossing the Fiatara barrier. These passages regularly bring rain or thaw to the low fens, though precipitation can begin as snow where cold air remains. Air rising onto the western Sentinel slopes cools and gives the higher northern forests more snow and longer-lasting cover. Blackwater's winter is consequently chill, wet, and changeable, with intermittent snow and freezes between maritime incursions; deep snow and continuous winter ice are more characteristic of colder high ground than of the fens.

South of this route, Voltara and Erbalta remain much more sheltered. When the strongest airflow crosses the Fiatara, it rises on the western slopes and loses moisture as rain or snow. Descending air then compresses and warms, reducing its relative humidity and clearing clouds: the plains receive dry **foehn winds** and sometimes a rapid thaw while the mountains receive snow. A wet passage instead maintains a southwest current through the lower country around the southern end of the range. If a front arrives while that moist supply continues, rising air produces precipitation over Voltara. Differences in wind angle, the duration of the southwest supply, and the depth of the moist air distinguish these outcomes even along similar storm tracks. One system can bring precipitation followed by clearing and a foehn thaw.

Winter continental high pressure often maintains cold, dry air in the sheltered plains and opposes sustained low-level maritime inflow through the southern approach. Moist air that does advance above retained cold air can still produce snow. Successful wet incursions occur, but are less frequent and sustained than in spring; mountain shelter and dry intervals leave central and southern Erbalta and Voltara with relatively low winter precipitation. Erbalta lies deeper in the lee and receives less dependable moisture than Voltara.

Passages well north of Mawar concentrate their wet weather along the northern Slate Sea shores and northern forests. Their southern circulation can still bring milder air into Blackwater, while their direct effects on the southern interior are small. Northern coastal snow is more frequent and lasts longer than at Mawar, but maritime rain and thaw interrupt it. Conversely, a low crossing south of Blackwater can leave the fens in cold easterly air, with snow if its precipitation reaches them. A track between Blackwater and Voltara favors warmer inflow over the plains; whether it brings rain, snow, or little precipitation depends on the moisture route and any retained cold air.

### Between passages

A departing low leaves a changing interval of brisk winds, showers, and sunny breaks. When its center moves east or northeast of Mawakel, northerly or northwesterly winds can draw existing cold air southward, sometimes reinforced by high pressure to the west. If that air is sufficiently cold, crossing the open sea supplies moisture for coastal snow showers. These outbreaks interrupt the milder maritime passages around Mawar and the Slate Sea. Severe winters prolong the cold spells and allow snow and ice to persist around Mawar.

As temporary ridges follow the storms, widespread precipitation subsides and winds often ease. Such a ridge can settle over Mawar even while the main subtropical high remains far to the south. Apporia, Chardon, and the Coastlands retain relatively mild maritime conditions; inland hollows around Chasa, Yeraad, and Valandros cool more on clear nights. Moist ground and lingering maritime air can sustain fog or low cloud between rain bands.

Farther inland and north, continental high pressure brings colder, predominantly dry intervals. In Voltara and southern Erbalta, limited snowfall and foehn thaws leave ordinary snow cover uneven: exposed ground clears while sheltered drifts remain. Cold settled spells can retain snow for weeks, and severe winters sustain it much longer. Northern Erbalta has longer freezes, while shaded Highveil woodland and the northern Ainumarya approaches retain snow and frozen ground after warmer, more exposed plains thaw.

In Blackwater, runoff, peatland storage, and slow winter drying keep the ground wet through the pauses in precipitation. Pools and exposed ground freeze during cold spells while some channels remain open; renewed maritime air brings thaw, and damp ground and fog persist through many quiet intervals. Snow lasts longer in the higher northern Sentinel forests. Prolonged continental cold can deepen the freeze across both, but regular maritime interruptions give the low fens their more variable winter.

## Spring

As the ridge and main storm belt shift northward, wet passages become less frequent around Chardon and northern Apporia, and sunny intervals lengthen across the southern coast and interior. Ocean storms generally weaken, but Mawar, Tawir, and the northern Slate Sea remain exposed to fronts and cool showers.

For Voltara, the retreat of continental winter high pressure makes sustained southwest moisture incursions through the southern Fiatara approach more frequent. More passages successfully carry moist air into the plains while their fronts provide lift. As the land warms, the arriving moisture also supports spring and early-summer showers. This produces a wetter spring even though ocean storms are becoming less frequent farther south. Erbalta receives less dependable rain because stronger mountain shelter limits how far these incursions penetrate; lake and forest evaporation supplement the incoming moisture.

Between passages, inland days warm rapidly while nights remain cool. Cold [[Lake Valandros]] water delays shoreline warming. Blackwater and the northern forests thaw more gradually, with snow lingering longest on higher and shaded ground, including the Highveil and northern Ainumarya approaches. Mountain snowmelt and springs feed rivers and soil moisture as the lower plains warm.

## Summer

The high expands around Chardon and the Gulf, with its offshore ridge extending along the Coastlands and persistent summer influence normally ending south of Mawakel. Most ocean lows are weaker than winter systems and pass north of Mawar, sometimes far enough north to leave even the peninsula settled. Southern dry intervals become long, while northern weather continues to alternate between maritime passages and temporary ridges.

### Storm passages

The northern Slate Sea shores receive maritime rain through their short summers. Closer passages also bring fronts to Mawar and Tawir; at Tawir, westerly air ahead of a low's northeastward departure turns northwesterly behind it, with both directions supplying moisture from the Slate Sea. Mawar's shelter is local and directional, leaving this approach open. Fronts, unstable maritime showers, and uplift release rain over the forest and exposed Fiatara slopes. Together with winter rain, these passages give Tawir ordinary year-round rainfall.

Northern systems carry moist air inland toward Blackwater and the western Sentinel forests, with greater precipitation on exposed slopes and less beyond intervening ridges. Inland warmth can support summer showers even where the maritime fronts weaken. Strong summer storms remain possible, though quieter spells occupy more of the season than in winter.

When the ridge weakens along its northern side, fronts can reach Valandros and the western forests, and southwest incursions can continue into Voltara. By later summer, a larger share of lows passes too far north to sustain that southern moisture route, while the stronger ridge lengthens settled intervals. Occasional fronts and thunderstorms still interrupt the plains' sunshine; Erbalta receives less reliable rain behind the Fiatara. Chardon, farther south beneath the ridge, remains predominantly dry during many of these inland and northern wet spells.

### Between passages

Clockwise flow around the offshore high supports north–northwest winds along exposed northern Apporian and Chardon shores, with a western component farther north. Northeasterly trades occupy its equatorward flank over the open ocean. Around sheltered waters and under weak pressure gradients, sea breezes, lake breezes, and local terrain become more influential.

Chardon's summer is very dry. Shallow marine air cools exposed shores and parts of the Coastlands while inland Chasa valleys heat strongly. A warmer, stable layer above confines this moisture near the surface, allowing fog or low cloud while suppressing the deep cloud growth needed for substantial showers. The layer's depth and local hill gaps govern how far its cooling reaches inland. Fog recurs intermittently, with warm sunny spells between episodes; it provides coastal cooling and local surface moisture without sustaining summer rains.

Valandros moderates nearby summer heat. Cool lake air can suppress cloud growth near the shore, while converging winds along a lake-breeze boundary help showers develop farther inland when sufficient moisture and instability are present. Warm plains and valleys contrast with the high Fiatara and Sentinels, where snowfields and glaciers persist; meltwater sustains rivers and riparian farming through dry periods.

%% Physical reference: [lake-breeze boundaries](https://www.weather.gov/apx/lake_breeze). %%

The Crimson Forest and Elderwood share the southern summer rainfall minimum. Winter recharge, groundwater, sheltered soils, and occasional showers supply their ordinary water, while humidity and shade reduce losses. Within the [[Crimson Forest]] and [[Elderwood]], elven magic renews summer mist, dripping foliage, and damp soils, sustaining exceptional lushness beside dry Chasa fields. Its workings and precise boundaries remain unresolved; see [[Magical Climate Departures#Crimson Forest and Elderwood summer moisture]]. Forest and mountain runoff also feeds the [[Yeraad]] marshes through local drying.

Toward [[Highveil Forest]], diminishing elven influence and stronger Fiatara shelter increase summer moisture stress and continental temperature contrasts, though exposed foothills retain wetter pockets. Chataan shelter strengthens the dry transition along the southern Elderwood margin and [[Myraeni Gap]]. The [[Forest of Nightmares]] has a separate, unresolved magical climate.

Around Mawar and the Slate Sea, temporary ridges bring fine spells between fronts. Western or northwestern maritime air can continue arriving along the northern side of the ocean high, keeping exposed shores cooler than sheltered interiors. Tawir retains damp, shaded woodland through these breaks; Blackwater's cool, often foggy conditions and short drying season help preserve wet ground. Sunny spells warm the northern inland forests more strongly than the exposed coast.

At the southern edge of this circulation, the Nevos monsoon's northern reach fluctuates around southern [[Emerald Bay]]. Its trough supports west–southwest inflow through the Darba gap. Active spells bring rain and squalls toward the canal approaches; quieter spells allow lighter winds or an easterly component around the Chardon high. Northern Apporia remains predominantly summer-dry. The low lies broadly north to northwest of the principal Nevos inflow; [[Dunmar and Nevos Circulation]] explains the connected system.

## Autumn

The ridge and main storm belt shift southward again, and strengthening ocean systems restore more frequent wet passages to the Coastlands, Chardon, northern Apporia, and the southern interior. Apporia's mountains divide exposed slopes from sheltered valleys, making the return of rain uneven across the peninsula. Farther north, passing systems increasingly bring lowland rain beside mountain snow, while continental cold begins to build between them.

Warm, settled spells can linger, but clear inland nights cool faster than exposed ocean shores. Valandros releases stored summer heat and delays cooling along its banks. Colder air crossing the open lake gains moisture and can produce stronger showers downwind, especially over rising land to the east; the same process continues into winter where water remains open. In Blackwater, autumn cooling further reduces drying and supports frequent mist, while returning cold spells bring freezes to the fens and longer-lasting snow to the higher northern forests.
