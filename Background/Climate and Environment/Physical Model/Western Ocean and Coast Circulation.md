# Western Ocean and Coast Circulation

**Scope:** Seasonal atmospheric circulation over the western ocean and its coast, from Apporia and Chardon north through the Coastlands to Mawakel and the Slate Sea, together with Tawir, Valandros, the western forests, and the Fiatara–Voltara–Erbalta interior. The northern margin includes Blackwater and the forests north of the Fiatara; the southern edge includes the northern fringe of the Nevos monsoon.

The western shores face the [[Endless Ocean]] and, farther north, the [[Slate Sea]]. The [[Mawar Mountains]] and [[Fiatara Mountains]] interrupt maritime air routes inland; lower hills and forested basins lead toward the much higher Sentinels. Western Apporia faces the open ocean, while the [[Gulf of Chardon]] lies partly sheltered behind the peninsula. This varied exposure determines how far ocean air penetrates and where it rises, loses moisture, or descends into sheltered country.

## The circulation framework

The broad driver of the climate and weather of the western coasts of Taelgar is the interplay among the subtropical high, which expands northward in summer and retreats southward in winter; passing ocean lows and fronts, which bring rain and snow; and mountains, which enhance precipitation on exposed slopes and limit how much moisture reaches the interior. These are developed in more detail in the sections below. 

### The ridge and the moving storm belt

The western ocean high is a broad area of high pressure, with an elongated northern extension called a **ridge**. Sinking air beneath it suppresses widespread rain. Farther north, temperate westerlies carry moving **lows** and their fronts generally eastward or northeastward. A front is the boundary between different air masses; lifting along it can produce a broad band of rain or snow well away from the low's center.

The ridge and the main storm belt shift northward toward summer and southward toward winter. Chardon consequently spends much of summer beneath the ridge, while winter fronts reach the city and adjoining country more often. Mawar and the northern coast remain within reach of ocean weather through summer, though passing lows decrease in frequency and  shift north. These are broad seasonal shifts: individual lows follow varying tracks, and the ridge expands, weakens, and changes shape between passages.

In winter, cold air also accumulates over the northern interior, where continental high pressure can sustain long cold spells. Ocean storms encounter this cold air as they approach the northern coast and forests. Their tracks determine whether a place receives milder maritime air, retains cold inland air, or experiences a change between the two.

### Winds around a passing low

Wind names describe where air comes from. Winds circulate counterclockwise around lows and clockwise around highs, with some inward flow toward a low near the ground. A place south of a low therefore receives broadly westerly winds; southeast of it, southwesterly winds; southwest of it, northwesterly winds, etc. The low itself can be moving eastward throughout.

For a low passing north of a place, this can give a sequence of southwest winds before passage, westerlies as the center moves past, and northwest winds afterward. The fronts and the air on either side of them determine when rain arrives and how temperatures change. A more southerly track can instead leave that place north of the low, in easterly winds and colder air.

Here, **passing ocean systems** means moving lows and their accompanying fronts. An **upper-air disturbance** is a related feature higher in the atmosphere, which can continue across mountains even when the surface circulation changes.

### Frequency and the weather between passages

Closely spaced systems leave short breaks between wet, windy spells. When their tracks move away or their arrivals become less frequent, settled intervals grow longer. A wet season can therefore contain fine days, and year-round rainfall can coexist with stretches of dry weather.

A departing low can leave brisk maritime winds, scattered showers, and sunny breaks after its main rain band has gone. As a temporary ridge moves in, widespread precipitation subsides and winds often ease. Such a ridge can pass through Mawar without the main subtropical high extending that far north. Moist air may retain low cloud or fog; clearer skies allow sunny days but faster cooling at night. A winter ridge following a cold outbreak can preserve frost and snow, while a summer ridge favors warmer days. More persistent high pressure prolongs these conditions by diverting approaching systems.

Wind also continues around distant pressure systems. Maritime air can keep arriving during a settled spell; fronts, unstable showers, and exposed slopes provide the uplift that can turn that moisture into rain. Near weak pressure gradients, sea breezes, lake breezes, and local terrain become more influential.

## Winter circulation

### Storm tracks and coastal rain

The subtropical ridge withdraws south of Chardon, and more active ocean systems reach the western coast. Their fronts extend the winter rain belt into northern Apporia, Chardon, the Chasa basin, and the western forests. A low passing farther north can bring rain to Chardon through its southern fronts; its center need not cross the city.

West–southwest inflow from milder ocean waters carries moisture into Mawakel and across the Coastlands toward Tawir. Exposed Apporian shores and headlands receive direct wind and ocean swell; the Gulf of Chardon has greater shelter, although winds crossing its waters can still raise rough conditions. Between systems, southern coastal lowlands remain ocean-moderated while inland hollows cool more readily, especially under clear skies. Upper-air disturbances can continue eastward across the Sentinels and help organize renewed weather beyond them; the connected account is in [[Taelgar Climate Model]].

A low departing east or northeast of Mawakel can place the peninsula in its western or southwestern circulation, drawing colder air southward from the north or northwest. Higher pressure to the west can reinforce that flow. The low transports existing northern cold air; a northwest wind becomes a snow-bearing outbreak only when that air is cold enough. Crossing relatively warmer open water supplies moisture and can produce coastal snow showers. Returning southwest maritime air brings rain and thaw, sometimes beginning as snow over lingering cold ground-level air.

### Rain, snow, and winter temperatures

The rain–snow boundary shifts with the arriving air and with elevation. Snow falling into a sufficiently deep mild layer melts into rain; colder air allows it to reach lower ground. Snowfall and lasting cover are separate effects: wet snow can melt promptly on a mild coast, while sheltered inland ground retains snow through a long dry interval.

%% Physical reference: [NWS explanation of how the vertical temperature profile determines precipitation type](https://www.weather.gov/ama/winterpreciptypes). %%

#### Along the coast, from north to south

- **Northern Slate Sea shores** have the longest coastal snowy season. Ocean passages interrupt cold spells with rain and thaw, while renewed northern airflow brings snow back to low ground.
- **Mawakel and Mawar** alternate rain, snow, and thaw. Ordinary coastal snow comes and goes; the Mawar relief and hills around the Sulqat retain colder sheltered interiors and elevated snowfields beside thawing shores. Sheltered inlets can escape strong winds while retaining frosty nights. Severe winters sustain much longer freezes.
- **Tawir and the upper Coastlands** receive mild, wet southwest passages, with rain in low country and snow restricted to higher ground near the [[Mawar Mountains]]. 
- **The southern Coastlands, Chardon, and northern Apporia** receive winter rains. 
#### Inland, from north to south

- **Blackwater Fens, and especially the northern forests on the western Sentinel slopes** have long, cold winters, with precipiation determined by the interplay of continental high pressure and maritime disturbances. Northern high pressure brings predominantly dry, cold spells, though cold and existing snow persist; when the high weakens or retreats, maritime disturbances bring unsettled weather, snow where cold air persists and rain where mild ocean air penetrates inland. In the low-lying [[Blackwater Fens]], lower elevation and greater access to mild maritime air make rain and winter thaws more common than in the higher Sentinel forests; precipitation and runoff, slow winter drying, and peatland storage sustain wet ground through alternating freezes and thaws. Some channels can remain open while pools or exposed ground freeze; fog and damp ground can persist between precipitation events.
- **Northern Erbalta**, east of the Fiatara, is more sheltered from maritime moisture. Rising air loses rain and snow on the exposed mountains, leaving the plains in a drier lee. Longer inland freezes allow limited snowfall to persist more readily than on the southern plains.
- **Central and southern Erbalta and Voltara** also have relatively dry winters. Tracks typically pass near or south of Voltara in the winter, bringing moist air over the mountains; more northerly tracks that deliver moist southwest air around the southern Fiataras are less frequent than in spring, and continental high pressure helps sustain cold, dry inland air. Flow crossing the [[Fiatara Mountains]] produce heavy snow on the summits and descend warmer and drier, producing episodic foehn thaws in Voltara and the plains. Limited snowfall and these thaws leave ordinary lowland cover uneven and intermittent: exposed ground clears while sheltered drifts remain. Cold settled spells can retain snow for weeks, and severe winters sustain it much longer.

  %% Physical comparison: [Chinook thaws along the Alberta foothills](https://albertaparks.ca/media/3331519/bcbc_final_web.pdf). %%

- **Highveil and the northern Ainumarya approaches** have stronger inland cooling and mountain shelter than the low southern forests. Snow and frozen ground last longer in shaded woodland and sheltered valleys, even when a maritime passage thaws more exposed lower ground.
- **Valandros and the adjoining foothills** occupy a variable rain–snow transition along the elevational gradient of the Sentinels. Western and southwestern air enters well south of the main Fiatara barrier, with partial shelter from the [[Beacon Hills]]. Southwest inflow from milder ocean waters produces rain near the lake and lower slopes, around the Crimson Forest, and snow in the higher elevations. 
- **Elderwood, Chasa, and the Yeraad low country** have mild rainy winters. The high Sentinels limit direct eastern continental air reaching these low southern forests, while maritime air moderates temperatures.

Across these latitude bands, elevation adds a separate contrast: uplift increases precipitation on exposed mountain slopes, and colder high ground retains snow long after nearby lowlands thaw. The high Sentinels carry substantial winter snow above the rainy southern forests as well as above the colder northern country.

## Spring transition

As the ridge and main storm tracks shift northward, wet passages become less frequent around Chardon and northern Apporia, and sunny intervals lengthen. Ocean storms generally weaken, but cool showers and unsettled spells can persist well into spring at Mawakel. Northern inland cold gradually relaxes, with snow lingering longest on higher and shaded ground.

For Voltara, this seasonal shift makes useful rain-bearing tracks more frequent. More lows pass through positions northwest of the plains, drawing moist southwest air across the Coastlands and around the southern end of the Fiatara through lower foothill country. The weakening continental winter high allows these incursions to penetrate inland more readily. Fronts and rising terrain release rain; as the land warms, the arriving moisture also supports spring and early-summer showers. Erbalta lies farther into the rain shadow and receives less dependable rain: stronger mountain shelter outweighs its cooler conditions and lower water loss. Lake and forest evaporation supplement the incoming ocean moisture.

Cold [[Lake Valandros]] water delays shoreline warming while nearby land warms in the sun. Mountain snowmelt and springs feed rivers and soil moisture downstream; higher and northern wooded foothills can retain snow after the lower Voltara plains have thawed.

## Summer circulation

### The ridge and coastal air

The high expands around Chardon and the Gulf. Its ridge extends offshore along the [[Coastlands]], with persistent summer influence normally ending south of Mawakel. Summer ocean lows are weaker on average than winter systems, and their centers usually pass north of Mawar, sometimes far enough north to leave even Mawakel settled. Closer passages still bring fronts and rain to the northern coast; strong summer storms remain possible. Across Chasa and northern Apporia, settled intervals dominate.

Clockwise flow around the offshore high supports north–northwest winds along the exposed shores of northern Apporia and Chardon, and a western component farther north. Northeasterly trades occupy its equatorward flank farther south over the open western ocean. Over the northern Nevos, the monsoon trough shapes the local pressure pattern, supporting west–southwest inflow through the Darba gap. Weak pressure differences near the ridge favor lighter or variable winds and local breezes around sheltered waters.

Chardon's summer remains very dry. Shallow marine air can cool the coast while inland Chasa valleys heat strongly. Cooling beneath a warmer, stable cap can produce fog or low cloud, but the cap confines the moist air near the surface and suppresses the deep cloud growth needed for substantial showers. The layer's depth and local hill gaps govern how far this cooling reaches inland. Fog recurs intermittently, with warm sunny spells between episodes; it provides coastal cooling and local surface moisture rather than a summer rainy season.

### Northern passages and inland rain

Beyond the persistent ridge, Mawakel alternates ocean fronts with fine spells beneath temporary ridges. At Tawir, a low passing to the north can supply westerly air, turning northwesterly as the center moves northeast. Both directions give access to moisture from the Slate Sea. The ocean high to the south or southwest can support similar winds along its northern or northeastern flank, but the arrival of a front, unstable maritime showers, or uplift over terrain determines whether that air produces rain.

Summer Slate Sea rain combines with the winter west–southwest passages to give Tawir ordinary year-round rainfall. Mawar shelter is local and directional, leaving the Slate Sea approach open. Sunny and dry intervals occur between wet spells; cool conditions, shade, and retained forest water limit drying and support moist, foggy woodland. Farther north, coastal districts retain maritime rain through their short summers, while sheltered inland forests have greater seasonal temperature contrasts and lower overall moisture. Summer showers can still be important there. Blackwater's cool, foggy summer gives its wet ground only a short drying season.

When the ridge weakens on its northern side, a passing ocean system can also draw moist air northeastward into Valandros and the forests, with frontal lifting and rising ground producing occasional rain. Chardon, farther south beneath the ridge, remains predominantly dry. By later summer, a larger share of lows passes too far north to favor Voltara's southwest moisture route, and the stronger subtropical ridge lengthens settled intervals. Thunderstorms and occasional fronts still interrupt the sunshine; Erbalta remains less reliably watered behind the Fiatara. High snowfields and glaciers persist above the warm plains, and meltwater sustains rivers and riparian farming through dry periods.

At Valandros, cool lake air can suppress cloud growth near the shore while convergence along a lake-breeze boundary helps showers develop farther inland when sufficient moisture and instability are present. This modifies the ocean-fed air locally; the lake does not replace its regional source.

### Forest moisture

Unlike Tawir, the Crimson Forest and Elderwood have an ordinary summer rainfall minimum. Winter recharge, groundwater, sheltered soils, and occasional showers provide their baseline water supply; humidity and shade reduce water loss. Within the [[Crimson Forest]] and [[Elderwood]], elven magic renews summer mist, dripping foliage, and damp soils, sustaining exceptional lushness beside dry Chasa fields. Its workings and precise boundaries remain unresolved; see [[Magical Climate Departures#Crimson Forest and Elderwood summer moisture]].

%% Physical comparison: [coast redwood forests](https://research.fs.usda.gov/silvics/redwood) illustrate the effects of stored winter moisture, humidity, shade, and fog on summer water loss. %%

Toward [[Highveil Forest]], diminishing elven influence and stronger Fiatara shelter increase summer moisture stress and continental temperature contrasts. Exposed foothills retain wetter pockets. Forest and mountain runoff feeds the [[Yeraad]] marshes through local drying; Chataan shelter strengthens the dry transition along the southern Elderwood margin and [[Myraeni Gap]]. The [[Forest of Nightmares]] has a separate, unresolved magical climate.

### Emerald Bay and the Nevos

At the southern edge of this circulation, the Nevos monsoon's northern reach fluctuates around southern [[Emerald Bay]]. Active spells bring west–southwest winds, rain, and squalls toward the canal approaches; quieter spells allow lighter winds or an easterly component around the Chardon high. These incursions leave northern Apporia predominantly summer-dry. The low lies broadly north to northwest of the principal Nevos inflow; [[Dunmar and Nevos Circulation]] explains its position and the Darba inflow route.

## Autumn transition

The ridge and main storm tracks shift southward again, and strengthening ocean systems restore more frequent wet passages to the southern coast. Warm, settled spells can linger between them. Inland clear nights cool faster than exposed ocean shores, and rain on low ground increasingly accompanies snow higher up. Apporia's mountains continue to divide exposed slopes from sheltered valleys, so the return of rain varies within the peninsula. In northern forests and wetlands, earlier frost and renewed snowfall begin the long cold season.

Valandros releases stored summer heat, delaying cooling along its shores. When colder air crosses open water, it can gain moisture and produce stronger showers downwind, especially over rising land to the east. This lake effect can also operate during winter where water remains open; its strength depends on wind, the available water surface, and terrain. In Blackwater, moist air over cool waterways supports frequent mist as autumn cooling further reduces drying.
