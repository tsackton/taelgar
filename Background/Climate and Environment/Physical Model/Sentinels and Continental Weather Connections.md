# Sentinels and Continental Weather Connections

**Scope:** The [[Sentinel Range]] as a mountain climate and a boundary between western ocean weather and the eastern continent, including its connections to [[Zimkova]], the [[Pekul Mountains]], the [[Vostok Plateau]], [[Greater Sembara]], and Aurbez. This note also develops the snow and glacier mechanisms of the separate [[Svinjo Mountains|Svinjo]] and [[Yengorza Mountains|Yengorza]] highlands where the northern continental interior meets Green Sea weather. [[Climate of the Sentinels and Northern Highlands]] defines the Sentinel and interior highland coverage; Svinjo, Isenborg, and Yengorza belong in [[Climate of the Green Sea and Its Shores]], and western forested foothills in [[Climate of the Western Coast and Interior]].

This is a qualitative physical explanation beneath [[Taelgar Climate Model#Sentinels and Northern Highlands|the fixed continental overview]]. [[Climate and Weather Constraints#The Sentinels]] records the required Sentinel outcomes and the weaker standing of proposed winter crossings; its [[Climate and Weather Constraints#Green Sea, Skaerhem and Ursk|Green Sea section]] supplies the separate northern mountain requirements. The mechanisms and local episodes below remain nonbinding model development, with no assigned snowline, glacier extent, climate normals, or pass-opening calendar.

## Moisture on the two sides

Western winter storms and eastern summer moisture reach different faces of the range. The [[Sentinel Range]] extends for more than 2,300 miles, so latitude, height and access to those moisture routes all change along it. A snowy northern western slope, a mild southern forest valley, and a dry high basin can belong to the same mountain system.

**Western precipitation increases where maritime air rises.** Ocean storms cross the Coastlands and western basins, lose moisture on intervening hills, and lift again over the Sentinel slopes. This gives much of the west a winter precipitation advantage. Some moisture spills across the crest, but air descending eastward generally becomes drier. A rain shadow is a reduction in moisture supply, not a guarantee of cloudless or snow-free weather.

**Eastern summer moisture arrives from the Green Sea.** The continental summer pressure pattern supplies maritime air to Sembara; exposed foothills and uplands can lift that air into showers or longer rain spells. Ardlas consequently retains a summer rainfall emphasis despite drier, frosty winter intervals. The inflow's reach varies with intervening terrain and the weather system. It does not require equally wet eastern slopes from northern Vostok to southern Dunmar, or a continuous summer-rain corridor across the crest.

**Northern interior cold is a separate influence.** Winter high pressure and cold outbreaks across Zimkova and interior Vostok reinforce cold already created by elevation and mountain shelter. The highlands can remain frozen while the Western Gulf lowlands thaw. Conversely, the recurring cool maritime ridge over the northern Green Sea in summer is not a permanent high over the whole Sentinel–Vostok interior; the revised continental circulation replaces that older explanation.

Upper-air disturbances can cross the mountains even when low-level maritime air is blocked. They may help organize the [[#Winter storms from Chardon to the Green Sea|southern winter storm corridor]], which draws a fresh moisture supply from the Green Sea. This explains how eastern winter rain can accompany a strong western rain shadow without carrying the same parcel of wet coastal air unchanged across the range.

## Snow, ice, and mountain temperature

Mountain climate requires both an elevation gradient and local exposure. Higher ground is generally colder, but sheltered valley floors can become colder than nearby slopes on a still, clear night. Cold dense air gathers in hollows, sometimes producing frost, fog, or a persistent inversion with warmer air above. Wind and mixing can end that arrangement abruptly. This mechanism supports Ardlas's frosty valley intervals and cold northern basins without making all low valleys colder than summits.

Snow depth is not a simple map of rainfall. Wind can scour exposed crests and deposit deep drifts behind them; shaded hollows lose snow more slowly than sunny slopes. A dry ridge and a deep snowbed can therefore sit close together. The elevation at which snow is falling during one storm also differs from the lowest snow remaining on the ground and from the terrain that retains snow through summer.

> [!tip] How a dry range can still have glaciers
> Glaciers persist where snow accumulation and ice supplied from higher terrain can offset losses over time. Cold high summits and shaded gathering basins can preserve ice even where nearby slopes are dry and most seasonal snow melts. A surviving glacier does not require snowfall every week, permanent snow in adjacent valleys, or a summer that never rises above freezing. See the [USGS explanation of glacier mass balance](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2009/3046/).

## Four-season behavior

### Winter

> [!image|hero]
> ![[assets/worldbuilding/taelgar-sentinels-winter-v3.png]]
> *Winter continental cold and western moisture supply across the full range. Snow persistence and pass conditions depend on height and exposure.*

Western ocean disturbances deliver predominantly winter rain to the naturally mild, broad low foothills of Crimson and Elderwood, without magical warming, and snow higher up. Northern and central high terrain repeatedly accumulates snow; a single warmer passage can raise the rain–snow boundary without removing the established high ice. Winds produce uneven loading across crests and gullies. In colder lower western districts, wet snow and thaw can alternate, while the far northern forests retain a longer cold season.

East of the crest, ordinary westerly flow is depleted of moisture. Cold continental highs and sheltered basins support dry, frosty spells across Ardlas, Breva, Zimkova, and interior Vostok, interrupted by snow-bearing disturbances. Breva's lasting ordinary winter snow is compatible with these drier intervals because cold ground loses snow slowly. Exposed plateau winds and quiet valley inversions represent different expressions of the same cold season.

Farther south, the Aurbez Plateau is broadly cold and dry, with episodic snowfall and milder breaks. The higher southern Sentinel passes close under winter snow even when nearby Dunmari or Andonne lowlands remain much warmer. Passing lows south of the Sembaran heartlands can draw eastern moisture inland and reverse the usual west–east precipitation contrast for an individual event.

### Spring

Low valleys warm before the snow-covered heights. Meltwater rises in streams while higher accumulation continues during colder storms. Southern and sun-exposed slopes usually lose seasonal snow earlier than shaded or northern terrain; different valleys can enter spring at very different times. A warm interval followed by renewed snow is an ordinary model possibility, not evidence that a pass has opened for the season.

Western disturbances still reach the range while eastern lowlands begin warming and developing summer inflow. Fronts, slope uplift, and maritime incursions make spring unsettled on both sides without erasing the drier eastern interiors. The southern highlands can receive important spring wet spells even though Aurbez's later summer is dry. Snowmelt helps supply the plateau's reliable streams independently of current rain.

Zimkova and the Vostok uplands retain colder nights and later thaw than Sembaran lowlands. Lower basins warm enough for growth while higher ridges remain wintry. The precise thaw order among named valleys depends on altitude, aspect, snow storage, and maritime access rather than a single date for the entire region.

### Summer

> [!image|hero]
> ![[assets/worldbuilding/taelgar-sentinels-summer-v3.png]]
> *Summer moisture approaches from different sides of the range, with drier southern high country and persistent high-elevation ice.*

Crimson and Elderwood warm under predominantly dry Mediterranean summer weather. Within these forests, adopted elven magic supplies or enhances summer moisture through recurring mist, canopy drip and damp soil; its exact operation and bounds remain unresolved. Weaker magical influence and Fiatara shelter toward Highveil allow more open, drier woodland before the continental northern forests. High terrain remains much colder. Northern and central snowfields and glaciers persist through periods of daytime melting; fresh high-elevation snow can interrupt otherwise fine weather. Warm weather in a valley therefore gives little assurance of conditions at the crest.

Green Sea inflow supplies humid spells and rain to Sembaran eastern foothills, especially exposed Ardlas slopes. Daytime heating can help form mountain showers when enough moisture is present; cloudy, longer wet spells also occur with passing systems. The more sheltered Vostok Plateau retains its cold, dry continental character overall, while lower basins have a usable warmer season. Summer rain need not be uniform across the northern highlands.

Southern high country remains broadly dry. Occasional moist incursions from the neighboring seasonal rain systems can produce storms on exposed foothills or peaks, but terrain and limited moisture prevent a continuous wet belt. Aurbez's easterly summer flow is commonly dry; an easterly wind alone does not prove Green Sea moisture has reached the mountain slope. Lower southern valleys can be warm while nights and exposed high passes remain cold.

### Autumn

Renewed western disturbances and falling temperatures begin rebuilding high-country snow. Initial falls may melt on sunny southern slopes while lingering in shaded northern hollows. High routes can become difficult before winter settles into nearby lowlands. Autumn rain on existing snow can increase runoff, followed by refreezing when colder air returns; neither the size of those events nor their frequency is fixed.

Summer Green Sea inflow weakens while fronts and colder continental outbreaks gain influence in the eastern highlands. Frost and valley fog return, with snow lasting progressively longer in Breva and the colder northern basins. The southern plateau and passes cool more slowly, but a dry, clear spell can still have sharply colder nights. A fine valley day and a wind-driven mountain whiteout can occur within the same regional weather sequence.

## Recurring mountain weather episodes

| Episode | Sequence | Contrasts to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Western storm and lee clearing | Moist western air rises, bringing rain or snow; some air crosses the crest and descends. | A wet western face can coexist with a relatively dry eastern valley. Spillover and local storms remain possible. |
| Sheltered winter cold pool | Clear, weak-wind weather lets cold air collect in a basin; cloud, wind, or a new air mass eventually changes it. | Frost and fog below may persist while exposed slopes above are sunnier or temporarily warmer. |
| Eastern summer upslope rain | Green Sea moisture reaches Sembara and rises against exposed eastern foothills. | Wet Ardlas slopes can coexist with drying western forests or drier interior basins; the whole range need not share a storm. |
| High-country summer storm | An ocean disturbance or a moist convective episode reaches cold high terrain. | Rain below, hail or snow higher up, and rapid loss of visibility are possible above seasonally warm valleys. |
| Southern thaw and renewed closure | A warm spring spell melts lower snow, followed by a colder passage over the range. | Visible thaw and rising streams do not establish sustained access through higher passes. |
| Local downslope thaw | Cross-range flow descends into selected lee valleys, warming and drying as it falls. | A brief warmer spell can interrupt eastern frost without changing the plateau's winter climate or thawing every sheltered hollow. |

These are candidate recurring episodes, not claims about particular played days or a measured frequency. The [Met Office discussion of foehn winds](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/types-of-weather/wind/foehn-effect) supports the downslope mechanism; the [National Weather Service explanation of valley fog](https://www.weather.gov/safety/fog-mountain-valley) supports cold-air pooling.

## Northern Green Sea highlands

The [[Svinjo Mountains]] form Vostok's eastern mountain barrier, with high passes and glacier-scoured cols above the wooded lower country. [[Isenborg]] occupies high terrain within that range. The [[Yengorza Mountains]] are a separate northern Ursk mountain setting. These highlands share the combination of northern continental cold, elevation, and intermittent maritime moisture; they are not additional sectors of the Sentinels. Their regional description belongs in [[Climate of the Green Sea and Its Shores]], under the [[Taelgar Climate Model#Northern shores and forest hinterlands|continental northern-shore account]].

**Winter and early spring** alternate cold, clear interior spells with snow-bearing disturbances. Moving northern lows can draw maritime moisture toward the highlands, with ascent over exposed slopes producing snow. The Green Sea supplies moisture on suitable approaches; cold, depleted air arriving across the interior can leave other slopes much drier. The favored face changes with the storm's track and local relief. On exposed saddles, falling and wind-driven snow can erase visibility even while a sheltered lower valley has less severe conditions. This accommodates the cold clear approach, natural whiteout on March 16, and clearing on March 17, DR 1749, in [[Session 91 (DuFr)]]. That sequence establishes an ordinary weather event, not an annual March storm or a known wind direction.

**Later spring** brings thaw to lower valleys while deep drifts and shaded snowbeds remain above. Wind transfers snow from exposed ridges into hollows and onto lee slopes, building cornices and uneven snow loads. Fresh snow can renew those loads; warming can weaken overhangs and permit avalanches after lower country has begun to thaw. These mechanisms retain the late-spring cornices and avalanche chutes described in the Svinjo note without assigning a hazard calendar to every pass. The [Colorado Avalanche Information Center's wind-slab](https://avalanche.state.co.us/wind-slab) and [cornice explanations](https://avalanche.state.co.us/cornice-fall) support the processes, without transferring Colorado's prevailing winds or dates.

**Short summers** allow melt and growth in lower country while ordinary glaciers persist at Isenborg and in Yengorza. The Isenborg glacier is directly encountered in [[Session 92 (DuFr)]], and [[Radomir]] records the Yengorza glacier. Repeated cold-season accumulation, snow gathered into favorable basins, and limited high-elevation summer melt provide the same qualitative balance described above. They do not require permanent snow across the neighboring grain-growing valleys of Ursk. Passing wet spells can bring rain below and fresh snow on sufficiently cold heights; maritime ridge spells or sheltered interior weather can instead be comparatively dry. **Autumn** cooling and renewed northern disturbances rebuild seasonal snow on high terrain before it becomes persistent in lower valleys.

K28 and E18 in [[Climate and Weather Constraints]] require ordinary glaciers and natural mountain snow hazards. Exact glacier boundaries, elevations, accumulation totals, and seasonal opening dates remain unknown. [[Green Sea Atmospheric Circulation]] supplies the neighboring maritime weather, but no single sea-level wind pattern determines conditions across every mountain face. Isenborg's magical lights and the deeper supernatural ice habitats do not supply the explanation for its ordinary glacier or for the observed natural whiteout.

## Winter storms from Chardon to the Green Sea

> [!image|hero]
> ![[assets/worldbuilding/taelgar-sentinels-winter-redevelopment-v1.png]]
> *Illustrative winter redevelopment: blue dashed arrows show the movement of a disturbance and a candidate low; purple shows surface moisture flow. The low and track are one possible episode, not an additional fixed seasonal center.*

The working model retains a candidate **southern winter storm corridor** connecting the western coast, the Sentinels, Greater Sembara, and the Green Sea. It supplies intermittent winter rain or snow to the Sembaran lowlands and Cymea. Sembara keeps its spring–summer precipitation emphasis, with useful cool-season wet spells; a pronounced winter drought is not required. The colder northern interior retains stronger continental drying.

- **Approach from the west:** disturbances in the winds high above the ground move eastward across the western coast and Chardon. A disturbance is a bend in the upper-air flow that can organize rising air and low pressure below. The Sentinels remove much of the incoming moisture and disrupt surface weather systems, but the disturbance overhead can continue across them.
- **Formation or reorganization east of the Sentinels:** a low develops or reforms downstream of the mountains. This process is called **lee cyclogenesis**. The candidate surface route runs from the eastern side of the Sentinels through the **Maseau–Mostreve sector, toward Tyrwingha and Cymea**, generally south of the main Sembaran agricultural heartlands. This is a broad corridor, not a surveyed track or a requirement that every low follow the same path.
- **Maritime moisture drawn inland:** while the low lies south or southeast of the heartlands, its counterclockwise circulation brings air from the **east or northeast**, across the Western Gulf and into Sembara. Moist air rises over colder inland air and over exposed slopes, producing broad areas of cloud, rain, or snow. This eastern moisture supply does not need to survive a crossing of the Sentinels. The low's precise position and the depth of the cold air determine local winds and precipitation type.
- **Departure over the Green Sea:** the low moves eastward offshore. Some systems weaken; others strengthen over open water and curve northeast into the northern storm track, toward waters off Ursk and the eastern sea. As a low moves away from Sembara, winds can turn northerly and then westerly, bringing colder or drier air and clearing intervals. Neither every low nor all its rain must travel the full route.

> [!tip] An eastbound storm can bring westbound winds
> A weather system's movement is different from the air circulating around it. On the northern side of a Northern Hemisphere low, air generally travels east → west even while the whole low moves east. The associated rain or snow can also extend well beyond its center. A sequence of these events can interrupt Sembara's prevailing winter westerlies without replacing them with a permanent winter monsoon.

The [[Western Gulf]] remains relatively sheltered in this model. Its ordinary winter rainmakers are weaker lows and the broad fronts associated with nearby systems; the most vigorous development usually occurs farther offshore. A rainy spell over farmland need not coincide with severe gales throughout the gulf.

These winter lows differ from the Cymean summer trough: they are moving weather systems organized by upper-air disturbances and temperature contrasts, rather than the recurring summer pattern driven by continental heating. Their passage may give Cymea appreciable winter rainfall, but does not yet establish the winter rainfall of all South Cymea or the Maritime Trade Peninsula. Mediterranean lee cyclogenesis is a useful physical comparison, not a demonstration that the proposed Taelgar track must occur.

## Neighboring regions and evidence limits

The western forests receive their principal maritime supply from the routes described in [[Western Ocean and Coast Circulation]]. Their lowland moderation does not extend to the whole high range. Conversely, the Sentinel snow regime does not require every western forest or the Blackwater Fens to freeze solid each winter. Blackwater's accepted February open-water observation remains local and qualified.

Eastward, the highlands grade into the more moderated Sembaran riverlands and coast. The [[Green Sea Atmospheric Circulation#Seasonal circulation over Sembara|Sembaran seasonal account]] places mountain moisture within the annual maritime inflow and passing winter storms. Cold air moving out of the northern highlands can reach Sembara and the gulf, but mountain severity is not a temperature prescription for Tollen or Tyrwingha.

Aurbez is an elevated, drier neighbor with important spring rain, warm summer days, cool nights, and episodic winter snow. The [[Aurbez Plateau]] receives reliable mountain-fed streams despite limited local rain. Its lower country can be hotter than the plateau, and seasonal meltwater does not require a rainy summer. Farther south, [[Dunmar and Nevos Circulation]] owns the Nevos monsoon; occasional moisture reaching southern Sentinel foothills does not join it into one continuous Sembaran monsoon.

[[Zimkova]] contains both high ridges and lower river depressions, so its climate cannot be represented by a single elevation. [[Ardlas]] supplies the short, mild, summer-rain growing-season constraint; Breva supplies firm bitter winter and lasting ordinary snow evidence. The Pekul and Vostok highlands extend the continental overview into more sparsely described terrain. Their detailed valley climates, storm exposure and transitions into adjoining northern grasslands remain geographic projections.

## Connections left open

> [!note] Connections not yet established
> A continuous bent trough linking northern Nevos, the country south of Aurbez, and southeast Sembara remains an optional hypothesis, not part of the required explanation. Regional heating, terrain, and several separate pressure centers may be enough. Any future combined pressure map must reproduce the local wind directions without implying that every low-pressure sector is wet. In rainy sectors, heat released when water vapor condenses can reinforce circulation; that does not demonstrate a connection across the dry interior.

The most useful refinements would locate representative wet and sheltered slopes, test southern high-country snow storage against any adopted elevations, and distinguish lower valley routes from high passes. Glacier boundaries, the duration of seasonal snow, exact cold-air pathways, and rain-versus-snow thresholds remain open. New local detail should be checked against both the western moisture routes and the eastern seasonal contrasts before it is generalized across the range.

The shared continental account and the effects on neighboring regions are in [[Taelgar Climate Model#Seasonal atmospheric model]]. Western forest coverage belongs in [[Climate of the Western Coast and Interior]]; the moving winter systems are distinct from the seasonal Cymean low in [[Green Sea Atmospheric Circulation]].
