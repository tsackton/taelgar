# Green Sea Atmospheric Circulation

**Scope:** Pressure systems, seasonal winds, and maritime moisture across the [[Green Sea]], including its connections to [[Greater Sembara]], the northern forests, Cymea, and the southern desert margin. [[Taelgar Climate Model]] supplies the continental framework; [[Climate of Greater Sembara and Aurbez]] and [[Climate of the Green Sea and Its Shores]] describe regional conditions. [[Green Sea Ocean Circulation]] maintains the water-circulation model.

This qualitative model uses a **dominant eastern subtropical ocean high**, a **secondary cool northeastern maritime ridge**, a **Cymean summer low with an offshore trough**, and a **separate Great Desert heat low**. Their changing arrangement supports the sailing opportunities in [[Trading in the Green Sea]]. The seasonal pattern is a sequence of recurring weather situations, with interruptions and regional differences.

## Atmospheric circulation

### Eastern subtropical ocean high

The principal warm-season high lies over the eastern ocean and extends a ridge westward into the Green Sea. It expands northward in summer and retreats toward the southern basin in winter. Irrla lies within its changing regional influence; the island is not the fixed center or cause of this high.

Clockwise flow around the high favors northward travel on its western flank, eastward travel on its northern flank, southward travel on its eastern flank, and westward travel on its southern flank. Nearby lows reshape those bearings. Between the high to the east and the Cymean low to the west, a southerly branch carries maritime air north through parts of the western basin, including offshore waters west of Irrla. The high's southern flank contributes to southern easterlies, reinforced in summer by lower pressure over the Great Desert.

The weak pressure differences near a ridge axis favor light or variable winds. Its margins can be breezy, cloudy, or rainy, particularly where maritime air meets a front or rises over land. Extending the ridge over a route can therefore strengthen a useful wind, turn it, or leave the route in a calm zone, depending on where the axis lies.

> [!tip] A pressure map is not a rainfall map
> Air generally sinks beneath a high and rises near a low, but rainfall also needs moisture and sufficient ascent. A cool maritime high can have fog and low cloud; a desert low can remain dry. Near the surface, Northern Hemisphere winds turn clockwise around highs and counterclockwise around lows, with some flow across the contours toward lower pressure. Wind names describe where the air comes from: an easterly travels west. See the [Met Office explanation of wind flow](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/how-weather-works/high-and-low-pressure/wind-flow).

### Cool northeastern maritime ridge

A smaller, recurring high forms over cool northeastern waters and extends a ridge west along the northern sea margin. It is distinct from the subtropical ocean high and from winter high pressure over the cold continent. Summer heating over Ursk's land does not supply a persistent cold land high.

In **July–August**, this northern ridge and a lower-pressure corridor to its south favor **east to northeast winds** across the northern shipping waters. The corridor extends offshore from the Cymean low, south of the Tollen–Ursk route. This combination, together with reduced influence from northern westerly storm passages, supplies the main summer westbound season. The eastern subtropical high alone would tend to put westerlies on its northern flank and cannot explain that reversal.

Cool water helps maintain cool near-surface air. Recurring bends and ridges in the upper-air flow provide a plausible way for the maritime high to rebuild; the [Okhotsk High](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/cpd/j_climate/hokkaido/column_okho.html) is a comparison for that mechanism. Its exact Taelgar placement and westward reach remain assumptions. The analogy permits cool, cloudy onshore spells, rather than implying uniformly clear summer weather or requiring extensive sea ice.

Ridge episodes can occur in late spring without establishing the full summer circulation. Spring westerly opportunities must remain useful into June, while summer interruptions must permit rain and meaningful warmth in Ursk's settled valleys. A permanent summer blanket of cold marine air across the whole interior would conflict with that agricultural requirement.

### Cymean low and Sembaran maritime inflow

Spring heating develops lower pressure over Cymea and adjoining southern land. In summer this low extends a trough eastward into the sea, remaining south of the northern sailing corridor. Its main center lies south to southeast of the Sembaran heartlands and west of the offshore northbound branch. This gives different winds in neighboring sectors:

| Position relative to the low | Favored air movement | Physical consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| East, over the sea west of Irrla | South → north | The low and the eastern ocean high reinforce a northbound maritime branch. |
| North, toward northern Cymea and the northern sea | East → west | Maritime air travels toward the western basin; the offshore trough helps establish northern summer easterlies. |
| Northwest, toward the Western Gulf and Sembara | Northeast → southwest | Moisture enters the Sembaran lowlands and exposed uplands. |
| West, along appropriately exposed western Cymean shores | A southward component | Alongshore winds can support the adopted summer upwelling on the gulf side of Cymea. |

These are broad sectors, modified by friction, hills, coastlines, and passing disturbances. Sembara's inflow and the offshore northbound route need not share one wind direction. The early spring coastal change can begin while the more northerly sea still offers westerly passages toward Ursk.

Warm-season moisture reaches Sembara in surges, showers, thunderstorms, and broader rainy spells. Between them, settled intervals allow drying. Uplift over the Mostreve hills and the eastern Sentinel foothills increases precipitation on exposed ground; sheltered valleys receive less. Low cloud can penetrate farther inland than immediate coastal cooling, and a shallow marine layer may bring gray skies without much rain until terrain or a weather disturbance lifts it.

> [!tip] A monsoon is a seasonal circulation
> The land–sea heating contrast helps reorganize winds and moisture supply, but individual rain events still need rising air. A wet season contains breaks, and an early change in coastal wind can precede sustained inland rain. The [National Weather Service monsoon overview](https://www.weather.gov/twc/Monsooninfo) illustrates these distinctions; its Earth calendar and rainfall intensity are not assigned to Sembara.

### Great Desert heat low

A separate summer low develops over the hot [[Great Desert]] interior. Air on its northern flank travels west, reinforcing easterlies across the southern Green Sea. This is a distinct circulation branch from the Cymean low and the Nevos monsoon.

A heated lower atmosphere can coexist with dry, stable air above it. Moisture can reach sea-facing slopes and generate coastal or upland rain while mountains, moisture loss, and suppression of deeper cloud preserve broad interior aridity. Thus the low does not turn the Great Desert into a rainy basin, nor does desert aridity require every southern coast to be dry. The [Saharan heat low observations of Engelstaedter et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015JD023123) provide a physical comparison for low-level heating beneath a different circulation aloft.

### Eastern Isles and possible magical influences

The Eastern Isles interrupt navigation and produce exceptional local weather. Their climate evidence is excluded from ordinary physical requirements by [[Climate and Weather Constraints#Retcons, exclusions and source authority]]. They neither form an atmospheric wall nor require a permanent chain-long low-pressure belt to maintain the eastern ocean high.

A recurring magical storm zone on the islands' outer side remains a possible addition. Lower pressure east of an inner passage could help supply southward winds there when higher pressure lies to its west. Its extent, seasonality, and influence on the ocean high are unassigned; it is not used to guarantee southbound winds all along the islands in every season.

Similarly, [[Orenlas]] might reinforce ascent or help the Cymean trough recur, but its local magical mists do not establish such a regional influence. Fog can occur under high pressure, and cooling or shading the ground can weaken a heat-driven low. The present seasonal mechanism does not depend on adopting either magical explanation.

## Seasonal circulation over Sembara

The annual moisture contrast grows from maritime inflow in spring and summer and reduced, more episodic supply in winter. A larger warm-season contribution does not require a winter drought. The fixed continental model retains useful cool-season rain and snow, frost and thaw in the lowlands, and stronger persistent cold toward the northern interior and higher ground.

In spring, milder Green Sea air can overrun land that is still cold, producing mist, low cloud, and prolonged light rain before warm-season thunderstorms become common. In summer, moist inflow meets heated inland air and terrain; the degree of lifting determines whether a day remains humid and bright, has scattered storms, or becomes broadly wet. In autumn, the sea releases stored warmth as the land cools, prolonging mild coastal spells while inland nights turn colder.

Winter cold outbreaks alternate with moving rainmakers. The proposed [[Sentinels and Continental Weather Connections#Winter storms from Chardon to the Green Sea|southern winter storm corridor]] lets disturbances crossing the Sentinels aloft organize lows east of the mountains. A low south or southeast of Sembara can draw fresh maritime moisture inland from the east even during the broader season of westerlies. Its track and the depth of the cold air determine whether precipitation is rain, sleet, or snow. Nearby fronts can wet the gulf coast without bringing severe gales throughout its sheltered waters.

> [!tip] A wetter summer can coexist with a muddy winter
> Cool air and dormant vegetation remove water more slowly than summer warmth does. Intermittent rain, snowmelt, and slow drying can keep winter ground wet even with less precipitation than in summer. A thaw or snowy week is an episode within the climate, rather than a separate seasonal regime.

## Summer transition toward Maseau and Aurbez

Hotter Maseau and Andonne lowlands grade into the elevated [[Aurbez Plateau]], where summer days can be warm but nights are cooler. Important spring rains give way to predominantly dry summer intervals, with easterly ash-bearing winds and occasional severe thunderstorms. Moisture loss along inland paths, shelter from the [[Mostreve Hills]] and [[Salt Hills]], and dry or stable air above the surface distinguish these districts from maritime Sembara.

A southward continuation of lower pressure can support the required easterly winds without placing a persistent rainy trough over Aurbez. Occasional deeper moisture incursions and passing upper-air disturbances permit storms. Their boundaries and frequency remain open. The broken uplands described in [[Geology of the Mostreve and Cymean Uplands]] supply varied shelter and gaps, rather than a continuous Himalayan-scale barrier.

## Seasonal cycle

The seasonal views show recurring pressure arrangements on a common geographic base. They describe broad tendencies rather than one simultaneous wind field for an entire season. Arrows show where air travels; ridge and trough axes indicate relative pressure without assigned values. The northern ocean storm corridor and the proposed southern moving-storm route are distinct from the recurring summer trough. Ocean currents are shown separately in [[Green Sea Ocean Circulation]].

### Winter

![[assets/worldbuilding/taelgar-green-sea-winter-v3.png|1400]]

*The adopted continental winter pattern, cropped to the Green Sea. Dashed purple arrows show occasional cold outbreaks; the northern low represents repeated storm passages.*

The subtropical ridge extends across the southern basin toward Medju, favoring settled weather and light or variable winds near its axis. Southern calms are most troublesome in **January–February**. Both continental heat lows weaken; no persistent summer Cymean low is needed to explain winter rain.

Across the northern sea, moving lows and westerlies gain influence. Maritime storm passages alternate with ridges and cold outbreaks from the northern continental interior. Ursk's coast receives moisture from open water, while inland cold can persist through interruptions that bring a coastal thaw. Skaerhem remains more exposed to rough weather than the sheltered Western Gulf; island relief and wind exposure allow differences such as frequent Pyhlla snow cover without imposing the same snowfall on Vetta.

A useful weather sequence is an approaching low with thickening cloud and moistening winds, a period of rain or snow, then a colder wind shift and broken showers or clearing. The exact order and wind bearings depend on which side of the low a place occupies. A following continental ridge can bring crisp inland frost while the coast retains cloud or showers. Strong cold-air flow over open water can enhance precipitation downwind, especially where rising terrain supplies further lift.

The southern storm branch brings intermittent wet spells to Sembara and Cymea. Its ordinary effects must retain the gulf's relative shelter: extensive rain does not require a powerful storm centered over the gulf. Harbor ice and exposed-sea ice remain separate questions; cold inland rivers do not imply a frozen Green Sea.

### Spring

![[assets/worldbuilding/taelgar-green-sea-spring-v3.png|1400]]

*A spring transition pattern: southern westbound and offshore northbound opportunities coexist with useful northern westerly intervals. Cool maritime ridge episodes can interrupt the pattern.*

Land warms before the northern sea. Developing Cymean and desert lows, together with the northward-expanding ocean ridge, open southern westbound opportunities from **late March–April**. The offshore western branch supplies northbound opportunities, connecting with northern westerly intervals into **June**. Early cool maritime ridges can briefly interrupt this pattern without replacing it for the season.

Sembara and Cymea alternate mild maritime wet spells with sunny warming intervals and renewed cold incursions. Warm, humid air moving across still-cold northern water can produce sea fog and low cloud; a fine inland day need not mean a clear offshore crossing. Exposed northern coasts warm later than the gulf shores, and inland Ursk's thaw proceeds unevenly by valley, elevation, and snow cover. Meltwater and rain can leave muddy ground after the main snow has gone.

For a northern voyage, several days of useful westerly weather can be followed by a headwind or a storm requiring delay. Selected Ursk trading ports are normally usable by early to mid-May in the trade model, with possible local magical help; that assumption fixes neither the opening of every harbor nor a general sea-ice calendar.

### Summer

![[assets/worldbuilding/taelgar-green-sea-summer-v3.png|1400]]

*The adopted continental summer pattern, cropped to the Green Sea. The northern easterlies represent the July–August tendency, between the secondary maritime ridge and the Cymean offshore trough.*

By **July–August**, the northeastern maritime ridge and the offshore Cymean trough favor the northern **E–NE** regime. Rebuilding ridges make westbound passages sufficiently recurrent to organize trade, while interruptions permit showers, storms, and changing winds. The main eastern ocean high supplies the separate northbound branch in parts of the western basin. Its northern end now meets the westbound corridor, so a favorable northbound leg no longer guarantees an easy onward passage to Ursk.

Cool maritime episodes bring low cloud, fog, drizzle, and cool onshore days along parts of the northern coast. Inland valleys can warm substantially when the marine layer is shallow or the wind changes; summer rain and usable warmth remain necessary for Ursk's grainlands. A ridge break or a front along the boundary of maritime and continental air can produce a rainy interval followed by clearer, warmer conditions. The northern forest and uplands retain shorter, cooler summers than settled lowlands.

Sembara, the gulf coast, and exposed Cymean slopes receive humid inflow, with thunderstorms inland and broader wet spells when disturbances organize ascent. Sheltered Cymean valleys and the Maseau–Aurbez transition can remain dry while exposed slopes receive rain. Western Cymean upwelling creates local cool coastal patches within a warm summer gulf, encouraging maritime haze or fog when sufficiently humid air crosses them.

Southern easterlies recur between the ocean ridge and desert heat low. Exposed southern mountains can intercept maritime moisture; sheltered Medju and desert interiors remain much drier. Irrla and the long Eastern Isles chain require local exposure and elevation to determine rainfall. The continental model's ocean-moderated island climate does not imply a single island-wide summer drought or a fixed rainy season.

### Autumn

![[assets/worldbuilding/taelgar-green-sea-autumn-v3.png|1400]]

*An autumn transition pattern. Dashed arrows illustrate selected southward and southwestward episodes around a returning southern ridge; they are separate opportunities, with no continuous fair-wind route implied.*

The land lows weaken and northern maritime ridging becomes less persistent. Returning westerly disturbances reopen eastbound opportunities from Ursk, particularly **mid-September–early October**, with greater storm exposure later. Land cools faster than the sea: inland frost and upland snow can accompany relatively mild coastal rain, and an early cold spell can be followed by a maritime thaw.

The southern ridge retreats far enough south for eastbound opportunities from southeastern Irrla by **late September or early October**. Selected island routes then use successive southward and southwestward opportunities toward Medju. Southward flow can occur on the eastern side of a westward-extending ridge or behind an eastward-departing low; southwestward flow occurs around a high's southeastern flank. These positions shift with weather and do not describe one fair-wind passage down the whole chain.

A plausible sequence is a mild, cloudy approach to a northern low, a wet or squally passage, and a clearer interval in which the next leg becomes favorable. Renewed inland cold can bring snow to the northern forests while the southern sea remains warm. Cooling and stronger disturbances promote mixing in the ocean, but calmer spells and local island shelter remain possible. December arrivals at Medju are ordinary trade-model targets; increasing light-wind delays precede the stronger winter calms.

## Ocean and neighboring-region connections

The northern cold current delays coastal warming and supports cool maritime air. The warmer southern and western basin supplies heat and moisture to Sembara and Cymea. These influence the atmosphere without fixing the pressure systems by themselves: imported water temperature, land heating, upper-air flow, and weather-system movement all matter.

The adopted [[Green Sea Ocean Circulation#The Western Gulf|Western Gulf exchange]] exports fresher surface water and imports cold, saltier water below. Summer winds favor western Cymean upwelling but northern coastal downwelling. Cold imported water can persist in either setting. Current arrows, wind arrows, and storm movement describe different things and should never be substituted for one another.

Across the Sentinels, western winter moisture and eastern summer moisture affect different slopes. The Green Sea can replenish moisture east of the mountains during moving winter storms; it does not carry the western maritime climate unchanged across the range. The Nevos-fed circulation in [[Dunmar and Nevos Circulation]] is a separate regional monsoon. A common summer heating cycle can affect both without requiring a continuous rainy trough through the dry interior.

## Physical weak points and open choices

- **Northern ridge reach and recurrence.** The cool-water/upper-air mechanism is plausible, but an Okhotsk comparison does not demonstrate a ridge extending across Taelgar's entire northern shipping corridor. Its persistence must accommodate July–August westbound commerce, spring and autumn eastbound travel, northern rain, and adequate inland summer warmth.
- **Offshore trough placement.** The trough must remain south of the westbound corridor while the Cymean center leaves northbound flow to its east and NE → SW inflow to its northwest. Moving it too far north disrupts the sailing geometry; extending it too far south and west as a deep moist trough would threaten Aurbez and desert aridity.
- **Autumn island route geometry.** The fixed continental account and trade requirements specify successive favorable opportunities. Their frequency and precise ridge shapes remain unmodeled. An ocean high centered east of an inner-island passage favors northward flow there on its western flank, so it cannot by itself justify southbound winds on that passage. Temporary ridges, passing lows, and any separately adopted magical influence must supply the appropriate local episodes.
- **Moisture delivery and gulf shelter.** The model needs recurring inland precipitation without severe storms becoming routine throughout the Western Gulf. The balance between weak moving lows, fronts, terrain rain, and thunderstorms remains qualitative.
- **Island and northeastern detail.** Irrla's old climate claims and exceptional Eastern Isles weather are excluded constraints. This model supplies maritime exposure and terrain effects without deciding every local rainy season, the undeveloped northeastern geography, or regional sea-ice extent.
