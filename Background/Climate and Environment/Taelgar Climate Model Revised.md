# Taelgar Climate Model Revised

Taelgar's continent spans tropical southern lowlands, a broad temperate middle, and a cold northern interior grading into subpolar and polar country. The western ocean moderates the coast and the inland districts open to its influence; farther north and behind mountain barriers, winters become longer and seasonal contrasts stronger. Around the Green Sea, warmer southern shores contrast with the colder northern coasts and forests. Mountains and plateaus create cooler country throughout these latitude belts, while the southern lowland basins can remain intensely hot.

This continent-scale reference describes the broad present-day temperature framework and provides a basis for developing regional climates and the transitions between them.

## Temperature framework

**Latitude sets the broad thermal pattern.** Southern lowlands have warm conditions through much or all of the year. Temperate regions have a distinct cooler winter and warmer summer. Farther north, the cold season lengthens and the growing season contracts. These are broad transitions, modified by terrain and maritime exposure.

**Maritime access moderates the seasons.** Air arriving from the ocean generally limits summer heat and winter cold. Its reach depends on the arrangement of coastlines, lowlands, and mountain barriers, as well as distance inland. The southern western forests lie on the ocean-facing side of the Sentinels and retain substantial maritime influence. Northern plains and sheltered interiors have stronger seasonal contrasts. The Green Sea also moderates neighboring land, but its northern coasts remain exposed to cold continental air.

**Elevation creates colder regions within every latitude belt.** Uplands have cooler summers and colder winters than adjacent lowlands. High mountains can sustain snow and ice above warm valleys; a moderately elevated southern plateau can remain hot while being cooler than a neighboring basin.

The descriptions below concern ordinary seasonal character. Brief frosts, snowfalls, heat waves, and thaws occur within these broader patterns; their frequency and persistence change with latitude, elevation, and maritime exposure.

#### Reading the real world comparisons

The regional parallels below describe temperature, terrain, and useful climate comparisons. Full Köppen codes are candidate combinations of temperature and rainfall; their seasonal rainfall conditions are stated alongside them.

In the convention used here, **A** climates have every monthly mean at least 18°C. **C** climates have a coldest monthly mean above 0°C but below 18°C and a warmest above 10°C; **D** climates have a coldest monthly mean at or below 0°C and a warmest above 10°C. Summer suffixes distinguish **a** (hottest monthly mean at least 22°C), **b** (cooler summers, with at least four months above 10°C), and **c** (short, cool summers). The letters **s**, **w**, and **f** indicate summer drought, winter drought, and rainfall above the seasonal-drought thresholds. These temperatures are monthly means.

**B** denotes aridity: **BS** is steppe and **BW** desert, with **h** for annual mean temperatures at least 18°C and **k** below that. A *k* climate can have hot summers. **ET** denotes tundra and **EF** perpetual frost. Aridity takes precedence over the temperature families. Definitions follow [Beck et al. (2018), Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6207062/#t2).

%%^End%%

## Regional temperature patterns

### Western coast and Apporia

The western seaboard grades from warm southern peninsulas and lowlands to cooler northern shores. Coastal moderation is strongest where ocean air has direct access; inland slopes and mountain interiors are colder in winter.

| Area | Broad temperature character |
| --- | --- |
| [[Apporia]] and the southern [[Coastlands]] | Mild lowland winters and warm to hot summers, with cooler mountain interiors. Coastal districts have less seasonal variation than sheltered inland valleys. |
| [[Gulf of Chardon]] and [[Chardon]] | Mild winters and comfortably warm summers. Maritime influence limits sustained summer heat, while sunny, warm spells remain common possibilities. |
| Upper Coastlands | A temperate maritime coast, becoming cooler northward. Summers are moderate to warm and winters comparatively mild, with stronger cold toward the northern margin and higher ground. |
| [[Mawakel Peninsula]] and coastal [[Mawar Confederacy]] | Cool summers and winters alternating cold, snowy spells with maritime thaws. Inland and elevated districts are colder than the coast. |
| [[Slate Sea]] coast | A cooler northern continuation of the maritime coast, with a longer cold season toward the [[Far North]]. |

%%^Campaign:none%%

#### Real world parallels and Köppen candidates

Apporia and the southern Coastlands have the thermal range of Mediterranean peninsulas such as Italy. Chardon's comfortably warm summer is closer to the San Francisco coast, with greater scope for sunny warmth. Farther north, the useful parallels progress through coastal British Columbia to southeastern Alaska and coastal Norway; these illustrate the contrast between moderated shores and colder mountain interiors.

For Apporia, **Csa** fits hotter summers with summer drought; **Cfa** fits similar warmth with sufficient summer rain. Chardon and the warmer Upper Coastlands suggest **Csb** where summers are dry, or **Cfb** with wetter summers. Cooler Mawar and Slate Sea shores approach **Cfb/Cfc**, grading toward **Dfb/Dfc** where winters become persistently colder.

%%^End%%

### Western river basins and forest belt

The lowlands south of the Fiatara admit maritime air into the Chasa and Valandros country and toward the western Sentinel forests. Rising forest terrain moderates summer warmth, while ocean influence limits winter cold. Farther north, increasing latitude, mountain shelter, and exposure to northern cold strengthen the seasons.

| Area | Broad temperature character |
| --- | --- |
| [[Chasa River Valley]] | Mild lowland winters and warm to hot summers, warmer inland than at Chardon. Lowland frost is occasional and localized, becoming more frequent on higher ground and in cold-air pockets. |
| [[Yeraad]] basin and [[Yeraad Marshes]] | Warm southern lowlands with mild winters and warm to hot summers, grading into cooler foothills and forest country. |
| [[Lake Valandros]] and [[Tawir Forest]] | A temperate transition between the milder southern basins and colder northern country. Summer warmth and winter cold increase away from maritime influence; the lake moderates its immediate shores. |
| Southern [[Ainumarya]] and the [[Elderwood]] | Cool to mild winters and moderate to warm summers, with low-to-moderate seasonal variation. The lower forests retain the gentlest seasonal range. |
| [[Crimson Forest]], [[Highveil Forest]], and northern western forests | Increasing winter frost and snow and shorter summers toward the north and higher slopes. This grades into long, difficult winters in the far northern Deno'qai forests. |
| [[Voltara]] and the [[Erbalta Plains]] | Moderate summers and changeable, moderately cold winters around Voltara and southern Erbalta. Cold spells become longer and more frequent farther north, and the growing season shortens. |

%%^Campaign:none%%

#### Real world parallels and Köppen candidates

The Chasa and warmer southern basins resemble Mediterranean wine country and inland coastal California, with mild lowland winters and warmer summers than the open coast. Valandros and Tawir form an intermediate forest-and-lake belt. The southern Ainumarya and Elderwood parallels run from redwood country to the Pacific Northwest, while the northern forests approach colder Canadian forest climates. Voltara and southern Erbalta share a northern German temperature character; increasing continental exposure strengthens the northern Erbalta seasons.

The southern basins suggest **Csa/Csb** with summer drought, or **Cfa/Cfb** with wetter summers. Moderated forests fit **Cfb**, or **Csb** with a sufficiently dry summer. Northward, **Dfb** and then **Dfc** become useful comparisons. Voltara lies near the **Cfb–Dfb** thermal transition; sufficiently dry Erbalta grasslands would instead fall within **BSk**.

%%^End%%

### Central mountains and high plateaus

The continent's mountain systems form colder belts within the surrounding lowlands. Their southern foothills can be warm while high terrain remains cold; northern ranges combine elevation with a longer winter season.

| Area | Broad temperature character |
| --- | --- |
| [[Fiatara Mountains]] and [[Mawar Mountains]] | Cold uplands above more moderated coastal and inland districts. The highest Fiatara summits retain glaciers and extensive snow even in summer. |
| Northern [[Sentinel Range]] | Long, severe winters and short summers, with extensive cold high terrain and a transition toward the Far North. Lower slopes are warmer than the high peaks. |
| Central and southern Sentinels | Strong contrasts between relatively warm lower valleys and cold high mountains. The southern foothills have a longer warm season; elevation remains the principal control on summit and pass temperatures. |
| [[Zimkova]], [[Pekul Mountains]], and [[Vostok Plateau]] | Cold winters and shorter summers across the highlands, becoming colder with elevation and continental exposure. Lower basins and sheltered valleys have warmer growing seasons than the higher plateaus. |
| [[Aurbez Plateau]] | An elevated continental regime with cold winters, warm summer days, and cool nights. Adjacent lowlands are warmer. |
| [[Alta Tonaro]] | Warm to hot summers and cooler winters across the dry northern foothills of the Chataans. Dissected plateaus and ridges have cooler nights and colder winters than the sheltered river valleys. |
| [[Chataan Mountains]], [[Darba Highlands]], and [[Yuvanti Mountains]] | Cooler terrain within the warm southern latitude belt. Low passes and foothills can remain warm; high ridges and mountain interiors have substantially colder conditions. |

%%^Campaign:none%%

#### Real world parallels and Köppen candidates

The Alps and Rocky Mountains provide useful parallels for the continent's changing climate with elevation. Zimkova and interior Vostok resemble Yellowstone and the northern Rockies extending toward Alberta; Aurbez draws on the Colorado Front Range. In the southern ranges, warm foothills can rise through cool forest belts to cold summits. Alta Tonaro's dry hills, plateaus, winter rain, and occasional summer storms suggest semiarid Mediterranean foothills, particularly southeastern Spain.

Forested mountain belts can pass from **C** climates into **Dfb/Dfc**, with **ET** on cold high terrain and **EF** at the coldest summits. Dry plateaus such as Aurbez can fall within **BSk**. Alta Tonaro suggests **BSk** or **BSh**, depending on annual warmth; wetter foothill pockets with the same winter-rain pattern approach **Csa/Csb**. These elevation and moisture transitions can occur within a single mountain system.

%%^End%%

### Sembara and the upper Istaros

East of the Sentinels, temperate riverlands connect colder northern highlands with the warmer interior south. The Western Gulf moderates nearby lowlands, while elevation and distance from the coast increase local seasonal contrasts.

| Area | Broad temperature character |
| --- | --- |
| [[Sembara]] and its central riverlands | Warm summers and distinct winters. Lowland winters are changeable, with cold spells and thaws; northern and western districts become colder toward the highlands. |
| [[Addermarch]] and the [[Mostreve Hills]] | Warm summers and cool to cold winters, with milder lower valleys and colder, snowier uplands. |
| [[Tyrwingha]] and the southern [[Western Gulf]] shore | Relatively moderated temperate lowlands, with warm summers and milder winters than exposed inland or elevated districts. |
| [[Tollen]] and the lower [[Volta]] | Moderately warm summers and winters that frequently alternate frost and thaw. Snow often melts between events. The main lower river normally remains open; sustained freezing is exceptional. |
| [[Duchy of Maseau]] and [[March of Andonne]] lowlands | Hot summers and cooler winters, with greater seasonal contrast than the immediate gulf coast. The land becomes colder as it rises toward Aurbez and the Sentinels. |
| [[Upper Istaros]] lowlands and historical [[Isingue]] | A warmer temperate transition toward the southern interior, with warm to hot summers and relatively mild winters. |

%%^Campaign:none%%

#### Real world parallels and Köppen candidates

Sembara combines French lowland and New England seasonal characters, with Addermarch drawing on the mid-Atlantic Appalachians. Tollen is London-like with somewhat warmer summers and colder winter spells. Tyrwingha represents the milder maritime edge of this belt; Maseau and Andonne have hotter inland summers. The upper Istaros and historical Isingue combine southern French warmth with the longer warm season of the southeastern United States.

**Cfb** is a useful comparison for Tollen and moderated Sembaran lowlands; hotter districts approach **Cfa** where summer rainfall is sufficient. Colder inland and upland districts approach **Dfb**, or **Dfa** with hotter summers. **Csa** is a candidate for warm districts only where summer drought is strong enough. Sembara's retained warm-season rainfall is compatible with the **f** classes even when winter is drier.

%%^End%%

### Northern Green Sea and the Far North

The northern coast and its hinterland have increasingly long winters toward the north and interior. Maritime influence limits some extremes along the shore, while uplands and broad inland forests retain stronger cold. Summer warmth remains important in the inhabited coastal and river districts.

| Area | Broad temperature character |
| --- | --- |
| Coastal [[Vostok]] and [[Skaerhem]] | Cooler summers and later spring warming than the Western Gulf. Winter freezing is more frequent or persistent than at Tollen, with maritime thaws still possible. Snow cover varies between islands and elevations; smaller island rivers freeze regularly. |
| [[Ursk]] lowlands and settled valleys | Substantial cold winters followed by warm summers. Seasonal contrasts increase inland. |
| [[Great Northern Forest]] and the [[Ket]] hinterland | Long, severe winters, a late thaw, and short summers. Winter hardship increases northward, while lower and sheltered districts retain a usable warm season. |
| [[Svinjo Mountains]] and [[Yengorza Mountains]] | Colder uplands and mountain interiors within the northern belt, with shorter summers than neighboring lowlands. |
| [[Far North]] | Long, bitter winters and short summers, grading from subarctic country into polar conditions. |

The Tollen–Skaerhem contrast is a gradual transition across several hundred miles and several degrees of latitude. More frequent exposure to northern cold contributes to Skaerhem's winter severity; the northern Green Sea current also supports cooler summers and delayed spring warming. Regular freezing of smaller island rivers is compatible with an ordinarily open, large tidal lower Volta.

%%^Campaign:none%%

#### Real world parallels and Köppen candidates

Skaerhem and coastal Vostok resemble the colder inhabited coasts of Scandinavia and the North Atlantic, with cool summers, delayed spring, and recurrent winter freezing. Ursk's settled lowlands are closer to St. Petersburg and Moscow in their winter-to-summer contrast. The Great Northern Forest and Ket grade toward the long winters and short growing seasons of boreal Canada and northern Eurasia; the Far North continues into tundra and polar country.

The maritime-to-continental transition spans **Cfb/Cfc** to **Dfb/Dfc**, depending on winter cold and summer length. **Dfb** is the strongest broad comparison for settled Ursk, with **Dfc** increasingly useful in the northern forests and colder uplands. **ET** fits the tundra end of the Far North; **EF** applies where even the warmest monthly mean stays at or below freezing.

%%^End%%

### Nevos shores and southern mainland

The southern mainland grades from a subtropical coast and hot interior basins into tropical lowlands. Plateau height and proximity to the sea modify temperatures within this warm belt; deserts, grasslands, and jungles are distinguished by moisture as well as temperature.

| Area | Broad temperature character |
| --- | --- |
| [[Darba]] and the northern [[Nevos Sea]] coast | Warm subtropical conditions with a noticeable cooler season. At Darba, coldest-month daytime temperatures are commonly in the 50s–60s°F, with occasional hotter days and cooler nights. |
| [[Illoria]] and the southern Nevos islands | Warm maritime conditions, with subdued seasonal variation and cooler elevated interiors. The strength of the cool season varies with latitude. |
| [[Dunmar]] and the [[Hara Basin]], including [[Tokra]] and [[Karawa]] | Hot summers and a cooler winter season, with stronger daytime heating inland. Basins and lowlands are warmer than nearby uplands, with substantial cooling after sunset. |
| [[Garamjala Plateau]] and adjoining [[Xurkhaz]] lowlands | A hot interior region with local elevation contrasts. The moderately elevated plateau can be less hot than the Hara Basin; lower river districts remain warm to hot. |
| [[Nayan Floodplains]] | Warm to hot lowlands with a gentle cool season. Winter cooling diminishes southward toward and beyond the [[Shrev]]. |
| Lower [[Istaros]], [[Drankor]], and the [[Awakened Jungle]] coast | Tropical warmth through the year. |

%%^Campaign:none%%

#### Real world parallels and Köppen candidates

Darba's temperature parallel is the subtropical Australian coast, with a warm growing season and a mild, noticeable winter; Illoria extends the warm maritime character into island settings. Tokra and Karawa draw on the hot basins of the southwestern United States. Garamjala is a hot plateau desert, with elevation relieving some basin heat. The Nayan Floodplains resemble the South African lowveld, trending toward warmer tropical conditions south of the Shrev. Rio de Janeiro provides a useful temperature parallel for Drankor's warm coast.

Darba suggests **Cfa** with sufficient cool-season rain, or **Cwa** with a strongly dry winter. Dry Dunmari basins and Garamjala span **BS/BW**, with **h/k** set by annual mean warmth. The lowveld transition suggests **Cwa** at its cooler end and **Aw** in warmer seasonal lowlands. Tropical Drankor and the southern jungles approach **Af**, **Am**, or **Aw**, according to the strength and duration of the dry season.

%%^End%%

### Southern and eastern Green Sea and the Far South

The Green Sea's southern shores belong to a warmer belt than its northern forests. Inland deserts have greater daily and seasonal temperature contrasts than nearby maritime districts. Farther south, tropical lowlands have progressively weaker winter cooling.

| Area | Broad temperature character |
| --- | --- |
| [[Cymean Peninsula]] coasts | Mild winters and warm to hot summers. Maritime moderation is particularly important along the coast facing the main Green Sea. |
| [[Plataca Hills]], interior [[Cymea]], and [[Mistfold Mountains]] | Cooler and more seasonal than the low coasts, with colder winters and increasing summer cooling at elevation. |
| Southern Cymean lowlands and [[Medju]] | Warm to hot lowlands with relatively mild winters. Immediate coastal districts have less temperature variation than inland basins. |
| [[Great Desert]] and the subtropical interior of the [[Far South]] | A broadly hot interior belt, with cooler nights and a cooler season away from the tropics. |
| [[Irrla]] and the distant southern Green Sea peninsulas | Maritime moderation at low elevations and cooler interior highlands. |
| [[Eastern Isles]] and the far eastern shores | A long north-to-south transition from cooler northern islands to warmer southern shores, with strong ocean exposure. |
| [[Orekatu]] and other tropical southern lowlands | Tropical warmth and a weak temperature-based winter season. |

%%^Campaign:none%%

#### Real world parallels and Köppen candidates

Cymea combines Italian coastal warmth with the stronger seasonal contrasts of the mid-Atlantic United States inland; the Plataca Hills and Mistfold Mountains add cooling with elevation. Medju and the Great Desert draw on North African and Egyptian heat and aridity. For Irrla and the Eastern Isles, large maritime islands provide the useful structural parallel: ocean-moderated shores, cooler highlands, and a changing temperature regime along a long north–south chain. Orekatu belongs to the tropical lowland forest belt.

Coastal Cymea suggests **Csa** with summer drought or **Cfa** with wetter summers; cooler uplands approach **Cfb**, then **Dfb** where winter cold is sustained. Medju and the Great Desert suggest **BWh/BSh**, with **BWk/BSk** possible in cooler elevated districts. Maritime **C** climates provide comparisons for temperate island shores, grading toward tropical **A** climates in the south. Orekatu's tropical forests suggest **Af/Am**, with **Aw** in more strongly seasonal districts.

%%^End%%

## Geographic basis

On the continental basemap, small hexes measure 24 miles between opposite edges. Dashed latitude lines mark the equator, tropics, and Arctic Circle; dotted and solid lines mark five-degree intervals. The regional divisions above follow major coasts, watersheds, forests, and mountain systems in the [[Gazetteer]] rather than fixed climate boundaries.

This qualitative model combines adopted temperature constraints with geographic projections for less-developed areas. [[Climate and Weather Constraints]] supplies the retained climatic requirements and evidence limits.
