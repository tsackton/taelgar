# AGENTS.md — Climate and Environment

These instructions supplement the vault's root `AGENTS.md` throughout this directory. The human introduction is `Climate and Environment.md`; the current revision sequence is in `Climate Revisions Plan.md`.

## Purpose

This library helps DMs and worldbuilders understand Taelgar's climate and choose useful default conditions for places, including undeveloped country. Preserve the richness of the climate while keeping the explanations readable and information easy to find.

The physical model is revisable. Played observations must be preserved at their actual scope; adopted worldbuilding requirements retain their documented standing. Model predictions do not become independent evidence merely by appearing in the atlas. Use this distinction when working, without repeatedly explaining it in the climate prose.

## Document responsibilities

| Document | Purpose and level of detail |
| --- | --- |
| `Taelgar Climate Model.md` | Explain the connected continental system: broad temperature and moisture patterns, seasonal circulation, major barriers, and connections among regional systems. Summarize and link regional mechanisms; do not maintain a second regional atlas. |
| `Physical Model/` | Explain regional mechanisms: air and water sources, pressure systems, seasonal changes, terrain effects, and the causes of regional contrasts. These pages and the continental overview describe the same model at different scales. Neither is an independently fixed version that freezes the other. |
| `Regional Atlas/` | Describe the climate to use in each region, combining established details with grounded predictions from the current model. Preserve useful seasonal and local detail. Do not reduce the atlas to a restatement of constraints or fill it with evidence assessments, circulation arguments, weather sequences, or developed applications. |
| `Climate and Weather Constraints.md` | Preserve the outcomes the model must accommodate, dated observations, sources, standing, consequential qualifications, and how the current model fits each constraint. Keep coverage assessments easy to scan so an agent can find gaps without rereading the collection. Its tables may be long and dense. |

Weather, biomes, and applications have their own purposes as described on the landing page. Do not absorb their work into the atlas or expand a climate revision into those collections without authorization.

## Writing and organization

- Write concrete descriptions and connected explanations. Each sentence should describe Taelgar, explain a mechanism, or help the reader locate useful information.
- Harshly purge routine audit language from model and atlas prose: statements that quantities are unassigned, events do not establish annual calendars, predictions are not constraints, or a description does not authorize some further inference. Delete this boilerplate rather than collecting it in another appendix.
- Preserve substantive distinctions. Patchy ordinary snow and prolonged cover in severe winters describe different conditions worth retaining. Whether a snow observation was played belongs in the constraints.
- State each detailed fact or explanation once in its appropriate home. A short overview or causal reminder can help; repeating the full account in an introduction, seasonal table, local section, conclusion, and comment does not.
- In the atlas, give each distinct climatic area one complete seasonal account in a predictable format. Describe nearby variations without repeating the shared climate. A geographic volume may contain several climates and need not have one common seasonal calendar.
- In physical pages, organize around the causal system and its seasonal behavior. Place maps beside the explanations they support. Preserve explanatory depth where it makes the system understandable.
- Keep regional climate analogues and useful climate classifications with regional descriptions. Keep analogues for physical mechanisms with their explanations. Explain shared physical concepts once at the appropriate level.
- Do not invent unsupported geography, measurements, or mechanisms to complete a template. Use existing model predictions as defaults; preserve genuinely unresolved alternatives briefly where they affect a choice.

## Assumptions and physical problems

Put consequential model assumptions beside the mechanisms that depend on them. Preserve genuine unresolved physical problems specifically and briefly in the relevant physical page, using comments for review material under the root conventions. An uncomputed quantity is not, by itself, a physical problem.

Keep constraint-specific coverage labels, model-fit explanations and links, and a concise dated coverage summary in the constraints document. Update them when relevant model changes affect the assessment. General physical weaknesses and conflicts with other notes belong with the relevant physical explanation or review work, not in this coverage assessment. Preserve source qualifications and conflicts that define a constraint's actual meaning, exclusions, and distinctions between requirements and preferences.

## Revision practice

Follow the root authorization, Git, metadata, preservation, and verification rules. This guide does not authorize rewrites by itself. Review physical explanations before rewriting their continental summary and regional predictions. When redistributing detail, preserve unique useful content, geographic coverage, and affected links; keep maps and prose consistent. Put editorial revision progress in the plan; maintain constraint coverage in the constraints document.

## Maintaining a consistent library

Each detailed explanation has one maintained home. Before changing a regional model, check the continental account, shared systems, neighboring regions, accepted constraints, and affected maps. Revise related accounts together within the agreed scope. Record outstanding edits in the revision plan; record a genuine unresolved physical problem briefly beside the relevant mechanism, using comments under the root conventions. Established facts in play can require changes to the physical explanation.

Keep established requirements, adopted model choices, hypotheses, predictions, and unmodeled areas distinct when assessing evidence and revising the model; do not turn these distinctions into repeated labels or disclaimers in descriptive prose. Individual weather observations need not describe an average year. Group downstream evidence under the climatic pattern it supports; fuller farming, river, and navigation consequences belong in applications.

Use linked Gazetteer regions to identify the places covered. The atlas volumes are browsing groups, and their boundaries need not coincide with either Gazetteer borders or sharp changes in climate. A prediction for a new place does not become independent evidence for the model.

The maintained constraints contain the accepted statements, scope, and qualifications in full. Sources support verification. Temporary review material may provide additional provenance, but essential evidence and navigation must remain in the maintained library. A future weather generator should derive from this human-readable model rather than maintain a competing set of climatic assumptions.
