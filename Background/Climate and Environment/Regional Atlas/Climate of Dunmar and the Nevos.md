# Climate of Dunmar and the Nevos

The Nevos-facing coast, wooded Darba valleys, inland grazing country and Garamjala desert share a strong seasonal contrast, but receive very different amounts of water. Summer maritime inflow waters exposed western slopes and loses moisture as it crosses the uplands. Farther south, warm seasonal floodplains approach tropical forest; northward across the Chataans, Alta Tonaro has winter rain and a predominantly dry summer.

## Regional coverage

- **Nevos shores and islands:** the [[Nevos Sea]], [[Illoria]], [[Darba]], coastal [[Western Dunmar]], and the maritime approaches toward [[Emerald Bay]].
- **Interior basins and deserts:** [[Dunmar]], the [[Hara Basin]], [[Tokra]], [[Karawa]], the [[Garamjala Plateau]], and adjoining [[Xurkhaz]] river lowlands.
- **Southern lowlands:** the [[Nayan Floodplains]] and the transition along and south of the [[Shrev]].
- **Monsoon-facing terrain:** [[Darba Highlands]], [[Rudhan Heights]], [[Copper Hills]], [[Yuvanti Mountains]], [[Chataan Mountains]], and [[Alta Tonaro]].

This note follows Nevos moisture inland and the transition between monsoon-facing slopes and sheltered basins. Apporia's coastal profiles belong in [[Climate of the Western Coast and Interior]]; the adjoining Sentinel high country belongs in [[Climate of the Sentinels and Northern Highlands]].

## The main climatic contrasts

**Coast to interior.** The summer monsoon brings air from the west and southwest across the Nevos. Two approaches matter: direct inflow across the Nayan Floodplains toward the western Yuvanti, and the more northerly route through the Darba Highlands between the Yuvanti and Chataans. The latter crosses an upland saddle at the Rudhan Heights. It allows moisture into Dunmar while still leaving much of the Hara Basin in rain shadow.

**Northwestern Hara to the desert.** The western and northern basin receive more dependable rain than the southern and eastern districts. Songara and Samtal have more grazing moisture than Karawa; Nashtkar and northwestern Garamjala receive more occasional monsoon rain than the deep desert. These are broad gradients shaped by wind exposure, height of land and distance from maritime moisture.

**Lowland to upland.** Inland basins heat strongly by day and cool substantially after sunset. The moderately elevated Garamjala Plateau can be less hot than Hara while remaining extremely dry. Chataan and Yuvanti ridges are colder than neighboring lowlands; a warm foothill journey does not establish conditions at a high pass.

**Monsoon country to winter-rain country.** The Chataans divide exposures as well as watersheds. Nevos-facing slopes collect summer rain; sheltered Alta Tonaro receives important winter rain from western disturbances reaching the Chardon side. Toward Emerald Bay, maritime weather crosses a shifting boundary between the Dunmari monsoon and Chardon's summer ridge.

The seasonal pressure pattern and its physical limits are developed in [[Dunmar and Nevos Circulation#Atmospheric circulation and monsoon]]. Nevos current, sea temperature, or sailing wind patterns are not developed yet and remain open. 

## Local climates

### Darba and the coastal highlands

Darba has warm subtropical conditions with a warm but noticeably milder winter season. Its rocky shores and lower [[Rudhan]] valley experience smaller temperature swings than the sheltered interior. Summer rain swells the river, inundates low ground and supplies the wooded valleys of [[Coastal Dunmar]]. The northern rocky headlands and mountain valleys differ from the gentler country spreading southeast toward Nayan.

Across the Darba Highlands, wetter western and southern districts grade toward the arid, rocky Rudhan Heights and Copper Hills. Seasonal forest occupies sheltered valleys and river corridors among grasslands and savannas. Rivers diminish during the dry months, and forest vegetation becomes drier, but the wetter country retains year-round rivers. Heavy wet-season recharge carried forward through groundwater, river flow and shaded soils supports forest and perennial water. 

The western and northwestern edges of the Darba Highlands, functionally the southern foothills of the [[Chataan Mountains]], see occasional winter rain, though still experience substantially more rainfall during the monsoon season. 

### Nayan Floodplains and the western Yuvanti

Western Dunmar is warm to hot, with a warm winter, strong premonsoon heat and a wet summer. Moisture arrives directly from the Nevos across the Nayan lowlands before rising against the western Yuvanti. This route does not depend on air first passing through the Darba saddle. The resulting summer rain and river floods support the established savannas, rice paddies and settled river valleys of [[Western Dunmar]].

The [[Nayan Floodplains]] remain seasonally variable: humid, flooded country during active rains gives way to drier open ground as the rains withdraw. The Shrev regularly floods during the monsoon. Farther south, the landscape grades toward tropical forest. More persistent tropical maritime moisture, a longer rain-bearing season, or both can support the forest. Their relative importance remains open; the Shrev is a geographic reference, not an abrupt climatic boundary.

### Hara, Tokra and the northern grazing country

The Hara Basin has hot summers, a warm winter season and a hot, generally clear premonsoon interval. Daytime heat is stronger than on the open coast; shade, elevation and proximity to water create local relief. Even during the hottest part of the year, cooling after sunset can make the night feel sharply different from the afternoon.

The [[Songara Plains]] and northern basin receive more recurring monsoon rain than the southern basin. Tokra lies on the transition toward the drier interior, with arid scrub and semi-desert on the western approach before the wetter Darba country is reached. [[Samtal]] receives foothill storms and water from the northern highlands, supporting seasonal grazing in country that remains much drier than the Nevos-facing west. Rainfall and water arriving from the mountains are separate contributions to that landscape.

South of Tokra, the [[Sukal Plains]] lie more directly behind the Yuvanti barrier. Rain becomes unreliable even within the nominal wet season. Farther downriver around [[Askandi]] and the [[Lower Hara Valley]], water carried by the [[Hara]] matters more than local rainfall. A rising river can announce upstream monsoon rain while the surrounding country remains dry.

### Karawa, Nashtkar and Garamjala

Around Karawa, rain is sparse and usually arrives in occasional monsoon storms. A storm can briefly green a canyon or fill a dry wash while adjacent desert receives little water. Oases and aquifers remain important through the long dry intervals. Farther east, [[Nashtkar]] and the northwestern Garamjala margins retain occasional rain and scrub, but the deep [[Garamjala Desert]] has the weakest moisture supply.

The plateau is a hot desert despite its elevation. Its western rain shadow and distance from reliable inflow permit long dry spells; occasional strong incursions do not create a dependable season of widespread rain. The [[Istaros]] and adjoining Xurkhaz river districts form a different kind of watered landscape. Their flow comes from a much larger catchment and supports riparian vegetation and irrigation within an arid climate. Greener riverbanks do not imply wetter conditions across the plateau.

### Chataans, Alta Tonaro and the high Yuvanti

On exposed southern Chataan slopes, summer rain feeds seasonal streams and waterfalls and moistens the sheltered woods, which are supported by occasional dry season rains sustained by coastal moisture and uplift. Across the ridges, Alta Tonaro's arid hills, dissected plateaus and canyons receive less monsoon moisture. Winter rain and rare summer thunderstorms sustain its sparse grasses, juniper and cedar. High ridges and plateaus have cooler nights and colder winters than protected river valleys, while sunny low ground is hot in summer.

The Yuvanti likewise separate a wetter maritime face from sheltered eastern country. Neither range has a single temperature or rainfall regime from foothill to summit. Cold-season snow is physically possible on high terrain, but the ordinary snow extent, its persistence and any local glaciers are unassigned. The established Sentinel glacier and winter-pass requirements belong in [[Climate of the Sentinels and Northern Highlands]], and are not automatically extended to these southern ranges.

### Illoria and the northern Nevos approaches

Illoria retains a warm subtropical maritime character, with smaller temperature swings on exposed shores and cooler elevated interiors. Its rainfall calendar is undeveloped. During active summer inflow, western and southwestern exposures can receive rain while sheltered slopes are drier. When northeasterly flow becomes more common in the cooler season, the moisture reaching an island depends on how far the air has crossed open water. The favored rainy slope can change with the wind.

These exposure contrasts do not establish a Dunmari wet season for every island, year-round rain across the archipelago, or a fixed dry season. Northward toward Emerald Bay, the seasonal balance of monsoon inflow, Chardon ridge influence and passing disturbances becomes more variable. The Apporian continuation is described in [[Climate of the Western Coast and Interior]].

## Four-season guide

The headings below use northern-hemisphere seasons for comparison with the rest of the atlas. Dunmari seasonal language is different: [[Dunmari Festivals]] marks the beginning of *summer* around April and its end around June, encompassing much of the hot premonsoon interval. These broad phases overlap between districts and years.

### Winter: the cooler dry season

Across most Dunmari lowlands, the monsoon has withdrawn. Drier continental or northeasterly flow favors settled intervals, lower river levels and a pronounced contrast between hot, sunlit afternoons and cooler nights. Darba retains its mild subtropical coastal cool season, with seasonably warm days and moderate night. Occasional fog in Western Dunmar's moist low ground is compatible with a dry season: fog does not require a new rainy season.

Stored water remains visible in the perennial Darba rivers and in the major inland waterways, while seasonal washes are dry. The [[Sukal]] can shrink to a trickle and nearly disappear in some years. The southern tropical margin has minimal winter cooling compared to northern Dunmar, and enough moisture to support tropical forests, but detailed rainfall is undeveloped south of the [[Nayan]].

Alta Tonaro differs from this pattern. Western disturbances can bring its winter rains, while the high ground of the [[Chataan Mountains]] and the [[Myraeni Gap]] prevent winter rains from reaching the Hara basin. Within the Chataans, altitude, exposure and shelter determine whether a particular spell is cold and wet, snowy at height, or dry on the sheltered side. No fixed lowland frost or high-pass calendar is assigned.

### Spring: heat builds before reliable rain

This is the Dunmari summer, the hot pre-monsoon season. As the land heats, sunny, dry weather becomes increasingly demanding in Hara, Karawa and Garamjala. Roads and open ground remain dusty; river valleys and the shaded Darba woods retain moisture longer than bare slopes. Coastal humidity or isolated clouds can increase before sustained rain reaches the interior. An early shower does not establish that the monsoon has arrived everywhere.

The late-March to early-May 1748 heat in Karawa and Garamjala is a retained example of this phase. Its cooler nights express a large day/night contrast, rather than an absolute cold-temperature requirement. 

On the northern Chataan side, moisture left by winter rain can support a different seasonal landscape as Alta Tonaro moves toward summer dryness. In the southern lowlands, the same broad warming coincides with changes in tropical rain-belt access, but does not fix a common spring onset for Nayan, the jungle coast and Illoria.

### Summer: advancing rains, active spells and breaks

Rain commonly establishes in June or July, first in accessible western districts, then farther into the interior. The usual progression is Western Dunmar and Darba, the Yuvanti foothills and Copper Hills, Tokra and Songara, then the more distant northern peaks, Raven's Hold, Karawa and Gomat. Sparse rainfall at Askandi and in deep Garamjala can come later. [[The Dunmari Monsoon]] describes this sequence during an unfolding season; its intervals are not fixed annual delays.

During active periods, repeated rain wets the coast and exposed slopes, rivers flood and roads become muddy. The wetter districts can be cloudier and less fiercely hot by day than during the clear premonsoon interval, while remaining warm and humid. The interior receives less regular rain: intense local storms may alternate with dry ground and sunshine nearby. Alta Tonaro remains predominantly dry, with only rare summer thunderstorms.

Breaks occur when the trough shifts or the inflow weakens. Inland rain can pause while coastal or foothill showers continue, and clearer, hotter weather returns. The June–September occurrence window allows variable onset and retrea; no duration or number of active spells is assigned.

### Autumn: retreat and moisture carried forward

As sustained inflow weakens, dry intervals lengthen and nights moderate. A working expectation is that the least well-supplied interior districts lose dependable rain first, while exposed coastal slopes can retain showers. Retreat need not reverse the arrival order, and an isolated late storm can interrupt the drying.

Soils, pasture and rivers retain the effects of the earlier rains after the rainy weather itself subsides. The productive Dunmari plains of November 1748 fit this postmonsoon phase. November harvest and growth do not require a November monsoon. Drying roads can improve overland travel while remaining low ground or a swollen river still complicates a particular crossing.

Toward Alta Tonaro, renewed western disturbances eventually restore opportunities for winter rain, with timing left variable. Illoria's changing wind exposures and the tropical southern coast are not assigned the same autumn drying sequence as inland Dunmar.

## Water, landscapes and travel

The principal practical distinction is between **rain falling locally**, **water arriving from upstream**, and **water retained from an earlier season**. Their timing need not coincide. This explains the contrast between dry Hara country and its cultivated riverbanks, the forested Darba valleys through the dry season, and the flood that can reach Askandi before local rain.

| Setting or route                      | Climatic implications                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Darba and the lower Rudhan            | Wet-season floods affect low ground while rocky rises remain above the flooded flats. Coastal forests and perennial streams remain more dependable sources of moisture than the country toward the saddle.                |
| [[Tokra-Darba Road]] and Copper Hills | Dusty premonsoon travel changes to mud and difficult stream crossings during rain. Dry hills can produce brief torrents; one storm does not make the whole route equally wet.                                             |
| Nayan and Shrev lowlands              | Summer rain and flooding support the established agricultural landscape, while the moderately cooler dry season changes the balance between open grazing ground and watered river margins.                                |
| Northern Hara and Samtal              | More regular grass and water support grazing compared with Karawa and the southern basin; local storm tracks and mountain-fed water still matter.                                                                         |
| Karawa, Nashtkar and Garamjala        | Long dry intervals make wells, oases and known river routes important. A distant storm can send a flood through a canyon under locally clear skies.                                                                       |
| Xurkhaz river districts               | Irrigated fields and riparian vegetation depend on the larger river system within a persistently arid regional setting. Local rainfall is not a substitute for that supply.                                               |
| Nevos crossings toward Illoria        | Summer W–SW inflow can oppose westward sailing. The August 1748 voyage onward to Chardon met headwinds and increasingly variable northern winds, yet remained possible; this is an example, not a passage-time guarantee. |

These implications do not set planting dates, yields, flood depths, river discharge or annual route closures. The [[Day of Renewal]] follows the first monsoon rain locally, making variation between communities part of the established seasonal pattern.

## Weather examples and remaining choices

From play: inland premonsoon heat in spring 1748, Tokra's first rain on July 9 that year, wet western travel later in July, August monsoon headwinds at sea and cool postmonsoon productivity in November. These are individual years and places. Tokra's July 9 onset was slightly late rather than extraordinary; it is not an annual start date. See: [[Climate and Weather Constraints]] for details and sources. 

Ordinary weather can include a clear hot interval before the rains, a humid coastal spell while the basin waits, several wet days on exposed slopes, an isolated storm over dry desert, a sunnier monsoon break or a cool postmonsoon morning above still-green riverbanks. Stormcaller magic and supernatural local environments are outside this ordinary account.

The main undeveloped choices are Illoria's annual rainfall pattern; the strength of occasional cool-season coastal rain; the length and variability of monsoon spells; snow persistence in the Chataans and Yuvanti; and the water balance and terrain controls at the southern Garamjala–jungle transition. No precise forest boundary or shared Drankor–Orekatu calendar follows from this atlas.

The continental overview's [[Taelgar Climate Model#Dunmar and the Nevos|Dunmar and Nevos comparisons]] provide broad temperature and landscape analogies. They range from Darba's subtropical summer-rain climate through semiarid Hara to desert Garamjala, and from Nayan's seasonal lowlands toward wetter tropical forest. These are comparisons, not imported rainfall calendars or calculated climate classifications.
