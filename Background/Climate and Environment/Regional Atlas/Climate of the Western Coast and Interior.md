# Climate of the Western Coast and Interior

The west contains several climates connected by ocean access. Around Chardon, northern Apporia, and the southern river basins, mild rainy winters give way to a dependable summer dry season. Northward along the coast and exposed western forest belt, summers become cooler and their dry intervals shorter and more frequently interrupted. Mawakel remains exposed to maritime weather throughout the year. Inland, western forest slopes intercept moisture, while increasing Fiatara shelter gives a separate transition through productive Voltara grassland to the drier Erbalta Plains.

## The main climatic contrasts

Coastal moderation, rainfall, and river water have different limits. A cool, foggy morning at Chardon can accompany hot sunshine inland. A wet mountain catchment can maintain marshes or irrigated fields below a dry slope. The wooded country east of Valandros is therefore not a continuation of the sheltered Erbalta steppe, nor does summer water in the Yeraad imply a rainy summer throughout its basin.

## Chardon, the Coastlands, and Apporia

[[Chardon]] and the [[Gulf of Chardon]] have mild winters and comfortably warm summers. Fog recurs along the coast, but sunny warmth remains common. The southern [[Coastlands]] and sheltered northern Apporian lowlands have stronger summer heat; higher Apporian interiors are cooler, and open western shores receive more direct ocean wind and swell than the gulf coast. The gulf's relative shelter from severe storms does not make every crossing calm.

The Upper Coast is a productive, river-crossed farming belt. Moving north, summer drying becomes less persistent and maritime wet spells become more dependable. The coast remains generally milder than nearby hills and mountain interiors, but increasing latitude brings more winter cold. The [[Beacon Hills]] and [[Chardon Hills]] mark changes in exposure rather than a single sharp climatic boundary.

Higher interiors can be substantially colder than a rainy harbor. Rain replenishes agricultural soils and streams, while wet ground can slow overland travel. |
| Spring | Milder, brighter intervals lengthen between rain passages. Inland lowlands warm sooner than cool exposed shores. | Fog or low cloud can linger at the coast while nearby fields are sunny. Farming and travel benefit from drying intervals, but the season does not switch abruptly from wet to dry on one date. |
| Summer | Chardon and northern Apporia have a dependable dry season. | Autumn | Northern disturbances become more active, and rain returns progressively to the southern coast. Northern [[Apporia]] retains the winter-rain pattern, including the conditions supporting [[Raziolo]]'s winter wheat and olive groves. This does not prescribe the climate of every western coast or interior valley. Around southern [[Emerald Bay]], the variable northern fringe of Nevos summer rain can bring wetter, squally spells, interrupted by lighter or more easterly winds. These incursions become less characteristic toward northern Apporia and Chardon. The detailed southern circulation belongs in [[Climate of Dunmar and the Nevos]] and [[Dunmar and Nevos Circulation]].

For sailing, distinguish the exposed ocean coast, the sheltered gulf, and the changing monsoon fringe at the canal approaches. The summer [[Session 47 (DuFr)|Darba–Chardon voyage]] includes monsoon headwinds and increasingly tricky northern winds while remaining viable. It establishes possible passage, not a guaranteed favorable bearing or a standard voyage duration. Wind assistance within the canal is a separate magical circumstance.

## Chasa, Yeraad, and the southern forest margin

The [[Chasa River Valley]] is warmer inland than Chardon, with mild lowland winters and warm to hot summers. Its grainfields, vineyards, orchards, and pasture fit a long growing season supported by cool-season rain and river water. Occasional winter frost is most plausible in sheltered hollows and on higher ground; it does not make the whole lowland basin a regularly freezing district. Along the [[Nahadi]], irrigated fields draw on water carried from Valandros rather than depending entirely on local summer rain.

The lower [[Yeraad]] is a warm basin of marshes, oxbows, and riparian woods. Water arriving from forested and mountain catchments sustains wet ground during summer drying. Nearby higher land can be much drier: the southern Elderwood margin and the [[Myraeni Gap]] have open woodland, scrub, and stronger summer moisture stress, with greener stream corridors. The Chataans shelter this transition from much of the southern monsoon.

| Season | Basin and river lowlands | Hills and forest margin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Winter | Rain renews soils and river supplies; lowlands are predominantly mild, with localized cold nights. Marshes and poorly drained ground remain wet. | Higher slopes are cooler. Exposed ridges and cold-air hollows differ from the broad lowlands, and rain can make rough upland travel slower. |
| Spring | Increasing warmth and remaining rain support growth; water from higher catchments continues to reach the lowlands as local drying begins. | Sunny slopes dry sooner than shaded gullies. The forest transition can remain green after nearby open ground begins to lose surface moisture. |
| Summer | Hotter inland afternoons and limited rain contrast with wet riparian ground and river-supported cultivation. | Scrub and open woodland experience greater drying than protected stream corridors. The Elderwood's shaded lower forest retains more moisture than its exposed southern margin. |
| Autumn | Warm intervals continue before repeated rains replenish the basin. Clear inland nights cool more strongly than the coast. | The first wet passages moisten dry slopes unevenly; sheltered forest ground and open ridges need not change at the same rate. |

The practical distinction is between **rainfall on the route** and **water arriving from elsewhere**. Dry summer weather can coexist with difficult marsh crossings, a substantial river, and irrigated fields. Conversely, the existence of the Yeraad wetlands supplies no reason to fill the Myraeni uplands with uniformly wet forest. Exact floods, river stages, planting dates, and harvest calendars remain local questions.

## Valandros and the western forests

[[Lake Valandros]] lies between the warmer southern basins and colder northern country. Its immediate shores warm more slowly in spring and cool more slowly in autumn than nearby land. The Beacon Hills provide partial shelter, while western weather can still reach the lake basin and rise into the Sentinel foothills. The lake modifies that air locally; the western ocean remains the broad source of moisture.

| District | Seasonal character | Distinction to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Valandros shores and surrounding hills | Temperate summers and variable winter cold; cool-season rain and upland snow, with summer dry intervals and maritime interruptions. | Shoreline moderation fades inland and varies with wind. Lake effects do not establish a uniform wet ring or a lake-freezing calendar. |
| [[Tawir Forest]] | A moist, lush forest with frequent fog, within the transition toward colder northern country. | Its basic damp character is required. Possible unusual or magical influence remains unconfirmed; ordinary regional drying does not erase the local foggy forest. |
| Lower southern [[Ainumarya]] and [[Elderwood]] | Relatively gentle, low-snow lowland winters and moderate to warm summers, with a recognizable summer dry period. | Winter recharge, sheltered soils, and occasional summer showers support the productive giant-conifer forest. Higher slopes are colder and snowier. |
| [[Crimson Forest]], [[Highveil Forest]], and [[Forest of Dreams]] | Increasing frost and snow and shorter summers toward the north and higher terrain; summer moisture is more dependable than at the southern forest margin. | Forest of Dreams retains cold winters and upland snow. Highveil's open forest and Crimson's geography do not independently establish different rainfall totals. |
| [[Blackwater Fens]] and adjoining northern western forests | Long, cold, wet winters and short, cool summers, with fog and locally variable ice. | Wetland storage, thawing weather, and differences between waterways and exposed ground allow uneven freezing; the specific February open-water observation stays local. |

**Winter** brings rain and wet snow to lower transitional districts and more persistent snow higher up and farther north. A milder maritime passage can thaw a lake-shore or lower forest route while the mountain approaches remain snowy. Blackwater's cold season stays long and wet despite such breaks. Some navigable water and little notable ice on the party's February DR 1748 route must remain possible; this gives no general promise that the whole fen stays open every winter.

**Spring** reaches southern low forest before northern uplands. Valandros can keep a shoreward breeze cool even while sunny inland clearings warm. Rain and meltwater leave streams and wet ground active as the growing season begins; shaded snow can remain beyond the first mild days. In the fens, thawing pools and soft peat need not make foot travel easier than during a preceding cold spell.

**Summer** combines southern forest drying with intermittent showers and more dependable moisture farther north. The Elderwood can remain lush while rainfall becomes infrequent because water is stored in soils and catchments and shaded ground dries slowly. Recurring magical summer fog is an optional model proposal that would reinforce this effect; it is not an adopted explanation for every forest. At Valandros, a cool immediate shoreline can contrast with warmer country inland, where a lake breeze or passing disturbance may help showers develop. Northern wetlands retain short, cool, foggy conditions rather than a southern dry-season landscape.

**Autumn** brings renewed maritime wet spells and earlier frost inland and upslope. The lake delays cooling locally, while cold air crossing open water can produce enhanced showers downwind, especially toward rising ground. Forested lower slopes may show autumn color while peaks already carry snow. This fits the retained late-September and early-October northern mountain observations without fixing the date of the first snow in every year.

For travel, forest shade and wetland storage can preserve mud after open ground has dried. A route rising out of Ainumarya can enter snow well before it reaches the high Sentinel country, especially farther north. Valandros and the Nahadi remain important links between the northern hinterlands and Chardon, but their established transport role does not define winter ice, storm delays, or an annual navigation schedule.

## Mawakel, the Mawar Mountains, and the northern coast

[[Mawakel Peninsula]] is cool and maritime, with year-round precipitation and pronounced changes between settled and unsettled spells. The [[Mawar Mountains]] divide it from Tawir and the continental interior. Exposed headlands, narrow sheltered inlets, the Sulqat country, and higher mountain approaches can experience quite different weather during the same passage. Sheltered does not always mean warm: an inland hollow can be calm and frosty while a windy coast thaws.

| Season | Coast and inlets | Interior and higher country |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Winter | Cold northern outbreaks bring coastal snow; returning maritime air brings rain and thaws. Severe winters can sustain cold and ice for longer, while others are much milder. | Snow persists longer at elevation and farther from direct ocean moderation. A thawing coast can lie close to deep snow on a mountain approach. |
| Spring | Rain and cold showers interrupt fine intervals; a late wet spring is possible. | Warming and snowmelt progress unevenly. Higher routes can remain wintry after a sheltered shore has become mild. |
| Summer | Cool, pleasant intervals alternate with cloud, rain, and renewed wind. Storms are less dominant than in winter but remain possible. | Exposed slopes receive more precipitation than protected valleys. Fine lowland weather does not eliminate cold, wet conditions higher up. |
| Autumn | Rain and storm exposure increase, though warm days and calm seas can linger between systems. | Forest routes cool earlier; higher snow and cold nights can return while the coast remains relatively mild. |

The [[Mawar Ep 5 - Intro Draft|Mawar campaign retrospective]] preserves a severe winter, a late wet spring, a cool fine summer, a lingering warm autumn, and a subsequent milder winter. These illustrate year-to-year range, not an average timetable. Around [[Hamri]], calm water after a storm is compatible with a stormy maritime setting, and exposed swell can outlast the local wind. The physical page treats the placement of milder sheltered and colder snowy districts flexibly; the Gazetteer's precise compass-side pattern is not a required climatic boundary.

Northward along the [[Slate Sea]], the maritime belt becomes colder and the snowy season longer toward the [[Far North]]. Exposed shores still differ from sheltered interiors and high ground. This is an extension of the continental model into lightly developed country, with no assigned settlements, crop limits, snowfall calendar, or exact southern edge of polar conditions.

## Fiatara, Voltara, and the Erbalta Plains

The [[Fiatara Mountains]] separate wet, maritime approaches from the drier plains to the east. Their highest summits retain glaciers and heavy snow even in summer; this is a played constraint, not merely an inference from elevation. Meltwaters and springs supply rivers descending toward the plains. The fact that a field or riparian grove receives mountain water does not make its local rainfall abundant.

[[Voltara]] and southern [[Erbalta Plains|Erbalta]] have strongly continental seasons: warm to hot summer days, cooler nights, and cold winters interrupted by recurring thaws. Ordinary lowland snow cover is intermittent and uneven, although snow can last through prolonged cold spells and much longer in severe winters. Important late-spring and early-summer rains support productive grassland, with less dependable rain later in summer and additional cool-season precipitation. The annual precipitation maximum remains unassigned.

Western-ocean moisture reaches around the southern Fiatara, supporting a transition from southern forest through woodland and grassland into Voltara's productive hinterlands. Farther north and deeper in the mountain rain shadow, precipitation becomes less dependable and Erbalta's grazing landscape drier, though wetter foothills and slopes can remain wooded. This inland gradient follows shelter and moisture access, while the exposed western forest belt gains more dependable summer moisture northward. Longer freezes, more persistent snow, and shorter growing seasons become more characteristic in northern Erbalta.

| Season | Voltara and the sheltered plains | Fiatara high country |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Winter | Cold, often dry spells alternate with weather passages and recurring thaws. Snow around Voltara is intermittent and uneven, with sheltered drifts and longer cover during severe winters; farther-northern districts retain snow more readily. | Exposed western terrain receives substantial snow. Valleys and slopes behind the barrier receive less moisture without becoming entirely snow-free. |
| Spring | Warming and important late-spring showers favor grass growth. Southwestern wet spells reach Voltara more readily than deeply sheltered Erbalta. | Snowmelt feeds lower rivers while colder storms can rebuild snow higher up. Thaw progresses unevenly by slope and elevation. |
| Summer | Warm to hot days and cooler nights, with useful early-summer rain and less dependable moisture later. Fronts and thunderstorms interrupt sunny, dry spells. Drier grazing country contrasts with irrigated and riparian fields. | Glaciers and summit snow persist. Storms, fresh high snow, and changing melt conditions remain possible above warm plains. |
| Autumn | Cooling, rain passages, and dry intervals alternate; northern frosts return sooner. | New snow lasts progressively longer, while sunny or lower slopes may continue to thaw between events. |

Voltara's farming and sheep grazing require usable warmth and moisture, but they fix neither crop calendars nor the extent of the productive steppe. In drier Erbalta, established farming is concentrated in irrigated or riparian ground among a broader grazing landscape. Around Voltara, winter travel can cross bare frozen grassland beside surviving drifts, with muddy roads during thaws. Caravans moving between the plains and Valandros can encounter dry open country, muddy foothill passages, and a wetter lake basin on the same journey. A mountain expedition needs its own weather assessment: midsummer on the plains is not evidence of clear paths on the highest Fiatara.

## Evidence limits and adjoining regions

The required western outcomes are collected under K15–K16, K19–K22, K31–K32 and weather groups E10–E16/E21–E22 in [[Climate and Weather Constraints]]. Gazetteer landscapes and played episodes constrain this atlas, while individual rainy, frozen, calm, or warm days do not supply climate normals. Magical canal winds and locally heated Mawar conditions remain outside the ordinary baseline.

Local fog coverage, Valandros freezing, Blackwater's ordinary ice distribution, the reach of southwestern rain into Erbalta, and the detailed climates of western and interior Apporia remain open. No precise forest boundary, snowline, storm frequency, or crop calendar follows from these qualitative descriptions. Real-world parallels and possible Köppen fits remain in the [[Taelgar Climate Model#Western Coast and Interior|continental western overview]] rather than serving as extra evidence.

The neighboring high passes, icefields, and interior uplands are described in [[Climate of the Sentinels and Northern Highlands]]. The Nevos monsoon and the southern mountain transition are in [[Climate of Dunmar and the Nevos]]. Return to [[Regional Climate Atlas]] or [[Climate and Environment]].
