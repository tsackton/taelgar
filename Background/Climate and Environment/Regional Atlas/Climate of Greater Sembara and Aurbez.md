# Climate of Greater Sembara and Aurbez

Greater Sembara has four distinct seasons: spring rain and rising rivers, warm humid summers, autumn cooling, and changeable winters with frost, snow, rain, and thaws. Maritime influence softens the Gulf shores; cold increases toward the northern interior and higher ground. Southwestward, moisture becomes less dependable, and the hotter Maseau and Andonne lowlands give way to Aurbez's dry, elevated country.

This atlas develops the fixed [[Taelgar Climate Model Revised#Greater Sembara and Aurbez|continental overview]]. [[Climate and Weather Constraints]] maintains established requirements and recorded weather. More detailed seasonal sequences and local effects below are qualitative model predictions, not new canon, measured averages, or an annual farming calendar.

## What organizes the climate

The Western Gulf supplies moisture and limits temperature extremes near its shores. Spring and summer maritime inflow produces the broad warm-season rainfall emphasis; moving disturbances still bring useful cool-season rain and snow. A winter with less precipitation can remain muddy because evaporation and plant water use are low, while intermittent thaws add surface water. A wetter summer need not mean daily rain.

The Mostreve Hills form broken ridges and intervening gaps. Maritime air rises on exposed slopes, while some southwestern slopes and valleys lie in shelter. Lower gaps admit weather inland rather than sealing off the interior. The Salt Hills and mountain shelter toward Aurbez add to inland moisture loss, helping distinguish dry plateau country from the humid Sembaran riverlands.

The mechanisms and maps are in [[Green Sea Atmospheric Circulation#Seasonal circulation over Sembara|the atmospheric model]], [[Green Sea Ocean Circulation#The Western Gulf|the Gulf ocean model]], and [[Sentinels and Continental Weather Connections]]. The atlas describes their local consequences; exact storm tracks, rainfall amounts, and inland limits remain open.

## Local climatic contrasts

### Sembaran riverlands and foothills

The central riverlands have warm summers and distinct but variable winters. A cold spell can freeze exposed ground, while a later thaw exposes grass and leaves liquid puddles on roads. Snow lasts longer northward and toward the western highlands than in lower, more maritime districts. Summer warmth and spring–summer rain support the established productive farmland and woodland.

Within a river district, well-drained slopes and raised ground can be workable while floodplain fields remain wet. Low hollows can hold colder night air during clear, quiet weather; exposed slopes can be windy, and upland showers need not reach every valley. These are useful local predictions, not new boundaries or frost dates.

The [[Enst]] retains both a spring meltwater rise and a later summer rise. Those are catchment requirements, not a single flood calendar for every Sembaran river. Rivers draw water from highland snow, rain, and storage across their watersheds, so flow at a town need not match that day's local weather.

### Tyrwingha and the southern Gulf shore

Tyrwingha is the moderated, comparatively well-watered coastal edge. Winters are milder than exposed inland or elevated districts; summers are warm, with maritime moisture and rain. Recurring cool-season wet spells remain part of the climate. Sea breezes can temper a warm afternoon near the shore while sheltered inland ground stays hotter; their reach and regularity are unassigned.

The northern slopes of Mostreve provide wooded uplands above the fertile lowlands. Terrain-facing maritime winds can produce persistent low cloud and rain there even when nearby sheltered districts have breaks. [[Tyrwingha]]'s wheat, wine, and timber are established land-use anchors. They require workable growing and harvesting weather, not a uniformly wet coast or a claim that all wine country has dry summers.

### Addermarch and the Mostreve Hills

Addermarch's lower valleys have warm to hot, humid, rainy summers and relatively mild winters with little lowland snow. Higher ground is cooler, snowier, and harder to cross in winter and the spring transition. The broad contrast with wetter, more wooded Tyrwinghan exposures is retained, but the hills contain many local departures.

The steep [[Westcliff]] ridge, the [[Maerwyn Mountains]], and the lower [[Valenfray Hills]] and [[Tyrwinghan Hills]] create separate slopes and gaps. The [[Aure]] and its tributary valleys provide milder, settled corridors; higher meadows and exposed ridges have a shorter season and more wind. Snow on a high road can coexist with rain, running water, and mud below.

Climate is only part of the landscape contrast. The [[Mostreve Hills]] and [[Addermarch]] also distinguish poor rocky or sandy ground from more productive valleys. Open high pasture does not prove an arid regional climate. Orchards, small farms, forestry, highland cattle, and sheep are grounded land uses; warm-season grazing on high ground need not imply year-round access or cropping there.

### Tollen and the lower Volta

Tollen has moderately warm summers and winters that frequently alternate frost and thaw. Winter snow often melts between events, and rain occurs in warm and cool seasons. The main lower Volta normally remains open; sustained freezing is exceptional. This relatively mild tidal setting differs from colder coastal Vostok and the frequently frozen rivers of Pyhlla farther north.

The Gulf remains relatively sheltered from severe storms and retains open water through winter, while exceptional winters may freeze protected harbors. Sheltered does not mean that fronts, rain, gusts, fog, or difficult river approaches disappear. The adopted freshwater surplus drives a fresher surface export through the straits over deeper, saltier inflow, with wind and tide complicating individual passages.

[[Hydrology of Tollen]] places flood-prone low ground along the southern riverbanks and tributary mouths. High river flow and high tide can therefore make low approaches difficult even without a severe regional storm. The relative timing of tides, catchment rain, and melt matters more than assigning a universal wet month. The exact flood frequency and reach remain open.

### Maseau, Andonne, and the Salt Hills

The lowlands toward Maseau and Andonne have hotter summers and greater annual temperature contrast than the immediate Gulf coast. Spring rain is important; summer has long dry intervals interrupted by thunderstorms. Inland paths and shelter reduce moisture, while the Salt Hills mark part of the transition toward Aurbez. A sheltered field can dry rapidly after a shower even while a more maritime Sembaran district remains humid.

Summer easterly winds can carry ash from the Plaguelands into the Andonne country. Occasional deeper moisture incursions bring severe storms, including hail, lightning, and flash floods, without turning the season broadly wet. Ordinary wind transport and the pre-existing ash hazard should remain distinct from any proposed magical control of regional weather.

The [[March of Andonne]]'s ranching and scattered dryland trees reflect both dry summers and damage from ash. Crop losses there cannot be used as a rainfall measurement. The more sheltered and watered margins differ from the exposed plains, and Maseau as a whole should not inherit the harshest conditions near the Plaguelands.

### Aurbez Plateau

Elevation cools Aurbez above the adjoining hot lowlands. Summer days can be warm while nights cool markedly; winter is cold and generally dry, with episodic snow. Important spring rains and occasional severe summer thunderstorms punctuate a drier regime. Sunshine does not ensure warmth on an exposed winter road, and a sheltered plateau valley can differ from a windy rim.

The plateau's streams and [[Aursen]] marshes do not imply rainy summers. [[Aurbez Plateau]] explicitly attributes reliable streams to Sentinel snowmelt and places trees and small farms along watered corridors, with drier scrubgrass on exposed hills. Snowmelt and other catchment storage can maintain local water after rain becomes scarce; the relative contribution and duration are unquantified. Local access to water can make the plateau more usable than the ash-exposed plains despite its broadly dry rainfall climate.

Routes climbing beyond Aurbez into the southern Sentinels enter a distinct high-country regime. Winter snow closes higher passes, with snow possible outside winter and glaciers at the highest peaks. A workable plateau road is not evidence that a cross-range pass has opened; the details belong in [[Climate of the Sentinels and Northern Highlands]].

### Upper Istaros and historical Isingue

The adjoining Upper Istaros lowlands form a warmer temperate transition, with warm to hot summers and relatively mild winters. Seasonal rainfall supports the historical productive lowlands, while moisture deficits strengthen toward the southern interior. The exact rainfall calendar is less developed than in Sembara or Aurbez; it should not be filled by extending either neighbor's season unchanged.

The climate baseline is separate from the magical fertility associated with historical Isingue and from the subsequent damage of the Plaguelands. Neither extraordinary fertility nor present desolation establishes ordinary rainfall. This atlas does not infer modern agricultural recovery or land cover from the historical climate. Farther south, [[Southern Tropical and Desert Circulation]] treats the separate desert and tropical connections.

## Four seasonal patterns

### Winter

Sembaran lowlands alternate cold spells with maritime or storm-related thaws. Rain, sleet, and snow depend on the depth of cold air and the path of each disturbance. Northern and elevated districts keep snow longer; Tyrwingha and Tollen more often have rain, patchy snow, and wet ground. A frozen morning can turn muddy later without ending winter.

The proposed southern storm corridor allows disturbances crossing the Sentinels aloft to draw fresh Green Sea moisture inland. It supplies intermittent wet weather without requiring severe Gulf storms to be routine. Between systems, continental air can be cold and dry. Aurbez is generally drier and colder than the coast, but occasional storms can lay snow across the plateau; no particular river-freezing rule follows.

### Spring

Green Sea air increasingly replaces cold western or continental flow. Early mild incursions over cold land favor mist, low cloud, and prolonged light rain. Clear intervals bring rapid daytime warming, while a renewed cold spell can return snow to uplands. The DR 1720 transition described in [[April Around Asineau]] is an example of this progression, not a fixed date at which all Sembara changes season.

Rain and mountain melt raise rivers, support winter grain and pasture, and periodically leave fields too wet to work. Mostreve high roads lag behind lower river valleys. Southwestward, soaking spring rain is particularly valuable before the summer dry intervals. Aurbez streams can swell from mountain melt even when local showers are limited, and a thawing plateau does not establish safe passage through the snowy mountains beyond.

### Summer

The riverlands and exposed Mostreve slopes are warm, humid, and comparatively rainy. Maritime inflow can produce bright humid days, scattered afternoon storms, or more extensive rain when a disturbance supplies stronger lift. Dry intervals remain ordinary and necessary for soil work, haymaking, and harvest. Northern or western foothills stay cooler than the lowlands; the immediate Gulf shore is moderated relative to inland heat.

The same broad circulation has a different expression in sheltered Maseau, Andonne, and Aurbez. Most days in a dry interval bring little useful rain, and ash-bearing easterlies can cross the Andonne plains. Occasional severe storms arrive with deeper moisture and lift. One flooded gully after a thunderstorm can sit beside dry pasture; storm rainfall does not necessarily replenish an entire district. Aurbez's cooler nights remain distinct from hotter adjacent lowlands.

### Autumn

Stored warmth in the Gulf prolongs mild coastal spells while inland nights cool more quickly. Rain-bearing disturbances alternate with clear, cooler intervals; the latter can bring local frost inland before maritime lowlands experience sustained cold. Growing conditions retreat first from higher and more northern districts, with snow returning to the highest routes.

Rain can establish autumn-sown grain, but wet spells also interrupt harvests and travel. The recorded October Enst flood was exceptional rather than a normal autumn flood season. In the southwest, rivers may diminish after the dry summer and declining snowmelt, while briefly moderate temperatures ease travel between heat and winter cold. This is a broad pattern, not a guarantee of low water or dry roads on every autumn journey.

## Farming, rivers, and travel

The [[Heartlands]], Semb and Wistel valleys, and [[Cleenseau Region]] require a dependable productive growing season. Winter grain, summer crops, pasture, and orchards constrain the model more strongly than a particular Earth climate label. Rain must supply soil moisture while leaving ordinary drying intervals. Persistently saturated fields or routine rain-ruined harvests would contradict the required agricultural character. Crop timing and detailed practices belong in [[Agriculture and Seasonal Landscapes]].

The seasons affect different activities at different times. Spring water benefits growth but can delay carts and sowing on heavy ground. Summer storms can interrupt haymaking while supporting pasture and later crops. Autumn rain helps establishment of winter grain but complicates harvest and road travel. Winter mud, thaw, and snow can make a nominally open lowland route slow even without a hard closure.

Elevation and water access matter as much as broad rainfall. Addermarch's orchard valleys and upland grazing need different weather allowances. Aurbez's watered small farms and the Andonne ranchlands should not be given the same crop expectations as the Sembaran heartlands. Floodwater in a river does not automatically irrigate neighboring high ground, and no new irrigation system is assumed.

The Western Gulf supports frequent shorter voyages through much of the year, interrupted by local weather and currents. Long northern passages follow the seasonal windows in [[Trading in the Green Sea]]. An open Tollen harbor does not guarantee easy strait passage or accessible inland roads, and a strong outward surface current may impede an inbound ship even under otherwise moderate weather.

## Evidence and model limits

The [[Climate and Weather Constraints#Sembara, Tyrwingha and Addermarch|Sembaran requirements]] retain four seasons, productive agriculture, warm-season rainfall emphasis, coastal moderation, and Mostreve elevation and exposure contrasts. The [[Climate and Weather Constraints#Aurbez and the Andonne|Aurbez requirements]] retain important spring rain, dry summer easterlies, occasional severe storms, and variable cold winters. These regional differences have stronger standing than any proposed detailed storm mechanism.

Recorded weather tests plausible variability: the Asineau–Veltor–Peydon winter of DR 1720 included cold, exposed grass, and later liquid puddles; spring DR 1715 around Addermarch mixed late snow or rain, sunshine, and cold gray weather; Tollen visits recorded cold winter docks and a pleasant warm sea breeze in spring. No individual event supplies a normal temperature, annual snow date, or guaranteed river access. The October DR 1719 Enst flood remains explicitly unusual.

Several limits matter for future detail. The inland reach of maritime moderation is not measured. Broken Mostreve ridges and the Salt Hills offer plausible shelter, but the precise boundary between humid riverland and dry southwest remains open. Winter storm redevelopment and occasional Aurbez moisture incursions are hypotheses, not established storm tracks. The Gulf's adopted exchange also needs a consistent heat and water budget before it can support calculated temperatures or local ice limits. No complete sea-ice scenario is adopted.

Real-world parallels and tentative climate classes are retained in the continental overview. They help compare coastal, inland, and upland regimes; they do not determine temperatures, precipitation totals, frost dates, river-freezing thresholds, or agricultural calendars here.
