# Climate of the Green Sea and Its Shores

The Green Sea joins cold northern forests, warm Cymean coasts, dry southern margins, and a long chain of ocean-exposed islands. Its recognizable sailing seasons connect these climates without making weather uniform across the basin. A damp, chilly northern passage can coincide with warm inland fields, a humid Cymean coast, and dry heat near Medju.

This atlas develops the fixed [[Taelgar Climate Model#Green Sea and Its Shores|continental overview]]. Established requirements and individual observations are maintained in [[Climate and Weather Constraints]]; the seasonal details below are qualitative model predictions where no local evidence exists. They provide plausible conditions for play, not new canon or a weather calendar.

## What organizes the climate

Three contrasts matter at local scale: latitude, exposure to the sea, and elevation. Northern shores encounter continental cold more often than the Western Gulf and southern basin. A cold current also delays northern coastal warming, while warm southern inflow moderates southern shores. Inland valleys can warm strongly after the thaw even while cold water keeps nearby headlands chilly. Mountains preserve snow above those valleys and change which slopes receive maritime rain or snow.

The seasonal winds arise from the eastern subtropical ocean high, a secondary cool northeastern maritime ridge, and the Cymean low and offshore trough. The separate Great Desert heat low affects southern winds without requiring a rainy desert. Their geography and four seasonal maps are in [[Green Sea Atmospheric Circulation]]. Irrla does not generate the basin's primary high, and a hot Urskan land high is not the summer explanation.

The adopted current model brings cold water west along the northern shore and warm water west through the southern sea, then north west of Irrla. Their shared offshore return runs east north of Irrla. These are distinct routes: a coastal cold current can run opposite to a vessel's useful offshore current. [[Green Sea Ocean Circulation]] explains the seasonal changes, Western Gulf exchange, marine productivity, and limits of this qualitative circulation.

## Local climatic contrasts

### Coastal Vostok and Skaerhem

These shores have cooler summers, later spring warming, and more frequent or persistent winter freezing than Tollen. The contrast develops gradually with latitude, northern cold exposure, and the coastal current. Maritime air still brings thaws, rain, and wet snow; the islands are not uniformly locked into uninterrupted deep winter.

Exposed headlands and channels experience much more wind and spray than sheltered shores. A plausible local contrast is low cloud over cold water while a protected valley clears and warms. Snowfall also varies with storm approach and relief. [[Pyhlla]] retains frequent winter snow cover and regular river freezing, while [[Vetta]] does not require huge winter snowfall. These facts do not establish a common island snow depth or an annual sea-ice pattern. Skaerhem's storminess and marine bounty remain compatible with substantial local differences.

### Ursk's coast, valleys, and northern forest

Ursk has substantial cold winters and a meaningful warm summer. The settled grain valleys, including the [[Yamgov]] district, must retain enough warmth and rain for their established agriculture. Cold water and summer maritime easterlies can cool the immediate shore without suppressing warmth throughout the inland lowlands. During ridge breaks or sheltered weather, inland days can be much warmer than the coast; rain-bearing disturbances replenish soil moisture between settled intervals.

Northward and into the [[Great Northern Forest]], winters lengthen and spring arrives later. Summer rain, snowmelt, and relatively low moisture loss sustain boreal woodland; that does not require continuous wet weather. Clear summer days and usable sheltered growing sites coexist with cold hollows, wet ground, and a short season. The Ket hinterland is more strongly continental than the coast. Its river freezing and the difficulty of thaw travel belong to freshwater and catchment conditions, not a marine-ice calendar.

### Svinjo, Isenborg, and Yengorza

The Svinjo are Vostok's eastern mountain barrier; Yengorza is the distinct mountain country north of Ursk. Isenborg occupies high Svinjo terrain. Their climate combines northern latitude, elevation, continental cold, and intermittent maritime snow. The Sentinel Range's west-wet/east-dry pattern should not be copied onto them: the exposed face changes with local relief and storm track.

Winter and early spring alternate clear cold with snow-bearing disturbances. Falling and windblown snow can erase visibility on saddles while a sheltered valley remains much less severe. Valley thaw leaves high drifts, shaded snowbeds, cornices, and avalanche terrain. Warming or fresh wind-loaded snow can therefore make late-spring travel dangerous after lower roads have cleared.

The short highland summer supports plant growth on lower slopes while ordinary glaciers persist around Isenborg and in Yengorza. Autumn snow rebuilds first on high terrain. No snowline, glacier area, or dependable pass-opening date is assigned. [[Sentinels and Continental Weather Connections#Northern Green Sea highlands|The physical highland account]] explains these processes separately from Isenborg's magical lights and supernatural ice settings.

### Cymea and its uplands

Cymean coasts have mild winters and warm to hot summers, with moderation strongest beside the sea. Maritime rainfall includes summer moisture on exposed coasts and recurring cool-season wet spells. [[Western Cymea]]'s wine, cattle, and fishing fit this broad character; they do not fix rainfall totals or a universal Mediterranean summer drought.

The Plataca Hills divide western districts from the eastern plains, while the Mistfold Mountains occupy the southern part of the peninsula. Higher terrain is cooler and more seasonal than low coasts. Moist air rising over exposed slopes brings cloud and enhanced rainfall; valleys behind a ridge can be warmer, brighter, and drier during the same episode. Which coast is exposed changes with the wind, so neither all of Cymea nor every coastal slope shares one rainfall regime.

The summer model also permits cold upwelling patches beside west-facing Cymean shores under southward winds. These can favor fog or cooler coastal afternoons locally without making the whole peninsula cold. Opposing east-facing shores have different wind-driven water movement. The relevant orientation is developed in [[Green Sea Ocean Circulation#The Western Gulf|the Gulf ocean model]].

### Southern shores, Irrla, and the eastern islands

Southern Cymean lowlands grade from warm maritime country toward the hotter, drier Medju coast and desert margins. Immediate shores have less temperature variation than inland basins. Occasional coastal moisture or a passing storm need not penetrate the arid interior. Medju's winter light-wind delays concern sailing, not a requirement that every winter day be rainless or cloudless.

Irrla and the distant southern peninsulas have ocean-moderated lowlands, cooler highlands, wetter exposed slopes, and drier sheltered districts as a model prediction. Existing Irrlan climate and ecological descriptions are excluded as constraints; accepted trading geography supplies no independent rainfall requirement. More detailed local seasons remain open.

The Eastern Isles span a large north–south temperature gradient. Their northern islands are cooler and more affected by the northern storm belt; farther south, warm maritime conditions have a weaker cold season. Relief, exposure, and changing winds produce local wet and dry sides. The baseline does not require a continuous atmospheric wall or a permanent magical storm belt. Local magical departures need their own treatment and cannot calibrate ordinary basin weather.

The Far North similarly remains a broad projection: long, bitter winters and short summers grade toward tundra and polar conditions. Its remote coastlines, snow distribution, and local seasonal timing are largely unmodeled.

## Four seasonal patterns

### Winter

Northern coasts alternate cold continental outbreaks with maritime disturbances bringing wind, rain, sleet, or snow. Inland Ursk and the forest retain stronger and more persistent cold; mountain saddles can become dangerous in blowing snow. The same disturbance can bring rain to a lower shore and snow inland or above it. Thaws are compatible with a cold winter and need not clear all snow.

Northern westerlies and storms make long passages hazardous even when eastward movement is possible. Cymean lowlands are much milder, with intermittent wet spells rather than an imposed dry winter. Farther south, the subtropical ridge favors settled intervals and increasing sailing delays around Medju, strongest in January and February. Winter conditions therefore differ sharply between a northern exposed crossing and a southern ship waiting for useful wind.

### Spring

The northern sea and snow-covered hinterlands warm slowly. Cold coastal water can sustain fog or low cloud when milder air arrives, while inland clear spells begin the thaw. Rain and snowmelt produce muddy forest roads and rising rivers; shaded ground and uplands lag behind open valleys. A warm afternoon is not evidence that a mountain route or river crossing has become dependable.

Spring westerlies support eastbound northern voyages through June, and northbound opportunities develop in the western basin. Southern westbound travel from Medju opens in late March and April. Around Cymea, growing land–sea contrasts bring increasingly humid air and showers to exposed districts. Wind reversals develop unevenly, so a useful southern departure can coexist with unsettled northern approaches and uncertain port access.

### Summer

Recurring July–August easterlies and northeasterlies create the northern westbound season. The cool northeastern maritime ridge must extend far enough west, with lower pressure south of the route; the large subtropical high alone cannot produce that corridor. During these episodes the northern coast can be damp, cloudy, and cool. Inland sunshine and ridge breaks must still leave Ursk's valleys a warm, rain-supplied growing season.

Cymea and the western basin receive maritime inflow around the Cymean low, with humid days, showers, and thunderstorms where air rises. Sheltered valleys have longer sunny intervals. The southern sea has a separate easterly component toward the desert heat low, while Medju and the desert margins remain much drier than the moist Cymean exposures. A low-pressure area is not itself proof of rain.

### Autumn

Land cools faster than the sea. Northern inland nights turn frosty while maritime shores retain mild spells; cold air crossing warmer water can bring showers, gusts, and locally heavy precipitation. Mountain snow returns before lower valleys settle into winter. Increasing storm activity gives clear, brisk intervals between deteriorations rather than one continuous autumn gale.

Northern westerly opportunities return around mid-September to early October, allowing eastbound departures from Ursk. Southeastern Irrla also gains late-September and early-October eastbound opportunities as the ridge retreats. Later southbound and southwestbound legs toward Medju require successive favorable situations. A high east of an inner-island passage supplies northward flow on its western flank; southbound weather needs a different temporary ridge position or a passing low. The whole itinerary cannot be assigned one continuous fair wind.

## Travel, water, and livelihoods

[[Trading in the Green Sea]] controls the navigation requirements. Ordinary same-year Tollen–Ursk round trips use spring or early-summer eastbound travel and summer westbound returns. Autumn departures from Ursk and selected southeastern island circuits are also ordinary possibilities. Onset varies, and ships need time for weather, trade, currents, and channel access; these are opportunities rather than guaranteed passage days.

Northern eastbound ships can seek the offshore eastward return, then cross the cold coastal branch to approach ports. Westbound ships can use that coastal current but accept less sea room and greater navigational hazards; the offshore option is slower and roomier. The direct crossing does not assume safe intermediate harbors in the Skaer Islands. Early- to mid-May access to selected Ursk ports remains a trade-model assumption, possibly aided locally by magic, not evidence for a coastwide thaw date.

The Gulf is relatively sheltered from severe storms and never freezes as a whole in the retained model. Exceptional protected-harbor freezing remains possible. Neither a snowy Pyhlla nor a frozen Ket establishes sea ice offshore. No high-ice or low-ice scenario, onset, or clearance calendar is adopted here; [[Green Sea Ocean Circulation#Sea ice and freshwater freezing|the ocean note]] preserves the distinction.

On land, the broad pattern favors a short, concentrated northern growing season and longer warm seasons toward Cymea. Ursk's grain valleys are an explicit requirement; the forest and mountains should not be used to cap all Urskan farming. Thaw mud, river changes, and mountain snow affect overland travel at different times, so a workable coastal voyage says little about a highland route. Cymean wine and grazing are grounded locally; crops and harvest dates on undeveloped islands remain open.

## Evidence and model limits

The maintained [[Climate and Weather Constraints#Green Sea, Skaerhem and Ursk|regional requirements]] distinguish established seasonal character from mechanisms. The December DR 1748 Skaer visit encountered no substantial sea ice; the March DR 1749 Svinjo approach changed from cold clear weather to a natural whiteout and back to clearing; the Ursk journey in spring DR 1749 moved through snow, mud, and weakening ice before growth near Yamgov by May 1. These events fit the model without prescribing an annual schedule elsewhere.

Three physical questions remain consequential. The northern summer ridge must recur across a long enough corridor while allowing both Urskan warmth and spring/autumn westerlies. Autumn island routes need sufficiently frequent changes in pressure geometry. The adopted warm and cold inflows and Gulf exchange require a consistent water and heat budget; their existence alone does not calculate coastal temperature or prove an ice boundary. These limits are developed in the two physical circulation notes and should accompany any more precise regional prediction.
