# Climate and Weather Constraints — Review Draft

**Draft for review, 26 September 2026.** Proposed content for [[Climate and Weather Constraints]]. The existing [[Taelgar Climatic Model#Climatic Constraints|live constraints section]] remains unchanged.

This note records what the physical climate model must accommodate. Broad climatic requirements are separated from weather on particular dates. Crops, rivers, roads, festivals and vegetation are grouped as supporting evidence for climate; they do not each require another climate-model row or a downstream simulation.

## Reading the tables

- **Established** means a retained played fact or explicitly adopted requirement. **Flexible** means accepted reference or worldbuilding character whose details may change. Mixed cases state which part is firm. These are reviewed interpretations, not blanket endorsements of every claim in the linked source.
- **Campaign weather** normally represents plausible, broadly typical conditions unless qualified. A single year can be exceptional and does not establish a fixed onset date, freeze date, rainfall season or event frequency. Prep and backstory examples retain their different standing.
- **Covered** means the current physical model accommodates the required pattern. **Partial** means a meaningful retained element still lacks treatment. **Unmodeled** means no regional treatment addresses that requirement, even if circulation in a neighboring region exists. **Tension** would identify an active incompatibility; it is distinct from a gap.
- Coverage refers to the developed sections of [[Taelgar Climatic Model]] and its linked physical models, assessed on 26 September 2026. Repeating a fact in its old constraints table is not model coverage. A covered weather example is possible within the model; the model need not generate that exact historical day.
- New model choices—such as the offshore ridge’s reach, Voltara’s rainfall calendar or the selected forest-moisture mechanism—remain model choices. They do not acquire independent source authority by appearing in this register.

The draft contains **32 consolidated climatic requirements** and **22 weather groups**. Broad requirements: **23 covered, 2 partial, 7 unmodeled**. No active incompatibility is identified among these retained requirements; the partial and unmodeled rows are gaps, not conflicts.

**Supporting observations and limits.** Monsoon floods, muddy roads, renewed vegetation and festivals support **K01**, rather than separate climate requirements. November harvest and productivity belong to the cooler postmonsoon season. Darba’s seasonal forests and year-round rivers support **K03** without requiring substantial winter rain. These water and landscape facts are compatibility checks, not a demand for river or agricultural models.

## Retcons, exclusions and source authority

- **Dunmar:** the early winter-rain, spring-bloom desert design is superseded by the monsoon model. Early “cold nights” mean a large day/night contrast, not necessarily absolute cold. Premonsoon “first clouds in months” means generally clear skies. November fertility does not extend the monsoon into November. Obsolete religious/historical rain accounts supply no modern rainfall calendar.
- **Sembara:** retain four seasons and greater warm-season precipitation, but release the old strictly dry-winter design and numerical climate classes. Southern campaign weather allows winter rain and snow. Most northern local details remain less constrained.
- **Green Sea:** [[Trading in the Green Sea]] controls sailing requirements. The older [[Green Sea]] overview is unreliable and creates no constraints. **Irrla is entirely excluded as a source of climate constraints**; its climate is to be determined by the model. Neither accepted trade routes nor apparent ecological clues restore local Irrlan constraints.
- **Magic and irrelevant environments:** the Plaguelands, Eastern Isles, Sea of Storms, Circular Island, Orenlas’s supernatural local weather, magical Chasm weather, dragon-lair conditions, Feywild/elemental scenes and explicit magical storms or cold are excluded from ordinary physical-climate requirements. This applies even to apparently ordinary Eastern Isles vegetation or weather. High-atmosphere cold and deep magical ice-worm habitat are irrelevant to surface climate. Ordinary northern glaciers and ordinary snow seen by scrying remain usable evidence.
- **Mixed cases:** Tawir’s basic forest/fog and Blackwater’s played February conditions remain qualified requirements. Possible magic is not a confirmed explanation or a reason to discard these observations.
- **Unreliable precision and brainstorming:** old Skaer seasonal sketches, precise Ket freeze dates and ice thickness, proposed drier northern forests, obsolete central-steppe ideas and raw development calendars are not requirements. Nor are real-world analogies, an older climate map’s authority, or duplicated planetary/calendar assumptions. Accepted broad character is stated above.
- **Undeveloped or unlocated material:** the undeveloped Far North and distant eastern/northeastern coasts impose no constraints. Lavnoch contributes no separate requirement. Unlocated mountain snow, redundant elevation-only observations, rejected minor backstories, dubious noncanonical Etienne travel material and rejected historical weather anecdotes remain outside this register.
- **Navigation and chronology:** ship-specific capabilities do not constrain regional winds. The Emerald Song’s ordinary surrounding weather is retained, but canal magic and voyage duration do not set a natural wind speed. Wellby’s revisable backstory does not impose an early pass-opening date.

## Maintenance

When adding a requirement, state the smallest climatic fact supported by the evidence, its place and season, its standing, and any accepted exception. Attach downstream observations to an existing pattern when they add no distinct climatic bound. Keep source weather dates separate from typical seasonal descriptions. Reassess coverage after changing a physical model, and preserve unmodeled regions without inventing a model to fill the table.
