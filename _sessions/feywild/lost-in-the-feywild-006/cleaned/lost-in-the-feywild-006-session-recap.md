#### Short
We begin in the library, where the party questions a clockwork research assistant. 

#### Intermediate
In the library outside the central prison, the party questions a clockwork research assistant about the people who have passed through the locked door. The assistant recalls an order member who consulted *Allegories for the Initiated* before entering and reports that 112 visitors later emerged as mushroom men.

#### Long
We begin in the library outside the central prison. After reconsidering their tangled obligations to Hulda, Lord Endless Ending, and the prisoner, the party turns to the limbless clockwork figure watching over the library. It explains that it has read every volume, measures the passage of centuries by listening to their paper age, and out of boredom, has taken to writing stories about everyone it meets.

Although forbidden to answer direct questions about the prisoner, it remembers an unnamed human member of Arithrimos Lamperum who consulted *Allegories for the Initiated* before entering the central room. The assistant confirms that the glass key could open the complex's front door, but warns that 112 of the prison's visitors -- roughly two thirds of everyone who entered -- returned as mushroom men while the rest never emerged.

#### Short
Prompted by the clockwork automaton, the party reads a work on soul bindery written by a member of the Arithrimos Lamperum. Edric recoils at this evil, and the party is decides to enter the prison and to free the prisoner. 

#### Intermediate
Prompted by the clockwork automaton, the party reads *On the Science and Possibilities of Soul Bindery*, by a member of the Arithrimos Lamperum, which advocates harnessing captive souls. Edric senses profound wrongness in both the book and clockwork assistant; convinced the prisoner is being exploited, the party chooses to enter the prison door and free the prisoner. 

#### Long
Meanwhile, Txarro consults his lizardfolk ancestors, who suggest that returning the glass key to Lord Endless Ending could bring both good and ill, but there is no obvious evil path here. Seeking to learn more from the library, the party asks the automaton for information, who directs them to *On the Science and Possibilities of Soul Bindery*. Its unnamed Arithrimos Lamperum author enthusiastically proposes using captive souls to power liches, golems, and other devices. 

The party begins to suspect that the central prisoner is being exploited for power. Menestheus, feverish and fading, denies knowing of such work but continues to defend the order's mission. Edric then senses that both the tome and the assistant violate the proper order of life and death, convincing the group to enter the prison and perhaps free the prisoner, though before they do Txarro restores himself from the fungal infection and burns the book on soul bindery.

#### Short
As the party prepares to enter the prison, the automaton takes the name Aurae and tells each party member [[Aurae's Allegories|stories about themselves]]. Proceeding with Aurae on Edric's back, they evade the transformed Menestheus and enter the prison through The Child's door.

#### Intermediate
As the party prepares to enter the prison, the assistant takes the name Aurae, tells four personalized [[Aurae's Allegories|allegorical tales]], and joins the party in a makeshift backpack on Edric's back. At three superimposed doors showing the prisoner at different ages, the group choose The Child; Menestheus transforms and lunges for Txarro, but the group escapes into the lightless prison.

#### Long
As the party prepares to enter the prison, they ask the assistant if it wants a name. It accepts the name Aurae and tells each party member an [[Aurae's Allegories|allegorical tale]]: Edric must decide how to help a stolen sun and its grieving godmother, Kaito is a wandering seed searching for a place to grow, Ayveen is a forester seeking where a displaced sapling belongs, and Txarro is a protector confronting the forgotten monster beneath a healer's house.

After hearing these tales, the party straps Aurae into a backpack on Edric's back, and steps towards the prison door,  passes many shelves, noting a copy of *The Journey of the Souls*, along with a commentary and refutation, as well as *The Riven Veil* and several other works of Marcion of Iridel. 

At the prison door, they find three doors occupying the same space, showing the captive as a child, a joyful young adult, and a frightened, prematurely aged figure deep in the grip of some addiction. They choose the child, but as they prepare to step through the door, Menestheus completes his transformation and reaches for Txarro with impossibly long limbs. Txarro becomes a tiny anole lizard, evades him, and follows the others through the door. This leaves the glass key engaged in the lock behind them, and the door swings closed leaving them in impenetrable darkness with Menestheus still outside.
