## Mon, 5 Feb 2024 10:55:00 -0500 — Mike Sackton <msackton@gmail.com>

Subject: Taelgar: Asineau Loose End

One loose end from last session it would be best to try to resolve by email
is what - if anything - you are going to do about Asineau.

The lord has fled, presumably back to Embry, where his wife and kids are
currently wintering. He left a legal document renouncing his claim to the
land, and his heirs claim to the land.

None of your have passed the Sembaran bar exam (yet) but as best you
understand, the basic legal process for here would be for the baron (who
you don't know really at all) to decide what to do:

* keep the manor as a baronial manor, appoint a castellan to run it, and
have the profits of the land sent to the baron to pad their coffers
* grant the manor to someone, usually as a favor/to gain loyalty

You know from stories about Cleenseau that it was granted by the queen,
instead of the baron, but none of you are expert enough to understand when
the monarch would get involved here.

The next step in the process is basically for the baronial land clerk (a
woman named Giselle, in Valit (the Warin/Sabine village)) to send off a
message to the baron.

You have two clear opportunities here:

1) You can certainly influence what Giselle will say in her message. You
have a lot of credit with Sabine, who in turn has a lot of influence with
Giselle. So anything you want her to say, or any particular way you want to
phrase the message to the baron, you can get her to do.

2) You have an opportunity to establish facts on the ground, for example
encourage Anselm to take up residence in the abandoned manor and try to
straighten out the finances (or whatever else you want to suggest). Again,
you have a lot of credit with the power brokers in the region so they will
likely go along with whatever plan you suggest.

You can also certainly ask for advice, i.e from Jonathon (the steward),
Sabine, Rinault, Lord Murtha, Catherine de Brune, or anyone else.

Another option is just to do nothing, which is totally fine. But I'd rather
resolve anything you do want to do by email if possible.

Mike

Subject: Re: [Thantos] Taelgar: Asineau Loose End

So on this point, it actually brings up something I wanted to get into...

Thought I'd get into a bit of a meta discussion for a moment...Mike I don't know exactly what you had in mind for your arc, but you've suggested a number of times it kind of depends where/what we want to push at, and I thought I'd offer up my thoughts on see where other folks are at.
So this campaign has been different from your standard D&D run so far in that we've gotten very invested in the nitty-gritty and lives of people of a particular region...and I'm really, really liking it. I think most of us are used to the 'world-tour' style of D&D campaign where you're going all over the place and most of your political involvement in the world involves stabbing the big bad in the face at some point. I don't think I've been in a tabletop game before that's really emphasized the regional management and politics side of things, and I'm absolutely into it. I think we've had a good balance between town management sessions and than jumping into dungeon crawls, they're both fun and the movement between the two styles of play keeps both feeling fresh.

It may well not be practical in this case, but I was kind of wondering if there was a way to finagle us getting the lordship from the Baron...not because Robin in character has any particular aspirations to be a lord (he's a knight errant), but Dan the player has been very much enjoying the captain of the guard stuff and organizing the various regional efforts, and I kind of want to do more of it. I'm feeling like moving into higher positions of authority could be a lot of fun (not sure how others feel, but I have the impression everyone else is enjoying that side of things too).

As a side note I've always been someone who finds the sort of back-room high level dealings of conflict scenarios really interesting as a compliment to the in your face action. I remember Peter Jackson commenting that he was having a difficult time figuring out how to handle the Council of Elrond because "it's essentially a committee meeting, you can't film that!" But...I like the Council of Elrond, I think that kind of stuff is engaging!

I'm not suggesting we turn the game into Sim Barony or something, and as said I don't know what Mike has in mind exactly for the arc or how everyone else feels, but I'm enjoying this side of the game as a compliment to the typical kick-in-the-door-and-stab-things side of play, and I'm keen to do more of it if folks are on board.

"stuff", and I'm happy to take things in whatever direction the party
pushes. Cleenseau as a region is not likely to be the main setting for
"stuff" and "more stuff", but it could certainly be a base of operations at
higher levels.

location of adventuring for all of 20 level arc, it could certainly remain
your homebase for a long while, and so if becoming say the Lord of Asineau
seems fun, it is a good goal.

To answer your specific questions:


>    1. What is the general state of the village? I know this lord was
>    basically leaching off the place, but is it in dire need of particular
>    services, is it functional as a community at the moment?
>
> Basically it is fine. Lord Valbert wasn't beating people or stealing or
anything, and he wasn't really a sadist or anything, he was just selfish.
Basically, he inherited the place, sold a bunch of farmland and spent the
cash, saw his annual income drop by 50%, realized he wasn't going to be
able to pay the *honorus* tax (a tax the baron charges on each manorial
lord), and hastily imposed 3 really unpopular taxes: one on flour, one or
bread, and one on charcoal. So anyone buying those things from the miller
or baker or bringing charcoal into the manor from Cleenseau or selling it
locally has to pay a small tax. This is all legal, but pretty unpopular. I
don't feel like trying to work out the exact wages of a peasant, but think
of these taxes in the aggregate as high-but-not-crushing.

A more troubling tax is a fishing tax he charges on the fisherfolk of the
village, in the spring, with the threat of confiscation of the
boat/net/other materials if it wasn't paid. This has not actually been
collected yet for this spring, but last spring a couple of the poorer
fisherfolk lost their boats/traps/nets. These take some labor to rebuild,
and Lord Valbert was in the habit of effectively confiscating the fishing
equipment if you couldn't pay, then hiring the person he confiscated it
from as a fisher for pennies a day (where Lorin would keep and sell the
catch). These are usually communally owned by families, i.e. a lot of net
fishing requires 3-4-5 people to work. There are probably a couple of
families that are fearing this fate come spring, because they don't have
enough coin to pay the tax.


>    1. The three guards, can we get a sense of what their reputation is?
>    Were they eager participants for instance in the whole abusive search
>    thing, or were they just basically putting up with their lord's directives
>    because it's their job? If they seem competent I'm inclined to make sure
>    their pay is guaranteed one way or another (what is a town guard's monthly
>    pay?) while everything gets sorted out. If they have a reputation as
>    abusive jackasses then we'll want them replaced ASAP.
>
> There are three guards:

Connor, a man of about 35. He grew up in Asineau, and his family is still
around. He was enthusiastic about carrying out Lord Valbert's orders, and
the word around the village is that people thought he was a little bit in
love with Isolde and was always trying to impress her by copying what she
did. He comes across as pretty average in every way, and his wife is often
snide to him and he is nasty to her. His younger sister has fond memories
of him as a kid though, and tells a story of how he heroically fended off
three stray hungry wolves from a pack of village children playing in the
woodlot, but worries that man is gone.

Matias, an older man, about 55, looking to retire.  He was Lucas Asa's
bodyman and valet, as well as a guard, and stayed on by default when Lord
Valbert came to the village. He is clearly sick of Lord Valbert, but has
nowhere to live other than the manor. He has two grown kids in the village,
but you get the sense he is somewhat estranged from them, since his wife
died a few years ago.

Elbeth, a younger woman, not quite 30. She came to the village from Embry
with Lord Valbert, and no one in the village knows her that well. She is
aloof and withdrawn most of the time. She has broken off three engagements
with various men in the village, and is rumored to spend a lot of her free
time watching the lizardfolk in Ganboa, and has been heard to express a
desire to see fairies, or elves. Skilled with a sword, the best fighter of
the three guards.

As far as payment:

The manor funds - in terms of coin - are in a bit of rough shape, as Lord
Valbert stole most of the coin. But the manor is well stocked with flour,
dried and salted fish, cheese, salt pork, butter, hens, and has a small
herd of cows, many of whom are pregnant and near to term. I'm not actually
sure exactly how much a guard earns in coin (maybe ~1 gold/day? or a little
less?) but even without a source of funding, the manor could pay guards in
goods for several months, and something like a good dairy calf would be
worth a decent fraction of a year's pay.

A few other potential sources of funds:
* the military will sometimes pay a *honorus* to a lord who hosts a
garrison in a particularly disruptive way. Encouraging Ida to hire mostly
people from Asineau would be a rapid injection of coin into the village.
* it is common for the people of a village to give the lord gifts in
circumstances like an inheritance or a new lord coming. This could be an
easy way to replenish the coffers

Lords fundamentally need funds for 3 things - to keep up the temple, to
keep up their household, to pay their baron the *honorus*. Lord Valbert
didn't quite dare steal from the temple, and wanted to spend a lot more
money on his household than is usual, so he got squeezed on the
*honorus* (which
is bad, the baron can through you in debtor's prison if you won't pay what
you owe them), so he panicked. But the *honorus* isn't due until August, so
there isn't really a current pressing need for coin.


>    1. The other towns are organizing fortifications, I assume since Ida
>    is planning on locating the garrison there that she'll see to that. Can we
>    just appropriate the manor in the meantime if she needs it?
>
>
Yes, I think with Valbert having fled, her plan would be to appropriate the
manor as long as you guys + Rinault + Murtha supported her. She'd probably
end up paying a hefty fee to the new manorial lord in payment, once she
gets her funding from the crown.
