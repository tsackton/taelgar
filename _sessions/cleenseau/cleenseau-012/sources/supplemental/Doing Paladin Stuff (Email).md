First point, magic is rare. Just to set the right flavor, my rough estimate
is over the ~15 million people in Sembara, there are probably 15,000 or
less who can cast spells. (So about 1 in a 1000). And of those, 8 in 10 can
cast 1st level spells or less (i.e. some are like El - they can generate
minor and somewhat uncontrolled miracles but not actually even cast 1st
level spells). And probably only a dozen can cast 5th+ level spells.

Second point, how one gets access to magic is ineffable but as far as
anyone knows not really something you can *teach*. All four of you can cast
spells, but gaining access to your magic was unusual in each case - Robin
has always been able to perform minor miracles, for reasons he doesn't
really understand. Celyn connected with the Wyrdling in a specific way.
Viepuck was adopted by a strange patron. Izgil connected with the moon in
an unexpected way.

That isn't to say the blessing of the gods couldn't come to someone new
(there are plenty of stories of magic developing later in life) but it is
not something you can really train for in your experience (which is
actually quite limited). There are schools of arcane magic in other places
- Tollen, Chardon - but that is seen as a bit suspicious in Sembara and
none of your characters would really know much about it or how one might
learn to be a wizard.

It is certainly possible for Odo or Beatrix to become leveled characters,
but probably not paladins. (But of course never say never)

Fourth point: divine magic does very much come from the gods. You can't
just "mystically cast divine spells" without some connection to a god, from
which the power flows. Magic is magical, so if a *player* said that they
wanted to play a paladin who was more generic, rather than dedicated to a
specific god, there are plenty of in-world ways that could work, but as far
as any of your characters know, these types of divine miracles are pretty
tied to the divine.

(Remember, in Sembara the organized religion is very secular, in that the
temples and temple administrators and such are not seen as particularly
connected to the gods; and the *actually divinely inspired* people are
pretty unorganized, i.e. Robin and Celyn would not be atypical).

Fifth point, almost every human in Taelgar is "religious" at some level -
the gods are palpably real and although from a playing-the-game perspective
it doesn't come up necessarily as much as is realistic, if you were
actually in Taelgar talking to someone in Cleenseau the gods would feel
ever-present. So I'm sure if you asked Odo or Beatrix about their
experience of the gods they would have some specific details and
connections.

Meta: What you should take from the above is that making some of the NPCs
into more important NPCs is a cool idea, but to gain magic would require
something a little more than "we train them a lot". But also if Robin wants
a paladin-in-training side-kick, I can invent some type of religious
connection. But fighter levels would be a lot more appropriate in game.

Mike

Oh, yeah, for sure. The gods you are familiar with (the Mos Numea divines)
are actually best thought of as archetypes rather than people, per se. They
aren't the Greek gods with family drama and deep personalities but rather
archetypes that could represent a lot of things to a lot of people.

You could easily imagine the Father (the god of justice, and the sun)
having some paladin who is hell-bent on justice above all, but
fundamentally the gods are best seen as on humanity's side against the
dangers of the world. (Not always perfectly, and not always clearly, but
still, mostly).

The entire Christian idea of original sin and impure thoughts and things
like that also just simply doesn't exist.
