> 2 - Unless anyone objects I think encouraging people to train with and carry spears is a good idea.

Train with and even be issued is a good idea. Carry around town is…. Complicated. A 6’ spear is not convenient to have with you at all times, which is the whole reason people carried swords. That will be a distinct change to culture and probably put people on edge.

Would we want to start working on something like boarspears as standard local arms? Or even just lash a crossbeam? Undead can just walk up the shaft, having the poke-and-control-distance could help.

I have to admit I do not have as firm a grasp of effective military tactics in medieval warfare as some of you. You should take it as a given that (a) Ida is a skilled military leader and (b) although she sometimes seems a bit short, she seems to genuinely respect you and clearly has the best interests of the region at heart. She will happily adjust tactics based on your advice, and I'm thrilled if you guys will enjoy it to have you map out the best tactical approaches for fighting undead. You can, if you don't want to get into the weeds on this particular topic, to just declare "we came up with a sensible plan". But again, get into the weeds if you want, definitely don't let me discourage you. If you are getting into the weeds, keep in mind three things:

(a) tell me what you are trying to accomplish, i.e. why do you want them to use spears?
(b) remember the key thing about zombies: they keep coming no matter what until they fail a save
(c) remember the key thing about skeletons: they are vulnerable to bludgeoning damage

Sectioning this to its own thread...

I love nerding out about medieval tactics, so getting into the weeds is fun for me.

I suggest spears because they're easy to use with minimal training, cheap, and great for holding a position, keeping zombies at a distance, etc. A couple people with spears can easily hold a door, a small group can block an alley or street.

Boar spears as Chris mentions are even better for keeping bad things at bay.

Yes bludgeoning is best, but you don't want untrained peasants to have to get in close. The idea is spears can let people block off safe areas and stay alive until trained fighters (the garrison or us) can get there to help. If people manage to pin a zombie down, then folks can close range and smash them with hammers, axes, clubs, furniture, or whatever. But spears let clumps of people work together easily without exposing themselves to as much risk as going into close quarters combat.

The point about them being inconvenient to carry around is a good one, but easy as they are to make, you could prop up a few outside every couple of buildings or something for people to just grab. If they can't get to one in an emergency, hand axes and hammers can do in a pinch, bit in terms of stuff an untrained mob can use with reasonable effectiveness, spears are hard to beat.

I am less nerdy about military tactics but everything said here seems sensible to me. I think the classic “officers have swords/maces and rank and file have spears and shields” modality seems to stand up here. We should probably start thinking of our ranks in 3 buckets and organize them accordingly:

Tier 1: Standing Army. Trained soldiers under the direct command of Ida. 100 People.

Tier 2: Town Watch/Guard. Regional fast-react defense meant to provide patrols, awareness and ability to react to small threats quickly. Should probably seek to have no fewer than 20 for the major settlements, 10 for the smaller places, and 5 for the tiny villages. Total number probably around ~200 across the whole region.

Tier 3: Mustered militia. These are not paid and are only trained part-time. They have other jobs but when it’s a “hey there’s an orc/skeleton/zombie/other army at the gates” they know how to use a spear or a bow and can organize under the other tiers in squads and companies. Standing army acts as the officers, Town watch the sergeants and squad leaders. Militia form the rank and file. Ideally the militia would train with their sergeants and officers for a week once they are organized and we have enough of the above, and then they would do like 1 day out of every week to keep their basic skills sharp.

I think this is a good basic model, and as I recall it even has some historical precedent. I believe that back during the England vs. France days of medieval conflict, that men in the English countryside were expected to practice with the bow one day a week so there was always a semi-trained archer force to call up if necessary (though I would certainly suggest doing this on a voluntary 'defend your community' sort of model rather then trying to conscript people).

There's also old Scandinavian culture where folks were farming one season and then going off 'a-viking' the next...obviously not the culture here, but just saying there is historical precedent for the kind of thing Matt is talking about.

Also on the morale front, I think it will probably actually be somewhat helpful in restoring general confidence. People usually feel better about a situation if they're involved in some effort to deal with it rather than just sitting and waiting for the inevitable.

You have some things going for you - it is winter, so planting isn't until
March/April, and therefore a decently large portion of the peasantry is
freer-than-normal. They aren't entirely idle (you have to tend livestock,
cut wood, make charcoal, repair tools and plows, etc) but it is definitely
the "slow" part of the agricultural calendar (summer, between planting and
harvest is the other slow time).

The total population of the region is:

*Cleenseau Manor*
Cleenseau itself - 1150
Taviose - 30
Aulout - 100
= ~1300

Elsewhere:
Asieneau  - 300
Valit - 250
Beury - 500
= 950

Ganboa (Lizardfolk) - 50

So you are looking at a total population of about 2300, of which you have
50 lizardfolk, a handful of dwarves, 50-60 halflings, the rest humans.
Basically everyone lives in one of those 7 named villages though, there are
no scattered farms or smaller settlements. Only Cleenseau is walled,
although Asineau has a stout stone manor house, and Beury is rapidly
building a wooden palisade. In addition to that, there is a metalsmith and
weapons-making community here - one of the profit centers of Cleenseau is
taking reclaimed metal from the abandoned dwarven mines in the south (i.e.
around Ausson's Crossing, from the mini-series we did), forging it into
weapons, and then selling them west to the army.

At the moment, the region is on a sort of "semi-war" footing. Ida is
organizing the army through the traditional lines, paying for things, etc.
In this model it is hard to support more than say 10% of the population as
part of a "full time" military force (town watch/guards, or soldiers),
although the core of Ida's existing army (50 people) wouldn't count for
that. So on "semi-war" footing you are basically looking at ~280 people
across guards and soldiers and with some capacity to equip them, with some
trade-offs between better equipment and number of folks being equipped.
Churning out cheap spears and shields is probably easy. Crossbows and
swords take longer, although the garrison has equipment to equip about 80
soldiers.

If you want to get really geeky about this, you can assume a community of
about 10 trained weaponsmiths (a couple of whom are master-level) plus
another 25 apprentices and simple blacksmiths who could be redirected, and
tell me what you would want them to do. Or I can come up with some hand
waving, or one of you who is geek-ier about medieval weaponry than me can
come up with some hand waving. Most of these folks will be working on
projects already (and winter is probably a popular time to work on weapons
and such because it is easier to hire cheap extra labor) but could be
diverted with money or persuasion. Materials would likely not be an issue,
there are decent stocks of metal and wood in town - labor time is the
bigger cost. Some of these folks would be working on weapons for the army
already, but plenty would be working on private projects or other types of
orders that they'd have to cancel or delay.

Other notes on equipment: hunting is not really a big thing here - the
woods are broadly too dangerous to be a good source of deer and the like,
and not a lot of people go south of the Enst. There are some part-time
caravan guards around, but most of the place is pretty basic peasantry, and
a hayfork or woodcutting ax is the main metal sharp things people would
own. Some slings for hunting squirrels and such, and the garrison has, as
mentioned about, full military equipment for about 80 soldiers but
otherwise equipment, more than people, is likely going to be your biggest
constraint.

A full war footing would involve appropriating materials and labor, and
would allow you to field a significantly larger force (up to say 35% of the
population, more if in desperation you start accepting the older, the
younger teens, etc). It would also let you utilize the full resources of
the smiths without worrying about funding. A full war footing sustained
past the end of March (it is mid January now) though would have a very
significant impact on the planting though.

Basically, at the moment the population is scared, and worried, but no one
is *quite* thinking of this as an invasion. Some public preaching could
certainly make it easier to transition to a full war footing - but that has
its own challenges as well (i.e. making everyone feel like a full scale
undead invasion is imminent could result in some people fleeing, etc).

On an in character level, things do feel like they're leaning that way...particularly with the Fellburg siege and the apparent dissolution of order in the army, it does create the impression this region is on its own against whatever is going to hit.

On a meta level...I mean, of course a war is going to hit, we're in a D&D campaign, but I try not to be too meta...

There might be something to be said for going aggressively full tilt during the winter months before the harvest so by the time spring hits and people need to start planting, we're solidly prepared for whatever. This could have a number of regional political and military benefits...if the army isn't brought under control and everything is in chaos, having a region like this one be a solid point of stability lends passive benefits to the surrounding regions as well...even if we can't say, mount a full expedition to help out the near-ish cities, if we have our towns solidly defended, that frees up resources elsewhere and it's one less vector of attack the enemy can throw at others with if they hit a figurative wall in Clenseau.

If we could at some point down the road mount an expedition, obviously that might help a lot with morale elsewhere.

We don't want to panic people though or drive them to flee if it's not necessary, so it's a complicated question.
