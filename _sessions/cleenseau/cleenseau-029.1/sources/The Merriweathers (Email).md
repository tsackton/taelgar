So this is conversation that Celyn will have in the vicinity of the Merriweathers and whoever of the party wants to come talk clothes (he will definitely encourage Robin to come see about things on the principle that a) Robin likes bright colors in his clothing and b) as Lord he probably needs at least a little wardrobe that has not been stabbed, clawed, bitten, or afflicted with the effects of dark magic, even if Viepuck can fix it all) but not for the ears of the village in general.

He will ask how much of the conversation from Veltor they are aware of— the thing where the people there looked at his ‘well-off peasant’ clothing, said, “We can probably help you”, and got an earful of clothing nerdery— and recap or pick up from there as need be.

A few things will be very clear from his approach to this:
	- Celyn is *extremely analytical* about clothing choices and does things very much on purpose and with intent.
	- He is very familiar with clothing from a theatrical standpoint of presenting a character (it has probably come up in random conversation that his job with the theatre company he toured with was “assistant costumer and general dogsbody” so this makes sense).
	- His concerns include matters of physical presentation, addressing how the body is seen, which is somewhat distinct from the theatrical training but definitely has mutual support with it.
	- He is very _aware_ of clothing as a matter of social class and its implications.

(Viepuck already knew these things, because Celyn had been on and off having conversations to help Viepuck’s psychic costuming have more “I am choosing clothes with intention and understanding their social implications”. Celyn believes the party grifter would benefit from this knowledge.)

(On a game mechanics level: Celyn is proficient with a disguise kit and this is a big chunk of how that comes out in his day-to-day life.)


Celyn presents the Merriweathers with the following problem:

Celyn’s current wardrobe is deliberately chosen to present himself as a reasonably respectable but fundamentally unremarkable peasant.  He actively wishes to be overlooked and underestimated and ideally somewhat forgettable, and his clothing choices were made with that in mind.  He is very clear that he is a _farm boy_ in origin and at heart, and he has no desire for social climbing. What he has suits his sense of self, and is in fact tailored to meet some of his particular and specific needs.

At the same time, he is both one of the ‘Heroes of Cleenseau’, *and* the Lord’s consort in Asineau, and these require the presentation of a _different character_, and he explicitly frames that in theatrical terms.  He is aware that he can get away with being eccentric by the standards implied by those circles because of being god-touched by the Wyrdling in specific, but he needs a broader palette of possible presentation.

Most specifically, as someone who is now threaded into the function of Asineau, he needs to develop a Lord’s Consort character who can suit the part as need be in terms of poshness of attire for purposes of presiding over events or encounters with other lords and their families, but which does not suppress his sense of who he actually _is_.  He is clear that he needs to be authentic in the role, not merely playacting, with a vehemence that is perhaps a little unnerving.  (Also he wants probably a nice shirt he can shove into his bag that won’t wrinkle horribly when he does so he can pull it out when they’re invited to some fancy dinner while they’re on the road, because he is sure they will be on the road again sometime and he’d been feeling the need for a shirt of a more middle-class nature for that purpose for a bit.) He presumes that a rustic style can be done with fancier fabric, or something, but he would love to talk through how it works and the implications of clothing to get the nuances right for purpose.

(Celyn’s feeling about his role in Asineau is that he is responsible for morale, both as his default duty as a hypomanic hope gremlin _and_ as Robin’s partner.  And he knows that proper theatrics are actually important to morale functioning properly. Which means correct costuming.  He is genuinely more bothered by not having the correct costuming for this role than he is by the class discomfort, because he takes his responsibilities there seriously.)

(Also, which may come out, he thinks that if he gets the Lord’s Consort performance down properly, when he dresses normally it’ll be harder for people to connect the Morale Officer persona with Just Plain Celyn.  He is very philosophical about code-switching. “Has anyone told you you look kind of like—“ is his end goal.)

(He bets he can teach Robin how to use clothes to signal when he’s on duty as Lord and when he wants to be left be to be quiet, also. Similar skillset.)



In terms of specific things for how this particular puzzle is solved, he has requirements about cut and fit and tailoring particularly involving shoulders and hips, wants his clothing to have two contrasting colors (his default is blue and yellow, which vibes with his coloring, but he can be talked into other colors if they have a good idea), and is curious if they have better binder tech than what he has (which is *extremely* basic).  He also does not want to deal with clothing that restricts his range of motion substantially even if it _is_ appropriately fancy.
