## Sun, 10 Dec 2023 22:57:02 -0500 — Mike Sackton <msackton@gmail.com>

Subject: Re: [Thantos] Entering the portal

On armor:

Mithril isn’t common — you would have never heard of it in game. You certainly can’t buy it.

Robin’s best armor is actually studded leather right (12+4 from dex), or half plate (15+2 from dex) with stealth disadvantage. He can’t wear anything heavier than chain (str not high enough) and chain is only 16 (+0 from dex). So basically studded leather is Robin’s optimal armor.

Celyn is slightly different - technically best possible armor is breastplate (14+2 from dex) which beats studded leather (12+3 from dex) without adding disadvantage on stealth. (Half plate would be even better but with stealth disadvantage).

But both characters are optimized for Dex, and ultimately if you are headed for 20 Dex studded leather is your best choice as it maxes out at 12+5 which is better than everything except full plate (18+0 from dex).

On weapons:
Rapier is the only d8 finesse weapon. Most finesse characters dual wield short swords, but we’ve ended up with two sword-and-board finesse fighters which is a bit unusual but kinda locks you into rapier. Although a magic shortsword is better than a non-magic rapier.

## Mon, 11 Dec 2023 12:36:16 +0000 — Dan Walters <arcadinal@protonmail.com>

Subject: Re: [Thantos] Entering the portal

So, I've been giving this some thought. A couple of points...

- Thematically I'm not at all attached to rapier as a weapon, something like a typical one-handed arming sword feels way more appropriate, or aesthetically even something like a war hammer (I often feel like swords are way over represented in fantasy). I get stuck with it because if you're a dex build there are very few finesse weapons and unlike 3rd edition there's no way to use non-finesse weapons as though they were. So, you kind of get pigeonholed into rapier even though I don't really feel like it's actually appropriate (short swords don't really fit either).

- I'm actually not super attached to having a dex build either in principle, and it might round us out a little more if I leaned into a somewhat more typical strength and heavy armor build, the issue is that while it might benefit the party it comes at an unfortunately severe personal cost. A big reason for picking a dex/minimal armor build was for the Stealth skill. While it might seem strange to hold my entire build hostage to one skill, the trouble with armor builds is that any time the party is doing anything that's not a standup fight (sneaking, climbing, fording, searching, etc.), the guy in heavy armor basically has to sit out and let everyone else do stuff. I don't like that, I want to be able to participate in all the various activities the party does, I want to be able to skulk around in the woods with Celyn without being liability, but strength builds get massively hosed in terms of having things to do anytime you're not immediately punching something.

If we can think of a way to work around that, I'm a 1000% open to reworking things, but I just really do not want to be pigeonholed into someone who shows up for five minutes to stab the troll and than has to go sit in the corner whenever anything else is going on, which has been my prior experience playing armored fighters.

Daniel Walters

## Mon, 11 Dec 2023 14:35:30 +0000 — Matthew Rand <rand.matt@gmail.com>

Subject: Re: [Thantos] Entering the portal

One thought:

Not sure if this is a homebrew or actual rules but one group I’m in has been running with a concept called “Group checks” including stealth. The way is works is essentially everyone still rolls, and you have a shared stealth score which is equal to the lowest score among the top half of rolls.

i.e. in a 5 person party you have rolls of
24
19
17
12
6

The group stealth score would be 17. What this essentially represents is having some things happening without having to describe every detail (more stealth people ranging out a few steps, looking for things that will make noise, padding armor joints, etc) with the more stealth folks able to compensate for the less stealth folks and ending up with a sort of average. Rolls still matter but it insulates the “one heavy armor person” problem Dan describes. It works pretty well most of the time.

In a 4 person party Mike would just have to rule is it the top 2 scores or the top 3 with the lowest being the group score? There may be other ways to handle it too. But I think if Dan would prefer a strength based character except for this detail it’s a way to handle it with rules that avoids specific gear being required.

Basically I want you to think about what would be aesthetically pleasing
and fun from an in-game perspective. I assumed you built Robin as DEX based
because you didn't like the flavor/aesthetics of knight in shining armor.
But if it is really just because you are worried about missing out on fun
game stuff because of a low(ish) Stealth check, then...I think you are
underselling the advantages of STR (i.e. carrying Viepuck across the goo
would have been pretty easy with a STR 18). Also, I fudge a lot of stuff to
allow you to use Acrobatics/DEX when really you should be forced to use
STR/Athletics, to make the DEX build not particularly disadvantaged against
the STR build. But I think that gives STR a bit of a perception of being
useless because you often can sub in DEX based on me trying to make the DEX
build not feel weak compared to a STR build. There are some clear
advantages though especially if you develop shield shoving and grappling
and pushing people around as means of battlefield control.

As far as Stealth, there are really three directions you could go:

(a) totally absolute crap: plate armor, DEX +0, no proficiency bonus
(b) ok: plate armor, +1 or +2 DEX, prof bonus
(c) stealth monkey: light armor, +3 or +4 DEX, prof bonus

Which one you pick kinda depends on what your goals are:

(a) do you want to be a skirmisher who is at least to some extent built
around sneaking up on enemies and attacking from unseen positions, and who
would be a decent choice to send ahead to scout when staying hidden is
paramount, i.e. a rougish/swashbucklerish paladin?
OR
(b) do you just want to be able to kinda do ok when sneaking past the
sleeping guard (i.e. not be a liability) or when hiding to set an ambush,
but probably not particularly good as a standalone scout?

If it is more (a), you really should stick to a DEX build. If it is more
(b), and you are happy to let Celyn be the real stealth monkey (which is
kinda what he's built for) then there are a lot of options...

1. Celyn can grant advantage to one person for stealth checks basically at
will (as long as it is only 1 person at a time). So as long as you are with
Celyn, he can cancel the disadvantage from heavy armor (Kiya, probably you
should try to remember to use this feature... Someone should always have
the stealth advantage on, it isn't limited per day)
2. In the general situation of "the whole party is trying to sneak past the
guard to infiltrate" the base, I would run group checks - so 2 failures/2
successes is all you need. I might tweak what happens a little based on the
rolls but broadly one really bad stealth character would always be able to
be covered by the rest of the party.

I actually think if you think about the most recent couple of sessions, I'm
not sure you made a single Stealth check, but...

(a) crossing the goo would have been somewhat easier with a good STR (and
as an side, falling into the goo would have been a lot less dangerous with
a good STR as it was a STR save to avoid negative effects)
(b) having a 20 rather than 18 AC (full plate + shield = 20) would have
definitely made a few hits in misses, in particular during the "big zombie"
fight. He only had a +4 to hit, I don't remember the exact rolls, but I'm
pretty sure the one hit would have been a miss.
(c) a couple of the doors and fallen rubble would have been things a strong
person could have more easily pushed aside, esp in the sense that with an
18 STR you'd be more likely to ask questions like "can I just move the
rocks"

On the other hand, the Shadow STR drain would have been more scary and
impactful.

In past situations, the two times I think there have been meaningful

definitely been a group check) and maybe a couple of times trying to stay

I am a big believer in making aesthetics work. I guess I have a few
thoughts or suggestions

1. The party itself would be better balanced and you'd be mechanically
stronger by swapping STR and DEX and switching to full plate and a
different weapon. AC 20 is meaningfully better than AC 18, having someone
with high STR is useful in the party, and using a broader range of weapons
and plate armor opens the door to more cool items with less work on my part.
2. Slow, dumb, and incompetent is somewhat up to you and how you play
Robin, but you are correct that you'd be less good at stealth. In practice
I don't think that will matter in group situations, as Celyn can
compensate for you, but it does nudge Robin's role more towards decoy from
hiding person (but think about the fight with the ogre zombie, it was
actually a great strategy to basically have Robin suck the hits and
everyone else snipe from cover, and I think it was both fairly mechanically
effective and satisfying for everyone?). But again, even having
disadvantage at stealth doesn't have to mean you are tromping around like
an oaf - narratively it could just mean your armor softly clanks sometimes,
especially when Celyn's magic isn't around to compensate. That said, it
does meaningfully change your chances of success... stealth checks would go
from say between 40% and 80% successful to between 10% and 30% successful
(between adding disadvantage and dropping from +4 to +1 DEX )
3. I am a big believer in aesthetics - I don't know if you find the mental
image of Robin walking through the dungeon in full plate with his great
helm covering his face appealing or unappealing. There is also the question
of "non-adventuring" scenarios -- outside of a few rare situations I'm not
going to *force* you to not wear armor, but you might find the image of
Robin putting on his armor each morning before walking the town unappealing
or "not what Robin would do".
4. There are also some middle grounds -- if a STR based character is
appealing, but you really dislike the aesthetic of full plate specifically
(but not, say half-plate or chain mail) we can discuss and work out a
mechanically effective variant (although you'd probably end up with AC 19
not AC20). You could also say, have the undershirt/chain part of plate mail
give you AC 15/no stealth disadvantage and the full suit give you AC
18/stealth disadvantage, then Robin maybe walks around town without his
full armor on or something and you sometimes have a slightly worse AC

So I would encourage you to start with the aesthetics --  is Robin wearing
full plate, some type of not-quite-full plate, or light/minimal armor?
