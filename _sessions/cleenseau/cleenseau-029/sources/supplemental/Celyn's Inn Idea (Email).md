Celyn's thoughts on an Inn is actually something I'd been thinking about as well and that would be important to Robin given he is a knight of the Charitable Wanderer. Robin's brand of faith is very practically oriented, and his version of a praying is less 'I will go to a chapel now and be solemn for three hours' and much more 'if I want this to happen, I'm going to go and DO something about it."

Having an inn as a place of rest and sanctuary for travelers kind of feels like a bit of a religious thing for him, it's a practical thing that makes the lives of travelers easier, or even if someone in town needs another place to stay for some reason. All this is to say, we should have one.

Ha, I was wondering if Robin might feel like that about it.  (And pondering Wanderer-thematic names actually.)

If I’m understanding Mike correctly our current use-case for an inn are:
	- people who have crossed the bog heading west and might want other accomodations than the deeply mediocre ones in Beury
	- the usual but rare ’someone travelling needs a place to crash out’ that doesn’t have systematic plans
	- people who are coming to town to train with/work with/ogle the Heroes Of Cleenseau


It seems likely that if we establish a regional competition games (which is a really clever addon to the ‘the party is training people’ thing, Dan) that will establish some additional demand, but to start with it will be scant.  I am guessing an overall loss on the town budget ledger until we hit the level 9 break, at which point other draws will bump it to neutral, and at 13 when we get the actual Pub (which Mike will have to tweak for second effect) it will be financially positive.

It being a loss does not mean not doing it, but it does mean there has to be other economics to support it.

(A note: the above is player noodling.  Celyn is 100% not qualified to be an accountant, he does *not* have the attention span for desk work it has insufficient physical activity and not enough shiny objects.)



Operating on If You Build It They Will Come: possibly the thing to do here is to build something small but expandable that can share the stableyard with the Stable, with options to build additional stable space when it becomes necessary.  Then, to find a person or family who has been displaced by the crisis and who is in need of a place to be, hire them to maintain it, put them on the manorial household budget until the thing grows into something self-sustaining.  At this point food for the mini-inn would roll into manor expenses as well, because there isn’t enough to support an additional cook.

Thus we have a start point _and_ have done the “find someone in need of help and actually helped them in practical terms” thing by giving someone a home and a job.

money = better.

I'm trying to balance three things....

(a) some degree of realism - I don't want Asineau to be a special snowflake
unlike anywhere else on Taelgar
(b) some degree of "mini game" to give you a fun mental activity around
"Sim Manor"
(c) some degree of D&D 5 E "bastion gives you nice little D&D benefits"

All at once, which is a bit tricky to be honest. I'm working on a few
principles though...

1. No matter what you do, it isn't realistic for Asineau to suddenly be
three times richer than Cleenseau - or even as rich. It just doesn't make
sense for the local economy.
2. 1000 GP is a lot of gold. Rosalind in Cleenseau probably brings in 3000
- 4000 GP/year at best. Maybe more like half that.
3. I want you to be able to build stone walls and a castle, but that's
going to have to be funded from earnings from adventuring, not profits from
Asineau - I know so far you haven't found a lot of treasure, but there are
lots of treasure hordes out there, it is just not the type of adventure
you've mostly been on. There will be plenty of opportunities to get 10,000+
GP from adventuring over the next arc of the campaign.

So from that perspective, it is easier to balance things if you don't try
to push Asineau into an income generating machine that produces 5,000 GP /
year or whatever, even if that's what you'd need to realistically build
stone walls and a castle - and even if that's what a traditional "SimManor"
style game would imply is the "right" way to do things.

So I think it makes sense to try to balance the SimManor aspect of things
with a bit of "D&D perks" aspect of things, which is easier to give out as
it doesn't break the semi-realism of the local economy.

Does that make sense?

Mike

What specifically about income streams? There are a couple of ways to run
an inn:

(a) you could build something now, by spending money. In that case, it
doesn't have any special bastion abilities, but you have an inn, and that
can have benefits in game. In general, I'd expect it would generate enough
income to support a barkeep but no meaningful extra profits. Over time,
that would likely change, but it would be slow. Perhaps mechanically would
provide some extra income on feast days and whatnot to let you have some
fancier and more frequent festivals without paying extra, and there are
other little benefits to having an inn that will come up.
(b) you could wait for 9th level and just declare an inn into being, in
which case the world will find a way to provide it (ala the Ferrystones and
your Smithy).

If you go with (b), when you declare the inn, you'd have to decide what it
is/does - either an info gathering place, in which case I'd use the trophy
room rules (i.e. free info or find a trinket), maybe slightly tweaked, or
for income generation, where I'd use some variant of the gambling den
rules, where you earn some variable income around 50GP per bastion turn.
Although I do want to balance some of the income generation with "realism",
i.e. two special facilities that earn 50 GP/week is not a lot of gold per
se, but also kinda throws the local economy out of whack...

Mike
