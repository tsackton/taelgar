Subject: The Situation in Asineau

Hi all,

To advance the "SimManor" conversation here is some background on the
current situation in Asieneau when you arrive back in the region, around
April 1st, 1720.

*Background*
Asineau
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/sembara/barony-of-aveil/cleenseau-region/asineau/>(pronounced
like the French, Ah-zee-noh) is a small fishing village of about 300 people
nestled on the banks of the Enst. It is the only village in the manor
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/sembara/barony-of-aveil/cleenseau-region/manor-of-asineau/>
(the
rest of the manorial land is farmland and a strip of woodlot separating
Asineau manor from its neighbors. (There is a map of the region
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/sembara/barony-of-aveil/cleenseau-region/cleenseau-region/>).
The nearest neighbor to Asineau is the lizardfolk village of Ganboa
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/sembara/barony-of-aveil/cleenseau-region/ganboa/>
.

As you all recall, the lord of Asineau fled in the night (motivated in part
by Viepuck's mischief). The estate was in relatively poor shape, as the
previous lord had been selling lands to Catherine de Brune
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/people/sembarans/catherine-de-brune/>
and
overtaxing his people to support a somewhat lavish lifestyle. Before you
left the region in January, you had done a few things:

   - centralized the militia (under Captain Ida Rosfeld
   <https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/people/sembarans/ida-rosfeld/>)
   in Asineau and build a rough and ready palisade around the town
   - started the process of exchanging the previous lords' tapestries and
   fine furniture to Catherine in exchange for restoring the manor's demesne

You then rode off to fight undead, leaving Anselm
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/people/sembarans/anselm/> (the
temple administrator of the temple in Cleenseau) loosely in charge
(although, in practice, you had mostly turned the town into a garrison town
and you expected that Ida was running the day-to-day).

(This index of npcs
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/campaigns/cleenseau-campaign/cleenseau-campaign-index-of-npcs/>,
which I should really update, might be useful for background. For
"SimManor" purposes, you might want to read about taxes
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/sembara/taxes-in-sembara/>,

*Recent Events*
When you return to the Cleenseau region quite a bit has changed. Rosalind
and her entourage are back and settled in Cleenseau, and the mood across
the region is one of hope and renewal. A number of events have changed the
landscape:

   - The Army of the West
   <https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/groups/sembaran-army/army-of-the-west/>
has
   been restored and its command reorganized. Ida has been promoted to major
   and now commands the Enst Regiment. Colonel Leclerc, the colonel who helped
   her out, and used to command the South Watch, has been promoted to General
   of the Army of the West. The garrison has left Asineau and returned to
   Cleenseau, and needs a captain.
   - The castellan of the Manor of Valit
   <https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/sembara/barony-of-aveil/cleenseau-region/manor-of-valit/>,
   Sabine de Brune
   <https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/people/sembarans/sabine-de-brune/>
has
   resigned her post, and returned to the de Brune family estates near
   Eskbridge (far to the east). (You might recall her as the woman you saved
   from the baroness' prison, who had a fey companion
   <https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/people/fey/istarias/>). The
   manor of Valit is currently empty, and news is that the appointment of the
   new castellan will fall to the new Baron of Aveil, once they are appointed.
   - Catherine de Brune, the wealthy scion of the de Brune family is
   delighted at Robin's appointment as the Lord of Asineau, and throws an
   elegant dinner shortly after your return. Privately, she informs you that
   her family has instructed her to do anything she reasonably can to support
   you, in gratitude for your actions in saving her cousin Sabine. You get the
   sense there is an unstated assumption that you will not mention Istarias
   (Sabine's fey companion) to anyone - although Catherine seems honestly
   grateful, and her offer of help isn't primarily a bribe to keep you quiet.
   - Eleanor
   <https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/people/sembarans/eleanor/>,
   the temple steward, is seriously ill again, and does not expect to survive
   the summer. She seems too frail for Robin's magic to do more than ease her
   suffering, although you never know.
   - Wymar Essford
   <https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/people/sembarans/wymar-essford/>,
   Rosalind's ailing and senile father died about two weeks ago. Rosalind
   tells you he lived to see her one last time and he recognized her. But the
   question of who shall be her heir clearly weighs on her.

Numerous rumors are spreading about the lich, the fey, the party's death
and resurrection, and so on. If you thought you were heroes before, this is
ten times more. The common wisdom in the region is that Queen Elaine II is
off to a brilliant start to her reign appointing Robin as lord of Asineau.

*Physical Plant*
The town of Asineau is roughly mapped here:
[image: image.png]

The manor of Asineau is just under 2,000 acres. This consists of about 20
acres for the village itself, another 30-40 acres along the riverbank of
unfarmable land, and then about 1000 acres of farmland, 600 acres of
pasture, and 200 acres of woodlot. The farmland is comparatively marginal,
and currently it is farmed in a two year rotation (so only half the fields
are planted each year), leading to about 500 acres planted a year, for a
yield of about 4,000 bushels of grain per harvest. The pasture and woodlot
supports a small number of horses, plus pigs and sheep. There are a handful
of dairy cows, but no substantial beef production. Of the ~100 able bodied
villagers, about half farm and tend livestock, and the remainder fish
(although during planting and harvest the whole village turns out to help
in the fields). There is currently a wooden palisade built around the
village, although the wood might be better harvested for boat building or
other activities as the palisade is relatively poorly made and needs
repairs or to be torn down.

As far as buildings and non-food production, there is the notable manor
(more details later), plus a temple of the Wyrdling and a combined mill and
bakehouse just outside the village. The manor house also has a kitchen with
a large hearth for baking bread. Beer and other spirits are imported from
Beury or Cleenseau, and charcoal is imported from Tavoise. Wood is locally
harvested for boatbuilding and there are a few carpenters and boat builders
who do occasional work - although no proper industry., There is a
ropemaker/netmaker in town, the only one in the region outside of
Cleenseau. There is no blacksmith - the nearest blacksmith is in
Beury. The Great
South Road
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/roads/great-south-road/>
runs along the north side of the village, and there are several small docks
and a beach on the south side, near the Enst
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/rivers/wistel-enst-watershed/enst/>.
The Enst is quite wide here - 500 feet or so - and provides substantial
fish, especially during the shad and trout runs in March and April. The
major grains are wheat and millet, and some barley is grown as well.

*Manor*
The manor house was built in the early 1600s, before the hobgoblin wars,
and is three stories in the main part, with a more recent one story
extension and a relatively large stable complex. The main house has a Great
Hall on the main floor as well as a solar and a small study and a large
kitchen and attached bakehouse. The second floor has a suite of chambers
for the lord (a sleeping room and dressing room) as well as an adjoining
suite for the lord's partner, a guest room, and a small, disorganized
scriptorium that holds a small number of household records, and is the
steward's workspace. There is also a storage loft, for linens, silver, and
other household valuables. The third floor has two family rooms (for
children or other family members), as well as some additional storage space
in the eaves and two cramped sleeping dormitories for servants. There is
also a cellar with cool storage for preserved food. The cellar has a thick
trap door and can double as a short term prison.

The extension contains two small suites for the steward and the guard
captain, a weapons locker / armory, and servant's dormitory in the attic
(cramped, not a full floor), and several storage rooms. It is connected to
the stable, which has six stalls for horses or other livestock, and a
hayloft. There is a small vegetable garden and chicken run outside the
stable.

*Economy*
Asineau is a subsistence economy and although there is (usually) adequate
food, it is not a wealthy place. There are no exports to speak of, although
salted and dried fish is traded locally for charcoal, beer, and the
occasional side of beef, and the local boat builders are occasionally hired
outside the village, as is the ropemaker. The agricultural calendar is
based around the spring shad and trout runs in the river, the spring
planting, and the fall harvest. Winters are lean, and depend on preserved
grains and meats, cheese and butter, and some fish (eel and catfish,
mostly). If the fish run doesn't come in the spring, March can be a
desperate month, and people do starve if the harvest fails.

The manor itself earns income in a number of ways:

   - the manor directly holds about 50 acres of land and then has various
   leasehold agreements for another 400 acres. These are varied and complex
   but ultimately about 1/5 of the agricultural production is returned to the
   manor (about 500 bushels of grain)
   - of the remaining 1600 acres of land, it is all crownhold
   <https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/sembara/land-holding-in-sembara/#folkland>
(meaning
   taxes are owned from it) and therefore the deca (a tithe of 1/10 of the
   harvest) is due to the manor (about 350 additional bushels of grain)
   - in addition, the manor is owed about 800 person-days a year of labor
   service, of which about half is used to support the 50 acres of directly
   owned farmland

Additionally the manorial lord is allowed to numerous rights of taxation
including:

   - the right to tax inns and taverns, on crownland
   - the right to collect tolls from bridges, ferries, gates, and roads
   - the right to collect docking or landing fees for fish
   - the right to collect market taxes on goods brought to market
   - the right to establish local monopolies on mills, bakehouses, and
   breweries (or charge fees)
   - the right to establish local market monopolies (i.e. only the lord is
   allowed to sell charcoal at the local market)

The previous lord had established four major taxes:

   - a tax on the purchase or import of charcoal into the demesne (charged
   typically as a small percent of the purchase price, in kind or coin)
   - a tax on bread or milled grain from the miller or the manorial
   bakehouse (always charged as a percent of the grain or bread)
   - a tax on boats each spring, before they were allowed to fish, charged
   in coin
   - a provision of one fish in 25 caught, for the use of the household

Additionally, the manor has a pretty significant surplus of food, which the
previous lord typically sold for coin in Beury or Cleenseau. This left the
village in general closer to starvation than it really should have been.
The manorial household would usually have plenty of food, whereas the
remaining villagers would typically go a bit hungry in the leaner months
and would eat a lot more fish than others.

*Manorial Obligations*
The manor currently supports a number of people:

   - Five guards (and the family of two of them)  = 12 people
   - The temple administrator (Eleanor) and El, the disciple of the
   Wyrdling = 2 people
   - Three stablehands = 3 people
   - Bertram, the stablemaster and his wife Susanna, the steward/secretary,
   and their youngest child = 3 people
   - A cook and assistant cook, and the assistant cook's large family (who
   mostly serve as scullions and maids) = 8 people
   - Three additional servants = 3 people

Which is about 30 people, or 1/10th of the town.

Additionally, there are several taxes the manor owes, but that's enough for
now. I'll provide some more information on taxes and the specifics of
Robin's appointment later.

Mike
