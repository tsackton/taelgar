# Notes from Celyn's Background (Email)

Kiya Nicoll's October 15, 2025 description of the [[Clawyn]] and [[Nefyn]] economy was a detailed account of assumptions behind Celyn's views. Mike Sackton replied on October 16 that the picture broadly made sense, while leaving particulars such as the location of Clawyn's milling open. The comments about possible crafts and exports remain estimates where Kiya framed them that way.

Kiya described Clawyn as a wine-producing village that relies on Nefyn for grain and some other necessities. She named a potter, smith, cooper, and weaver in Clawyn; local wood and goat fiber helped support work there, while glass bottles, grain, salt, and smithing metal came from elsewhere. She described Nefyn as a nearby market and source of grain, cattle, and specialized services. She expected Clawyn to have communal cooking or food storage associated with a meeting hall, but did not establish every such arrangement as a settled fact. Mike noted that grain would ordinarily be stored before milling, and did not decide whether Clawyn had its own miller or received flour from Nefyn.

In Kiya's more detailed picture, Clawyn had no grain fields or proper inn. Communal vegetable plots, fruit trees, berries, goat meat and milk, and guarded foraging supplemented imported grain; the vineyards occupied the better slopes and goats grazed the poorer ones. She envisaged nettles and goat fiber for local cloth, supplemented by imports. A family associated with the meeting hall might tend its bakehouse and shared food stores, freeing others for field and vineyard work. She proposed modest wood, kindling, and foraged-goods exports, with any cutting near the fey forest subject to negotiation. She identified thatching straw and blacksmith supplies among the imports. The possible saw pit, slaughterhouse arrangement near Nefyn, beehives, and exact clay source were suggestions rather than settled details.

Mike also supplied a regional economic picture for Cleenseau: food self-sufficiency, imports of metals, ceramics, glass, and luxuries, and exports of finished metalwork, grain, and some charcoal. He described crop, livestock, fish, and fiber sources, and gave Beury, Valit, and Taviose distinct local trades. Much of this is already present in [[Cleenseau Region]] and the settlement or manor notes. His further ideas for Asineau industry were explicitly open to refinement.
