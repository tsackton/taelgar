## Wed, 15 Oct 2025 18:57:45 -0400 — Kiya Nicoll <darkhawk@mindspring.com>

Subject: [Thantos] How I am thinking about SimManor (Asineau stuff)

Seemed worth laying this out and I have some sit-down time between the infinite tasks at the moment so. These are my thoughts about what to build in the intersection between the mechanics, the worldbuilding, and the noisy boy who lives in my brain, and then I can shut up about Watsonian/Doylian nonsense for a bit.



Celyn’s opinion is based on being a farmboy from a village that orbited around being a source for a specialist product; he is very aware of the interplay between subsistence agriculture and trade.  His home village is about this size, though set up substantially differently both on the economy and perceived need to build defenses (Mike had said to me that Tyrwinghan villages rarely have walls by comparison to Sembara), but his opinion is informed by the fact that Asineau does not produce a good sufficiently in demand to provide coin and barter trade for the missing necessities, unlike home.  (Other than rope, which to be fair is a very in-demand basic commodity.)

This means that he will, in character, be interested in things that fix his perception of village problems: first, the food scarcity issues; second, production of domestic necessities; third, trade imbalances.  The second and third roll into the question of “what can be produced here that will either not need to be imported or can be sold as surplus elsewhere in order to get necessities”, and he doesn’t care what those things are particularly (it’s not terrain suited to what he knows from home at all), he just believes in finding _something_.

He is absolutely a person who will look at “we want to establish a smithy” and want to figure out where we would get the iron from, because there is no local production. Or, probably more accurately, want *Izgil* to figure out where to get the iron from, because Izgil knows more about that sort of thing, Celyn just knows that that sort of thing is an actual problem even if he has no head for the actual particulars. :}



I am guessing, meanwhile, that the arc is very likely to be that the last bit of this campaign arc will land us at level 9 in time for the multiple-year hiatus, which works very well, narratively, for “they settle in and build Asineau up” on the Watsonian level and gives time/space for the construction of the next wave of Special facilities as part of the nature of the resulting story.

So, looking at the established worldbuilding what I perceive is this:

- Asineau remains centrally located, and among the services we can offer is training guardsmen and militia, including giving experience with magical effects.  This is a substantially party-driven economic thing, and should probably mostly be filed as ‘used to gain income for special projects such as building out the wall and/or upgrading facilities’ right now rather than part of the default engine, because of that.  (In the long run, the Special for this is Training Area, a level 9, at which point it can be a low-key self-managing income engine.)

- The current state of cottage industry can be bolstered easily with a Workshop (level 5) which will probably - crossing the Doylian/Watsonian border - indicate the party’s presence has attracted a few craftsmen and stabilized the economics somewhat, making it more possible to have local self-sufficiency and low-key trade.

- having more stable local production plus diplomatic overtures to the lizardfolk in Ganboa (and seeing what they want to trade for and what sort of shelter in case of monster attack they might want/accept) will make progress towards establishing an actual market in town plausible, which will feed into manor income as well as improving standards of living.  I think this exists 90% in the Watsonian realm, but it is possible that it might be helped on a mechanics level by anchoring it with a Storehouse (5)? It does not do so directly - the written mechanics are all about party money manipulation - but I can see an argument for knock-on Watsonian effects.

- Valit has a river crossing but the map does not show whether the resulting track on the east side goes to Beury, to Asineau, or both.  Valit is probably a reasonable source of good timber and forestry goods, so the establishment of a market and supplying goods that Valit has need of will likely move some of Valit’s trade energy from Cleenseau to Asineau, assuming adequate road.  (Question is of course what does Valit want.)

- Cleenseau has three inns; Beury has one, but it’s mediocre.  There is potential space for a trade inn along the roadway, for people who don’t want to push as far as Cleenseau and want better accomodations than Beury offers, which I would propose as a possible thing to develop during the intermission between Plot Arc 1 and Plot Arc 2.  (There is no Special that matches this; the closest one by my reckoning, the Pub, is a level 13. Aside from the levels, that needs a reliable grain surplus.)

- with market activity established, it will be easier to support a Smithy (5) with imported iron, and given I was explaining the village situation to Kevin as “so poor it didn’t even have a smithy” based on my vague knowledge of historical-period economics that is a Thing.

- whatever Izgil wants beyond Workshop stuff may or may not feed the economic engine of the village at this point, depending.


The level 9 break opens up a bunch of other things, and how I’m looking at it is very much shaped by the fact of the known “and we get a couple of years”, which means, for example, picking up a Stable head who can set up a horse breeding program (which is a middle to upper class economic engine) rather than putter along just doing maintenance.  We have a whole gap in which we will have our success at reconceptualizing the economy here with our tinkering.

In the long run Celyn wants to expand the Garden (2000 GP investment by the book); he wants to add the herb component to have a steady source of his medicinal teas, and that also produces the sort of raw material that would support an apothecary in town (no Special mechanic unless that’s the minion working herb side, but that’s producing trade goods and thus feeding the market engine).  (Discussion to be had with the Ganboa folks, because herbs are their trade goods, but probably not the heavily land-bound ones.  But maybe one of them would like to be an apothecary, who knows. Brokering their stuff through reliably might be appreciated.)  This is probably an in the break thing to do, along with expanding other things like the Workshop (adds three tools to the list and more minions).

(Not going to noodle the level 9 stuff right now. For one thing this is long enough. Level 9 gets more into ‘interesting decisions that affect town character’ than ‘basic economic repair’.)

Worldbuilding note: Taelgar is littered with a lot more dangerous beasts
than Earth, which creates a general problem of safety that is much more
extreme than in the real world. Sembara and Tyrwingha have solved this
problem in very different ways. In Tyrwingha, the close relationship with
the fey leads to a widespread belief (mostly true) that although the fey
themselves have their downsides, they largely keep out the zombies and
goblins and skeletons and the like. Not entirely, but enough to bring the
level of danger down to something more like on Earth.

In Sembara, on the other hand, the manorial system grants tax revenues to
the local lord to defend the populace. Fortifications are dramatically more
common and the populace is much more likely to hide out in a walled
fortification when things go bad.

Note: Cleenseau has four inns (see Cleenseau
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/sembara/barony-of-aveil/cleenseau-region/cleenseau/cleenseau/>).
Three are outside the walls, the fourth, the halfling owned Crossroads Inn,
is inside the walls (and the biggest and most important and famous of the
four). One thing to remember is that the region is a bit isolated. The road
goes east from Cleenseau, but it is 15 miles from Beury to Dalit through
the East Bog
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/sembara/barony-of-aveil/cleenseau-region/east-bog/>.
So travelers have a strong tendency to want to spend the night in Beury,
get an early start, and cross the bog in a day. It isn't to say another
trade inn is impossible, but the geography makes Asineau a somewhat weak
spot for an inn as far as it goes. A decent inn could maybe attract
westbound travellers who make good time through the bog though, although
eastbound travelers would rarely stop so close to Cleenseau.

The map of Sembara on the wiki doesn't have all roads or rivers marked. All
the major rivers are marked, but some significant roads are missing. There
is a road (the South Watch Road
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/roads/south-watch-road/>)
from Cleenseau to Dunfry (which is the headquarters of the South Watch of
the Army of the West). Historically the entire area west of Aveil and
Ainwick is pretty lightly settled. It was a large and rich place hundreds
of years ago, but during the Great War (in the 1540s, so ~180 years ago)
significant Bad Terrible Things happened in the western regions, and it has
never really recovered. It is now very much dependent on the army for its
economy. There are roads both north and south from Dunfry, and a more
significant road from Ainwick to Dunmore. The center of gravity of the
region is really more in Dunmore and along the Wistel. The connection to
Cleenseau was built by Cece I in the 1660s, to provide a more efficient
path to the southern marches (Marches of Enford) but historically people
would have traveled up the Wistel past Ainwick and then south to get to
Dunfry.
