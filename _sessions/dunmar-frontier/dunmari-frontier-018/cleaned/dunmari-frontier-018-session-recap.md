#### Short
In Karawa, Candrosa warns the party about the Old Woman’s bargains and explains that someone must have summoned the demons at Raven’s Hold.

#### Intermediate
Around Seeker’s illusory map, Candrosa and Alesh hear the party’s account of Raven’s Hold. Candrosa tells of the Old Woman of the Dusts, whose cures can carry terrible costs, though he does not recognize Agata’s name. Kenzo recounts Vola’s captors planning to take her to Agata after a ritual. Candrosa explains that demons need a summoner to enter Taelgar, leaving the party to wonder who opened the way and how Agata was involved.

#### Long
Gathered with Candrosa and Alesh around Seeker’s illusion-enhanced map, the party asks about the Old Woman of the Dusts. Candrosa remembers her being old even in his childhood, but his order has no record of her before the Great War. He tells of a father who surrendered a thumb to save his child, then dreamed of a creature using it to kill; the nightmares eventually drove him to take his own life. Seeker recognizes similarities to the sacrificial magic that interested his brother, while Alesh wants nothing to do with such bargains.

Kenzo connects the story to Agata and recounts Vola’s captivity at Raven’s Hold: an orc and two jackalweres intended to wait for a ritual before taking her to Agata. Candrosa does not recognize that name, and neither the Old Woman’s identity nor the captors’ connection to the demons is settled by his account. He explains that demons cannot readily enter Taelgar without someone opening a way, though they may spread afterward. Delwath recalls demons fighting in the Great War. Killing the demon has removed a threat, but Candrosa stresses that discovering who summoned it remains essential.

#### Short
Alesh traces the southern gnolls’ possible route from Raven’s Hold, but Candrosa refuses to explain the loss of Shakun’s protection and leaves to pray.

#### Intermediate
Alesh shows that the gnolls could have traveled south from Raven’s Hold before the demon died, then continued through Kharsan into the desert. Candrosa wonders whether its death explains their wasting condition. When pressed about Shakun’s lost protection, however, he insists that its origin is not his story to tell. He defends the still-unfallen temple and its powerful objects, gives directions for seeking the Old Woman, and withdraws to pray.

#### Long
Turning back to the map, Alesh explains that the southern gnolls need not have come from another demon: they had time to leave Raven’s Hold before the demon was killed. Her band followed them through Kharsan toward the Garamjala Desert, moving with purpose instead of hunting; many looked almost like zombies. Candrosa wonders whether the demon’s death eleven days earlier cut off the power sustaining them, but Delwath warns that the wasted creatures still fought fiercely. Alesh also reports avoiding an isolated orc scouting party rather than risking a larger force nearby.

Questions about Shakun’s protection receive fewer answers. Candrosa says he still feels the goddess’s presence, but refuses to explain the protection’s origin, even privately. Seeker and Kenzo suggest looking beyond troop movements to ley lines and other planes. When Delwath asks what remains in Karawa worth besieging, Candrosa brings out his crescent-topped staff and insists that the Temple of Shakun has never fallen and still contains powerful objects. Seeker’s brief look through the purple gemstone goggles reveals little about the staff; Alesh recognizes the goggles as Drankorian. Overwhelmed, Candrosa leaves to pray. Before going, he describes how to seek the Old Woman: reach the mesa’s eastern edge, turn south, and follow an animal behaving strangely.

#### Short
With the temple’s mystery unresolved, the party weighs an eastern investigation and asks Havdar about supplies, armor repairs, and his plans to hunt orcs.

#### Intermediate
Before approaching Agata, the party wants better questions to ask her. Delwath proposes investigating Kharsan and using the Mirror of the Past at the mysterious stone house. Alesh advises them about water and horses, while Havdar offers no quick solution for the damaged armor. He hopes to pursue the reported orcs soon, wants the party to join him, and asks them to persuade the centaurs to remain in Karawa.

#### Long
After Candrosa leaves, Alesh points the party toward surviving water stores at Ikram’s and Havdar’s desert-trained horses. Kenzo wants to understand the larger powers at work, while Delwath is wary of approaching Agata without knowing what to ask. They recall Hralgar’s claim that she seeks revenge, but cannot tell how much his far-seeing revealed or what relationship, if any, lies behind it. Delwath favors examining Kharsan, where he reappeared, and returning to the empty stone house with the Mirror of the Past before seeking her out.

Practical preparations bring them to Havdar. His people have some skill at repairing armor, but no forge, and the damaged half-plate would take at least a week to mend. He wants to leave sooner to investigate Alesh’s orc report and invites the party to accompany him. His other concern is Karawa: the centaurs helped break the siege, but their intentions are uncertain, so he asks the party to secure their continued help. Wellby’s axe beak has also become more manageable during the previous journey—it eats from his hand and comes when called—though riding it comfortably will take more work. The party heads to the centaur camp to speak with Tharandros.

#### Short
Over food and wine, Tharandros describes Agata’s fey origins and the echo realms, then warns of an unnatural silence around Candrosa and the temple.

#### Intermediate
Tharandros welcomes them with antelope and wine, identifies Agata as descended from an archfey, and describes the other realms as distorted reflections of Taelgar. His people’s pact leaves Agata south of the old road and the centaurs to the north. More troubling is the silence he senses around Candrosa and the temple, where the land’s echoes should sound. The party considers missing power, a stolen object, and Agata’s involvement, but finds no explanation.

#### Long
Tharandros receives them beneath an open-sided shelter, pleased that the northern demons are gone, and shares roasted antelope and wine. He recognizes Agata as a descendant of an archfey. By an agreement with his people, she keeps to the country south of the old trade road while the centaurs occupy the northern plains. Explaining her nature leads him into the echo realms: ancient magic was separated from Taelgar, leaving resonances in the land and distorted reflections beyond it. Seeker builds an illusory model, but Tharandros compares the realms to warped mirrors reflecting different pasts and futures. He distinguishes the land’s echoes from Seeker’s runic magic and cautions that a fey creature’s realm does not neatly determine its morality.

Karawa troubles him because those echoes fall silent around the temple and Candrosa. His tribe’s seer warned against approaching the Speaker while this absence clings to him. Agata’s personal presence at Raven’s Hold would have broken their pact, but the party cannot establish that she was there; Tharandros only speculates that her desire for revenge follows defeat in the Great War. Delwath describes the bronze-door vision interrupted by his mysterious benefactor, and Tharandros doubts that a true divine vision could be dismissed so easily. Seeker wonders whether something was stolen or traded away. Tharandros allows the possibility, while warning that Agata could not simply overpower a god and would be dangerous to bargain with.

#### Short
Tharandros agrees that the centaurs will remain, and the party decides to ride east with Havdar before returning to the mystery in Karawa.

#### Intermediate
Tharandros describes a dangerous crossing to the Wild east of Raven’s Hold, near the Kharja’s source. He promises to keep his tribe in Karawa to watch for any spread of the silence. The party postpones proposals to infiltrate the temple or seek more visions, choosing to ride east with Havdar, investigate the orcs and Kharsan, and later return by the stone house before approaching Agata.

#### Long
Asked how to reach the other realms, Tharandros explains that crossings occur where the reflections draw close. He knows one leading to the Wild east of Raven’s Hold, near the Kharja’s source, but urges caution. He has no particular wish to recover Hralgar’s eyes. His concern is the silence: the centaurs will remain to guard the wider land against its spread, even while its cause remains unknown.

Seeker suggests entering the temple covertly, and Wellby proposes another attempt to seek Shakun’s visions, but Delwath rejects an immediate intrusion into sacred ground. Instead, he proposes riding east with Havdar, looking into the orcs and haunted Kharsan, then returning west to investigate the stone house before seeking Agata. Tharandros still wants answers about the temple, but considers the orcs the more immediate danger; the silence has not changed during his stay. That remains the plan as the group finishes its discussion. Theories about a stolen focus, deception, or manipulation remain unresolved, and Kenzo’s earlier journey using Candrosa’s staff has still not been shared with his companions.

#### Short
Later that evening, Kenzo privately asks Tharandros about his dreams and jade, raising a possible motive for the siege and learning of the Order of the Awakened Soul.

#### Intermediate
While his companions organize supplies, Kenzo slips away to Tharandros and describes his dreams. The centaur senses unusual perception in him, but cannot explain the jade pendant’s magic. Hearing about Kenzo’s earlier journey through Candrosa’s staff, he wonders whether the besiegers wanted the staff itself. He also directs Kenzo east toward the wandering storytellers of the Order of the Awakened Soul, whose extraordinary abilities resemble Kenzo’s own.

#### Long
Later that evening, while the others organize supplies, Kenzo returns alone to Tharandros. He describes his recurring dreams and lets the centaur touch the jade pendant without removing it. Tharandros senses something akin to his own people’s perception of the Plane of Souls, but the pendant’s magic does not resemble the land’s resonances. Kenzo plans to examine it with the Mirror of the Past once the mirror recharges. He also argues that Candrosa seems grieving rather than malicious; Tharandros senses loss and suspects that religious obligations constrain the Speaker.

Kenzo then gives a broad account of his earlier journey using Candrosa’s staff. Direct passage into the Dreamworld strikes Tharandros as extraordinary power, suggesting a possible reason to besiege an otherwise emptied town: someone might have wanted the staff. This is a new theory, not an answer, and reinforces his determination to remain. He thinks Kenzo’s unusual dreams could reflect outside influences reaching a receptive mind, while the jade seems to hold a different sort of magic.

For another lead, Tharandros describes the Order of the Awakened Soul, wandering Dunmari storytellers who seek forgotten graves and histories. Their hidden repository is only rumored, but he has personally seen members move and strike with unusual power resembling Kenzo’s abilities. Kenzo demonstrates the resemblance. Tharandros advises looking east and repeats a rumor that they can travel as birds.
