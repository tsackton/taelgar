#### Short
The party secures its captive and returns to Havdar, whose warriors have lost four companions in the coordinated orc attack.

#### Intermediate
After tending their wounds and binding the captured cleric, the party searches the canyon rim and finds a recurring emblem: a screaming face above a rising sun. Down in camp, Havdar reports four dead and an attack from both the north and the heights. He introduces Aram, a holy warrior of Aagir, and they arrange to question the prisoner before the funeral.

#### Long
Seeker heals Wellby while the others bind the unconscious cleric before the stone hand holding him disappears. As they recover, Kenzo questions Delwath about the shadows surrounding his old armor, but they find no explanation for its altered appearance. The dead orcs carry little food or travel gear and no useful treasure; their shields and painted faces bear a screaming face above a rising sun.

They carry the captive down to Havdar’s camp, where four warriors have died. Havdar describes a main attack from the north while another band killed the flank watch and brought spellcasters onto the canyon rim. The coordination and fire magic caught his warriors by surprise. Scouts have gone to follow the attackers’ tracks, and Aram, a holy warrior of Aagir, is preparing the dead for cremation. With sunset approaching, the party decides to wake the prisoner first.

#### Short
The captive, Gorkil, names Grash as his master and boasts of returning stronger after death, then magically compels Seeker to kill him.

#### Intermediate
Aram revives Gorkil, who claims that Grash, the Blighted One, rules Kharsan’s haunted dead and will restore fallen followers tenfold. He scorns Thark’s silence and Uzgash’s peaceful farmers, whose treasure Grash covets. The party debates a possible connection to Shakun’s lost protection, but gains no explanation or location for the farmers. Gorkil ends the questioning by magically commanding Seeker to slit his throat; his corpse joins the burning orc dead.

#### Long
Aram’s healing wakes the bound prisoner, who speaks Common and calls himself a servant of Grash, the Blighted One. Grash began speaking to his people about a year ago, he says, while Thark has fallen silent. He places his new master in the darkest heart of Kharsan and claims that death will only make his followers ten times stronger. Wellby urges Havdar to burn the orc corpses rather than risk their return.

Speaking privately in Dunmari, the companions wonder whether Thark’s silence is connected to Shakun’s lost protection. Delwath cautions that silence does not prove a god is gone. Kenzo and Seeker consider seeking answers through planar travel, but Seeker remembers losing his younger brother and Delwath fears another imprisonment. Candrosa’s dream-travel artifact might offer a safer way to learn more; the monastery and its connection to the failed cleansing remain another lead.

Questioning resumes. Gorkil describes Grash as a dark spirit commanding Kharsan’s haunted dead, although he admits never standing in his presence. He derides Uzgash’s people for farming and raising animals, and says their treasures, especially old things, should belong to Grash. This prompts speculation about the Society of the Open Scroll, Kenzo’s jade fragment, and what the rival treasure hunters might want, but establishes no connection. Before the party can decide what to do with him, Gorkil’s voice takes on magical force and Seeker obeys his command to cut his throat. They add his body to the pyre and tell Havdar what they have learned. Rivers, oases, and underground water might sustain peaceful orc settlements, but Gorkil has given them no useful location.

#### Short
They then honor Havdar’s dead, receive Camana’s bowstring and trade for a jumping ring, before sharing an evening of practice and stories.

#### Intermediate
Havdar invites them to the funeral, where Aram blesses four separate pyres and Havdar remembers the fallen. Wellby accepts Camana’s Flaming Bowstring and asks to hear about its former owner; Kenzo trades his Dunmari spear for a Ring of Jumping. The two practice leaping and redirecting arrows, then join the camp’s meal, music, and stories before a quiet night.

#### Long
Havdar calls the companions to the funeral. Each fallen warrior has a separate pyre, and Aram invokes Aagir and places divine icons to watch over the dead. Havdar speaks of those he has lost. Afterward he offers Wellby the bowstring carried by Camana, his dead scout leader, which can set arrows alight. Wellby accepts it and asks to hear Camana’s story.

Seeing that Kenzo seldom uses his Dunmari spear, Havdar offers a trade: an old ring decorated with a silver hare, apparently Drankorian, that greatly extends the wearer’s jumps. Kenzo accepts. He and Wellby spend part of the evening combining those leaps with practiced arrow redirection. Dinner, music, drinking, and stories fill the remaining hours, and the camp passes the night without trouble.

#### Short
The next morning they choose the monastery, ride a wide southern detour, and reach the dry Kharja on May 10.

#### Intermediate
The returning scouts find the attackers’ camp and no comparable orc force nearby. Leaving Havdar to remain in camp, the party sets out on May 6 for the monastery, taking a wide southern route to avoid Kharsan. They ride quickly through the canyonlands, then slow in the barren desert, practicing their new abilities along the way. On May 10 they turn east and camp at the dry Kharja, declining to investigate possible trees farther south.

#### Long
The scouts return on May 6 after finding the attackers’ supplied camp six or seven miles northeast. Forty-two orcs died in the attack, and the scouts have found neither another force of that size nor signs of the undead gnolls. Havdar intends to remain in camp another week before resuming patrols around Bas Udda. The party chooses the monastery south of Kharsan, hoping to learn more about the failed cleansing without entering the city. They recall that the monks could not locate the old attempt precisely and that Delwath appeared on a bridge outside Kharsan’s gates.

With replenished food and water and Aram’s blessing, they leave after the hottest part of the day. Their route swings well south before turning toward the Kharja. They ride quickly on May 7 and 8, passing dry scrub, dwindling springs, and canyonlands. Wellby completes Baxter’s training as a riding mount, though combat and firing from his back remain problems. Kenzo practices jumping and redirecting arrows, while Seeker works glass into connected orbs resembling an interplanar orrery. On May 9 they reduce their pace as the terrain becomes bare rock and sand.

Turning east on May 10, they reach the dry river late in the day. High western banks overlook the eastern floodplain, and shrubs suggest water remains below the surface. A distant dark patch might be trees, but there is no visible smoke. Delwath considers searching there for peaceful orcs and answers about their silent god. With no stronger evidence of a settlement, they camp beside the river and keep to their plan of heading north.

#### Short
Following the river north, they reach Kharsan’s outskirts, where Wellby uses a hawk to survey the ruins and find cover toward the monastery.

#### Intermediate
They turn north on May 11 and sight Kharsan in the afternoon, with royal tombs along the western cliffs and an unfinished orc fort south of the city. Wellby feeds a desert hawk and shares its sight through Beast Sense. He sees a looted city, a shadowed palace, and orcs building with salvaged stone. The monastery appears intact and still, its fountain flowing; a route east and behind a hill offers cover for their approach.

#### Long
They follow the dry Kharja north the next morning, resting in shade as the heat builds. By three or four in the afternoon, a monumental statue and the ruins of Kharsan come into view. Twelve stepped tombs line the western bluffs, including one marked by an immense kneeling figure of Bhishma. Kenzo and Seeker recognize the burial tradition of Dunmar’s first dynasty. South of the city stand an unfinished fort and, farther away, the monastery. Wellby’s spyglass reveals figures hauling stone from the ruins.

Wellby lures a hungry desert hawk down with meat, wins its agreement to share its sight, and casts Beast Sense while the others guard him. From above, Kharsan looks repeatedly stripped of useful materials. A large temple, stadium, canals, workshops, barracks, and stables remain recognizable, while unnatural-looking darkness clings to a palace within its own walls. Orcs carry stone to the maze of walls and enclosures forming their new fort. Its banners bear the same screaming-face-and-sun emblem as the attackers. Fewer than a hundred orcs are visible, and several enclosed rooms are guarded, but the hawk will not risk their bird-hunting archers to discover what is inside. The western tombs have varied shrines, and the northernmost appears unfinished.

Open floodplain would expose an approach to the city, but a route east and behind a hill offers shelter toward the monastery. Its five buildings appear substantially intact: towers, dormitories, cloisters, and a temple decorated with scenes of daily life. Water still runs from Bhishma’s cheeks into a central fountain, though Wellby sees no movement. The companions retain his rough map and remain outside, with a concealed approach identified and the monastery still unexplored.
