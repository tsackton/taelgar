#### Short
Camped west of Raven’s Hold, the party questions Vola about the Society of the Open Scroll and agrees to travel with her toward the distant storm.

#### Intermediate
Over dinner west of Raven’s Hold, the party debates returning to Karawa or investigating the stationary storm. Vola argues that the Society of the Open Scroll is a better guardian of lost knowledge than Agata Dustmother, but admits she does not know what its leaders understand about the danger. Eager to warn Chardon, she agrees to accompany the party toward the storm and carry a message to Havdar in Tokra. Wellby offers her his horse, and they prepare to leave in the morning.

#### Long
As sunset falls over the plains west of Raven’s Hold, Vola returns with birds for dinner. Seeker works on glass goggles that will let others see the ley lines, while Delwath watches lightning flicker on the western horizon. Returning to Karawa would take four or five days, but Wellby favors investigating the storm, and Delwath does not want to leave a danger at their backs. Riswynn is more concerned with returning her family’s shield to Tharn Todor; Kenzo urges them to question Vola before deciding. Over roasted birds and vegetables, Vola argues for cooperation against Agata Dustmother. She defends the Society’s preservation of lost knowledge, while conceding that dangerous knowledge cannot be shared freely. Her own expedition sought old Drankorian and Dunmari treasures, and she came to Raven’s Hold following rumors of a chimera’s hoard. She says her captors intended to take her to Agata in the desert, and wants to reach Chardon quickly to warn the Great Library and other treasure hunters. Delwath’s account of destroying Cha’mutte’s cursed staff reveals their differing views on what should be preserved. They nevertheless agree to travel west together: the storm is only a small detour for Vola, though she will not promise to enter it. Wellby offers his horse, Vola agrees to warn Havdar in Tokra, and the party keeps its heavy supplies for the slower journey.

#### Short
After two days across the burned plains, they reach the storm, leave Vola outside with the supplies, and prepare to follow a ley line inward.

#### Intermediate
They set out with Wellby’s new axebeak following behind and camp that evening in land scorched by lightning fires. A vulture scouting for Wellby reports a clear center containing a white rock on a black rock. The next afternoon, the party reaches the storm’s edge, where Seeker uses his goggles to show Wellby the ley line leading inward. Vola stays outside with the remaining horses and supplies; the others prepare a lightning rod held aloft by Mage Hands and send Taster ahead.

#### Long
The next morning, Wellby returns to camp with an axebeak, later called Wellby Junior, and rides behind Kenzo while Vola takes his horse. They head west, foraging as they go and giving Seeker time to work on his goggles. By evening the living plains give way to blackened ground, burned trees, and scavenged carcasses left by fires sparked from the storm. A giant vulture scouts for Wellby and reports no obvious route through the weather, but a clear area at its center: a white rock resting on a black rock. Encouraged, they continue the next morning and reach the outskirts around two or three in the afternoon. Thunder and lightning fill the sky, while mist and rain hide the interior. Seeker lets Wellby try the goggles and see the ley line pointing into the storm. After discussing shelter and the floating disc, they choose to go straight in. Vola agrees to camp outside for the night with the remaining horses and gear. Seeker and Delwath will keep a metal lightning rod overhead by passing it between Mage Hands. Wellby refuses to risk his axebeak, but communicates with Taster and arranges to receive the rabbit’s scouting impressions. Riswynn takes Taster and rides her own horse forward.

#### Short
Inside, runes compel Delwath to stab Seeker, and the wind later scatters the party before Wellby reunites them in the calm eye.

#### Intermediate
Following the ley line through ash and sideways rain, they find runes forming on the ground. Delwath warns that the writing commands him to attack Seeker, then does so; Riswynn restrains him with Hold Person while Wellby heals Seeker. The compulsion fades as they move away. Deeper inside, Riswynn’s horse bolts and Taster flees, then the wind blows Kenzo and Riswynn aside. Wellby returns for them on a rope held by Delwath, using telepathy and Kenzo’s driftglobe to guide everyone into the calm eye.

#### Long
They follow the ley line through smoke, ash, and gathering mist, with lightning flashing overhead and rain sweeping sideways above surprisingly dry ground. Large runes begin forming in the dust. Delwath reads an order to attack the dwarf and warns the others, but cannot resist: his summoned sword strikes Seeker. Riswynn casts Hold Person on him, while Wellby applies healing mud to Seeker’s wound. Seeker tries Mold Earth to erase the writing, but it reforms, demanding that he ask a question. Distrusting it after the attack, he refuses. At Riswynn’s urging they press onward; Wellby slaps Delwath, briefly clearing his mind, while Seeker covers the ash with an illusion of green grass. The runes trail behind them before the compulsion finally fades. Ahead, the wind grows deafening and visibility collapses. Riswynn’s horse throws her and bolts, and Taster flees into the darkness. Delwath, Seeker, and Wellby reach the calm center, but Kenzo and Riswynn are blown away from them. Wellby ties a rope around his waist, leaves its other end with Delwath, and plunges back into the storm. His telepathic instructions keep the pair in place until Kenzo releases his driftglobe; its faint light becomes the beacon for their rescue. Together they emerge into the eye, facing a white marble tower atop a jagged prow of blue granite.

#### Short
Beneath the tower they find an abandoned campsite, a Sibyl-marked grave, and unusual materials surrounding a powerful ley-line node.

#### Intermediate
They take shelter beneath the granite, out of sight of the tower’s windows. An old camp and a grave marked with the Sibyl’s symbol suggest earlier visitors, perhaps Society treasure hunters, though their identities remain unknown. Three ley lines meet beneath the tower. Seeker senses gold and silver near its summit, unfamiliar material inside the granite, and something unusual in the marble walls. Delwath decides to investigate invisibly.

#### Long
The tower shows no lights or movement, but its windows overlook the open ground, so the party moves into shelter beneath the granite prow. Here they find a campsite apparently used by two or three people for several days, roughly three or four weeks ago. A nearby grave bears the Sibyl’s holy symbol, prompting speculation that Society treasure hunters came this way. Their identities remain unknown, and tracks leading away disappear into the storm. Seeker confirms that three ley lines converge directly beneath the tower at a major node, larger than the one at Raven’s Hold. Pressing his hand to the granite, he senses gold and silver near the tower’s summit, an unfamiliar metal or ore inside the rock, and something beyond ordinary marble in the walls. With no clear sign of anyone watching, Delwath proposes turning invisible and climbing the worn steps to the entrance.

#### Short
His scouting ends in a leap from a window when a lightning creature attacks, and he returns with news of a voice offering answers about Agata.

#### Intermediate
Invisible, Delwath finds the entrance blasted outward and the ground floor wrecked, with blue and purple sparks reacting to his movements. He passes intact living quarters and reaches a study, where a towering bolt of living lightning emerges from the wall. Fleeing through a window, he avoids its blast and uses Misty Step to soften his fall. Back under cover, he reports a voice that answered his question about Agata with a warning of a desert power seeking betrayal, power, and revenge; he suspects another presence is trapped inside.

#### Long
Delwath vanishes into shadow and climbs the weathered steps. The doorway holds twisted remnants of iron and silver, apparently blasted outward from inside. Beyond it, the ground-floor hall looks as though a tornado tore through it: the damage seems recent, although the building is centuries old. Blue and purple sparks race through the walls, growing stronger whenever he disturbs the rubble. He climbs the intact staircase to living quarters with three smaller rooms and one larger room, all behind closed doors, then continues toward a third-floor study containing bookshelves and a white marble table. A ten-foot mass of crackling blue lightning emerges from the wall and moves toward him. Delwath retreats, then dives through a window as a lightning blast misses and strikes the wall. Misty Step reduces the fall, but he still lands hard and loses his invisibility. Returning to shelter, he describes a voice that spoke to him at the entrance and invited a question. When he asked what Agata Dustmother wanted, it described a power growing in the desert, reaching out to betray the people of the plains, and seeking power above all, and revenge. He also sensed distress as he reached the third floor, leading him to suspect that something else is trapped or tormented by the lightning creature.

#### Short
As they rest and debate the voice’s connection to the runes, sunset reveals that the familiar Fox and Hunter are missing from the sky.

#### Intermediate
Comparing Delwath’s account with the writing outside, they suspect a connection but cannot explain the voice, the lightning, or the storm. The runes conveyed meaning directly, and Seeker recalls stories of ancient giants using master runes. They consider possible ways to confront the creature, then take a short rest beneath the tower. Kenzo meditates and Seeker helps the others recover, until the emerging stars reveal a new mystery: the Fox and Hunter are absent.

#### Long
The others wonder whether the lightning is imprisoning another being, whether it controls the storm, and whether the earlier visitors died fighting it. These remain guesses. Seeker considers releasing the fire spirit in his elemental gem as a distraction or cutting the creature off from the ley-line node, but neither idea becomes a plan. Delwath’s account of the voice echoes the runes’ questions, suggesting a shared source without proving one. On reflection, neither Delwath nor Seeker can identify a language in the writing: its meaning appeared directly in their minds. Seeker recalls stories connecting master runes with ancient giants, though the symbols were not written in Giant and the tower’s doorway is human-sized. They settle into a short rest beneath the granite, with Kenzo meditating and Seeker’s Song of Rest helping them recover. As the sun sets and stars appear, they look up to find something wrong. The familiar Fox and Hunter are missing from the sky.
