#### Short
The party surveyed Circular Island from the Wanderer’s statue and chose a nearby ruined tower to explore.

#### Intermediate
From the Wanderer’s statue, the party spotted a ruined landing, distant divine monuments, and the remains of a town and its estates. A scarred region and the forested central mountain offered other possible destinations, but they chose the nearest tower, with Kenzo and Wellby scouting ahead.

#### Long
Kenzo walked up the side of the Wanderer’s statue while Delwath joined the climb. The view from two hundred feet revealed the pilings of a vanished pier below, stairs cut into the cliff, and distant statues of the Mother and the Night Queen. Their discussion of the Eight Divines and different traditions of worship gave way to a survey of the island: a ruined port, scattered estates and towers, an arena, a reddish scar, and forest around the central mountain. With much to investigate and the afternoon already underway, they chose the nearest tower. Delwath prepared a pilgrim disguise while Kenzo and Wellby moved ahead to scout.

#### Short
There, Kenzo befriended the gambler Ghesh and promised a golden circlet for an introduction to Megren and secrecy from Ra'ghemdros.

#### Intermediate
Four dragonets were playing dice beside the ruined manor. Kenzo joined them as a wandering monk collecting stories, and his losses encouraged Ghesh to explain how the black dragon Ra'ghemdros ruled through tribute and rewards. Ghesh’s friend Megren had recently earned her own tower with treasure from the mainland. Kenzo promised Ghesh an elaborate golden circlet in exchange for an introduction and his clan’s silence.

#### Long
Four small winged dragonets were gambling beside the manor’s damaged tower, and Kenzo’s soul sight suggested that one was cheating. Leaving his companions out of sight, he approached in Draconic as a traveling monk collecting stories and offered coins for a game. He also sensed dragonets scattered among nearby ruins, avoiding places marked by lingering death like the plague echoes at Kharsan. Several losses at dice made him welcome, and Ghesh, the most assertive of the gamblers, described the island’s ruler: Ra'ghemdros, a great black dragon who demanded shiny treasures and rewarded useful subjects with towers. Megren, a friend who had brought treasure from the mainland, seemed a promising lead. To secure an introduction and keep his visit quiet, Kenzo offered a golden circlet set with green alexandrite. Ghesh gathered his clan to agree to secrecy, with payment due at Megren’s tower. The arrangement had a limit: if Ghesh failed to return that night, the others would report the visit. He also warned that their route must avoid ruins haunted by red mist and visions of people dying.

#### Short
Megren confirmed that she had traded the jade to Ra'ghemdros, described the dragon’s dangerous lair, and mentioned rumors of a hidden elder.

#### Intermediate
Delwath made himself, Wellby, and Seeker invisible while Kenzo traveled openly with Ghesh, racing him through the air and learning which dragonets resented Ra'ghemdros. At Megren’s tower, Kenzo concealed his jade and heard how she had taken a green fragment from a mainland cave and exchanged it for her home. Her account of an acid-filled lair and a secondhand story about a destroyed forest camp offered warnings, while rumors of an elder suggested another source of help. Kenzo gave the dragonets carved likenesses and paid Ghesh the promised circlet.

#### Long
Ghesh led the way around the haunted ruins while Delwath, Wellby, and Seeker followed invisibly. Kenzo remained the visible visitor, using his raven whistle to race Ghesh through the air; his speed earned a complaint of cheating. Along the way, Ghesh distinguished his friends from Ra'ghemdros’s devoted followers, admitted that he would not mourn her death, and warned of voices in the forest and dangerous western ruins. At Megren’s well-kept tower, surrounded by a small dragonet settlement, Kenzo disguised the jade he carried with illusory flowers. Megren described finding a green, claw-sized fragment in a mainland cave and giving it to Ra'ghemdros, who rewarded her with ownership of the tower. Her route to the dragon led through old baths, beneath the water, and into a cavern of acid, shadowy tendrils, and eruptions. She also repeated Zadar’s story of a forest camp destroyed by the dragon and turned into an acid pool, making clear that she had not witnessed it. Rumors placed an elder somewhere in the forest, though she doubted them. Kenzo carved small likenesses of both hosts and handed Ghesh the circlet. Megren kept her carving but passed the offered elvish coin to Ghesh, having little need for another favor from the dragon.

#### Short
The party sought that elder in the forest, where Kenzo sensed him near the summit and resisted a corrupted memory pool.

#### Intermediate
After weighing theft, combat, and a bargain for the jade, the party decided it needed more information. They crossed the dry interior mostly invisible, avoided passing dragonets, and entered the forest in the evening. Kenzo sensed an ancient dragonet near the summit, and they followed the pilgrim road toward him. At a damaged pool, Kenzo resisted the disturbing reflections of suffering people; the group left it uncleansed and continued uphill.

#### Long
Once clear of their hosts, the companions compared ways to recover the jade. An infiltration of the acid-filled lair seemed dangerous, an outright fight uncertain, and a trade liable to end with Ra'ghemdros taking everything. They considered whether she might be bound to the island or whether the lair lay elsewhere, but neither theory was established. Seeking the elder offered a way to learn more before committing. They traveled about ten miles across dry scrubland, using invisibility for most of the crossing and avoiding two dragonets overhead. The forest was open and full of flowers, with an intact pilgrim road leading toward the mountain. Kenzo sensed the eldest dragonet near its summit. As daylight faded, they passed the sound of a waterfall and kept climbing. By a damaged pool, hollow-eyed figures seemed to gaze out of the murky reflections. Kenzo resisted its influence, leaving only an uneasy feeling. The party considered cleansing the pool but moved on, passing another and drawing closer to the summit before stopping.

They asked for an audience and whether it was safe to approach, then rested while waiting. Delwath also scried the recipient, seeing a grayish dragonet with a faint purple cast preparing a stone-and-pulley trap in the Temple of the Sibyl. The lynx waited nearby, apparently ignored. The brief view did not reveal the full trigger, but it gave the party warning of what awaited them. With their single short rest complete and no answer to the message, they resumed the climb.

#### Short
Farther up the road, an elven memorial shared the lives of the dead, and its trees revealed a forest that could no longer receive new memories.

#### Intermediate
At two trees joined by stone inscribed with drifting names, Delwath experienced the life and death of an elven quartermaster who became a soldier. Kenzo learned that the forest remembered the dead, had seen no pilgrims for centuries, and could no longer accept memories. He carved its vision of pilgrims into his staff, while Seeker sensed ancient stonework and an underground tunnel complex.

#### Long
After another half-hour walk, they reached a memorial where pale stone curved between two unusually tall trees. Hundreds of Elvish names drifted through its surface. Delwath’s touch brought a memory of an elf caring for soldiers as a quartermaster and later dying in battle. Kenzo felt the wider presence of the remembered dead and addressed the trees, which spoke as one forest. They recalled no pilgrims for about seven centuries and explained that some memory pools had not survived intact. They had come when the island changed from a refuge into a memorial; now they could preserve what remained but receive nothing new. In a shared image, humans, elves, and a stoneborn walked the moonlit road through a forest more vibrant than the one around them. Kenzo carved that memory into his staff. The island’s connections to Hkar and the dead of the Downfall prompted further questions, but the cause of the forest’s isolation remained unknown. Seeker’s examination of the stone suggested construction roughly three thousand years old and an underground tunnel complex, though he could not determine its precise position.

#### Short
At the summit, they disarmed the trap and befriended Dimitaur, whose knowledge of Rai and the island helped them weigh their next move before resting overnight.

#### Intermediate
Wellby disarmed the Sibyl temple’s tripwire, and the party traced a booming divine voice to a dragonet beneath the floor. Showing their jade persuaded him to emerge as Dimitaur, Rai’s old companion. He spoke of Rai’s failed attempt to heal Isingue, the unknown whereabouts of Apollyon’s phylactery, and Ra'ghemdros’s claim to be Cha'mutte’s daughter. The dragon’s mysterious duty and Apollyon’s western tower suggested possible leverage, but no plan was settled. They agreed to consult the hobgoblin wizard in their mirror and rested with Dimitaur through the night.

#### Long
The summit held eight temples, a bell tower, and abandoned facilities for pilgrims. In the Temple of the Sibyl, Wellby found a tripwire linked to a swinging stone slab and used a rope to hold its tension while disabling it. Inside, a booming voice demanded proof that they were friends of Rai. Seeker sensed someone below the floor and Wellby found a peephole; after learning that the hidden speaker could initially only hear them, Kenzo brought the jade into view. An elderly dragonet emerged with a staff and wand and asked how they had acquired the fragments. Kenzo told him of his inherited piece and those recovered from the Elderwood, the merfolk, and Ursk. The dragonet introduced himself as Dimitaur, who had known Rai for centuries and lived on the island for thirty-five years.

Dimitaur recalled Rai leaving thirty-nine years earlier and warned that killing Apollyon required finding his phylactery, whose location he did not know. He had opposed Rai’s attempt to heal the great Seed of Chaos at Isingue and could offer no certainty about his present whereabouts. He would share information about Drankor but refused to return there, having fled the growing dread around the tomb. Of Ra'ghemdros, he knew chiefly rumors and her own boasts: she claimed Cha'mutte as her father and seemed charged with guarding something on the island. He knew no reason she could not leave it and asked that his association with Rai remain secret.

He also described dragonets traveling with the people of Hkar, an island moved by ancient magic, and Apollyon’s old western tower above a forge he had never explored. Cha'mutte’s enmity toward Apollyon suggested a possible basis for bargaining with the dragon. The companions wanted advice from the hobgoblin wizard in their mirror before choosing between negotiation, combat, or the western ruins. They settled in with Dimitaur for further conversation and an overnight rest, leaving the next expedition undecided.
