#### Short
Below Stormcaller Tower, the party finds an unfamiliar sky, a recent grave, and a vision of lightning and pain.

#### Intermediate
Resting below Stormcaller Tower, the party notices the missing Fox and Hunter constellation and wonders whether the storm has displaced them in time. Seeker uncovers a recently buried man whose clothing seems broadly contemporary, while Wellby receives a vision of a robed man, an approaching storm, and blinding pain.

#### Long
After a short rest beneath the blue granite outcrop below Stormcaller Tower, the party studies the twilight sky. The Fox and Hunter constellation is missing, and Delwath recognizes the stars of his childhood, before Beryl's ascension. Seeker's ley line visualizer still shows three lines converging beneath the tower; his recollection that time can run differently in the mirror realms offers a possibility, but no explanation is certain. Vola remains outside the storm with the horses.

Seeking a more tangible clue, Seeker excavates the shallow grave marked with the Sibyl's sign. The human corpse is only beginning to decompose, wrapped in an improvised shroud without obvious identifying possessions. Kenzo finds the clothing familiar enough to doubt that its wearer died centuries ago. Meanwhile, sparks gather on Wellby as he tries to speak with the tower. Instead of a name or an answer, he receives scattered images: a robed man on the platform, an approaching storm, lightning, and pain.

#### Short
Climbing to the tower's platform, they discover an iron sphere inscribed with binding magic, but its water guardian drives Delwath back after he ties a rope to it.

#### Intermediate
Kenzo climbs to the platform and lowers a rope, bringing the others to an iron sphere standing in an overflowing pool. A serpentine water guardian attacks anyone approaching it, frustrating attempts to inspect the Drankorian inscription from a distance. Coated in Oil of Slipperiness, Delwath reaches the sphere, recognizes powerful binding and control magic, and attaches a rope before the guardian strikes and grapples him. The oil lets him escape, leaving the sphere intact.

#### Long
Kenzo climbs the tower with the help of Seeker's floating rune, then lowers a rope for the others. Gusts sweep across the platform, where a hollow globe of intersecting iron bands stands in an overflowing pool. A serpentine water guardian lunges when Seeker approaches. Mage Hand draws its attacks, but Riswynn's attempts to clear a small patch with Shape Water cannot make the pool safe. Delwath detects a complex mixture of conjuration, enchantment, and illusion; a telescope helps reveal Drankorian writing, but the curved inscription cannot be read in full from a distance.

Kenzo calls out in Drankorian without receiving an answer. Delwath applies Oil of Slipperiness and uses Mage Hand as a distraction before entering the pool. Close examination reveals an incantation of binding and control, though what it binds remains unclear, and an iron rod securing the sphere to a submerged marble block. He ties a rope through the bands, but the guardian turns on him, striking and catching him in its coils. The oil allows him to slip free and retreat beyond its reach. Seeker offers healing; the guardian remains in the pool, and the sphere is still intact.

#### Short
Leaving the sphere intact, they question a tormented voice that longs to become a storm again, then angrily demands to be left alone.

#### Intermediate
Unsure what the sphere restrains, the party postpones destroying it and tries to learn more. Wellby reaches a tormented presence that remembers being a storm and knowing Sigga, a giant from halfling stories. Its tone turns angry, declaring itself the storm and demanding solitude; with sparks gathering on the walls, the party prepares to enter the tower.

#### Long
The party hesitates to destroy such powerful binding magic without knowing what it might release. Roughly an hour has passed during the climb and investigation, and the clearer stars now seem subtly misplaced as well as incomplete. Wellby again attempts to communicate, reaching a presence that asks why travelers disturb its torment. It remembers being a storm, wants to become one again, and says it knew Sigga, a giant familiar from halfling tales.

The apparent plea for help gives way to anger: the voice declares itself the storm and orders the intruders to leave it alone. The party cannot tell whether these changing messages come from one being or several. As sparks collect along the tower wall, they turn toward the entrance, leaving the sphere's destruction for later.

#### Short
Inside, empty silver settings and an eyeless giant's bust precede a misty stairway that opens onto an apparently sunlit mountain.

#### Intermediate
Inside the white marble tower, five columns hold empty silver settings, and a giant's stone bust is missing its eyes and whatever once filled the settings on its crown. Lightning follows the party indoors, so they climb together through the mist above. The stairs emerge onto a sunny, barren mountainside beneath an enormous hall, although the tower remains visible below them.

#### Long
Beyond the entrance lies a white marble chamber with five pointed columns, each topped by an empty silver setting. An adjoining room contains an eight-foot stone bust of a giant. Its eye sockets are empty, as are the settings on its five-pointed crown. Neither the carving nor the columns shows any extra magic beyond the tower's general aura.

With lightning following them inside, the party stays together and takes the stairs upward. The sparks fade as mist closes around the stairway. They emerge into apparent daylight on a barren mountainside, with an immense columned hall ahead and clouds obscuring the land below. Looking back, they can still see the tower's interior down the stairs.

#### Short
There, Wellby's exchange of stories with the blind giant Hralgar awakens memories of Eudomes, until chains appear and the mountain dissolves.

#### Intermediate
Wellby greets the blind giant approaching from the hall while the others hide, trading stories with someone who believes the present lies centuries in their past. Questions about the tower draw out confused memories of visiting the wizard Eudomes, but the giant cannot explain his missing eyes or find a way to leave. Glimpses of marble beneath the mountain suggest an illusion, and the party guides him toward the stairs. A chain appears around his wrist; naming himself Hralgar and blaming Eudomes, he struggles as the mountainside vanishes.

#### Long
The others hide among the rocks while Wellby greets the giant approaching from the hall. Roughly twenty-five feet tall and dressed in fine robes, he feels his way with a staff, both eyes missing. Kenzo's Soulsight finds him calm and contemplative. He offers stories and omens in exchange for tales of comparable worth, so Wellby recounts Raven's Hold and its history. Yet the giant does not recognize the Dunmari, remembers Sigga as young, and regards the Great War as impossible news from the future. He gives the year as 5031 by the true reckoning, which the party takes to mean the dwarven calendar.

Wellby's description of the tower and its captive storm unsettles him. He remembers traveling as wind to visit Eudomes, a wizard occupying a much older elven tower, and an invitation to exchange a valuable omen. He cannot recall the bargain or explain his missing eyes. Insisting that this mountainside is his home, he denies that any stairs lead back to the tower. As he struggles to concentrate, Riswynn glimpses a marble floor beneath the landscape.

Seeker finds the same three ley lines converging below them. The others emerge from hiding, and Wellby offers to guide the giant, whose staff passes through apparent rocks. Kneeling to find the stairs, he discovers a chain around his wrist and blames Eudomes for betraying him. He gives his name as Hralgar, while the party explains that their year is 5881 in dwarven reckoning, or 1748 in Drankorian reckoning. Hralgar pleads to remain at home, but chains pull back his arms and the mountain disappears.

#### Short
Back in the ruined tower, Hralgar recounts his imprisonment, and the party plans to search for a way to free him before his clarity fails.

#### Intermediate
The party stands beneath the tower's shattered roof, with Hralgar chained to the floor in tattered robes. He remembers Eudomes seeking omens of plague and ruin under Emperor Apollyon, then binding him, perhaps to protect the tower and the Occulta Ludum's knowledge. His account of someone taking his eyes leads the party to suspect that Chardonian explorers stole the statue's gems and awakened the storm. They plan to search the tower and library before attempting to break the sphere, while Hralgar urges them to hurry before he loses clarity.

#### Long
The sunlit mountain gives way to the tower's ruined upper chamber, its shattered roof open to the night sky. Hralgar lies chained to the floor in tattered robes, and his voice now matches the one Delwath heard earlier. Painfully lucid, he recalls that Eudomes had fallen out with the new emperor, Apollyon, and sought omens of a coming plague and ruin. The wizard's purpose remains uncertain: he might have hoped to prevent the disaster, profit from it, or escape it. Hralgar thinks his binding may have been intended to protect the tower and the knowledge of the Occulta Ludum.

Hralgar says someone took his eyes and woke him. The party suspects that treasure seekers removed gems from the statue and triggered the storm, perhaps carrying their finds to the Society of the Open Scroll in Chardon. The connection remains a theory. Hralgar says he would be free if his bonds were broken, and could break them himself if he could see. The party decides to search the tower and its library before attempting to destroy the sphere. Hralgar urges haste, unsure how long he can keep his mind clear; the search has yet to begin.
