#### Short
At a midday camp beside the dry Kharja, Kenzo connects his visions to the Order of the Awakened Soul, and the party sets out for Bhishma’s monastery.

#### Intermediate
Beside the dry Kharja, Kenzo asks about Rai and recounts the visions prompted by his jade fragment. He suspects the fragment and his mysterious guide may be connected to Rai, but the firmer lead is the instruction to seek the Order of the Awakened Soul, which Pava and Avaras identified with Bhishma’s order. The party decides to investigate the monastery, following the riverbed and planning to shelter the mounts beneath the cliffs.

#### Long
At their midday camp five miles south of the monastery, Wellby sketches the approach while Kenzo asks what his companions know of Rai. Delwath recalls an elusive mage of elven blood; Seeker remembers childhood stories featuring the foul-mouthed, purple-scaled Dimitaur. Kenzo describes the vision Candrosa helped him enter: a jade hand, a robed figure, and an instruction to seek the Order of the Awakened Soul. Pava and Avaras have since identified that order with Bhishma’s monastery. He also recounts the dragon and its shadow seen through the mirror, wondering whether the jade is a fragment of Rai and whether Rai still protects the world. Neither the fragment’s origin nor the dream figure’s identity is established. The party discusses the order’s masters, who left thirty years ago and never returned, but knows neither their purpose nor whether their disappearance connects to the recent troubles. With the tower and Hall of Stories to investigate, they choose to approach during the quiet heat of the day, follow the dry river, and leave their mounts beneath the cliffs. Wellby entrusts Baxter with watching the horses.

#### Short
There, they discover two adult manticores and two young in the ruined dining hall, along with sacred doors that invite kneeling prayer.

#### Intermediate
After leaving the mounts beneath the cliffs, they work around to the ruined buildings. A lizard warns Wellby that larger creatures emerge later in the day, and he soon hears heavy sleeping and unsettling laughter. Delwath makes himself and Kenzo invisible for a closer look: two adults sleep while two young manticores play cruel games nearby. They also find doors depicting Bhishma and instructing visitors to kneel and pray, but first must decide what to do about the creatures threatening their horses.

#### Long
The hot walk takes about ninety minutes, bringing them to the cliffs around two o’clock. Rather than climb the crumbling sandstone, they follow the river’s bend past dry water pipes and abandoned terraces, reaching the monastery buildings around half past two. A lizard tells Wellby that the larger creatures come out when the sun is lower. Scouting ahead, he hears deep, lionlike sleeping noises and laughter like an evil child’s. Delwath turns himself and Kenzo invisible, then approaches through the kitchen while Wellby and Kenzo peer through a hole in the ruined hall. Inside, two adult manticores sleep as their young torment one another. A kick of gravel briefly draws the young creatures’ attention to the apparently empty doorway. The scouts also discover tall stone doors depicting Bhishma kneeling and deified, with an inscription inviting those who seek her grace to kneel and offer prayers. They withdraw to their companions, worried that the manticores will hunt the mounts once the day cools.

#### Short
To protect the horses, they lure the young away with Kenzo’s driftglobe, ambush the adults, and kill all four manticores.

#### Intermediate
Concern for the horses wins out over bypassing the lair. Kenzo’s driftglobe draws the young away, letting him and Delwath approach the sleeping adults invisibly. Delwath places Seeker’s metal net over one adult, which Seeker kills with Heat Metal and Shatter. The other tries to escape through the roof, but Kenzo and Delwath pursue it and Wellby’s arrow finishes it. The returning young are quickly killed; the nest holds four gnoll skeletons but no treasure.

#### Long
Delwath initially favors pressing on to the tower, but the party decides to remove the threat to the horses. After considering blocked exits and illusions, they settle on a diversion: Kenzo leaves his driftglobe beyond the kitchen doorway, drawing the young away so he and Delwath can approach the adults unseen. Delwath carries Seeker’s metal net, ready to turn it into a weapon with Heat Metal. The opening blows are uneven, but the net catches over one adult’s wing, and Seeker’s thunderous Shatter and heated metal kill it. The other manticore scrambles toward the opening in the roof. Kenzo dashes over the rubble in pursuit, Delwath strikes it with Booming Blade, and Wellby puts an arrow through its eye before it escapes. The young return to the hall and are dispatched in another two rounds. While the others keep watch, Delwath searches the nest: there is no treasure, but four gnoll skeletons lie among the animal bones.

#### Short
The noise draws six of Grash’s orcs; five are killed, and their captured captain reveals that Grash needs watchers to observe the monastery.

#### Intermediate
Six orcs come from the bathhouse to investigate the battle. With Kenzo enlarged and Delwath protected by Armor of Agathys, the party kills five before Seeker persuades the fleeing captain to surrender with Suggestion. The prisoner says they feed the manticores and watch for visitors, with relief due the next afternoon; ghosts keep them out of the tower. Despite his boasts about Grash, he admits that his master cannot see the monastery without watchers. They leave him bound in a dry bathhouse pool with food and water.

#### Long
The thunder draws six orcs from the bathhouse. Seeker doubles Kenzo’s size, and Delwath casts Armor of Agathys as the party prepares to ambush the newcomers. The orcs spot Delwath, but Wellby’s arrows and Kenzo’s enlarged quarterstaff strike from hiding. One attacker dies from the cold of Delwath’s protective magic. When the orc spellcaster commands Kenzo to grovel, Kenzo resists, and Wellby answers with an arrow and a command of his own: “Die.” The captain tries to flee; Seeker drops the enlargement spell to cast Suggestion, urging him to surrender and answer their questions. Kenzo shrinks as the captain lays down his weapons. The prisoner explains that his detail watches visitors and feeds the manticores, expecting relief tomorrow afternoon. He says ghosts prevent them from entering the tower and refuse to obey Grash. He describes Grash as a giant cloaked in shadow, rejects Thark’s worship, and insists their fallen comrades only appear dead. His boasts falter when pressed about Grash’s sight: Grash cannot see this place and needs the watchers. Once the spell ends, he struggles against his bonds. The party debates killing him, then confines him alive in a dry bathhouse pool, leaving food and water and barring the doors.

#### Short
Kenzo opens Bhishma’s doors with prayer, and beyond the preparation rooms they enter an immersive vision of her flight from home.

#### Intermediate
With the prisoner secured, Kenzo kneels before Bhishma’s doors and prays; they glow and open. Inside, the party takes six vials of holy water from rooms arranged for initiation. Beyond them, a dark corridor reveals a luminous mural of Bhishma leading her people away from home, conveying wind and fear as well as images. Following the story means entering the mysteries, a prospect that troubles Wellby and Delwath.

#### Long
Turning back to the sacred doors, Kenzo kneels and offers a prayer to Bhishma. A glow surrounds the doors as they open onto rooms containing moldering robes, an empty font, and vials of holy water. Six vials remain intact; four are cracked and empty. The party takes the six, and Kenzo recognizes preparations resembling those used in initiation. Farther inside, faded inscriptions give way to a corridor whose darkness defeats even darkvision. A luminous mural emerges: Bhishma leads her people away from their home. The scene carries sensations and emotions with it, letting the watchers feel the wind and the fear of departure. They can follow the story or turn back. Wellby and Delwath hesitate, uneasy about entering another faith’s mysteries.

### recap-006 | The Mysteries of Bhishma

#### Short
Wellby and Delwath turn back while Kenzo and Seeker continue; Kenzo alone accepts death and returns as Bhishma’s disciple, receiving new sight and a message to restore Shakun’s stolen connection to Karawa.

#### Intermediate
Wellby turns back, with Delwath accompanying him, while Kenzo and Seeker experience Bhishma’s memories of flight, famine, and Jeevali’s sacrifice to heal the land. Offered the chance to give their own lives for Jeevali, Seeker stops short, but Kenzo dies; Seeker’s healing cannot revive him. Kenzo then gasps back to life, crediting Bhishma and Laka. He sees painted stories, birds, flowers familiar from his dreams, and a fountain where Seeker sees bare walls and earth. When Kenzo prays about Shakun, they both receive an answer: she is with Bhishma but cannot reach Karawa because something connecting her to the world has been taken. A final image shows Kenzo a beating stone heart, which he calls the heart of Shakun.

#### Long
Wellby decides to turn back, uncomfortable intruding on another faith’s mysteries, and Delwath goes with him. Kenzo and Seeker continue into Bhishma’s memories. They experience her flight through hostile mountains as her brother fights hobgoblins, the division of her people beside a river, and their westward search for a home amid ash, darkness, and hunger. Jeevali’s sacrifice brings healing rain. The people rejoice and praise Bhishma, but she feels grief and shame at the goddess’s death. As her brother pursues conquest at Aagir’s command, Bhishma turns to fasting and prayer, offering her own life for Jeevali’s return. The mysteries ask Kenzo and Seeker whether they will give their lives for Jeevali, offering repeated chances to retreat. Both continue at first, but Seeker forces his eyes open as death approaches. In a bare corridor, he finds Kenzo without breath or heartbeat. Healing Word does nothing.

Kenzo finally gasps back to life, weak and barely standing. He says that he died and that Bhishma and Laka brought him back. Where Seeker sees gray walls and empty earth, Kenzo now sees painted stories of Bhishma and the gods, birds, flowers, and a flowing fountain. Its water feels cool and wet but cannot quench thirst. The flowers are vividly familiar from his dreams, although he has never seen them before. They wonder whether this ancient sanctuary stands where Bhishma made her sacrifice.

Kneeling at the fountain, Kenzo asks what happened to Shakun and how they can help. Over several minutes, Seeker watches the wind arrange sand into writing, while Kenzo sees droplets become crystalline butterflies. Both can read Bhishma’s answer: Shakun is with her, as always, but cannot reach Karawa; something that helps her reach the world has been taken and must be found and returned. As the message ends, Kenzo sees a beating stone heart and names it the heart of Shakun. Seeker bows in thanks, and the sanctuary’s colors dim with the effort of the communication.
