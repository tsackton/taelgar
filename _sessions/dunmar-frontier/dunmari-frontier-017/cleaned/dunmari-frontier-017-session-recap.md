#### Short
Over breakfast outside the storm, Seeker, Delwath, Kenzo, and Wellby share memories of family, childhood, and the homes they have left behind.

#### Intermediate
Over breakfast outside the storm, Seeker recalls his brother Frankar’s secret divination machine and disappearance. Delwath remembers baking sisila-fruit cakes for his elders and fears what has become of his homeland. Kenzo describes poverty and magical pollution in Chardon, alongside the visions that still draw him north. Wellby remembers his great-great-grandfather’s stories and a childhood spent traveling the Green Sea; for him, the road itself is home.

#### Long
Over breakfast outside the storm, the companions share childhood memories. Seeker tells of his younger brother Frankar, called Frank, a gifted rune-worker who secretly built a machine to divine the future through the deaths of white salamanders. After a violent storm, Seeker found the machine shattered on the keep’s summit, a burned salamander among its remains, and no sign of Frankar. Roughly thirty years later, he still does not know his brother’s fate.

Delwath remembers making cakes from rare sisila fruit for his elders while darkness spread beyond his homeland; returning might mean discovering that everything familiar has changed or been destroyed. Kenzo recalls Chardon’s poverty, the pollution of chalyte, and the gulf between those who benefit from magic and those who suffer its consequences. His childhood visions still exert an unwelcome pull toward the city. Wellby’s memories follow his family’s travels around the Green Sea, through Sembara, Tollen, and Cymea. His great-great-grandfather taught him that all places and times were one, and Wellby eventually left the family’s familiar circuit to find his own road.

#### Short
They turn to Hralgar, Agata, and the tower letters, finding possible connections but no answers, then discover that the mirror is recharging and the purple goggles sharpen nearby details.

#### Intermediate
Seeker’s journal brings them back to Hralgar, the poisoned water, Agata Dustmother, and the missing eyes. They suspect connections to Delwath’s return and the search for magical objects, but cannot establish them or trust the storm’s account of Agata’s vengeance. The tower letters offer no fresh answers. Delwath finds the mirror slowly recharging, while Seeker and Kenzo test purple goggles that reveal minute details nearby. A damaged nameplate on the mirror suggests Eudomes; the goggles’ full abilities remain unclear.

#### Long
Seeker opens his journal to compare these memories with their recent discoveries. Hralgar explains the storm, a summoned demon was associated with the poisoned water, and Vola described Agata Dustmother as an unnaturally old human woman. The claim that Agata seeks vengeance came from the storm itself, whose account they hesitate to trust. Delwath recalls escaping his imprisonment by something like cutting through it with shadow. His return, Hralgar’s awakening, the missing eyes, and Chardonian seekers of magical objects might be connected, but the companions cannot yet say how.

They revisit the tower letters, Eudomes, and the Occulta Ludum, discussing the Great Library’s preservation of knowledge carried west after Drankor’s fall. Exhaustion defeats further attempts to translate or remember useful details. Delwath examines the mirror and sees it slowly drawing in magical energy, even away from a strong source. Seeker and Kenzo try the purple goggles: distant scenery blurs, while nearby objects become exceptionally clear. The silver mirror’s intricate frame and damaged wooden nameplate reveal surviving letters that suggest Eudomes, and a horn carved with a running horse shows fine workmanship and old wear. The goggles help them examine magic, but their full power remains unknown. With three heavily laden packhorses, the party prepares to walk south toward Karawa and make an early camp.

#### Short
On the road south they kill an emaciated gnoll leader and five skeletal followers, then recover from exhaustion during the journey to a final camp north of Karawa.

#### Intermediate
Only a few miles south, an emaciated gnoll leader and five skeletal followers force the exhausted party to fight. Wellby’s arrows and Delwath’s magic weaken them; Seeker’s Shatter destroys two, and Kenzo kills the leader before the companions finish the survivors. The bodies seem to have consumed their own flesh, yet offer no explanation for their animation. After camping away from the dead, the party resumes travel on April 17. By April 23 they are half a day north of Karawa, rested, with Seeker mapping ley lines along the route.

#### Long
After lunch and only a few miles of walking, the party spots six gnolls moving west: an emaciated, armored leader with a polearm and five skeletal or nearly skeletal followers. Too exhausted to outrun them, the companions prepare ranged attacks while Wellby sends his axe beak, Taster, clear of the fight. Their opening shots mostly miss, but Wellby wounds the leader, Delwath brings Eldritch Blast and Hex to bear, and Seeker’s Shatter destroys two followers. Kenzo kills the leader with his Fist of Planar Power, provoking a furious response in which one survivor bites him. Delwath’s Green-Flame Blade, Seeker’s Vicious Mockery, Wellby’s bow, and Kenzo’s final blows bring down the remaining three.

The dead offer little treasure. The goggles reveal an ordinary damaged weapon, while the gnolls’ bodies suggest that they have eaten their own flesh and somehow remained animate. Their westward course might lead from Raven’s Hold, but the party cannot establish their origin. The companions move away from the corpses and camp for the night.

They set out again on April 17, gradually recovering from their exhaustion as the grassland gives way to dry scrub and rocky desert. On April 23 they camp beside the old trade road, about half a day north of Karawa. Seeker has mapped the ley lines along the journey: one briefly follows the road before veering away, but none visibly leads toward the known node beneath the Red Mesa.

#### Short
That night, the recharged mirror shows the ivory scroll’s creation in a dark workshop, prompting questions about necromancy and planar magic without identifying its maker or purpose.

#### Intermediate
At the roadside camp, Seeker uses the now-understood goggles to identify the Mirror of the Past. Its images are true, but their order and context can mislead. Delwath holds the recovered ivory cap before it and asks about the scroll’s creation and last use. They see ivory being carved, writing burned into skin, and a horned, long-tailed creature lifting the finished object. The vision feels old but yields no identity or location. Delwath’s knowledge of necromancy and Kenzo’s understanding of the planes suggest possibilities without settling what happened.

#### Long
That night, another attempt to interpret the Occulta Ludum and recall the mirror’s earlier viewpoint produces little. Seeker has, however, worked out the Goggles of Object Reading during the journey: once attuned, they can cast Identify once a day. He uses them on the recharged mirror and learns that it reveals true images from the past of something reflected in it, though chronology and missing context can make those images misleading.

Delwath holds up the ivory cap recovered beside the ashes and summoning circle at Raven’s Hold, asking how the scroll was first made and last used. A dark workshop appears in the glass, with a forge, tools, and shadows moving rhythmically. Ivory is carved into pieces of a case while letters burn into skin resembling human or dwarven flesh. A black-gloved hand lifts the assembled scroll, and a creature with two horns and a long, barbed tail emerges before the image fades. It differs from the creature they fought. The vision feels old, but neither its place nor its maker is identified, and no clear account of the scroll’s last use follows.

Delwath associates flesh, fire, and blood with necromancy and magic binding the material world to realms such as the Abyss and Nine Hells. Kenzo explains his limited reach toward the Plane of Consciousness, while Seeker wonders whether direct manipulation of planar power could replace rune-work. Stories of bargains, summoned servants, and corruption crossing planar boundaries offer explanations to consider, but none establishes the scroll’s purpose or the source of the gnoll threat.

#### Short
The next day they reach a devastated Karawa, where Candrosa explains that Tharandros’s centaurs relieved the temple siege and thanks them for sending help.

#### Intermediate
On April 24 they walk into Karawa past ruined buildings, damaged palms, a cracked oasis basin, and nine large graves. Havdar escorts them to Candrosa and Alesh at the surviving temple. Candrosa describes more than fifty gnolls attacking after the party’s departure, with roughly thirty remaining to besiege the defenders. Nearly forty centaurs broke the siege several days later and stayed to help. Candrosa had not realized the companions sent them, and thanks them when their part in the rescue becomes clear.

#### Long
The following morning they cover the remaining ten to fifteen miles to Karawa and find roughly a third of the town in ruins. Palms have fallen or burned, the oasis basin is cracked, and nine large graves lie nearby. Havdar arrives with four centaurs and another Dunmari rider to escort them in. The temple’s fortifications have held, but Ikram’s and Delwath’s apartment are destroyed. Havdar leaves to check the patrols and find Tharandros while the companions join an exhausted, shaking Candrosa and a travel-worn Alesh in a temple outbuilding.

Candrosa had feared them dead. More than fifty gnolls attacked a few days after their departure; perhaps half continued west, leaving roughly thirty to besiege the temple. Three or four days later, nearly forty centaurs broke the siege. That relief came eight or nine days ago, and the centaurs have stayed to protect the survivors and help them recover. Candrosa knows that Tharandros was promised knowledge of dreams, but had heard only of a chance encounter and warnings of danger to the north. He had not understood that the companions persuaded the centaurs to come. Once they explain, he thanks them, while Alesh presses to exchange news of the south.

#### Short
Alesh reports gnolls heading into the Garamjala, a strange raven, an empty desert house, and orc scouts; Candrosa describes weakening defenses and suggests the Old Woman of the Dusts as a possible lead on Agata.

#### Intermediate
Alesh describes ruined Bas Udda, unsettling Kharsan, and red-mist nightmares beside the Kharja. Her scouts followed fifteen or twenty gnolls southwest into the Garamjala, turning back for lack of supplies; an unusual raven shadowed the group. Candrosa explains that Shakun’s misleading paths and illusions have weakened since early in the year. Alesh also found a recently tended but empty desert house and saw two orc scouts. Asked about Agata, Candrosa offers stories of the Old Woman of the Dusts, whose magic demands disturbing prices, but cannot confirm they are the same woman.

#### Long
Alesh’s route took her through ruined Bas Udda and past Kharsan, quiet by day but oppressive enough to feel haunted. Camping five miles south beside the dry Kharja brought dreams of choking red mist and dead bodies, which she tentatively connects to the old Red Death. After watching the area, her scouts followed fifteen or twenty gnolls, roughly half skeletal, southwest toward the Garamjala. Without supplies for a desert pursuit, they turned back. A large raven followed the gnolls for hours, vanished, and returned from the northeast before heading southwest again; Alesh cannot explain its movements. Seeker uses an animated map to compare these reports with the other attacks, but no common origin emerges.

Candrosa sketches the boundary once protected by Shakun’s confusing paths and illusions. Its strength began declining gradually early in the year, and Shakun stopped telling him some things. He will explain the failing defenses, but not reveal every secret of his order.

Alesh also found a sandstone house at the desert’s edge, with a garden, water, goats, and fed chickens, but no occupant. It looked recently tended; she took nothing and left a chest unopened. She suspects magic sustains the water. Two orc scouts seen near a canyon seemed unusually organized to her, and she avoided following them closely.

Kenzo asks about Agata Dustmother. Candrosa does not recognize the name, but suggests the Old Woman of the Dusts, said to have lived about a day south of Bas Udda for generations. He has never met her. In the stories, desperate people seek her magic and pay a dear price: a parent might surrender a thumb to save a child, then dream of that severed thumb being used elsewhere. She could be Agata, but neither that identity nor any connection to the empty house is established.
