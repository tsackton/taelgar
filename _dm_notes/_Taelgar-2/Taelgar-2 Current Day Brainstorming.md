# Dark Taelgar in the Current Day

This note captures the current-day tone and broad structure of dark Taelgar. It is not yet a history of how the world reached this state, and the exact technology available remains provisional.

See also: [[Taelgar-2 Species After the Godfall]], [[Taelgar-2 Magic]], [[Taelgar-2 Technology]], and [[Taelgar-2 Concept Art]]

## Core Vibe

Dark Taelgar is a broken world rather than a world recovering from an old catastrophe. The gods are dead or unreachable, divine magic has failed, several peoples are extinct or departing, old systems of abundance have collapsed, and large parts of the world are difficult to cross or even understand.

The setting combines different kinds of darkness in different regions:

- In the west, the primary influence is *Arcane*: a spectacular city of magical and industrial progress built on class oppression, refugees, extractive colonies, and collaboration with hobgoblin power.
- In eastern Dunmar and the Plaguelands, the primary influence is *Dark Sun*: isolated fortress-cities, scarce water and safety, ruined landscapes, and hobgoblin sorcerer-kings or magically enhanced warlords.
- The Green Sea can develop its own distinct interpretation of dark Taelgar. It does not need to resemble either Chardon or eastern Dunmar and can remain undefined for now. One possibility here is a more traditional pirate/steampunk setting, especially in the far East where the chaos of the west might seem remote and unimportant. 

The Godfall is the shared catastrophe, but the idea is that it allows a variety of darker themes to co-exist across "Dark Taelgar". All are unified by a few principles of magic, outlined in: [[Taelgar-2 Magic]]

There is also a general degree of shared technology, focused on a particular magi-tech / steampunk vibe, but technological progress can vary across the world. See more in [[Taelgar-2 Technology]].

Together these create a shared system of [[Taelgar-2 Mechanics|playable classes and species]] that should be unified across the setting. 

## A World of Enclaves

The world is not uniformly industrial or technologically advanced. It consists of fortified enclaves of power connected by a small number of vulnerable routes.

Chardon, major hobgoblin capitals, a handful of ports, mining settlements, and fortress-cities may possess advanced arcane technology. Between them lie abandoned roads, poisoned rivers, unstable wilderness, refugee settlements, work camps, hostile borders, and ruins nobody can maintain.

A useful image for political and economic maps is a set of dots connected by thin lines. States control cities, ports, canals, mines, stretches of river, and defended transportation corridors. They need not not meaningfully control all the land between them (though in some cases they might). 

Advanced transportation allows powerful institutions to extract wealth from distant enclaves without making the territory between them safe or prosperous. Technology therefore reinforces fragmentation rather than overcoming it.

## The West and the Chardonian Order

In the west, Chardonian arcane elites have made lasting deals with hobgoblin powers in order to survive, prosper, and build their city of wonders.

Chardon provides arcane knowledge, finance, manufactured goods, refined chalyte, weapons, ships, and access to its ports. Hobgoblin states provide disciplined armies, grain, raw materials, captive labor, and control of large parts of the hinterland. Their relationship is not necessarily peaceful or equal, but it is structural. Chardon is not simply the heroic human realm resisting the hobgoblins. Its wealth and security depend upon collaboration with them.

The Chardonian elite publicly celebrates mortal ingenuity: when the gods failed, mortals built their own miracles. That story obscures who performs the labor, who is displaced from the land, and what bargains were required to make those miracles possible.

The Chardonian Empire is less a continuous territorial state than a network of protected ports, company compounds, mines, canals, river corridors, military bases, and subject cities. Beyond its walls and defended routes, Imperial authority may become thin very quickly.

### Chardon

Chardon is a siege city that became an empire without losing its siege mentality.

High Chardon has clean water, chalyte lights, impossible towers, airships, academies, gardens, safe magical conveniences, and carefully controlled public spaces. Its residents understand the city as the last and greatest center of civilization.

Low Chardon has refugee districts, chaos-glass refineries, poisoned canals, dwarf foundries, goblin warrens, halfling dock communities, orc neighborhoods, military checkpoints, and workers physically damaged by unstable magic and industrial labor.

Citizenship, food, clean water, magical care, education, safe housing, and freedom of movement are privileges controlled through charters, employers, guilds, and political patronage. Chardon's southern provinces and subject ports exist largely to keep the city supplied.

Hobgoblins are visibly present inside Chardon as diplomats, military advisers, traders, industrial representatives, prisoners, spies, and members of treaty delegations. The hobgoblin powers are not distant monsters. They are governments with which Chardon negotiates, trades, and collaborates between wars and crises.

## Hobgoblin Power

Hobgoblins are one of the few peoples whose bodies, magical traditions, political institutions, and religion did not collapse with the Godfall. Their disciplined armies and administrative systems allowed several legion-states to become permanent powers.

These states need not form a single empire or share identical politics. Some may be austere military dictatorships; others may be personal realms ruled by magically empowered commanders; others may operate as networks of camps, plantations, tribute settlements, and fortified cities.

Hobgoblin rule is brutally ordered. Movement can require permission, labor can be assigned, and conquered populations can be taxed, confined to work camps, or enslaved. Hobgoblins generally provide enough food and security to preserve useful populations, but do not recognize those populations as equals.

Hobgoblins need not worship [[Cha'mutte]]. They may venerate him as First Marshal, Liberator, or Founder while considering worship itself a weakness of soul-bearing peoples. To them, the Godfall can be proof that they were the only people made honestly for the world as it truly is.

## Eastern Dunmar and the Plaguelands

Eastern Dunmar and the Plaguelands are the most *Dark Sun*-like part of the setting.

Rumors tell of isolated settlements gathered around wells, surviving rivers, old temples, and fortified ruins. Hobgoblin sorcerer-kings or magically enhanced warlords rule some of these places, controlling water, food, protection, and access to chaos glass. Other areas may be held by free-orc confederacies, Dunmari remnants, monsters, or powers that have no interest in the western world.

The exact rulers, cities, and supernatural powers of this region are not yet defined. In Chardon and the west, this is the dangerous wilds. From Chardon and its southern provinces, these lands are only known through refugees, fragmentary merchant stories, propaganda, military reports, and rumors of impossible tyrants.

The setting should preserve room for eastern Dunmar and the Plaguelands to become a collection of sorcerer-king domains rather than resolving them into one realm.

## The Green Sea

The Green Sea is deliberately undefined in the current brainstorming.

The destruction of the passes through the [[Sentinel Range]], the collapse of old trade networks, dangerous seas, and the difficulty of long-distance travel allow the Green Sea to develop in relative isolation. It can have its own political order, technological path, and interpretation of the Godfall.

Stories reaching Chardon should be inconsistent and difficult to verify. The Green Sea exists as another world beyond the mountains until a future campaign needs to define it.

## Chaos Glass and Chalyte

The exact technology of dark Taelgar remains to be settled, but the conceptual distinction between its two important magical materials is clear.

**Chaos glass** is common, energetic, toxic, and unstable. It forms in Godfall scars, damaged ley lines, battlefields, old divine sites, and other places touched by Cha'mutte's death. Ground into powder or incorporated into crude engines, it acts as a gunpowder analog and an industrial fuel. It can power firearms, cannon, furnaces, demolition charges, and dangerous improvised devices. Raw chaos glass is available to armies, workers, criminals, rebels, and poor communities, but its use damages bodies, minds, machines, and landscapes.

**Chalyte** provides control, containment, enchantment, and precision. It can stabilize chaos glass and make otherwise dangerous effects reliable. Refined chalyte is rare, expensive, and controlled by governments, chartered houses, military organizations, and elite institutions. Note that chalyte, like in Taelgar Prime, still requires refining to be safe, and unrefined chalyte combined with chaos glass is probably the most dangerous combination of all. 

There are possibly other kinds of "extraplanar glass", including the potential for crystallized "divine energy" that might allow for rare relics. 

## Travel and Transportation

Long-distance travel is harsh, dangerous, and politically restricted despite the existence of advanced vehicles.

### Skyships

A chalyte skyship is typically a flying naval vessel. It requires a large and delicate chalyte lift system, chaos glass for propulsion or emergency power, specialized engineers, highly trained navigators, constant maintenance, and access to one of a small number of equipped skyports.

Skyships carry officers, diplomats, spies, refined chalyte, military supplies, messages, elite passengers, and small quantities of valuable cargo. They cannot economically move grain, refugees, ordinary travelers, or entire armies.

Only Chardon, major charter houses, and the strongest hobgoblin states possess them. Seeing a skyship over a provincial town is a political event.

Skyships travel primarily along established skyways between prepared ports. Their chalyte systems gradually lose stability, while chaos-glass engines damage themselves with use. Charts record reliable winds, safe altitudes, chaos storms, military borders, and possible emergency landing sites. Leaving established routes is possible but dangerous.

Crossing the Sentinels or flying over the Plaguelands may be nearly suicidal because of violent weather, distorted magic, hostile rulers, and the absence of repair facilities. This helps preserve the isolation of the Green Sea and eastern Dunmar.

### Local Industrial Infrastructure

Factories, pumps, trams, mills, and railways exist where technicians, fuel, security, and refined chalyte are concentrated.

Chardon can have steam-powered factories, elevated freight lines, passenger trams, chalyte waterworks, and short industrial railways connecting docks, refineries, and nearby settlements. A mining province might have a heavily defended railway from a mine to a river port while villages twenty miles away remain accessible only by foot or pack animal.

There is no implication that a continent-wide rail system exists. Tracks outside defended territory are easily sabotaged, bridges cannot be maintained, fuel and clean water are scarce, and every ruler along the route demands control or tribute. Furthermore steam engines require either dangerous chaos glass (prone to exploding) or expensive chalyte, and so are not a mass-market technology. 

See more in [[Taelgar-2 Technology]].

### Maritime Trade

Ships, barges, and river convoys remain the only economical way to move food, timber, ore, refugees, and other bulk cargo. These operate along a limited number of protected routes.

The [[Old Chardon Canal]] is therefore more important, not less. Skyships can bypass it, but grain fleets and ore convoys cannot. Control of the canal, the Apporian ports, and the waters around Illoria is central to Chardonian power.

### Communication

Messages can move much faster than people. Chardonian and hobgoblin elites may communicate through paired message stones, chalyte devices, or tightly controlled arcane networks.

This does not make the world socially connected. A governor in Illoria may receive instructions from Chardon within minutes while an ordinary person cannot leave the island without bribing a ship captain. Governments communicate with one another more effectively than ordinary communities do.

## Why Ordinary Travel Is Dangerous

Several overlapping pressures keep most people close to home:

- **Ecological collapse:** abandoned lizardfolk wetlands, unraveling elven forests, dead dwarven roads, poisoned rivers, and expanding deserts.
- **Lost infrastructure:** bridges, wayhouses, roads, canals, and safe harbors disappeared or became unreliable during the collapse of the old world.
- **Political borders:** hobgoblin states tightly regulate movement, Chardonian provinces require papers, sorcerer-kings exact tribute, and independent settlements distrust strangers.
- **Chaos weather:** red storms, unstable magic, strange winds, and regions contaminated by raw chaos glass.
- **The absence of divine aid:** reliable magical healing, food creation, disease control, resurrection, and protection from weather are gone or extraordinarily rare.
- **Predatory technology:** skyships are more likely to carry soldiers, tax collectors, or company agents than to rescue travelers.

Halfling [[Taelgar-2 Species After the Godfall#Halflings: The Quiet|Hearth Compacts]] may be among the few groups that preserve reliable routes between enclaves. Their caravans and ships make travel possible, but never easy.

## Religion and the Memory of Cha'mutte

The meaning of [[Cha'mutte]] remains fiercely contested.

To some orcs and political radicals, he is the Liberator who destroyed the beings that held whole species in bondage. To dwarven refugees, the last lizardfolk chroniclers, religious communities, and many ordinary humans, he is the Godkiller who murdered the world. Hobgoblins may honor him as the greatest commander in history without treating him as divine.

Chardon's elite condemn his atrocities while building their power from chaos glass, dead gods, refugee labor, and treaties with his armies' successors. The contradiction is central to the setting and should not have one accepted answer.

## Current-Day Tone

Wonder and scarcity exist beside one another.

A traveler can spend weeks crossing ruined marshes, bribing border guards, hiding from work-camp patrols, and searching for drinkable water. An Imperial skyship then passes overhead in an hour, carrying a Chardonian financier to dinner with a hobgoblin marshal.

The technology does not contradict the fractured world. It is one of the clearest signs of who benefits from that fracture.
