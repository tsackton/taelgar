# Species After the Godfall

Working notes for the reusable dark Taelgar setting. The Godfall is the moment when [[Cha'mutte]] killed the [[Embodied Gods]], broke the mechanism through which the incorporeal gods granted miracles, and left the peoples of Taelgar to face the consequences.

## Lizardfolk: The Last Cycle

[[Lizardfolk]] do not simply acquire human lifespans going forward. Their bodies suddenly begin reckoning the years that the magic of the [[Tharzen Anzinakoa]] had held back.

Elders age to death within days or months. Mature adults decline over the following years. The youngest survive for several decades, but lizardfolk reproduction and community life were shaped around long lives, low population turnover, and the guidance of elders and ancestors. They cannot adapt quickly enough. Whether reproduction itself also fails, or the last generations simply cannot reproduce quickly enough to reverse the collapse, remains undecided; the result is the same. Within a few decades, the lizardfolk are effectively extinct.

The last lizardfolk communities spend those decades watching their entire species grow old together. They leave behind extraordinarily productive wetlands whose maintenance nobody else fully understands. Once abandoned, some become fever marshes, salt flats, choking reed seas, or drained plantations.

## Elves: The Departure

No new [[Elves|elf]] is ever born after the Godfall. Existing elves remain ageless, but without [[Aldanor]] and [[Elmerca]] the world feels increasingly barren and alien to them.

Some fight hopeless wars. Some guard empty forests. Most gradually fade or depart Taelgar by whatever paths remain open to them. By two centuries after the Godfall, elves belong to memory. There may be rumors of a Last Elf somewhere, and maybe some elves linger hidden in Orenlas still, but these are just rumors and little is known. 

## Dwarves: The Great Famine

The underground halls of the [[Dwarves]] lose their magical light, water, ventilation, heat, and agriculture almost simultaneously. The [[Heart of the Mountain]] can no longer be found. No young dwarf receives a divine name or thuhr.

Older dwarves also suffer a sudden reduction in lifespan. A dwarf of one hundred and eighty years may age to death within months. Whole councils of elders disappear just when their kingdoms most need leadership.

Massive famines follow. Great numbers of dwarves flee the mountains, and many never reach the surface. The survivors become one of the largest refugee populations of the post-Godfall world and fracture between:

- Traditionalists preserving clan, inherited names, and the memory of the dead thuhr.
- "Nameless" dwarves who choose their own names and build new guilds, unions, and families.
- Refugee industrialists who sell dwarven knowledge to Chardon or to hobgoblin rulers.
- Bitter revanchists who dream of reclaiming the mountain halls despite knowing that the underground can no longer sustain them.

Dwarves become central to the industrial world that follows, but largely as a displaced people whose accumulated technical knowledge is exploited by others.

## Stoneborn: The Stone Silence

Every [[Stoneborn|stoneborn]] becomes literal stone when [[Entamba]] dies.

Mountain villages become fields of statues: people frozen at work, in prayer, embracing children, or looking toward the distant signs of the Godfall. Dwarves regard these places as sacred necropolises. Chardonian and hobgoblin prospectors sometimes regard them as convenient sources of worked stone, lost treasure, or valuable minerals.

The stoneborn are extinct from the moment of the Godfall.

## Halflings: The Quiet

[[Halflings]] survive biologically, but their shared ancestral mind and silent speech vanish instantly.

This is devastating because the ancestral mind was not merely a magical ability. It preserved history, connected scattered families, authenticated obligations, enforced reputation, and made every halfling part of one extended family. Halflings who had always been able to recognize one another as kin suddenly meet as strangers.

The Quiet produces several new halfling cultures:

- Some halflings replace shared memory with written contracts, account books, genealogies, and private ownership. Some become ruthless merchant dynasties, banks, and Chardonian financiers.
- Some halflings try to deliberately recreate halfling kinship through vows, communal property, mutual aid, and adopted family. These become caravans, dockside communities, smugglers, labor organizations, and extended chosen families.
- Some halflings try to preserve as many old songs and stories as possible, knowing they can no longer tell which apparent memories are authentic and which have changed in the telling.
- Some halflings use chaos glass, drugs, or experimental magic to try to hear the ancestral mind again. Sometimes they hear something. It is not clear that it is their ancestors.

Halflings are particularly central to the divided society of Chardon: financiers and chartered merchants above, mutual-aid networks and criminal families below, all descended from the same cultural catastrophe.

## Orcs: The Unbinding

Every [[Orcs|orc]] is freed from [[Thark]] at once.

Some regard [[Cha'mutte]] as the Liberator and build a religion or political identity around the person who gave them the first genuine choice their species ever possessed. Others believe that accepting this gift honors a genocide.  They call themselves free but refuse to praise the murderer who freed them.

Major strands of post-Godfall orc culture might include:

- Liberator cults that treat Cha'mutte's sacrifice as sacred.
- Penitent communities that believe orcs owe restitution to the world for benefiting from the Godfall.
- Warrior societies that freely preserve much of old orc culture because it is the only identity they know.
- Radical anti-slavery movements that become implacable enemies of hobgoblin rule.
- Mercenary companies whose members insist that choosing to fight is fundamentally different from being compelled to fight.

Orcs become one of the most politically and culturally diverse peoples of dark Taelgar.

## Hobgoblins: The Unbroken

[[Hobgoblins]] experience no comparable metaphysical catastrophe.

They already have no gods, no clerics, and perhaps no soulstuff. Their battlemages remain functional. Their officers still know how to move armies, ration food, build camps, and govern conquered populations. [[Cha'mutte]]'s magical supply system collapses, but enormous armies are already in the field while their enemies have just lost their gods, healers, elder leadership, nonhuman allies, and much of their food production.

The hobgoblins do not win everywhere, but several legion-states become permanent powers. Their societies are disciplined, hierarchical, and capable of turning captured populations into systems of taxation, assigned labor, work camps, or outright slavery.

Hobgoblins need not worship Cha'mutte. They may venerate him as First Marshal, Liberator, or Founder while insisting that worship itself is a weakness of soul-bearing species. To them, the Godfall proves that they were the only people made honestly for the world as it truly is.

## Playable Species

The four default playable species are:

- [[Humans|Human]]
- [[Dwarves|Dwarf]]
- [[Halflings|Halfling]]
- [[Orcs|Orc]]

Other strong possibilities include:

- **[[Goblins|Goblin]]:** Survivors caught between hobgoblin military hierarchy and Chardonian industrial exploitation. Goblin characters might be camp laborers, fugitives, scavengers, machinists, criminals, or members of independent communities.
- **[[Kobolds|Kobold]]:** Draconic rather than divine creations. Abandoned dwarven mines, chaos-glass deposits, ruined infrastructure, and industrial cities give kobolds a natural niche as miners, salvagers, engineers, and trap-makers.
- **[[Hobgoblins|Hobgoblin]]:** A possible but non-default character option. Defectors, exiles, diplomats, escaped junior officers, or members of dissident legionary traditions could work.

Other notes:
- Without [[Aerin]], kenku are metaphysically complicated and maybe lose sentience? 
- Centaurs likely survive and are very interesting but probably don't fit in a western urban one-shot. They may have a critical place in the more "Dark Sun" parts of the setting
- The plane touched may become radically more common. This opens up things like tiefling analogs, and maybe aasimar analogs. 
- It might be worth allowing either a rare few Stoneborn to have survived to become Goliaths, or inventing a new way for Goliaths to exist. 

It is unclear what the fate of the Feywild is in this world. Most likely it is closed off to the extent possible by the fey themselves, but this doesn't totally rule out a fey escapee character.
