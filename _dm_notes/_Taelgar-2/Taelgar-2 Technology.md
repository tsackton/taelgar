# Tecnology in Taelgar-2

This is a brainstorming note about technology in the current day of Taelgar-2 written by Codex and Tim Sackton. 

The underlying setting is described in [[Taelgar-2 Current Day Brainstorming]], while the metaphysical basis of chalyte and chaos glass is discussed in [[Taelgar-2 Magic]]. Character-facing consequences belong in [[Taelgar-2 Mechanics]], and the peoples who provide and endure much of the industrial labor are discussed in [[Taelgar-2 Species After the Godfall]].

The central technological principle is:

> Chardon can build almost anything once. It cannot necessarily build it safely, repeatedly, or at scale.

Taelgar-2 is an early industrial world with a narrow, spectacular layer of arcane supertechnology. [[Chardon]] may have factories, precision weapons, lifts, trams, prostheses, analytical engines, and skyships while most settlements still depend upon sails, waterwheels, draft animals, and human bodies.

## General Technology Level

The dependable technological baseline is early industrial rather than generally Victorian or modern. Printing presses, precision clocks, optics, improved metallurgy, steam pumps, factories, canals, sailing ships, and short railways are all feasible. There is no electricity, internal combustion, mass automobile culture, or ordinary aviation.

This intensifies tendencies already present in Chardon, which is known in Taelgar Prime for clocks, optics, fine weapons, scrolls, shipbuilding, arcane learning, and the processing of [[Chalyte|chalyte]].

The world remains technologically uneven. Major Chardonian and hobgoblin centers can maintain advanced infrastructure. Subject ports, mines, estates, and fortresses may possess particular machines serving extraction or military control. A nearby village might have nothing more sophisticated than a waterwheel and a blacksmith. A remote settlement may depend upon one miraculous pump that nobody there can repair.

## The Technological Stack

### Muscle, Wind, and Water

Muscle, wind, and water remain the foundation of the economy. Most people travel on foot, horseback, wagon, or ordinary sailing ship. Waterwheels drive rural mills. Barges move grain and ore. Refugees haul loads that wealthy Chardonians move with machines.

This keeps industrial civilization dependent upon rivers, canals, ports, draft animals, and enormous amounts of human labor.

### Steam

Steam converts heat into reliable mechanical motion. It operates pumps, cranes, mills, factory belts, ships, and short railway lines.

Ordinary fuel can produce steam, but chaos glass makes much smaller engines capable of much greater pressure. These compact engines are prone to sudden surges, contamination, and spectacular explosions. A mundane steam engine is large and cumbersome. A chaos-fired engine is compact, powerful, and dangerous.

### Chaos Glass

Chaos glass is common relative to chalyte but rarer, more dangerous, and less predictable than coal in the real world. It forms in localized Godfall scars, damaged ley lines, battlefields, dead divine sites, and other places touched by [[Cha'mutte]]'s death. It may be related in some fashion to the [[Seeds of Chaos]] in Taelgar Prime, though this is not crucial for world building. Whether deposits are strictly finite, slowly renewed, or capable of spreading remains undecided.

Chaos glass functions as both a coal analog and a gunpowder analog. Different preparations can release sustained heat, sudden pressure, light, or explosive force. It can fuel boilers and furnaces or be refined into propellant and blasting powder.

Its abundance is enough to transform civilization. Its limitations prevent industrialization from following a smooth exponential curve.

### Chalyte

Chalyte provides precision, containment, enchantment, and control. It can meter an unstable reaction, preserve a magical effect, translate a command into motion, or force chaotic energy into a repeatable form.

In ordinary speech, people call many advanced devices "chalyte-powered," but this describes two different technologies:

- **Pure chalyte devices** draw or store relatively small amounts of arcane power. They are clean, silent, precise, and extraordinarily expensive. These are similar to the classic magic items of Taelgar Prime. 
- **Chalyte-regulated devices** use chaos glass for high output while chalyte constrains that energy into a stable effect. These power most heavy magitech, and are much more tech flavored than traditional magic items. 

Chalyte is not consumed as quickly as chaos glass, but shaped crystals accumulate magical strain. Eventually they must be recalibrated, recut, or reannealed by specialists. Damaged crystals become unstable and can produce magical backlashes, addiction, or mental destruction.

This allows Chardon to retain control over devices it has already sold. A provincial governor may own a chalyte pumping station, but only Chardon can provide replacement cores and licensed engineers.

## Why Chaos Glass Does Not Scale Like Coal

### Deposits Are Inconsistent

Every deposit has a different "temper." Glass from one scar may release steady heat. Another may detonate under pressure. Another may react to emotion, nearby spellcasting, planar weather, or particular materials.

Industrial glass must be assayed, graded, crushed, and blended. A boiler designed for one source may explode when loaded with glass from another. "Ten tons of fuel" is not a uniform commodity without knowing where it came from and who refined it.

### Concentration Creates Resonance

A handful of glass can be handled relatively safely. Large quantities begin reacting with one another.

> Sufficiently concentrated chaos glass tries to become a single magical event.

A warehouse does not contain ten thousand inert charges. It contains ten thousand unstable possibilities beginning to resonate. Stockpiles can produce strange weather, shared dreams, heat without combustion, spontaneous fractures, mutations, or catastrophic chain reactions.

This limits fuel storage and prevents cities from accumulating mountains of glass beside enormous centralized power stations.

### Risk Increases Faster Than Output

Ten times as much chaos glass creates more than ten times the containment problem. It requires stronger isolation, more precise handling, more chalyte regulators, and larger exclusion zones.

There are therefore few economies of scale. A larger factory may produce goods more efficiently while becoming disproportionately expensive and dangerous to operate. Chardon continually builds larger works because its rulers want greater production. Low Chardon pays when those works fail.

### Refining Is Lossy and Dangerous

Refining raw glass can produce graded furnace shards, weapons powder, high-energy dust, contaminated slag, magically active wastewater, and unusable fragments that still cannot be safely discarded.

The waste remains dangerous. It cannot simply be buried, burned, or dumped into the river without consequences, although refineries often do exactly that.

### Consumption Is Unpredictable

A chaos engine might run efficiently for a month and then consume three times its normal fuel after a small magical disturbance. Some charges release less energy than expected. Others damage the machinery containing them.

Industrialists can calculate averages but cannot guarantee individual outcomes. Assay houses, refinery licenses, safety inspectors, and insurers become important political institutions and opportunities for corruption.

## Why Firearms Can Still Be Common

Firearms require very little glass per shot. Properly graded powder can be sealed into individual paper, ceramic, or metal charges and kept separate from other charges. Small, intermittent explosions are far easier to control than continuous industrial power.

- A pistol contains a few individually packaged charges.
- A cannon magazine is dangerous but manageable.
- A locomotive carries a serious hazardous-material stockpile.
- A city-scale generating station is practically a dormant catastrophe.

Cheap ammunition varies enormously in quality. Misfires, fouled barrels, premature detonations, and magical contamination are common among poorly manufactured charges. Chardonian military cartridges are much safer because their glass is carefully graded and stabilized.

Muzzle-loading pistols, muskets, scatterguns, and cannon can be widely available. Chardonian and hobgoblin troops may possess breech-loading carbines, precision rifles, grenades, and early revolvers. Elite weapons can incorporate chalyte mechanisms for greater reliability or specialized effects.

Melee weapons and armor remain relevant because most firearms have limited rates of fire, ammunition is expensive, glass powder is unreliable when wet or magically disturbed, and combat frequently occurs in streets, tunnels, ships, factories, and ruins.

## Manufacturing and Labor

Mechanical parts can be standardized within a particular charter house, legion, or factory, but chalyte systems remain difficult to reproduce and individually tuned. Components from rival manufacturers may be deliberately incompatible.

Machines multiply labor without eliminating the need for it. Refineries, mines, factories, railways, ports, and ships require armies of workers. Refugees are often cheaper than automata, particularly when employers are not responsible for the long-term effects of chaos exposure.

Dwarven knowledge is central to industrial development, but displaced dwarven engineers do not necessarily own the factories built from that knowledge. Goblins and kobolds can become important scavengers, machinists, miners, and repair workers. Halfling financiers and charter houses may help organize the capital and trade networks that sustain the system.

## Transportation

### Ships

Sailing ships remain the foundation of long-distance trade. Steam tugs, armored riverboats, and hybrid sail-steam warships can operate around Chardon, [[Illoria]], [[Apporia]], and the Chasa. Fully steam-powered ocean vessels are rare because of their fuel and maintenance requirements.

Skyships cannot economically carry grain, timber, ore, refugees, or armies. The [[Old Chardon Canal]], southern ports, river systems, and protected maritime corridors therefore remain strategically essential.

### Railways and Trams

Rail exists as isolated systems: elevated freight lines inside Chardon, passenger trams in wealthy districts, mine-to-port railways, plantation lines, and military routes between fortresses and supply depots.

There is no continental network. Track survives only where someone can patrol every bridge, protect every station, supply dangerous fuel, and compel workers to maintain it.

### Skyships

A chalyte skyship is a flying naval vessel. A chalyte lattice provides lift and compensates for weight, while steam or chaos engines provide propulsion. It still needs a large crew, ballast, artillery, specialized engineers, and extensive maintenance.

Skyships serve as warships, diplomatic vessels, couriers, and transports for small quantities of valuable cargo. Only Chardon, major charter houses, and the strongest hobgoblin states possess them. Most travel between equipped skyports along established skyways.

### Personal Vehicles

There is no general automobile culture. A wealthy Chardonian might possess a small chalyte carriage, mechanical palanquin, or steam coach, but such vehicles are conspicuous luxuries requiring prepared streets and regular maintenance. Most streets remain full of carts, animals, handbarrows, and pedestrians.

## Communication and Information

Printing is widespread in major cities. Chardon produces government proclamations, newspapers, technical manuals, propaganda, cheap novels, and revolutionary pamphlets.

Ordinary letters travel by ship, courier, or caravan. Semaphore towers and signal lamps serve defended corridors. Military and commercial dispatches travel on skyships. Paired chalyte communicators may allow nearly instantaneous contact between a small number of elite locations.

There is no electrical telegraph. A governor can speak with Chardon because the palace contains a device worth more than the surrounding village. Ordinary people may wait months for news.

## Medicine, Prostheses, and Bodies

The disappearance of divine healing creates tremendous pressure to develop anatomy, surgery, chemistry, prosthetics, sanitation, and public-health administration. Infection, childbirth, and industrial accidents remain deadly, and access is sharply unequal.

High Chardon can possess clean water, sterilized instruments, chalyte-assisted surgery, alchemical drugs, and articulated prostheses. Low Chardon has factory clinics designed to return workers to their shifts. Rural communities depend upon herbalists, midwives, bone-setters, and whatever knowledge survived the Godfall.

Mechanical prostheses are visible among soldiers and workers. Chalyte prostheses capable of sensation or supernatural strength are elite military technology. Cheap chaos-driven prostheses hiss, burn their users, and sometimes move without being commanded.

## Automata and Possible Construct People

The nature and prevalence of automata should remain uneven and rumor-driven. People commonly distinguish three categories, but nobody knows whether the categories are truly separate:

1. **Mechanical automata** repeat predetermined motions. They are expensive clocks, factory arms, loading machines, and toys.
2. **Arcane constructs** interpret limited instructions. They guard doors, carry cargo, patrol fixed routes, or work in conditions considered too dangerous for valuable laborers.
3. **Awakened machines** appear to think, remember, disobey, and fear death.

Chardonian authorities may deny that the third category exists. Workers tell stories about constructs that escaped the refineries. Academics argue that apparent intelligence is only an elaborate magical response. With the gods dead, no religious authority can settle whether these beings possess souls.

This leaves room for warforged-style player characters without establishing a common species or a single origin. A construct character might be a unique prototype, an escaped military creation, an ancient machine awakened by the Godfall, a refinery automaton changed by chaos exposure, or a person whose soul or memories entered an artificial body.

## Comparison to Other Magitech Settings

### Arcane

*Arcane* is the closest model for Chardon's visual and social character: an industrial city with firearms, lifts, airships, chemistry, prosthetics, polluted undercity districts, and extraordinary prototype hextech. Portable hextech remains new, dangerous, politically controlled, and potentially revolutionary.

Chardon should resemble *Arcane* at street level, but chaos glass imposes a harsher resource and reliability ceiling.

### League of Legends

Broader *League of Legends* supports a more elastic technological ceiling: unique energy weapons, powered suits, sophisticated automata, and inventions as advanced as particular characters require.

This is useful as the upper boundary of what Chardon might produce once. It is too technologically permissive to serve as the everyday baseline.

### Eberron

Eberron is a world of wide magic. Low-level magical effects are reliable, standardized, and integrated into society through rail, long-distance communications, airships, lighting, and magical industry.

Taelgar-2 can share Eberron's approximate technological ceiling and institutional monopolies while remaining far less reliable, less connected, and less widely distributed. Chalyte and chaos glass never become the dependable magical infrastructure that dragonshards and magewrights provide in Eberron.

### Classic Steampunk

Classic steampunk imagines a nineteenth-century industrial society extended through speculative steam aircraft, walkers, submarines, automata, and mechanical computation.

Taelgar-2 borrows the visual language of smoke, pressure, gears, foundries, and retrofuturistic machines. It rejects the assumption that plentiful fuel and successful mass production will continue pushing industrial growth forward. Chaos glass makes the industrial curve unstable, punctuated, and destructive.

In summary:

- Chardon's appearance is closest to *Arcane*.
- Its most exceptional inventions can approach broader *League of Legends* or Eberron.
- Its dependable underlying machinery is early industrial.
- Its capacity for safe mass deployment is lower than any of these comparisons.

## Magitech Science Fiction

The deciding question for a proposed technology is not whether Chardon could invent it. The better question is:

> What happens if Chardon can manufacture a thousand of them?

Technology fits the setting when it remains narrow, infrastructure-bound, degrading, dangerous in bulk, or dependent upon elite specialists. It threatens the setting when it becomes a portable and reliable solution to geography, scarcity, labor, or communication.

### Powered Exosuits: Strong Fit

Human-scale powered armor fits extremely well. Chalyte can translate the wearer's movements into mechanical force while a small chaos engine drives the limbs. Such suits might serve dockworkers, miners, refinery crews, soldiers, or Praecanti assault teams.

A damaged regulator can crush the wearer, lock the suit in place, or continue amplifying involuntary movements. For player characters, an Armorer artificer could simply be using such a suit.

### Giant Mecha: Strong as Singular Machines, Poor as Standard Units

A twenty-foot war machine can change a battle without changing the entire world. It requires a specialist crew, enormous fuel consumption, dedicated transport, and constant repair. Mud, weak bridges, sabotage, and fuel shortages remain serious threats.

One experimental walker guarding Chardon, one hobgoblin siege construct, or one abandoned machine in Apporia fits well. Regiments of standardized mecha would shift the setting toward a full magitech world war and diminish the importance of infantry, fortifications, monsters, and ordinary travel.

### Airplanes: Strong as Prototypes, Disruptive if Routine

Fixed-wing aircraft require lightweight reliable engines, standardized fuel, precision components, and frequent safe maintenance: exactly the conditions chaos glass resists.

An experimental aircraft can be fast, fragile, terrifying, and almost impossible to repair outside Chardon. A few military scouts would already have significant consequences. Ordinary air forces or commercial flight would bypass borders and dangerous routes, diminish the importance of skyports and skyships, and weaken the world's geographic isolation.

### Chalyte Energy Rifles: Strong as Rare Weapons, Poor as Replacements for Guns

A so-called chalyte rifle might use a focusing lattice around a sealed chaos-glass charge. It could project concentrated heat, force, or raw arcane disruption rather than literal coherent light. It might penetrate enchanted armor, damage constructs, sever wards, or leave distinctive planar burns.

Its crystal could survive only a few shots before requiring reannealing. A damaged lattice might direct the shot through its wielder. Such weapons fit as unique Praecanti devices, artificer inventions, or anti-skyship rifles. Common energy rifles would quickly displace firearms, armor, and many fortifications.

For a player character, an energy weapon need not deal more damage than a musket, heavy crossbow, or spellcasting focus. Narrative rarity does not require mechanical superiority.

### Other Technologies

Strong or manageable additions include:

- powered prostheses;
- exosuits;
- magical optics and sensors;
- room-sized analytical engines;
- short-range surveillance constructs;
- rare energy shields;
- experimental aircraft;
- unique intelligent automata;
- dangerous chaos-glass body modification.

Technologies that would undermine the current setting if reliable and widespread include:

- mass-produced airplanes;
- personal long-distance communicators;
- cheap autonomous labor;
- routine teleportation;
- portable limitless power;
- reliable magical food creation or water purification;
- mass healing;
- cheap general-purpose energy weapons.

The ideal Chardonian invention does something impossible while leaving the underlying social problem untouched, or making it worse.

## Current Working Model

- **Ordinary Taelgar:** early modern to early industrial tools, with most work performed by people, animals, wind, and water.
- **Chardon and major enclaves:** factories, steam engines, firearms, trams, pumps, precision manufacture, advanced medicine, communications nodes, and limited automata.
- **Imperial corridors and subject territories:** selected infrastructure designed to move grain, ore, fuel, soldiers, taxes, and messages toward centers of power.
- **Technological apex:** skyships, powered armor, giant constructs, energy weapons, awakened automata, and experimental aircraft, produced in tiny numbers and supported by fragile monopolized systems.

The technology does not repair the broken world. It determines who can profit from the breakage.
