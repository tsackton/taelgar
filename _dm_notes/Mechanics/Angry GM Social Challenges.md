### What happens when an action fails?

**[01:14:54]** Asolian asked, "Do I prefer to tick down patience when the players fail on an action or increase objections or invoke offenses or blocks or whatever, or is it just failure is just a loss of patience?" So here's the deal. Failure is only a loss of patience. You don't need to adjust anything on the stat block for a failed action. The cost of a failed action is the patience is ticking down, okay? And the reason why, the reason why you don't wanna do this double taxation, where you wanna have two costs for failure, is simply because this makes the whole thing easy for the game master to handle at the table.

**[01:15:44]** Okay, that timer, that patience, that is what we call an inertia mechanic. It means that if the players are not succeeding, they are failing, okay? At that every turn, if the players have not made progress towards success, they are sliding toward failure, okay? And remember too, by the way, patience gets ticked down whether the players succeed or fail. The encounter is always sliding toward failure, okay? That means that you as a GM do not need to worry about the failure state. You just need to tick, tick down the patience, and when the failure state happens, you need to invoke it.

**[01:16:32]** But beyond that, you don't need to do anything more than decide what success means. And success on an action only means a couple of things. It either means that you are decreasing an objection, you are increasing a motivation, you are providing the players with information, or you are giving the players a bonus forward on future actions. Those are the only moves you as a game master will have to make. You'll see this when we talk about running it at the table.

**[01:17:02]** So the fewer moves you as a GM have to make, the better off you are because running a social interaction encounter is a high cognitive load thing. Because remember, you're not allowed to just say, "Your action succeeded. You did three points of objections damage," or whatever. It's not like a combat where you can get away with that horseshit. You have to take the result of the action, and then you are going to have to translate it into the NPC saying something, right? And you're gonna have to filter it through the NPC's personality so that you portray them correctly.

**[01:17:43]** And not only that, but you have to signal to the players a good next move. And we're gonna talk about that also. You are going to have to suggest to them that this objection has been overcome, but there is another objection to overcome, or there is a motive that they could work on, or this, or they're making headway on the objection, but they haven't quite overcome it yet, and they could keep chipping away at that one, right? You have to communicate these things to the players. That's a lot of work to do as a GM.

**[01:18:20]** So you really don't wanna have to fiddle with the numbers more than is necessary. Hence, I have built this simple inertia mechanic that makes sure that the encounter is always moving toward failure if it's not moving towards success. And that's it. That's one less thing you as a game master have to worry about. So yeah, don't screw with anything else. Failure is just the timer ticks down, and then everything else handles itself.
