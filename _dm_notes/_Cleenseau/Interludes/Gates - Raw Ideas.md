The **Silver Vineyard** is an old and elusive crossing between [[Twilight's Grace]] and mortal [[Tyrwingha]]. It lies somewhere in the southern foothills near the source of one of Tyrwingha’s rivers, but its precise location shifts from time to time. The crossing is less formal than either the [[Twilight's Pool]] or the [[Evening Promenade]], and is often used by solitary travelers, messengers, lovers, and guests whose arrival is meant to seem accidental.

## In Twilight's Grace

On the Feywild side, the crossing lies within an elegant vineyard spread across a series of gentle terraces beneath the perpetual dusk of [[Twilight's Grace]]. The vines grow in long, ordered rows supported by arches and trellises of pale stone and silver metal. Their leaves are dark green above and softly silver beneath, and their grapes hang in clusters of deep violet, pearl-white, translucent gold, and other colors not found in mortal vineyards.

Some of the fruit gleams faintly from within, like stars seen through colored glass. Wine made here is served at the court of [[Archfey Ethlenn]], though different bottles are said to preserve different memories, longings, dreams, or perfect moments of conversation.

At the lower edge of the vineyard, the ordered terraces gather into a broad arbor of ancient vines. The trunks rise like columns, the branches form a high vaulted roof, and clusters of fruit hang overhead in the dimness. Beyond the arbor, the vines become increasingly wild and dense. Travelers leaving for [[Tyrwingha]] need only pass through them, and soon emerge among the wooded hills of the mortal world.

## In Tyrwingha

On the mortal side, wild grapes grow thickly across a broad region of the southern foothills near the headwaters of a cold river. They climb trees, trail over boulders, fill narrow gullies, and form dense tangles beneath the forest canopy. There are no roads nearby, and the crossing is generally reached only by hunters, shepherds, foresters, or travelers following streams into the hills.

Its exact location does not remain fixed. One year the crossing may lie beside a spring beneath an old ash; another it may be found several miles away, where the vines have swallowed a rocky hollow or grown across the mouth of a ravine. It always appears where the grapes are oldest, thickest, and most abundant.

At the heart of the crossing is a hidden arbor or vine hall. Ancient trunks and living branches form a vaulted chamber within the wild growth, and the ground beneath is strangely clear. Above the leaves, the sky is always dusk, regardless of the hour or weather outside: the sun has already set, the last colors linger low in the west, and the first stars are visible overhead.

Those expected in [[Twilight's Grace]] enter the arbor and seem to know immediately which grape is meant for them. It may be silver among black fruit, perfectly ripe among green clusters, or entirely ordinary except for the certainty with which it draws the eye. Once eaten, the far side of the arbor opens into the ordered terraces of the Silver Vineyard.

Those who are unexpected but welcome receive no such certainty. Nothing tells them to eat the fruit, and no clear path presents itself. Yet while wandering, resting beneath the vines, or tasting a grape out of curiosity, they may discover that the grove has quietly opened and that the wild growth ahead has become a cultivated row beneath the dusk of Ethlenn’s realm.

Those unwelcome in [[Twilight's Grace]] find no grape meant for them and no path beyond the arbor. The first fruit they eat may leave them pleasantly light-headed; the next brings flushed cheeks, loose speech, or uncertain footing. Further grapes produce escalating drunkenness, nausea, emotional indiscretion, and eventually the miserable realization that they have eaten far too much fruit in a remote forest for no sensible reason. The vines remain closed, and the traveler must retreat with stained lips, unsteady steps, and whatever dignity they have managed to preserve.
