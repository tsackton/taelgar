Here are **15 compact Twilight’s Grace ideas**, aimed at giving the party places to wander and people to bump into without immediately turning everything into a quest. I’m leaning into fixed sunset, animal companions, gifts, etiquette, and Ethlenn’s elegant but lightly uncanny rule.

- **The Market of Borrowed Things.** No one sells anything, but visitors may borrow clothes, weapons, songs, scents, or even moods, provided they return something of comparable interest.
    
- **Aster Vey, Keeper of Arrivals.** A beautifully dressed fae who remembers everyone who has ever entered Twilight’s Grace, but only by the first thing they said upon arrival.
    
- **The Orchard of Evening Bells.** Silver fruit chimes when touched, each note recalling a moment when someone chose mercy over vengeance; one tree remains stubbornly silent around the party.
    
- **The House of Second Hospitality.** An inn for guests who have already offended one host and need somewhere to learn better manners before trying again.
    
- **Lethren and his enormous white hare.** He is an elderly court gossip who claims to know nothing important, then casually mentions three buried scandals and an archfey’s impending visit.
    
- **The Pool of Unfinished Reflections.** Reflections here show what someone almost became: abandoned careers, forsaken loves, roads not taken, and occasionally futures that may still be possible.
    
- **The Vesperwrights.** Artisans who build objects from sunset color, shadow, birdcall, and promises; they are fascinated by mortal steel because it stays obstinately itself.
    
- **Lady Orineth of the Seven Veils.** A minor noble whose veils each conceal a different emotion; tonight she is missing the veil of embarrassment and behaving with catastrophic honesty.
    
- **The Court of Small Disputes.** Talking animals, pixies, household spirits, and offended trees bring grievances too minor for Ethlenn, such as stolen nests, insulting songs, and improperly reciprocated berries.
    
- **The Lantern Menagerie.** A garden where every lantern contains a tiny living scene from a lost or fallen fey realm, including one Greymalkin recognizes but refuses to discuss.
    
- **Myr of the Empty Saddle.** A rider whose animal companion vanished years ago; they offer to guide the party anywhere in the realm, but always leave space beside them for someone unseen.
    
- **The Feast of Returning Names.** Once each cycle, people may reclaim names they abandoned, traded, or outgrew; several names arrive without anyone willing to claim them.
    
- **The Dusk Conservatory.** Musicians play songs that alter nearby weather and vegetation, while their conductor desperately tries to prevent an argument from causing a thunderstorm indoors.
    
- **Sir Caldris, Knight of the Last Courtesy.** A courteous, melancholy fae charged with escorting dangerous guests safely through the realm, including guests he privately believes should be thrown into a ravine.
    
- **The Road That Waits.** A path outside the court only appears to travelers who genuinely intend to leave; it keeps vanishing whenever someone decides they might stay one more night.
