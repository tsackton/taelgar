These are all **postwar cleanup episodes**: recognizable allies, consequences of Malach’s reign, and a problem that ends cleanly after one or two sessions. The underlying campaign callbacks are grounded in the earlier notes, while the specific incidents below are new possibilities.

## 1. A Soldier’s Funeral Without a Body

**Hook:** A mile beyond Eskbridge, the party overtakes Claire of Fellburn and Bertrand Leclerc escorting a coffin west under an Army of the South banner. At the evening camp, Claire privately admits that the coffin is empty and asks Robin whether a deserter deserves a funeral.

A soldier from the detachment deliberately sent wandering into the badlands during Malach’s sabotage was eventually declared dead, but Claire has received credible word that he is alive nearby. He fled after obeying an order that got most of his squad killed and has spent months hiding under another name, convinced the army will execute him if he returns. Claire wants the truth, Bertrand wants to protect the company’s reputation, and the dead soldier’s family is already preparing the burial. The Army of the South survivors and corrupted orders are established campaign threads.

**Session shape:** Follow the deserter’s trail through one or two roadside settlements, reconstruct the disastrous order, and find him protecting a small community under an assumed identity. The climax is a tense negotiation, possibly complicated by military police or relatives who arrive expecting a body.

**Resolution:** The party determines whether he deserted out of cowardice, trauma, conscience, or some mixture of all three, then helps Claire settle the matter honestly. The coffin is either filled, buried empty with the truth acknowledged, or repurposed for the remains of soldiers who were never recovered.

Old version: Claire of Fellburn and Bertrand Leclerc are escorting an empty coffin west for one of the soldiers who joined Ida’s command, but they privately believe the supposed casualty deserted. The missing soldier was last seen carrying sealed orders from the same corrupted military bureaucracy that repeatedly sent troops into traps during the lich’s reign.

## 2. The Lake Wizard’s Stolen Work

**Hook:** At an inn on the Sembaran road, the party finds the surviving Lake Rin wizard arguing with guards who refuse to pursue a thief over “wizard rubbish.” She recognizes the party, produces a smashed specimen case, and tells them someone has stolen preserved tissue from the eel-hybridized undead that killed her companions.

She had gathered the remains to understand and prevent further forced transformations, not reproduce them. The thief is a minor alchemist who believes the hybridization process could create workers or soldiers able to survive underwater, and has established a crude laboratory in an abandoned mill or riverside tannery. The wizard wants the material recovered, but confronting the work also forces her back toward the trauma she avoided after the original massacre. Her survival, specialization in transmutation, and horror at the nonconsensual eel-zombie transformations come directly from the Lake Rin episode.

**Session shape:** Investigate the theft, locate the improvised laboratory, rescue one or two experimental victims, and destroy or secure the stolen material. The wizard can assist intellectually while the PCs handle the physical danger, with a contained combat against unstable undead or aquatic hybrids.

**Resolution:** All surviving specimens and notes are accounted for, the victims are treated, and the wizard decides what parts of her research can safely continue. She gains closure without being “cured” of her trauma, and the party sees that one of their earlier victories mattered beyond killing the monster.

Old version: The surviving transmutation wizard from Lake Rin has assembled a painfully cautious new adventuring company and is transporting recovered material from the undead eel experiments to a serious scholar. Someone has stolen one sealed specimen, and she is certain the thief intends to reproduce the forced hybridization that killed her former companion

## 3. The Missing Rangers

**Hook:** Tristan and Remille are waiting at a crossroads because they heard the party had crossed at Eskbridge and have been riding hard to intercept them. They have finally located Enzo and Adra Brightwood, but freeing them without help may turn a rescue into a massacre.

The Brightwoods are held at an isolated royal safehouse by former agents of Malach who insist they acted under magical compulsion and are now terrified of execution. The prisoners are alive because their captors want respected Rangers to testify that some royal servants were victims too, but the increasingly desperate group will not release them until promised safe conduct. Tristan wants to storm the place; Remille suspects at least some of the captors are telling the truth. The Rangers’ search for Enzo and Adra was an explicit unresolved thread.

**Session shape:** Approach and investigate the safehouse, establish who is enthralled, guilty, frightened, or lying, then negotiate or execute a rescue. Combat remains possible if one faction among the former agents panics or tries to murder witnesses.

**Resolution:** Enzo and Adra are reunited with Tristan and Remille, and the individual captors receive specific outcomes rather than becoming an ongoing conspiracy. The reunited Rangers can finally leave the stage together, wounded but whole enough to choose what comes next.

## 4. Evidence from the Northern Caves

**Hook:** A small troop reaches Eskbridge carrying locked chests, several wounded soldiers, and a dead officer wrapped in a cloak. They recognize the party and explain that the supposedly cleared guerrilla caves contained years of hidden correspondence, forged uniforms, and military orders documenting Malach’s sabotage.

Someone attacked them on the road, not to seize the chests but to burn them. The attackers are surviving collaborators and compromised officers whose names appear in the papers, now making one last attempt to erase evidence before it reaches a trustworthy magistrate. This builds directly on the campaign’s recurring corrupted military orders and the caves the regional forces were sent to clear.

**Session shape:** Escort the troop and evidence to a named official over one dangerous stretch of road, identify a traitor or frightened informant within the group, and survive one planned ambush. The investigative portion determines which chest the attackers truly need and who tipped them off.

**Resolution:** The evidence reaches lawful custody, the responsible local collaborators are exposed or killed, and the surviving soldiers receive recognition rather than blame for following deliberately disastrous orders. The episode closes one corner of the lich regime’s machinery without revealing a larger hidden organization.

Old version:
A battered group of soldiers recognizes the party and reports that the old guerrilla caves were cleared of undead, but the troops discovered stores of weapons, forged uniforms, and years of cached correspondence. Someone had been preparing a mortal insurgency beneath the cover of the zombie crisis, and the soldiers carrying the evidence are now being hunted.

## 5. The Heroes Are Dead Again

**Hook:** At the first busy inn west of Eskbridge, the party hears a bard singing about their deaths in the Feywild. The innkeeper then presents Robin with a freshly circulated notice stating that the party’s deaths have been legally certified and that their property and offices may now be reassigned.

The false declaration began as bureaucratic cleanup: they disappeared into the Feywild, credible witnesses reported them dead, and several institutions needed decisions made. The immediate problem is one claimant who has used forged testimony to seize money, equipment, or an office belonging to one of the PCs or their allies, and who is now fleeing before news of the party’s return spreads. This recalls the earlier rumor that the party had died and been replaced by fey impostors.

**Session shape:** Establish that the party is alive without accidentally getting detained as possible shapeshifters, trace the fraudulent claim, and catch the claimant at the next market town or river crossing. The comedy of proving one’s own existence can give way to a short chase, hearing, or confrontation.

**Resolution:** The death declarations are formally reversed, the immediate theft is repaired, and one wonderfully awkward official must issue a proclamation explaining that the heroes have ceased to be deceased. Nothing more sinister than opportunism and wartime confusion is required.

## 6. Ida’s Company Is Being Disbanded

**Hook:** The party encounters Ida’s soldiers camped beside the road, stripping insignia from shields and dividing stores into neat piles. Ida shows them an order dissolving the emergency company and asks for help with one final obligation before her people scatter.

A remote post or village supplied the company during the crisis and is now threatened by a manageable but genuine problem, perhaps bandits occupying an old watchtower, undead trapped in a quarry, or soldiers who refused the stand-down order and became brigands. Ida cannot abandon the people who supported her, but she also cannot take a soon-to-be-disbanded company on an unauthorized campaign. Her company’s growth to one hundred soldiers, based at Asineau, was directly created through the party’s regional defense efforts.

**Session shape:** Join a final reduced muster, travel to the threatened site, and resolve one contained military problem alongside familiar soldiers. The PCs provide the exceptional force while Ida’s troops evacuate civilians, hold a perimeter, or perform the disciplined work they were built to do.

**Resolution:** The obligation is fulfilled, the company returns without opening a political fight over its dissolution, and Ida conducts a final ceremony releasing the soldiers from service. Whether some later reenlist elsewhere is left as background, not a dangling adventure hook.

Old version:
A detachment from Ida Rosfeld’s enlarged regional force intercepts the party carrying orders to dissolve the company and redistribute its soldiers now that the lich is dead. Ida believes the order is politically motivated and asks the party to help prove the company is still needed before her people are scattered into units commanded by the same bureaucracy that previously sent soldiers into traps.

## 7. The Rescued Prisoners Need a Home

**Hook:** A familiar survivor from Wakog’s camp finds the party near a roadside market and asks them to come quickly before frightened villagers drive a refugee caravan away. The displaced families were promised temporary land and shelter, but rumors now claim they collaborated with the orcs or carry some hidden corruption.

The accusation has been encouraged by a local landholder or merchant who wants the promised property, but it is not entirely fabricated: one refugee did cooperate with Wakog to keep children alive and has concealed what they did. The problem is to protect the innocent without demanding that the entire group pretend nobody made ugly compromises in captivity. This calls back to the prisoners and survivors rescued from Wakog’s organized orc camp.

**Session shape:** Stop immediate violence, investigate who spread the rumors, uncover the collaborator’s actual actions, and expose the material motive behind the agitation. A final confrontation could be a hearing, a mob scene, or a brief fight with hired intimidators.

**Resolution:** The refugees receive the agreed shelter, the profiteer loses their scheme, and the survivor who collaborated is judged in context rather than through rumor. The group settles somewhere offscreen, making this a humane coda to an early campaign rescue.

Old version:
Balthazar, Nerissa, and several survivors from Wakog’s camp are escorting displaced families west, but the settlement promised to receive them has abruptly refused entry after rumors that some were collaborators or magically altered by the orcs. The party can help secure shelter, expose who started the rumor, or persuade one of their established regional allies to accept responsibility for the refugees.

## 8. Rosalind’s Review

**Hook:** Rosalind’s carriage catches up with the party under heavy guard, carrying boxes of records from courts operating during Malach’s reign. She is reviewing one specific conviction nearby and asks the party to help because the condemned person’s execution is scheduled before her official authority can arrive.

The prisoner was convicted as a necromancer or conspirator on evidence manufactured through the same system that falsely blamed Night Queen clergy and neutralized effective opponents of the lich. Unfortunately, the prisoner is not wholly innocent: they committed a serious ordinary crime while hiding or resisting, and the local magistrate believes overturning the capital charge means letting them escape all punishment. Rosalind’s summons to Embry, correspondence with the party, and involvement in the political response are established in the notes, as are the regime’s false accusations against Night Queen clergy.

**Session shape:** Review witnesses and physical evidence, identify the forged or coerced testimony, locate one missing witness, and intervene before the execution. The ending is a compact judicial scene in which the party helps separate treason or necromancy invented by Malach’s regime from the lesser crime actually committed.

**Resolution:** The false capital conviction is overturned, a proportionate sentence or restitution is imposed for the real offense, and Rosalind’s review gains a concrete success. She departs with the next stack of records, while this particular injustice is fully and satisfactorily closed.

## Strongest shortlist

For **action and familiar allies**, I would keep **The Missing Rangers**, **Ida’s Company**, and **Evidence from the Northern Caves**.

For **roleplay and lich-era fallout**, the strongest are **Rosalind’s Review** and **A Soldier’s Funeral Without a Body**.

For a lighter palate-cleanser after the Feywild, **The Heroes Are Dead Again** has excellent comic scaffolding while still reflecting the chaos left by the campaign.
