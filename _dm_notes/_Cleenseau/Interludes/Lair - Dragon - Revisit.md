Yes, this is probably one of the best **post-arc interludes** because it is recognizably connected to a major earlier adventure, but it points toward treasure-hunting and planar ruin rather than immediately back toward Malach.

It also has a clean premise:

> [[Vraedhul]] is dead. Krauzeth is dead or defeated. Her lair remains, damaged, unstable, and full of things nobody sensible should leave unattended.

## Vraedhul’s Broken Lair

**Hook:** Word reaches the party through the Aben garrison or Rhiannon de Vaux that scavengers have begun entering the ruins of Vraedhul’s old lair. One group has already returned with a cursed object and no memory of the three companions who entered with them.

Vraedhul’s lair was destroyed when the Limbo weak point beneath it ruptured during the high tide of 1720. She died in the collapse, but the lair did not simply explode: parts of it were folded into Limbo, parts remained in the Material Plane, and parts now shift unpredictably between the two. Much of the hoard is gone, but her workshops, cursed creations, kobold records, and several sealed vaults may remain intact.

**Session shape:** Reach the crater through the damaged hills, explore a lair whose rooms have been rearranged by Limbo, and determine which portions are stable enough to enter. The party must contend with surviving warped servants, cursed objects that actively seek owners, and one major thing from Limbo that has taken up residence in the central crater.

**Resolution:** The party kills or drives out the new inhabitant, recovers a limited number of worthwhile treasures, and destroys or seals the most dangerous workshops. The site remains a strange ruin, but no longer an immediate source of cursed relics or missing treasure hunters.

# What destroyed Vraedhul?

I would make the answer a combination of **catastrophic failure and predation**.

Vraedhul had spent years drawing on Limbo through the planar weakness. She believed she was shaping it, but in reality she had built a reservoir of unstable planar energy beneath her lair.

During the high tide:

1. The weakness widened abruptly.
    
2. The geography of the lair began responding to contradictory thoughts and impulses.
    
3. Vraedhul tried to seize control of the surge.
    
4. Something in Limbo noticed the channel she had created.
    
5. It pulled the stored energy, parts of the lair, and Vraedhul herself back through the breach.
    

She was not defeated in a fair fight. She was caught inside a magical machine she had mistaken for a throne.

That feels appropriate for her.

## The creature in the crater

The thing occupying the lair now should not secretly be the true mastermind. It is a scavenger that followed the rupture.

A strong option would be:

### **The Ashen Contradiction**

A large Limbo predator whose body never settles into one form. At different moments it resembles:

- a many-legged reptile
    
- a cloud of black ash
    
- a burning skeleton
    
- a lump of molten stone
    
- something with Vraedhul’s silhouette
    

It feeds on magic given stable form, which makes Vraedhul’s crafted items ideal prey.

Mechanically, it can change defenses or damage types between rounds, but it need not be enormously complicated. Its main role is to make the crater clearly unsafe and to answer the question, “What came through?”

## What remains in the lair?

The surviving contents should feel like a **dragon-artificer’s estate sale conducted during an earthquake**.

### The workshop

Partially intact benches, molds, cursed prototypes, and notes written in Draconic or Kobold shorthand.

Useful discoveries might include:

- incomplete curse-breaking formulae
    
- records of who received certain cursed objects
    
- a list of treasures Vraedhul sought in the Plaguelands
    
- flawed but valuable magical components
    
- one object that was never cursed because she had not finished it
    

### The kobold quarter

This is where you can give the earlier dragon episode a little emotional aftertaste.

The party might find:

- Tizzit’s records of the death-clutch
    
- crude murals of Vraedhul, Zaurmath, and Krauzeth
    
- arguments between the three kobold factions scratched into walls
    
- supplies prepared for the flight north
    
- evidence that some kobolds stayed behind and died trying to save eggs, relics, or each other
    

That makes them feel like people caught in the collapse rather than generic minions.

### The hoard

Most of it should be gone.

What remains is scattered into three categories:

- **safe valuables:** gems, coin, art, mundane trophies
    
- **useful but suspect magic:** items requiring identification or cleansing
    
- **things that should never leave:** cursed objects, unstable planar tools, bound creatures
    

The party should come away rewarded, but not with the sense that they emptied an intact dragon vault.

# The strongest hidden discovery

One good piece of fan service would be a **ledger of cursed items Vraedhul sent into the world**.

Most entries can remain names and destinations, but one or two may correspond to:

- an item the party previously encountered
    
- a curse suffered by an ally
    
- a relic currently held by a temple or noble family
    
- something that caused trouble long before anyone knew Vraedhul existed
    

This would make the lair feel retrospectively important without opening a new arc. The party can recover the ledger, give it to scholars, and move on.

# Why this works as an interlude

Unlike Tharwa Fëa, Vraedhul’s lair does not need to reveal a major future truth.

It gives you:

- a callback to Krauzeth and the Aben garrison
    
- exploration
    
- a strange but self-contained monster
    
- treasure
    
- moral decisions about cursed items
    
- a satisfying answer to what happened in 1720
    

And most importantly, it can end with:

> The lair is still ruined, Vraedhul is truly dead, and the dangerous parts are sealed.

That is a proper full stop.

Of the current cleanup options, I would place this beside **Rosalind’s Review**, **The Missing Rangers**, and **Ida’s Company** as one of the strongest. It offers a different flavor, less judicial and military, more expeditionary and archaeological.
