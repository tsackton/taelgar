# E07 — Zaurmath's Broken Hall

Two compact environmental entrance proposals. Choose A or B. Neither is a full excursion into Limbo. Shared assumptions: [[04 Encounter Packet - Vraedhul's Broken Lair]]. The architectural expulsion is visible outside, but the route uses a coherent lower shelf beside it.

## Shared geometry and danger gradient

Draw an 80-foot-long, 60-foot-wide chamber. The entrance and onward tunnel are opposite each other. Both have stable 15-foot-deep landings; 50 feet of damaged floor separates them. A 5-foot-wide ledge follows the west wall but has a 15-foot break halfway along it. A continuous catching shelf lies 10 feet below the western side of the entire damaged floor; falling there does not initiate an extra improvised chain of hazards. Along the east wall, a ragged 10-foot strip has ceased to be coherent matter. This is **the breach**, visibly much worse than the central floor. The tunnel leads through the kobold network toward the shared discovery.

Near landing → 50 feet of changing floor → far landing and tunnel. West: narrow ledge with one 15-foot gap. East: avoidable unforming breach. The map does not rotate or rearrange after it is learned.

**Stable edges:** PCs can watch from the near landing without danger. One minute's observation explains the relevant local pattern; no roll is required for the central warning. A DC 14 Arcana or Investigation check reveals a useful manipulation sooner or supplies an advantage, rather than being the only way to learn the rules.

**West ledge:** crossing at a normal careful pace is safe except for the gap and one cracked outcrop. An adequate rope and anchor, sufficient natural jumping distance, flight, or another sound plan bypasses the corresponding uncertainty. Otherwise DC 14 Athletics to jump/climb past the gap and DC 14 Acrobatics or Athletics at the outcrop. One lead character can rig the route for everyone else; do not demand eight identical checks. Failure drops the character onto a shelf 10 feet below for **3 (1d6)** bludgeoning; it can climb back with an ally and rope or DC 12 Athletics, costing effort rather than entering the breach. These are intentionally safer, slower physical problems.

**Breach:** bones and stone lose their outlines before reaching it; a loose block becomes a string of unrelated surfaces. Say plainly that this is catastrophic unforming, not another ordinary stepping stone. It is never necessary to enter. A creature that deliberately enters or is displaced into its edge makes DC 16 Dexterity: **27 (6d8)** force, half on success, and is expelled onto the nearest coherent floor in either case. It cannot be trapped there taking automatic repeated damage. This local ejection is a safety boundary for these drafts, not a universal Limbo rule. Do not shove PCs into it through an unannounced random hazard.

## A — The floor remembers water

> A strip of black floor shines wetly. The carved dragon beneath your feet lengthens into a current, and a paving slab settles like a lily pad. Farther across, another patch of floor clears to glass. The tunnel beyond it remains dark and perfectly still.

Three 10-foot-square decorated floor plates remain in a straight line across the central gap: at 15–25, 35–45, and 55–65 feet from the entrance. The two edge-to-edge gaps are each 10 feet. The intervening floor is shallow liquid stone, with a coherent basin 10 feet below. The room's original brass handrail runs along the plates. It is broken but reachable; each plate and its railing are one of the dragon's surviving creations. The rails stay solid through every floor phase, and have small coherent footholds.

The plates alternate between **stone, wet stone, and flowing water**. Use three tokens. Start nearest plate stone, middle plate wet, far plate water. At the end of each round spent crossing, each advances: stone → wet → water → stone. Wet stone is safe for that round but visibly warns of the next change. Ordinary glass around the plates is firm, not a concealed additional damage trap.

**Working with the creation:** a character adjacent to a plate or touching its section of handrail can use an action to hold its existing stone possibility for three rounds, with no check once the local behavior is understood. The plate is visibly stone and does not advance during that time. The holder must keep contact with that rail section or plate; moving out of contact releases it. This needs no spell concentration and ends early if that character is incapacitated or voluntarily releases it. Count the next three end-of-round phase advances: the held plate skips all three, then returns to its saved phase after the third. It advances normally at the following round end. Early release likewise restores its saved phase immediately but does not add an extra advance. One character can hold one plate at a time. Holding a new one releases the previous plate into its previous phase; announce that before the choice. An Arcana check is useful for recognizing this before experimenting, not required forever.

**Crossing:** jumping a 10-foot gap uses ordinary movement and the character's actual jumping ability. A rope or bridge removes that uncertainty. Running onto a water-phase plate, or a plate changing to water beneath someone, causes a DC 14 Dexterity save to catch its rail: success hangs safely until the character uses 10 feet of movement to climb onto a coherent rail foothold or stone-phase plate; failure falls into the shallow liquid stone for **7 (2d6)** bludgeoning. An ally can pull them onto the rail with an action; otherwise DC 12 Athletics and 15 feet of movement to reach a solid rail foothold, landing, or stone-phase plate. The 10-foot-deep basin is rough but passable: wading/clambering through it uses triple movement and one DC 12 Athletics check for the crossing, with 7 bludgeoning on failure before reaching the far climbing point. It is a slow valid alternative, not secret bottomless water. No drowning or petrification is added. After one failure, give any newly learned phase behavior plainly.

**Pacing:** the party can coordinate plate-holders, make a rope line, use the ledge, or spend magic. One effective plan should lead to a crossing; do not require a skill challenge afterward. Prepared flight over the central floor is safe. The breach is beside the room, not a blanket ceiling of antimagic.

## B — Rooms trying to become wind

> Three stone ribs stand across the hall like unfinished doorways. Dust pours through the nearest one and stops abruptly on its far side. Beyond the next, the air moves hard enough to bend a hanging iron ring. At the last doorway, a carved leaf has begun fluttering though it is still part of the stone.

The central floor is coherent, but three 10-foot-wide lanes cross it at 20–30, 35–45, and 50–60 feet from the entrance. Old carved ribs above them are transforming the spaces they frame into gusts. The extreme breach remains on the east wall; gusts push **toward the west landing shelf**, never into the breach.

Each rib warns for a full round: dust gathers motionless, then the hanging ring stretches, then a gust crosses its lane. At initiative 20, announce the next active rib; on initiative 20 next round it blows for that round and the next rib begins warning. Cycle near → middle → far. A creature entering the active lane or starting its turn there makes one DC 14 Strength save, maximum once per round: **7 (2d6)** bludgeoning and pushed 10 feet west on failure; half damage and no push on success. The west side has a 10-foot-lower catching shelf as above. Simply waiting and crossing inactive lanes is possible; that is the reward for reading the pattern.

The dragon's decorated ribs still contain the possibility of a doorway framing still air. An action touching the rib or its accessible floor socket and explicitly supporting that possibility stills that lane for three rounds, no check after observation. Alternatively jam its moving ring with a sturdy tool and DC 14 Athletics, or suppress the local effect with Dispel Magic as a fourth-level effect: DC 14 with a third-level slot, automatic with fourth or higher. An inactive rib stays inactive for one minute after dispelling. No spell slot is required for an ordinary coordinated crossing.

Flight crosses the floor and gap but still meets a gust if it passes through its lane below the 20-foot ceiling. A character can avoid the active lane with timing; teleportation or appropriate wind-control magic can solve more. Do not give the gust an arbitrary ability to catch someone outside its marked space.

## Outcomes and balance

Success is reaching the far tunnel, with learned behavior and spent resources retained. A setback costs a little damage, positioning, or a rescue action; it does not randomly move the exit. A retreat leaves other entrances possible. Neither variant includes an obligatory monster or a final check after an effective solution. These are light traversal scenes for level 9–10, with an explicit extreme edge that can be avoided entirely. Do not increase DCs merely because the characters are level 10. Proceed through short narrated tunnels to [[VBL E08 - The Death View]].
