# G01 — The Generous Table

Optional supplicant-suite proposal. Two flavors; choose one. See [[04 Encounter Packet - Vraedhul's Broken Lair]]. These are self-contained rooms off the antechamber, not mandatory lessons or gates to the audience chamber.

## A — Excessive hospitality

> Four chairs wait around a table set for a meal. The cloth is embroidered with tiny balconies and stairways. A platter is already full. As you watch, one more pear squeezes up between the others. Then another. A silver handbell rings softly beneath the growing pile.

**Room:** 25 feet square; door stands open. A service alcove contains a silver bowl marked with a carved image of the same table, empty. On the table, fruit constantly replaces anything removed. Fresh food is wholesome. Only uneaten portions carried more than 20 feet from the table become ordinary ash. Eaten food nourishes normally and never transforms inside someone; there is no retroactive starvation or harm. The room can feed visitors, but cannot stockpile portable rations.

The cruel joke is hospitality that cannot accept a satisfied guest. Sitting is safe initially. Trying to leave with a full plate or lifting the cloth while service continues causes the nearest chair to slide in and trap the offender's legs. Announce the chair's closing arms first: the person can put down the plate or withdraw their hand without a roll. Deliberately proceeding means grappled, escape DC 13; an adjacent ally can release the latch with an action. A chair does not deal damage or grab again once someone is moving away from the table. Chair AC 13, HP 10, poison/psychic immunity if destroyed. Breaking it is an available crude solution, not a required fight.

**Exploration:** the empty table engraved on the bowl expresses a real dormant possibility. Put that bowl on a place setting and attend to the possibility of a finished meal: one action stills the service at that setting for a minute, no check. A different sensible demonstration of completion, such as clearing and turning over a plate while explicitly treating service as ended, works equally. DC 14 Arcana or Investigation can identify the relation before trying it; the clue is also visible without a check.

With two settings stilled at the same time, the whole service pauses. Any character can then fold and take the cloth with an action. Merely smashing the table also stops the service but tears the cloth; one hour of ordinary sewing repairs it, without another quest. No correct phrase or sincerity judgment is required. If the party simply leaves, the room is optional.

**Treasure — cloth of the offered seat:** once per dawn, an action unfolding the cloth produces a stationary 5-foot-square horizontal platform at a visible point within 30 feet, lasting ten minutes and supporting 500 pounds. It cannot appear intersecting a creature or object, move after placement, or crush anything. The cloth remains in its user's hands while the visibly clothlike platform appears. Folding the source cloth uses an action and ends the platform early. No attunement. When it ends, supported creatures fall normally; its visible embroidered border fades over its last round as a warning. This is a proposed utility item, not a way to fly the whole party indefinitely. It is not cursed.

**Exit and lesson:** a small door behind the service alcove can connect to another optional suite; the original door remains usable throughout. They have discovered that satisfying an existing possibility can change a creation's behavior. Do not add a final check to claim the treasure after it stops.

## B — The feast of borrowed senses

> The table is bare except for six little dishes and one cup. A dish holds a polished stone, another a coil of copper, another a lump of black salt. The labels name flavors: summer rain, first applause, an enemy's defeat. None of them is food.

Use the same room dimensions and open exits. There is no chair trap. The cup lets a willing taster experience a dish's assigned sensation; no stone is swallowed and no memory is stolen. A patient instruction scratched beneath the cup reads: "First taste. Then understand what was done to it." This is an observational prompt, not a binding contract.

The first sample is exhilarating. Taking another before clearing the cup confuses the senses for one minute: DC 13 Constitution or disadvantage on the next Perception check using taste or smell, then the effect ends. Never impose blindness, deafness, exhaustion, or a hidden lasting curse. Washing the cup or deliberately attending to its possibility as an empty cup clears it immediately. Any ordinary water works. Destroying the dishes also makes it ordinary and safe.

**Things to find:** the copper tastes like applause but has been repeatedly cut and joined; the stone's assigned triumph is polished over visible cracks. Vraedhul manufactured exquisite experiences out of damaged materials. There is no imprisoned soul demanding a side quest in these samples. If the PCs ask whether living people were ever treated this way, her wider legacy supports that possibility, but this particular room does not invent a current victim.

**Treasure — cup of the distant taste:** three charges, regains 1d3 at dawn. As an action expend one charge while looking at a visible substance within 30 feet; learn its actual taste and whether it is poisonous to the user without touching it or suffering its poison. It does not identify spells, curses, safe doses, an entire mixed meal's ingredients, or things behind cover. If actively used on a magically altered substance, it reveals the substance's present taste and any present poison, not its future form. No attunement, no curse. It works outside the room after being washed once. The six labeled sensations fade when carried out; they are evocative evidence, not six extra items.

**Balance and pacing:** either flavor is a short investigation with a small useful prize, not a resource-draining encounter. Free clever solutions are appropriate. The two treasures are alternatives, not rewards for clearing the same room twice.
