# G02 — The Perfect Reflection

Optional supplicant suite; two proposed flavors. Assumptions: [[04 Encounter Packet - Vraedhul's Broken Lair]]. This is a room to explore for a prize, not a moral test or a required confrontation with a character's backstory.

## A — A flattering mirror

> The mirror shows a richer room than the one you entered. Its chairs are whole, its lamps are bright, and its visitor looks magnificent. A small silver lens hangs from one corner. Your reflection reaches for it a heartbeat before you do.

**Room:** 20 by 30 feet, mirror on the far wall, open door at the near end, low dressing screen providing cover. The lens is a removable fitting. The reflection presents each observer as someone Vraedhul might have considered worth noticing, then visibly removes an imperfection: an old scar, dust, a poor coat. It is flattering in the manner of a predator choosing where to bite.

The back of the frame, readily reached around the side, bears two worked faces: one polished and admiring, one plain and unadorned. Setting the frame's pivot toward the plain face shows the actual room and releases the lens. It takes an action, no roll once found. An observer who explicitly attends to an existing accurate detail rather than the flattery can make that accurate possibility dominate for a minute, revealing the pivot if it has not been found. A blanket, turned mirror, clever reflection trick, or a safe dispel also works. Treat its active glamour as third-level for this draft's dispelling rule.

**Risk:** pulling the lens while the flattering image still dominates calls the observer's reflected hand out of the glass. Describe it reaching; a PC who pauses can withdraw without a penalty. Continuing uses an action and a DC 14 Wisdom saving throw. Success releases the lens. Failure deals **7 (2d6)** psychic damage and leaves the lens attached; the adjacent handler is restrained by the reflected hand, using the full restrained condition. The grip reaches only 5 feet from the intact mirror. An ally can release the character with an action; the restrained PC can also use an action to repeat the save, ending restraint on success. The grip vanishes if the mirror is covered, turned, or its frame destroyed. Withdrawing after the warning costs no reaction. No repeated automatic damage, forced self-harm, or permanent personality change.

**Treasure — lens of inconvenient clarity:** once per dawn, use an action to look through it for one minute. The user sees invisible creatures and objects within 30 feet that are in an otherwise visible line of sight. It does not see through walls, remove darkness, or reveal every illusion. No attunement. The lens also always shows the user's ordinary appearance when used as a mirror. That last quality is unsettling, not a mechanical curse. Broken glass elsewhere in the frame has no additional sale value worth adjudicating.

**Crude solution:** frame AC 13, HP 15, poison/psychic immunity. Smashing it releases the lens intact. An adjacent attacker faces one clearly announced DC 14 Wisdom save, taking 7 psychic damage on failure, none on success; the broken frame cannot sustain restraint. Attacking from beyond 5 feet is safe, as is first covering or stilling the mirror. The image does not become an independent enemy.

## B — The portrait that improves its guest

> An unfinished portrait stands beside an upholstered chair. The face on it is almost yours. While you look, a fine brush lifts itself and begins making a correction. Fastened to its frame is a silver brooch like a tiny open eye.

Same room, portrait in place of mirror. It is unfinished until someone sits; that person is not automatically compelled. Sitting lets the portrait offer a flattering corrected face, with the brooch as its removable center ornament. The back carries a plain study of the same guest room: holding attention on that unidealized version stills the brush. One action lets a helper remove the brooch safely. It is also safe to turn the painting to the wall or dispel its third-level active glamour.

**Risk:** the brooch is the prize, but its closed-eye setting visibly pierces the painted sitter's tongue. A character examining it learns plainly that wearing it uncorrected will twist the sound of their voice toward flattery. State the exact curse below before anyone attunes, without requiring a successful check. For this particular item, Identify also reveals that curse as a special property, rather than relying on a general assumption that the spell detects every curse. DC 14 Arcana reveals it through study; a failed study still sees the danger and can decline to wear it. Removing the brooch does not itself trigger the curse.

**Treasure — brooch of the admired guest:** requires attunement. Three charges, regains all at dawn. When the wearer makes a Charisma (Persuasion) check in conversation, they can spend a charge before rolling to add 1d4. It cannot force agreement, substitute for payment, or change a hostile creature's fundamental goals. This bonus is the useful benefit, not a license for mind control.

**Optional curse, disclosed before attunement:** during its cursed attunement, whether worn or not, the user's voice sounds ingratiating and they have disadvantage on Charisma (Intimidation) checks made by speaking. They can say every word they choose, criticize, warn allies, disagree, and communicate tactical information normally. There is no semantic censorship or test of the player's sincerity. This is a curse on the item's attunement, not on everyone who entered the room. Remove Curse ends that attunement and its restriction immediately; ordinary deattunement cannot do so while cursed. Destroying the brooch (AC 15, HP 10, poison/psychic immunity) ends it too. The active portrait can also correct it: spend an uninterrupted hour restoring its ordinary study with chalk, charcoal, or paint, no proficiency required. That permanently removes the curse but leaves the benefit. The 2-foot-square removable study-panel retains this correction magic independently of the room. The brooch must be present for the hour of restoration. Taking the portrait or that panel makes the remedy portable, so later removal never depends on returning to a vanished room.

**Alternative tone:** omit the curse entirely and retain only its flattering voice. The item remains modest for level 9–10. This is an offered choice for the DM, not a reason to hide a permanent penalty for touching treasure.

Both flavors can be resolved without combat. Keep the original entrance usable; they can inspect, leave, or return later. The room teaches distinguishing an appealing possibility from a useful accurate one without demanding a confessional speech from a player.
