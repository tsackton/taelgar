# E08 — The Death View

Two alternative framings of the **same shared discovery on Vraedhul's mountain**, reached after any entrance. Proposal packet: [[04 Encounter Packet - Vraedhul's Broken Lair]]. Neither adds a viewing-device puzzle, boss fight, or further admission test.

## Shared delivery

**Purpose:** reward entry with confirmation that Vraedhul is dead and that Limbo consumed her. **End:** the party understands those conclusions and can enter the supplicant antechamber. No check is required for either essential conclusion.

The formal route reaches this place through its admitted passage. The offspring routes reach it through the underground kobold network. Narrate that travel briefly unless the party has created a specific pursuit. All three converge before the view; do not give the offspring peaks separate substitutes for the payoff.

For both flavors, the nearest remains are hundreds of feet away over the central chaos, rather than immediately reachable from the viewpoint. The viewpoint is coherent and physically safe. Players may take time to look, talk, sketch, and consider what they have learned. There is no compulsory tremor that immediately pushes them onward. Distant intermittent unmaking conveys loss over time.

Make identity clear using a feature already known from kobold testimony, prior depictions, or the campaign's established description of Vraedhul. If no distinctive feature has been established, the surviving architecture here includes a relief of the three dragons: the great mother and two smaller offspring. It gives a direct comparison of skull shape and scale, not a riddle. Do not assume the players remember an unrecorded horn description. The relief is a proposed local identification aid, not new ancient history.

After describing the image, state what competent observers conclude: **these are Vraedhul's remains; she did not merely abandon the lair. Her body has been consumed and stretched through an eruption of Limbo.** This does not certify a full cosmological explanation, the fate of her soul, or the detailed source of every strange Plaguelands event.

## A — The skull in the wound

> The passage opens on an inward-facing balcony. Below, where the workshops should be, a pale movement turns through the mountain's roots. For a moment it resembles a storm seen from above. Then an immense jaw comes into view. Its teeth are still joined to the skull, though the bone behind them has been drawn into long white lines running through the turning chaos. The dragon is not lying in the ruin. The ruin has gone through her.

The skull remains recognizable each time the slow turn presents it. Some bone is ordinary and some has become repeating ridges or suspended arches. One claw, many times the size of a person, turns impossibly far from the jaw while still joined to it by a stretched line of vertebrae. This is a transformed skeleton rather than a fresh corpse or a fighting undead dragon.

**Three layers to describe:** first, recognizable death; second, a violent outward catastrophe; third, active residue still changing objects. A slab comes loose from a workshop wall, becomes narrow transparent blades, then folds into dark dust. The destruction is centered below and spreads outward into surviving parts, consistent with the workshops as the outbreak's origin.

Answer ordinary questions and plainly visible changes freely. A deliberate Arcana, Religion, Investigation, or other relevant examination can use one DC 14 roll for a genuinely uncertain technical interpretation; this is not a one-question limit. Suitable answers: the changes are still continuing; surviving forms resist them unevenly; already altered material seems to change again most readily. These observations support later exploration. Do not reveal the eruption's whole origin through one generic high roll.

## B — The spine across the workshops

> The wall ends at a broad window in the rock. Through it, an enormous line of bone crosses the empty center of the three peaks. It passes through a workshop roof, comes out as a white stair, and becomes bone again farther on. At its highest point hangs a skull too large for either of the younger dragons. Nothing supports it. Below it, the same stretch of mountain keeps attempting to become something else.

Use a static recognizable skull as the anchor while its stretched skeleton runs across the workshop void. It is **not an accessible bridge** to the other peaks. From the window, foreground rock, intermediate bone, and distant rubble make the impossible scale comprehensible. The nearest remnant is hundreds of feet away above the unformed workshops, rather than hanging within an easy reach from the balcony.

An old worktable can briefly be seen enclosed inside a rib that has become clear crystal. Then the table turns, and the rib closes like a hand. The image suggests a maker's controlled transformations becoming uncontrolled and recursive; it does not imply an unknown new monster is feeding below.

Use the same clear identity aid and optional additional observations as A. The view confirms death even if the PCs did not use the formal gate and never met the keeper. If they paid, it also reframes the tribute machine as authority continuing after its owner died.

## Information magic

Honor the actual spell, range, and targeting. **Speak with Dead** can answer if a PC reaches a sufficiently intact qualifying mouth; transformation alone is not immunity. It can report Vraedhul's experience up to death, including that the workshop's Limbo power overwhelmed her intended control. **Commune** can confirm an eruption from Limbo killed her and that its effects persist; further answers depend on what the contacted power knows under the spell's rules. **Legend Lore**, if the spell's requirements and the subject's significance are met, can connect her cruel transformations, the local weak point, and an eruption greater than her ability to command. None of these requires pretending she is alive.

The precise trigger and sequence remain undecided design questions, not a magical inability of the world to answer them. If the party asks a spell an exact question these drafts have not settled, pause to choose a consistent answer from the maintained backstory or flag that answer for preparation before play; do not invent a false no-information rule. The imagery can remain evocative while a costly information spell produces real information.

## Player questions and transition

- **Can we reach the remains?** They are in the central unformed workshops. Explain the visible severe danger and distance. Long-range viewing magic can inspect them. A viable expedition or retrieval plan would need separate construction; do not turn an ordinary fly spell into automatic death, and do not invent a prepared treasure on the skull.
- **Is she secretly alive?** No, not in these drafts. Do not undermine the accomplishment with a heartbeat or a wink. Her larger metaphysical legacy can remain strange without reopening the settled death.
- **What killed her exactly?** The immediate evidence is an eruption from Limbo. Sequence, causes, and wider consequences remain to explore. Avoid claiming a scavenger or a new predator did it.
- **Can we safely pause?** Yes. This is stable ground unless the party deliberately engages a danger it can see. The place's larger decay matters across absences, not every sentence of conversation.

An ordinary passage behind the viewpoint leads to the shared antechamber. Suggest its surviving doors, dust, and unnaturally luxurious fittings; the room-by-room expedition is available when they choose it. There is no extra checkpoint. The three guest-room proposals beginning with [[VBL G01 - The Generous Table]] can be placed off this antechamber later.

**Balance:** no combat, resource tax, or required check. The discovery is the opening's earned reward, not an invitation to spend another hour proving the premise.
