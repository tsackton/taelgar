# G03 — The Patient's Suite

Two optional guest-room proposals about Vraedhul's cruel cures. Choose one. See [[04 Encounter Packet - Vraedhul's Broken Lair]]. The patient is long gone: neither flavor introduces a new captive whom the party must rescue or a required side quest.

## A — The cure in the cabinet

> The bed has been made with extraordinary care. Beside it, a glass cabinet holds six little silver feet arranged on red cloth. A set of tiny crutches leans against the cabinet. On the wall, a painted dragon watches a limping guest kneel in gratitude.

**Room:** 30 feet square; cabinet on one wall, bed and bedside stool, a second open passage back to the antechamber or another suite. The mural is a cruel joke: Vraedhul improved a guest's useless leg but made the guest return repeatedly for relief. The cabinet is a proposed surviving creation, not proof of an unrelated canonical victim's identity.

The silver feet are parts of the mechanism. A seventh, empty velvet outline lies in the middle. A bottle of clear liquid rests in that outline, visually transparent enough to be missed at first but readily found by examining the cabinet. No roll is required after someone investigates it.

**How it behaves:** reaching into the cabinet while the little feet are tapping makes them animate and catch the intruding wrist. Show their preparatory movement first; withdrawing is safe. Continuing means a DC 14 Dexterity saving throw or grappled; escaping uses an action and DC 14 Athletics or Acrobatics. The feet do not attack or deal damage. An ally can turn them soles-up with an action to end the grip. Breaking the glass (AC 12, HP 5) offers access but does not itself stop the feet; breaking their wooden base (AC 13, HP 12) stops them. Poison and psychic damage do not affect the objects.

**A gentler manipulation:** place the bedside stool before the cabinet and attend to the creation as a finished, satisfied patient at rest. The feet become still, and the empty central outline becomes visible around the bottle. This needs one action, no particular phrase or roll. Covering or stilling the tapping feet with cloth and tools can achieve the same practical result. Dispel Magic ends the third-level active cabinet effect without destroying its contents.

**Treasure — three draughts of sure footing:** the bottle contains exactly three doses. Drinking one is an action and grants, for ten minutes, advantage on checks and saves made specifically to avoid being knocked prone or physically moved against the drinker's will. It does not resist planar removal such as Banishment. It also grants a climbing speed equal to walking speed on ordinary stone, without allowing ceiling-walking or ignoring a surface that ceases to be solid. Multiple doses do not stack. The bottle does not refill. The cabinet's repeat-fee mechanism is broken; there is no hidden dependency, relapse, or later curse. The limited supply makes using it at a later traversal challenge or a deliberate return through Zaurmath's passage meaningful.

**Learning:** the cabinet has possibilities of restless treatment and completed recovery; acting on the latter makes the object behave differently. They have earned a small actual reward and a glimpse of the dragon's humor, not another obligation.

## B — Pain kept in crystal

> A clear crystal rests in a padded frame beside the bed. Within it, something dark contracts whenever the room's light changes. The bed is spotless. The wooden chair beside it has deep hand-shaped dents in both arms.

The former guest's pain was extracted into a physical vessel, then kept as part of the accommodation. The dark shape is a stored magical sensation, not a sentient trapped person. Saying this when examined keeps the scene from becoming a rescue dilemma by implication.

The crystal is attached to its frame by three simple clips. Touching it directly transmits a small warning sting with no damage; describe the clear relationship between contact and discomfort. Lifting it barehanded deals **7 (2d6)** psychic damage once, then leaves the creature free to put it down. Cloth, tongs, gloves sufficiently thick to prevent skin contact, or Mage Hand can move it safely. Do not secretly bypass an effective protective plan. Holding attention on its visible clear outer shell while closing the frame's shutters calms it for one minute and also permits safe handling. DC 14 Arcana can identify this before testing; it is not the only solution.

**Treasure — vessel of the lesser hurt:** three charges, never recharges. A creature holding its padded frame in one hand can use a reaction when damage to it or an ally within 30 feet is rolled, before HP loss, concentration checks, or death is resolved, expending one charge to reduce that damage by **2d8**. It requires line of sight, cannot reduce damage below zero, and helps only one damage instance. The crystal darkens after each use; after the last it becomes inert clear quartz worth 25 gp. No attunement. It cannot store infinite damage, prevent a curse, reverse a death, or transfer pain to another unwilling creature. The padded frame is portable and included in the reward.

**Crude handling:** breaking the charged crystal ends the item and releases one **7 (2d6)** psychic pulse to creatures within 10 feet, DC 14 Wisdom half, then nothing more. Its hairline warning cracks and the frame's shutters make that risk legible. Crystal AC 13, HP 8; poison/psychic immunity to damage against the object. If the party asks what breaking it might do, tell them the stored sensation would spill out rather than concealing a gotcha.

## Outcomes and pacing

Both versions can be a brief room visit with one telling image, one practical manipulation, and limited treasure. None requires initiative unless someone insists on acting simultaneously against an active mechanism. No fight has the sole goal of killing a creature. Keep exits open and let a sound plan work. No balance increase for level 10 is necessary; these are small rewards whose usefulness comes from later choices.
