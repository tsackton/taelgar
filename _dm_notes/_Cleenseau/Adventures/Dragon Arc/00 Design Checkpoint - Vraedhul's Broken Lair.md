## Where the design stands

Premise design has been worked through: purpose, starting objective, graded outcomes, central challenge, main activity, inertia, momentum, and failure handling are recorded. The opening's principal relationships have been chosen, and first-pass cores exist.

The three beats are:

1. Enter the domain and discover Vraedhul is dead.
2. Go deeper through the supplicant rooms, exploring and learning how the dungeon works.
3. Emerge into the audience chamber, overcome its challenge, enter the personal lair, and earn the richest rewards.

Only the opening is substantially outlined. The guest-quarter crawl has a shape and purpose but no room inventory or actual map. The capstone has a role but no selected central obstacle.

The user wants to finish the outside and entrances now because the next planned game would cover the opening, with roughly three to four hours after wrapping up the previous arc. The broader initial estimate was two sessions, ordinarily about six hours each. These are planning context, not a fixed pacing system. If resuming after the original upcoming game, first establish what has occurred in play.

The user explicitly redirected the assistant away from an attempted audience-chamber design. Do not treat that deferral as a gap that must be filled before preparing the opening.

## What the adventure is trying to feel like

The party is level 9. This is a change of mood after heavy plot and intense decision making. The user wants:

- Interesting magical treasure that the players earn.
- The pleasures of a classic dungeon crawl: enter a room, investigate it, find things, handle danger, move onward.
- Opportunities to learn why the Plaguelands have recently been strange.
- Strange and cruel magical creations expressing the dead dragon's personality.
- NPC interaction as well as exploration and purposeful combat.
- A sense of a temporary opportunity being lost, without constant pressure to decide faster.

The dungeon should not be an abstract series of equally elaborate puzzles. The user specifically wants a sustained room-by-room section, probably in the supplicant quarters. It can expand to a whole session if everyone enjoys it, or be shortened by skipping optional material if it drags.

There is no adopted world-saving assignment, moral dilemma at every turn, mandatory creature rescue, or requirement to repair the entire planar disaster. Helping the maker is one route through one entrance.

## Essential campaign and fictional context

The PCs killed Krauzeth, Vraedhul's younger offspring, in an earlier part of the Cleenseau campaign. They have relationships with some of his kobolds and heard part of the family's story. Kobold testimony gives credible reason to believe Vraedhul is dead, but rumors and uncertainty remain. Confirming her death is worth doing, and the natural appeal of looting a dragon's lair supplies motivation without needing a newly invented patron.

Vraedhul was immensely powerful. She settled here because of a connection to Limbo and used its order-bending potential to infuse objects. She cultivated strange, cruel possibilities in precious things and people. The cruel joke is central to her favorite work. She enjoyed dangerous and cursed objects, sent curses into the world, and sometimes charged victims heavily to remove them.

She was unwelcoming and did not want unworthy visitors wasting her attention. Appropriate tribute could interest her through value, weirdness, cruelty, or possibilities for transformation. Her promises did not guarantee safety or honorable behavior. The tone should arise from power, appetite, and physical magic rather than fey bargain wording and loopholes.

She died about three months before this expedition in an eruption from Limbo. Zaurmath died quickly defending her. Krauzeth fled with kobolds and was later killed by the PCs.

Recent Limbo spill through weak spots is behind strange Plaguelands events. Residue is now unmaking the domain. The local outbreak began at the workshops, which are probably already pure chaos. The exact eruption sequence and its wider consequences remain material to discover and design.

## The physical plan to preserve

The domain has three peaks, one for each dragon, with the workshop complex around the bases between them. Dragons flew between peaks. The adopted internal connections are underground kobold tunnels; do not silently invent surface bridges or dragon-sized corridors joining all three peaks.

Entrance 1 is the formal visitor approach into Vraedhul's mountain. The other two approach the offspring lairs. Precise assignment of the sketch's B/C labels and entrance numbers to Krauzeth and Zaurmath is not fixed in these notes.

The core of Vraedhul's mountain contains:

- A shared antechamber.
- Supplicant suites, increasingly elaborate for favored or mocked guests, with individual passages to the antechamber.
- Kobold-tunnel connections to that antechamber.
- An audience or throne chamber.
- A huge personal lair containing favorite dangerous creations and the best treasure.

The personal lair may be a vast cavern or a mountain-top pavilion; neither is settled. The guest-room sketch is conceptual, not a fixed room count. The eventual crawl should have a short main route, optional suites, and some connections between routes. Its exact relationship to the shared discovery viewpoint remains to be mapped.

## The opening's adopted flow

Shared dangerous approaches lead to a choice of three entrances. Overcoming the chosen entrance earns access to a single shared discovery location on Vraedhul's mountain. Offspring routes use the kobold network to get there.

The entrances are alternatives, not three required trials. The PCs can attempt the main entrance immediately. The earlier idea that simpler offspring areas might help before attempting the richer main complex does not create a prerequisite to complete both offspring lairs.

The outside rewards reconnaissance. The user explicitly rejected finding useful tribute in the landscape. Its information lets the party choose the challenge best matched to their capabilities and willingness to spend resources.

The formal entrance might be physically shorter than the offspring routes. That prompted a pacing concern, not a decision to abandon the shared viewpoint or force identical route lengths. Its admission process and social possibilities can provide substantial play. Do not solve the concern by adding an unchosen extra gate after the entrances.

## Entrance 1: payment, judgment, and the Arborean maker

### What is settled

The formal entrance is a grandiose representation of Vraedhul, with a Wizard of Oz quality: theatrical scale, commanding voice, and an image of overwhelming authority. It has real power. The working physical concept combines a watching eye, an offering-handling claw, and a mouth that speaks and controls the passage.

A fitting valuable offering makes entry fairly straightforward. The players may be reluctant to surrender magic items; that is intended. Combat and stealth are difficult but legitimate alternatives. The keeper adds a social route, not a compulsory three-stage interview before payment can work.

The selected keeper is an Arborean maker, a being associated with cultivating magical wonders. Vraedhul bound something not naturally her ally and made its creative power serve her own purposes. Its exact species, name, gender, shape, original craft, and statistic block are open.

It was not finally selected as a djinni. A deva-like manifestation was mentioned as a possible lore framework, not adopted as its creature type. Do not assume standard D&D Arborea or celestial lore supplies the missing details.

### The three versions

The user chose these particular perspectives:

1. One believes it is back in Arborea.
2. One understands its actual predicament.
3. One believes it is still Vraedhul's guardian.

These replace earlier assistant suggestions about an ambitious new self or a servant that necessarily loves its authority. Whether the guardian version values its office, is merely compelled, or imagines a particular past moment remains open.

The versions disagree about what is happening. They are not simply one truth-teller and two liars, nor three separate judges to satisfy. The Arborea version must genuinely experience something different from the truth-aware one; it is not just the same captive in a friendlier mood.

The user adopted the party's ability to influence which version comes forward through actions and attention. The working presentation was one dominant perspective at a time, with recognizable shifts. No exact activation phrases, transition table, check procedure, or physical markers are fixed.

Possible examples, not selected mechanics: offerings draw out the guardian; engaging with creative growth draws out the Arborea perspective; attending to captivity or a binding component draws out the truth-aware version.

### The optional cooperation route

The party can gain cooperation and loosen the binding as an alternative to payment. The captive can help while the entrance's compelled defenses remain dangerous. A fight here can therefore involve disabling constraints or gaining an opening rather than killing the captive.

Full release versus partial loosening has not been chosen. There is no assigned wish, boon, homecoming quest, or promised later reward. Do not turn a useful optional interaction into a required rescue adventure.

Binding persists despite Vraedhul's death. An anchor in the entrance itself was a possible explanation, not a final mechanism. Physical anchors and what changes when they are disrupted are important construction work.

The truth-aware version recognizes its situation, but what it witnessed of Vraedhul's death or the eruption is unresolved. Useful testimony can coexist with the shared viewpoint as physical confirmation. Avoid making it omniscient or arbitrarily unable to explain itself merely to preserve a reveal.

## Krauzeth's entrance: combat with linked outcomes

The route has two connected parts:

1. An outer watchpost, with loyalist kobolds and a partially tamed creature. Alarm traps may supplement it.
2. A main encounter in which some kobolds delay intruders while others retreat into the tunnel network and destroy or seal access behind them.

The precursor objective is to prevent a warning. It need not be a full battle. The warning method should be observable and interruptible.

No warning means the passage is intact and the defenders unprepared. A warning means destruction has begun and is more advanced when the party reaches it. Those are outcome-dependent starting conditions, not yet numerical settings.

The main fight's objective is to secure access and get through before the route is lost. Killing all the kobolds might be too late if the passage is wrecked; success might also be possible with defenders still alive.

The causal trigger matters. The user objected to monsters supposedly destroying an entrance continuously for three months. The alarm starts the process. Do not replace that with an unexplained permanent demolition scene.

The precise loyalists, creature, handlers, alarm, battlefield, tools, and rate of destruction are not chosen. Existing named kobolds and their relationships to the PCs should not be reassigned casually.

The partly tamed creature is a current ingredient and is distinct from the discarded Limbo scavenger. It has no adopted enchantment-eating, feeding clock, or portal function.

## Zaurmath's entrance: a readable environmental crossing

Most of this lair is gone. Its surviving challenge is one substantial ruined chamber, with a visible onward tunnel and a breach on one side toward which reality grows less coherent.

The user required clear gradation:

- Relatively stable edges: observe, investigate, and prepare.
- Changing ground: the main crossing hazards, with recognizable precursors and opportunities to recover.
- Unforming breach: unmistakably extreme danger that can be avoided entirely.

Possible crossings include exposed outer ledges, with climbing and gaps, or a more direct route across changing material. Equipment, skills, and spells should genuinely help.

The character of Limbo comes from changes of substance and form, not just difficult footing. Examples offered included stone becoming water or glass, burning dust, and air becoming solid. Wet sheen before liquefaction and dust gathering before air hardens were illustrative tells. The adopted requirement is intelligible warnings and graduated danger; no exact effect cycle, damage, duration, check difficulty, or anti-magic restriction exists yet.

The geometry should remain understandable. The goal is to diagnose and negotiate the crossing, not survive arbitrary remapping or guess an invisible safe square.

## Shared discovery: the earned payoff

The user rejected several early corpse ideas and arrived at a distinctive image: Vraedhul's recognizable transformed skeletal or skull-like remains stretched through or intertwined with the central workshop chaos.

Reaching a clear view of this is an accomplishment earned by overcoming an entrance. The shared-view arrangement was preferred over giving every peak its own independent death-revelation scene.

The view should establish:

- Vraedhul is dead.
- An eruption of Limbo consumed her.

The detailed sequence and continuing consequences need further exploration. The initial scene should not explain the entire Plaguelands situation.

No further mandatory admission puzzle is required here. A viewing crystal or interpretive mechanism was once suggested but never adopted as a new obstacle. The exact terrace, opening, sightline, recognizable features, and route onward still need construction.

## Why the lair survives and what pressure should feel like

The user wanted active change but worried about frustrating navigation. The adopted explanation is accelerating unmaking: substantial structure survived, isolated transformations spread, previously altered things become easier to change, and pockets now join.

That explains both three months of survival and faster recent decay. The eventual state is an unformed wasteland or cyclone.

This is a vanishing opportunity, not a constant drumbeat. Patient experimentation during a visit is welcome. A lengthy absence can cost routes, treasure, or safety. An encounter can have immediate pressure, such as the kobolds' demolition, without turning the entire expedition into a minute-by-minute race.

No global numerical decay track or exact resting schedule is set. Do not invent one merely because a design template asks about inertia.

## The recurring attention principle

The adopted principle is:

> Directing attention toward one possibility already contained in a Limbo-altered creation helps that possibility hold its form or function.

The maker introduces this through incompatible versions coming forward. Guest-room objects can express it in other ways. The user agreed it could recur through the crawl and later challenges.

This is bounded influence through existing creations, not unrestricted imagination magic. Nor does it mean every room needs three personalities, three choices, or identical procedures.

The exact action, resource, concentration, check, duration, and opposition mechanics remain undecided. The principle supplies a coherent reason learned behavior can improve later outcomes; it is not a finished universal subsystem.

The assistant proposed an audience chamber requiring coordination of several creations at once. The user did not adopt that challenge and explicitly paused capstone development. Preserve that distinction.

## Failure and combat requirements

Every fight needs a concrete objective beyond killing the opposition, including fights the party initiates. The user cited another game's prisoner protection, alarm prevention, and key-carrying escape as examples of the desired tactical interest. These are reference patterns, not requested encounters to copy literally.

Failures can cost resources, position, information, access, or a particular treasure. Repeated mishandling or deliberate risk can produce substantial loss. A curse or other lasting effect must have a clear removal path. Removing it may become a side quest whose cost is time away from the decaying lair.

Losing Krauzeth's passage does not automatically end the whole expedition; another entrance can remain available. Incidental attrition is not a substitute for a meaningful encounter objective.

The intention is real danger and recoverable setbacks, with graded expedition outcomes. There is no secretly correct requirement to rescue every NPC, clear every room, preserve all treasure, or stop the planar disaster.

## Collaboration context for a future assistant

The user found the earlier discussion circular and asked whether the adventure-design skill was really being used. They wanted visible progression through premise, alignment, connected structure, and scene cores.

What will help:

- Start from the decisions here; do not ask the user to restate purpose, party level, geography, or the three entrance types.
- Ask one focused question when a real decision is needed, but keep the larger checkpoint visible. A succession of isolated yes/no questions is not itself good structure.
- When the user struggles with specifics, offer a few concrete images and interactions to react to. Explain what players see, what they can do, and what outcomes follow.
- Mark proposals as proposals. A pleasant response to a general image does not automatically adopt all its mechanics or every illustrative detail.
- Consolidate adopted decisions into usable cores before advancing.
- Preserve the current priority. Do not jump ahead to the capstone on the claim that the opening cannot be finished without it.
- Use diagrams or spatial sketches when topology becomes hard to visualize.
- Do not impose exact play lengths, a fixed scene quota, elaborate subsystems, or extra gates solely to satisfy a template.
- Keep preparation practical enough for a DM who has more time now than before the following game.

The skill's authorship boundary still matters: help articulate and refine the user's adventure rather than silently filling all unknowns and presenting the result as established. The current request authorizes documenting context; it does not authorize promoting this material into canonical setting pages or accepting new adventure ideas on the user's behalf.

## A useful way to resume

After reading the packet and the live skill:

1. Briefly state the current scope and acknowledge the opening's already-set structure.
2. If the original next game has happened, establish which material was played and what changed.
3. Continue with concrete outside design: a small coherent approach area, useful reconnaissance for each entrance, actual inhabitants and dangers, and purposeful objectives for any fights. No useful tribute cache and no revived stair-maintaining elemental.
4. Finish the formal keeper's play details: cues, gifts, binding, cooperation, and physical alternatives.
5. Finish the watchpost-to-demolition encounter and its outcome states.
6. Finish the environmental chamber's actual hazards and readable rules.
7. Finish the discovery's physical presentation and transition.
8. Begin a guest-room map and a few room contents. Keep the main route short enough to bypass optional material when needed.
9. Return to the capstone only when the user reopens it.

Do not restart with an adventure premise worksheet. The next useful move is a concrete outside or entrance design decision informed by the existing structure.

Arborea here means overwhelming untamed life. Limbo means unceasing transformation and the shaping force of will; full-plane encounters were atmospheric references, not a mandate to give level 9 characters those dangers or powers. Deva metaphysics and species are not final.
