## Running assumptions

Four level 9–10 PCs; no particular class mix or magic-item inventory is assumed. Mechanics use ordinary fifth-edition actions, attacks, saves, spells, and movement. All compact creature profiles are **original encounter shorthand**, not official monsters or tested CR assignments. Unlisted saving throws use the printed ability modifier; no hidden resistances, legendary actions, or immunities exist. Armor Class is AC, hit points HP, Difficulty Class DC. Damage can be rolled or use its printed average. Native-language communication and telepathy depend on the actual creature and spells, not an automatic universal tongue.

The intent is modest resource use at the river, an optional short rescue, then one substantial entrance challenge. Do not run all entrance fights as an attrition itinerary. Characters can rest in coherent ground unless they create a specific local pursuit; there is no new universal rest prohibition or minute-by-minute decay clock.

Use listed numbers for level 9. A level 10 party does not automatically require harder numbers. Each combat gives an optional adjustment for a strong, fresh party; choose it before play. If the party is depleted, reduce an enemy's HP or remove a defender rather than secretly changing attack results. Successful control spells, flight, teleportation, scouting, and other expensive abilities should genuinely help. Do not compensate for a clever bypass with surprise reinforcements.

## Common rulings

- **Information first:** describe danger and visible enemy work before asking for initiative or checks. No perception roll is needed to notice a creature plainly charging or workers visibly sealing a tunnel.
- **One uncertainty, one roll:** a careful sound method in a safe area works. Roll when failure would change the situation. Help, tools, and useful spells should reduce rolls, not produce a longer checklist.
- **Damage and objects:** object profiles apply to the local mechanism, not the entire mountain. Only damage that can reasonably affect the material counts; state any immunity on that object. Do not invent general spell or weapon immunity.
- **Limbo influence:** acting on a possibility already present can stabilize it. No universal concentration tax is added. Specific room effects state their duration and interruption conditions. This is not a new character power to reshape everything outdoors.
- **Lasting effects:** every proposed lasting curse lists a removal path. No curse is secretly imposed merely for looking at an item. Say what handling or using it risks.
- **Rewards:** outdoor scouting gives knowledge, not conveniently sufficient tribute. Creature remains are unstable debris rather than a substitute admission gift. Guest-room items are proposed actual treasure; review them against the party's current items before use.

## Balance references and limits

The official [2014 encounter-building guidance](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/basic-rules-2014/building-combat-encounters) gives four-PC thresholds of 2,200/4,400/6,400/9,600 adjusted XP at level 9 and 2,400/4,800/7,600/11,200 at level 10. Those budgets do **not** certify these homebrew creatures. Reviews instead check likely rounds, damage, action economy, objective timing, recovery, and common spells. Difficulty labels below are design estimates, not playtest results.
