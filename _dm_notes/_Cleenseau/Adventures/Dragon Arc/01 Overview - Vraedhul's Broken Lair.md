# Vraedhul's Broken Lair Overview

## Design context

This adventure continues the existing [[Dragon Arc - Backstory|Dragon Arc]] after [[Krauzeth]]'s death. It is intended to run for approximately two sessions as a classic D&D dungeon crawl emphasizing exploration, complex puzzle rooms, worthwhile treasure, and relatively little combat.

The adventure should provide a satisfying expedition into [[Vraedhul]]'s ruined lair without requiring a rescue mission or opening a larger campaign arc. Its central tension should come from deciding how much of the lair to explore and what treasure to pursue while a stranded creature from [[Limbo]] consumes the remaining magic.

## Brief overview

The PCs enter Vraedhul's ruined lair to scout the site and confirm that the dragon is dead. The Limbo incursion left the lair's geography confused and rearranged, but it is static during the adventure rather than changing around the PCs.

The PCs quickly find decisive evidence that Vraedhul died. They then discover a magical scavenger moving through the deeper lair and consuming enchanted objects. The creature is not evil: it is panicked by its fixed physical form, finds the stable matter of Taelgar grotesque and unnatural, and wants to return to Limbo. If it consumes enough magic, it can open a route home.

The PCs can hunt the creature, loot the lair before it reaches the remaining caches, or learn its nature and safely feed it cursed objects so that it can return to Limbo. Careful exploration and successful puzzle-solving should make it possible to send the creature home while preserving much of the worthwhile, noncursed treasure.

## Starting point

The PCs are asked or otherwise motivated to scout Vraedhul's old lair, assess its present danger, and confirm whether Vraedhul is dead. The exact quest giver and immediate evidence that prompts the expedition remain undecided.

## Adventure goal

The initial concrete assignment is to confirm Vraedhul's death and return with an accurate assessment of the lair.

Finding proof of her death completes that initial mission and reveals the adventure's deeper situation. From that point, the PCs choose how much treasure to recover and what to do about the scavenger. The final outcome is therefore partly discrete and partly determined by how much of the lair they understand and what they recover or sacrifice.

## Likely outcomes

- The PCs confirm Vraedhul's death, learn the scavenger's nature, feed it dangerous cursed items, send it safely home, and recover substantial worthwhile treasure.
- The PCs send the scavenger home but must sacrifice desirable magic because they did not find enough suitable cursed fuel or did not fully understand the lair's planar workings.
- The PCs kill the scavenger and claim whatever treasure remains.
- The PCs confirm Vraedhul's death and withdraw with limited treasure, leaving the scavenger and the deeper lair unresolved.
- Delay or repeated failure allows the scavenger to consume much of the best remaining magic and potentially open an uncontrolled route to Limbo.

These outcomes should not imply that killing the scavenger or helping it is the secretly correct choice. The creature's non-evil nature and desire to return home should be discoverable in time for the PCs to make an informed decision.

## Central challenge

The PCs must navigate Vraedhul's static but spatially scrambled lair, overcome complex room-scale puzzles, distinguish useful treasure from dangerous cursed objects, and decide how to spend their limited exploration time while the scavenger consumes the remaining magic.

## X-factor

The primary activity is exploration and acquisition. The likely repeating loop is:

1. enter or gain access to a displaced room or small complex;
2. understand its spatial rule, puzzle, curse, or other obstacle;
3. decide whether its information or treasure justifies the time and risk;
4. recover safe treasure, cursed fuel, or knowledge about the scavenger and planar rupture; and
5. use that progress to make later exploration or the final resolution safer and more effective.

## Inertia

The scavenger is actively consuming enchanted objects. Time spent exploring, retreating, resting, or failing can therefore reduce the treasure that remains. This pressure should affect the quality of the final outcome rather than create an immediate rescue or world-ending deadline.

The creature must have gained access to the surviving hoard recently enough for this accelerated consumption to be plausible. How it was released remains unresolved.

## Momentum

Exploration should improve the PCs' ability to achieve a favorable combined outcome:

- early puzzles teach principles used by later rooms;
- discoveries reveal which objects are cursed and how to handle them;
- tools or procedures from Vraedhul's workshop make cursed items safer to transport or feed to the scavenger;
- planar clues reveal that the scavenger wants to return home rather than simply destroy the lair; and
- deeper understanding reduces the amount or value of magic that must be sacrificed to open a controlled route to Limbo.

## Failure handling

Local failures should cost time, expose the PCs to curses or hazards, close off particular treasures, or allow the scavenger to consume another cache. A failed puzzle should change the situation or force a cost rather than halt the adventure.

Repeated failures can substantially reduce the available treasure or lead to an uncontrolled portal, but the PCs should ordinarily remain able to confirm Vraedhul's death and leave the lair.

## Design statement

The PCs enter Vraedhul's ruined lair to confirm her death, but discover a stranded Limbo scavenger consuming the remaining magic; they must explore the scrambled ruin and decide whether to kill it, race it for the treasure, or feed it cursed objects and help it return home.

## Dramatic question

How much of Vraedhul's hoard can the PCs understand and recover before the scavenger consumes it, and what will they do when they learn that the creature is merely trying to go home?

## Working structure

The adventure has an adopted two-session spine.

### Session 1: The Dead Dragon

- Reach and enter the displaced ruins.
- Learn how the static but scrambled architecture works.
- Encounter the first cursed treasure and establish that objects require careful handling.
- Find definitive proof that Vraedhul is dead.
- Discover evidence that something is consuming enchantment.
- End with the reveal of the scavenger, ideally by witnessing it feed or finding that a nearby cache has just been stripped of magic.

### Session 2: The Race Through the Hoard

- Explore the deeper lair for worthwhile treasure, cursed fuel, and knowledge of the planar rupture.
- Decide which routes and caches justify the time and risk.
- Learn enough to understand the scavenger's nature and possible return.
- Reach the planar heart of the lair.
- Kill the scavenger, withdraw with selected loot, or feed it cursed objects and open a controlled passage to Limbo.

A three-wing hub was proposed for the deeper lair, with functions corresponding to Vraedhul's workshop, the broken hoard, and the planar scar. A continuous looping route containing the same functions remains an alternative. Neither shape has been adopted.

## Alignment check

- The initial goal is concrete and can be completed when the PCs confirm Vraedhul's death.
- The central challenge concerns exploration, puzzle-solving, curse handling, and treasure decisions rather than incidental combat.
- The scavenger's consumption pushes play toward poorer treasure outcomes.
- Knowledge, tools, and recovered cursed objects improve the PCs' later options.
- Local failures impose costs without creating accidental dead ends.
- The scavenger's feeding and stripped caches should make the pressure visible.

## Open design questions

- Who sends the PCs to the lair, and what prompts the expedition now?
- What form provides definitive proof of Vraedhul's death?
- What recently released the scavenger into the surviving portions of the lair?
- Does the deeper dungeon use an open three-wing hub, a continuous looping route, or another shape?
- How is the scavenger's ongoing consumption tracked and communicated during play?
- What exact discoveries reveal that the scavenger is frightened, non-evil, and trying to return home?
- What planar process opens a controlled route, and how do puzzle successes reduce its magical cost?
- Which cursed items are available as fuel, and what makes each difficult to identify, handle, or move?
- What safe treasures are worthwhile enough to make route and delay choices meaningful?
- How many combats belong in the dungeon, and what functions do they serve?
- Does a surviving Limbo-damaged kobold improve the adventure as an unreliable guide or mouthpiece, or should the dungeon work without that NPC?
- If the kobold is used, are its three apparent minds the kobold before the catastrophe, the present kobold, and a fragment of the scavenger? This remains provisional and may be dropped for simplicity.
