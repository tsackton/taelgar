## Design context

A level 9 Cleenseau adventure following the party's killing of [[Krauzeth]]. It serves three purposes:

- Earn interesting magical treasure.
- Provide a change of mood from heavy plot and intense decisions to a classic dungeon expedition.
- Provide a vehicle for learning why things have been strange in the Plaguelands.

The initial scope was approximately two sessions, ordinarily about six hours each. Pacing is flexible: the guest-quarter crawl can occupy an entire session if enjoyable, or be partly skipped if dragging. Earlier estimates of a half-session crawl and a roughly two-hour capstone are loose planning ideas, not quotas.

At this checkpoint, the next game is expected to cover arrival and discovery, with roughly three to four hours available after wrapping up the previous arc. Preparation priority is to finish the outside and entrances, then start sketching the guest-quarter crawl. Audience-chamber and personal-lair development is deferred.

Exploration, experimentation, worthwhile rewards, and opportunities to interact with NPCs should drive play. Every fight must have a concrete objective beyond killing enemies, including fights initiated by the PCs.

## Brief overview

An eruption of [[Limbo]] killed [[Vraedhul]] about three months ago. Residual chaos is progressively consuming her three-peak domain. The workshops at its center are already largely unformed, while enough of the mountains, passages, and creations remain coherent to explore.

The party scouts the dangerous outer grounds and chooses among three entrances: tribute and social interaction at Vraedhul's formal entrance, combat at Krauzeth's abandoned lair, or environmental traversal through Zaurmath's devastated lair. Overcoming any entrance earns access to a shared viewpoint that confirms Vraedhul's death.

Deeper exploration passes through the supplicant quarters, then an audience-chamber and personal-lair capstone. Vraedhul's strange and cruel creations supply both challenges and desirable treasure.

## Starting point

Rumors leave uncertainty about Vraedhul's death. The PCs have credible reason to believe she is dead from kobolds who traveled with Krauzeth; they have killed him, have relationships with some of those kobolds, and know some of the family's history.

Confirming her death provides the initial objective. Looting a dragon's lair is already a strong motivation. No particular patron or new quest giver has been selected or is required by the current outline.

The shared approaches are dangerous and inhabited by elementals, chaos-altered creatures, and other hazards. Exploring them provides knowledge of the entrances and their relative demands. It does not provide useful tribute.

## Adventure goal

The opening has a concrete success state: reach an intelligible view of Vraedhul's recognizable transformed remains and confirm her death.

The larger expedition has graded success: recover worthwhile magical treasure and learn about the eruption and its continuing consequences. The richest rewards are in her personal lair. Confirming her death remains an accomplishment even if the PCs do not reach those rewards.

Stopping the eruption, closing a planar breach, or rescuing a creature is not an adopted adventure goal.

## Likely outcomes

- Best result: confirm her death, understand more of the eruption and Plaguelands disturbances, and recover substantial worthwhile treasure, including personal-lair prizes.
- Partial success: leave with proof, some discoveries, and some treasure while other mysteries and prizes remain inaccessible.
- Costly success: make gains but lose a particular prize, sacrifice valuable tribute, incur resource costs, or acquire a curse requiring time away to remove.
- Poor result: withdraw with little gained; continuing decay makes a return less promising.
- A failed entrance may become unusable, leaving other entrances available. Retreat carries incurred costs rather than resetting the situation.

Treasure recovered, knowledge gained, and proof of death can vary independently. There is no single all-or-nothing treasure threshold.

## Central challenge

Understand and overcome dangerous creations and localized Limbo transformations well enough to gain access, explore, and recover treasure from a progressively unmade domain.

The entrances offer an informed choice between different demands. The PCs can choose the challenge best suited to their abilities and willingness to spend resources:

- Formal entrance: an appropriate valuable gift makes admission fairly straightforward; avoiding payment is harder.
- Krauzeth's entrance: combat with warning and passage-preservation objectives.
- Zaurmath's entrance: skills, observation, equipment, and magic used to diagnose and bypass environmental dangers.

## X-factor

Exploration and acquisition, supported by social interaction, traversal, experimentation, and purposeful combat.

The repeating loop is to observe a place or creation, learn its behavior, choose an approach, overcome or work around its dangers, and gain access, knowledge, or treasure that improves later opportunities.

The supplicant quarters should deliver the concrete rhythm of entering a room, exploring it, and moving to the next. Not every room needs an elaborate puzzle.

## Inertia

Limbo is slowly eating the lair, with accelerating deterioration. Initially isolated changes were held apart by substantial surviving physical and magical structure. Altered material changes more readily; pockets spread and join, speeding the unmaking.

This supplies a vanishing opportunity without a constant countdown. Patient experimentation during a visit is compatible with the tone. Prolonged delays and absences can change routes, erase prizes, and make returning more dangerous. Landmarks and learned behaviors should remain useful; arbitrary maze resets would undermine the intended exploration.

Local urgency is possible within an encounter. On Krauzeth's route, raising the alarm starts demolition and worsens the main fight's initial conditions. This is a consequence of a warning, not destruction that has inexplicably continued for months.

## Momentum

- Outdoor reconnaissance reveals the three entrance types and helps the party choose a favorable challenge.
- Preventing Krauzeth's outer alarm preserves an intact passage and catches the inner defenders unprepared.
- Experiments reveal readable environmental warning signs and useful routes.
- Understanding the maker's perspectives allows deliberate influence over which version comes forward.
- A recurring adopted principle: directing attention toward one possibility already contained in a Limbo-altered creation helps that possibility hold its form or function.
- Guest-quarter discoveries should provide useful knowledge or tools for later challenges. Exploration improves readiness; it need not become a requirement to clear every room.

Exact later applications of the attention principle remain to be designed.

## Failure handling

Local failures change the situation, consume resources, or close a particular opportunity. Experiments can reveal useful information even when they go badly. Repeated mishandling or knowingly pushing beyond warnings can cost access to a prize.

Curses and other lasting effects must have a clear removal path. Removing one can become a side quest; time spent away from the lair is a meaningful cost because decay continues.

On Krauzeth's route, the earlier encounter's outcome changes the main encounter. An alarm means demolition is more advanced. Losing the passage can force another entrance even if the PCs defeat the defenders.

Environmental mistakes should allow recovery where appropriate, through lost positioning, rescue, equipment, health, or magic. The visibly unforming breach is avoidable; crossing it is not required to complete Zaurmath's route.

Combat outcomes must depend on concrete objectives beyond killing: prevent a warning, preserve or open a passage, interfere with a binding, or accomplish another encounter-specific task. Actual outside fights still need those additional objectives selected.

## Design statement

The PCs enter Vraedhul's decaying domain to confirm her death and recover magical treasure, but must choose an entrance, understand the dangerous possibilities in her creations, and earn what they can before the place is unmade.

## Dramatic question

What can the party understand and recover from Vraedhul's legacy while enough of it still holds its form?

## Working structure

The actual connected outline is in [[03 Scenes - Vraedhul's Broken Lair.canvas]].

### Beat 1: arrival and discovery

Shared dangerous approaches lead to a choice of three entrances. Solving the chosen entrance earns access to one shared discovery location on Vraedhul's mountain. The offspring routes connect through the kobold tunnel network.

- Formal: tribute to a grandiose likeness of Vraedhul, with a bound, Limbo-altered Arborean maker supplying the social element. Loosening its binding is an optional route through; difficult combat or stealth also remain possible.
- Krauzeth: an outer watchpost where a warning can be prevented, followed by a main fight over access to a passage that loyalist kobolds are beginning to seal.
- Zaurmath: cross one ruined chamber with graded transformation hazards and a visible onward tunnel.

The shared view confirms that Vraedhul is dead and that an eruption from Limbo consumed her. Details and consequences require more exploration. No additional admission challenge is needed to earn this revelation after overcoming an entrance.

### Beat 2: the supplicant-quarter crawl

A short main route with optional suites and some connections between routes. The suites grow increasingly elaborate for favored or mocked guests. Exploration provides treasure, experiments, and knowledge.

The original circulation includes a shared antechamber with individual passages to suites and connections to the kobold tunnels. How that plan becomes the exact traversable dungeon remains to be mapped. No room count, room inventory, or complete crawl map has been adopted.

### Beat 3: audience chamber and personal lair

The audience chamber followed by the personal lair is the adventure's capstone, with the best treasure and opportunities to apply learning. Its specific challenges remain deferred.

The proposal that the audience chamber requires coordinating several creations at once was not adopted before the user redirected work to the opening. Do not treat that proposal as a settled challenge.

## Alignment check

- Confirming death is a finishable opening objective with a recognizable, earned payoff.
- Reconnaissance improves an actual choice between payment, combat, and environment.
- Earlier outcomes change later conditions on Krauzeth's route.
- Attention and learned behavior provide a coherent recurring connection between creations.
- Accelerating decay threatens opportunity over meaningful absences without imposing constant haste.
- Local failures have costs and recovery paths; losing one entrance need not end the expedition.
- Further construction must make gift expectations, alarm actions, perspective shifts, environmental warnings, and failure costs perceivable.
- The crawl's optional material supports flexible pacing.
- All fights need meaningful additional objectives; planned and emergent fights must be checked against this rule.

## Open design questions and preparation order

1. Finish the outside: concrete terrain, inhabitants, scouting positions and information, hazards, and objectives for any fights.
2. Finish the formal entrance: the maker's appearance and voice, perspective cues and influences, tribute standard, binding components, extent of possible release, combat and stealth alternatives, and what it actually knows.
3. Finish Krauzeth's entrance: creature, handlers, warning method, optional alarm traps, demolition mechanism, battlefield, and alarm-dependent starting conditions.
4. Finish Zaurmath's entrance: exact transformations, reliable precursors, crossing routes, magic interactions, and recoverable consequences.
5. Finish the discovery: physical viewpoint, recognizable remains, evidence for the Limbo eruption, and transition to the guest quarters.
6. Begin the guest-quarter crawl sketch: room connections and a small set of concrete contents and discoveries.
7. Leave audience-chamber and personal-lair construction for later.

The recurring attention principle is adopted. Its exact encounter mechanics, limits, costs, and later applications remain open.
