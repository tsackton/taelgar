## Sources and anchors

- [[Dragon Arc - Backstory]] supplies the original dragon family, Limbo affinity, weak point near the Nimrathë, cursed-object craft, and catastrophe. It leaves Vraedhul's death uncertain; this adventure explicitly adopts that she died.
- [[Dragon Arc - Details]] supplies Krauzeth's flight, followers, and character. His northern refuge is not the original lair entrance being designed here.
- [[Vraedhul]] records the worldbuilding already saved separately: immense power, order-bending craft, strange and cruel possibilities, and precious objects and people as raw materials.
- [[Krauzeth]] and [[Zaurmath]] are the two offspring. The current discussion establishes Zaurmath's death defending his mother and the party's subsequent killing of Krauzeth.
- [[Limbo]] supplies unceasing transformation, the importance of will and attention, and transformation encouraging further transformation.
- [[Experiments upon the Planar Substance]] is a DM conceptual reference, including self-amplifying transmutation. It was written in DR 1727, later than the DR 1720 Cleenseau situation; it is not an adopted book or clue available to these PCs.
- [[Philosopher's Information Concerning Limbo]] and the recent Dunmari Limbo adventures supplied atmosphere and examples of readable warning signs. Their full-plane danger and freedom to reshape reality are not appropriate to this level 9 expedition.
- [[Arborea]] supplies untamed, overwhelming life and growth. The bound maker of magical wonders is a new adventure-specific being, not an established species from that note.
- [[Deva]] and [[Elven Arborea Workshop]] offer background precedent for Arborean manifestations and planar craft research. Neither establishes this maker's species or exact craft.
- The earlier split-minded kobold was an unadopted proposal in the previous adventure draft. Its conceptual interest is reused in the maker; that kobold and the scavenger fragment are not imported.

## Current situation

Vraedhul is dead. About three months before the expedition, an eruption of Limbo through the local weak point consumed her and devastated her domain. The recent wider spill of Limbo into Taelgar through weak spots accounts for strange events and disruptions in the Plaguelands.

Residue remains and is progressively unmaking the lair. The central workshops, where the local chaos began, are probably already pure chaos. Other places remain intact, have become mutable, or have passed beyond coherent form.

Zaurmath died quickly while defending his mother. His lair has the worst chaos among the surviving entrance routes and contains little surviving explorable space. Krauzeth fled with kobolds; the PCs later killed him and have relationships with some of his former followers.

Enough physical and magical structure survived for the domain to persist for months. Its deterioration is now accelerating.

## Relevant backstory and hidden truth

### Vraedhul and her craft

Vraedhul was immensely powerful and drawn to this location by its connection to Limbo. She exploited its capacity to bend order, cultivating strange and cruel possibilities in existing things and infusing objects with extraordinary properties.

Precious objects and people could both become raw materials. The cruel joke is the overriding character of her favorite creations. She enjoyed surrounding herself with dangerous and cursed objects; her personal lair housed favorite finished work.

She sent cursed objects into the world and sometimes charged heavily to remove the curses. She was unwelcoming to unworthy visitors and expected an appropriate gift before granting attention. Weirdness, cruelty, value, and potential for transformation could make an offering interesting.

Her promises did not provide reliable protection from her choices. The intended tone is dragon tribute, power, and material transformation; the entrance should not depend on fey bargain wording, contractual loopholes, or reciprocal obligations.

### Why the lair has lasted and is now changing faster

The adopted explanation applies Limbo's self-amplifying transformation to the surviving domain:

1. Substantial physical and magical structure persisted after Vraedhul died.
2. Early changes were isolated pockets.
3. Material already transformed became easier to change again.
4. Pockets spread and joined, accelerating the breakdown.
5. The eventual state is an unformed wasteland or cyclone of chaos.

The spreading-residue application is an adventure-specific extension of the Limbo principles, not an independently established universal rule.

The site remains in Taelgar. Local effects can resemble Limbo, but the PCs have not entered the plane itself. Much more direct or complete reality shaping, as seen in Dunmar's actual Limbo adventure, is not the baseline here.

### What the Limbo references contributed

The user asked for the recent Dunmari Limbo adventures as an atmospheric reference, then explicitly limited how directly they should be copied. Those adventurers were in Limbo itself; this party is level 9 and exploring residue in Taelgar.

Relevant reference qualities include matter behaving as another substance, surprising shifts of support or orientation, recognizable signs before a dangerous change, and stable forms sustained by attention and will. The Dunmar material also explored identity maintained through stories, roles, emotions, and routines. These qualities help explain why the maker's incompatible perspectives and the attention principle belong in this adventure.

For source atmosphere, see [[dunmari-frontier-135-session-recap]] and [[dunmari-frontier-136-session-recap]]. They include imagined structures and environments, people whose coherence depended on identity, and an impossible tower with altered material behavior. These are reference scenes, not adopted rooms, NPCs, treasure, or level-appropriate mechanics for Cleenseau.

The intended local distinction is important: stable mountain landmarks remain useful; individual areas or creations contain dangerous possibilities. A party member cannot simply imagine away the entire ruin. Most opportunities for willful influence should pass through Vraedhul's work. Observing one creation's behavior should teach something useful without giving a universal ability to change anything.

There is no chosen abstract attention resource, new concentration rule, automatic ability-check procedure, or escalating numerical chaos track. Those are construction decisions if needed, not established world rules.

### Her remains

There is no intact fresh corpse waiting in the personal lair. Recognizable skeletal or skull-like remains have been transformed, stretched through, or intertwined with the central workshop chaos.

A clear view toward the center makes her death recognizable. All three successful entrance routes give access to one shared discovery location on her mountain, before the supplicant-quarter crawl. The exact terrace, aperture, framing, and anatomical image remain to be finished.

The essential revelation is partial: an eruption from Limbo has consumed Vraedhul. The view does not supply the full account of the eruption or all its continuing consequences.

#### Why the discovery is a shared view

Several early corpse ideas failed to match the user's image. The user preferred remains transformed and intertwined with the workshop chaos, recognizable once the party earns a clear view toward the center. An ordinary dragon skeleton placed in a convenient room would lose the desired sense of the dragon being consumed by the catastrophe.

Two arrangements were considered: one shared viewing location on Vraedhul's mountain, or a different discovery view from each entrance mountain. The shared location became the working choice. The offspring routes approach it through the underground network; the formal route approaches through its own admission process.

The user considered access through an entrance sufficient to earn the discovery. A further compulsory device puzzle is not part of the adopted sequence. The exact framing might eventually use a terrace, opening, or other architecture, but none is fixed. A previously suggested viewing crystal was only a proposal.

The main entrance's potentially shorter physical route prompted discussion, not a requirement to make every route the same length. The important experience is that each chosen entrance can support a worthwhile opening and deliver the same meaningful payoff.

## Geography

### Three peaks and central workshops

The original domain included Vraedhul's lair, one lair for each offspring, supplicant accommodations, workrooms, and kobold quarters.

Working schematic, not a measured map:

            1          A
                        W       2
            B                      C

                        3

- A is Vraedhul's peak.
- B and C are the offspring's peaks; the precise assignment of the old map labels and entrance numbers to each offspring is not fixed here.
- W is the workshop complex around the bases between the peaks.
- Entrance 1 is the grand formal visitor approach into Vraedhul's mountain.
- The other entrances approach Krauzeth's and Zaurmath's lairs.
- Dragons flew between peaks. Underground kobold tunnels connect them; there are no adopted direct surface bridges or corridors between the peaks.
- The offspring routes reach Vraedhul's mountain through this network, without requiring traversal of the unformed workshops.

The shared outer terrain is dangerous and inhabited. No exact approach map or set of outside encounters is selected.

### Vraedhul's main complex

Core surviving areas:

- Supplicant suites, increasingly elaborate for guests she favored or mocked.
- A throne or audience chamber.
- A huge personal lair, with favorite creations and the richest treasure.

Working arrangement of those areas:

                S       S
        S                    AA       LL
                             AA       LL
            S        S

S marks suites, A the audience chamber, and L the personal lair. This records the rough relationship, not exact room counts or shapes. The personal lair may be a vast cavern or a mountain-top pavilion; that choice is unresolved.

Individual suite passages connect to a shared antechamber. Kobold tunnels also connect to it. The adopted crawl direction is a short main route with optional suites and some cross-connections; the actual passages and relation of the shared viewpoint to the antechamber still need mapping.

## Forces and motives

### The bound Arborean maker

The selected guardian concept is a being from Arborea that cultivated magical wonders. Vraedhul bound a creature not naturally her ally, finding the forced service useful and amusing. A captive maker used to collect tribute and enforce her cruel admission standards is the central contrast.

The maker supplies the social element at the formal entrance. It is associated with the grandiose representation of Vraedhul: the working image is a colossal likeness with eyes that examine, a claw that handles offerings, and a mouth that speaks and controls entry. Exact anatomy and the maker's physical integration remain to be constructed.

Limbo has changed the captive so that three incompatible versions coexist:

| Version | What it believes |
| --- | --- |
| In Arborea | It is back in its own realm. |
| Truth-aware | It recognizes its actual predicament and the present ruin. |
| Vraedhul's guardian | It is still serving as Vraedhul's gate guardian. |

These beliefs are adopted. Exact temperament, motives, visible signs, transitions, and what the truth-aware version witnessed remain open. The earlier proposal of an aspiring new self or a version that genuinely values Vraedhul's authority is not a replacement for these three versions.

The party can deliberately influence which version comes forward through actions, what they present, and where they direct the maker's attention. This provides a social challenge and a first example of the recurring attention principle.

An appropriate valuable gift should still make admission fairly straightforward. The NPC is not a requirement to satisfy three independent judges. Loosening its binding is an optional way through, involving cooperation and practical interference with the entrance's defenses. The precise act, difficulty, and extent of release remain undecided.

Its name, gender, exact creature type or stat block, origin, and former creations are not selected. A djinni was considered and then replaced by the Arborean-maker concept; a deva is not an adopted species assignment.

#### Character contrast and the reason for this choice

The guardian developed from an animated claw into the idea of a grandiose likeness of Vraedhul, then into a bound vassal operating through that likeness. The user wanted a social element among the entrances and liked reusing the interest of the old split-minded kobold without actually using that NPC.

A bound air genie was considered. The user then preferred a being associated with creating magical items, Limbo, or Arborea, and selected the Arborean maker. Vraedhul would have enjoyed binding something not naturally her ally and making it enforce her values. Demons and devils were unattractive because their danger would be too straightforward.

The core contrast is creative, overflowing life confined within a tyrant's representation and used to collect tribute and inflict cruel transformations. This does not settle whether the maker was originally gentle, generous, proud, territorial, or indifferent to mortals. Arborea in Taelgar is untamed and overwhelming nature, not automatically a polite fey court or a familiar D&D alignment category.

Likewise, calling it a maker does not yet establish that it grew any particular item. Living wood, metal flowers, enchanted resins, amber, and bone were images offered to explore the idea. They are not a selected catalogue or a general new rule about all Arborean beings.

No decision attributes all of Vraedhul's creations to the captive. Vraedhul remains the powerful author of the lair's legacy; the maker's precise contribution to her craft beyond gatekeeping is still open.

#### The three perspectives in play

Adopted facts are the three beliefs and the party's ability to influence which comes forward. The working presentation is one version dominant at a time, with observable changes. Exact transitions and indicators remain open.

The following are illustrative interpretations and sample lines, not additional fixed lore or final dialogue:

- **Believes it is in Arborea:** it might perceive the stone entrance as living growth and the visitors as travelers in its domain. It could be curious about what an object might become, or treat a hazardous transformation as ordinary cultivation. Example: "Closed? Those branches haven't finished growing. You'll damage them."
- **Understands the truth:** it recognizes the present ruin and its captivity. It could warn the party about a compelled movement, explain a binding component, or cooperate in loosening it. Example: "When the hand closes, step back. I can feel it beginning, but I cannot always stop it."
- **Still Vraedhul's guardian:** it conducts admissions and judges worthiness through tribute. Example: "The mistress has no interest in your excuses. Show me what you brought." Whether it believes she is alive, imagines an earlier time, values the office, or simply experiences the role as an obligation has not been settled.

Their differences concern what each believes is happening, rather than a simple division into a truthful voice and two deliberate liars. Do not turn the encounter into a formal logic puzzle about choosing the only honest statement. The user wants a powerful changed being to interact with.

Possible ways of influencing it include showing an offering, engaging with its creative work, and drawing attention to the binding or its present condition. Those were examples, not a mandatory sequence or exact phrases. The player-facing opportunity is to understand the perspectives and act on them.

The truth-aware self is not automatically an omniscient source on the eruption. Its witnessed events and retained memories need to be chosen. Nor should it automatically be prevented from saying anything useful: giving practical help is part of the optional social route.

#### Keeping payment and cooperation distinct

The formal entrance remains fundamentally about payment. A substantial appropriate gift should let the party take that route without solving the keeper's whole identity. Its taste is connected to weirdness, cruelty, value, and material potential; no exact price, rarity threshold, or uniquely correct gift is selected.

The optional cooperation route asks more of the party: understand the being, reach a perspective capable of helping, and practically loosen its constraints while the defenses remain dangerous. This can combine conversation, investigation, and action. Freeing the being is not a compulsory new adventure mission.

No outcome has yet been fixed for complete release versus partial loosening. It is not established whether the maker can leave, fully control the image, or return to Arborea. Likewise, no wish, boon, promised reward, or future quest has been assigned.

Vraedhul's own unreliability is part of the history and tone. It should not be used to negate the adopted straightforward payment route arbitrarily. The keeper's present behavior, the fiction of the binding, and the practical advantage of a good offering still need coherent construction.

### Loyalist kobolds at Krauzeth's entrance

Loyalist kobolds remain in or occupy the old lair. A partially tamed creature and its kobold watch form the working outer defense. Alarm traps may supplement it, but exact traps are not chosen.

A warning prompts an organized retreat into the kobold network and an attempt to seal the passage behind them. They have not spent three months continuously destroying the entrance. Some delay intruders while others undertake the triggered destruction.

Their exact loyalty, leaders, knowledge of the PCs and Krauzeth's death, and relations to named earlier kobolds remain open. No existing named faction member has been assigned to these defenders.

#### Why the defense has two parts

The first proposal was a fight to preserve an entrance from creatures destroying it. The user correctly objected that those creatures could not sensibly have spent three months continuously doing that. The adopted solution is a warning event that triggers a response.

The watchpost is the precursor encounter; preventing its warning determines the inner defenders' starting condition. With no warning, the passage is intact and they are unprepared. With a warning, retreat and destruction are already underway. This is a persistent consequence of earlier play, not merely a second unrelated fight.

The warning should be something the party can recognize and interfere with. No specific bell, messenger route, signal magic, trap, or number of actions is chosen. The precursor need not become a full battle: evasion, distraction, and other approaches can serve its objective.

The main fight involves competing enemy jobs. Some kobolds hold the party back; others act on the passage. The party needs to preserve access and get through. The exact destruction could be collapse, sealing, or another method appropriate to the eventual map. No demolition clock, durability total, automatic failure threshold, or reinforcement schedule exists yet.

The partly tamed creature is a distinct current ingredient. It is not the abandoned magic-eating Limbo scavenger. Its nature, temperament, vulnerabilities, relationship to handlers, and possible alternative interactions remain open.

### Outside inhabitants

The outside should contain chaos, elementals, and other dangers. Some may be remnants of Vraedhul's servants and others may be uncontrolled inhabitants of the changed landscape. No particular creature roster or encounter has been adopted.

#### What the outside is for

Shared dangerous approaches precede the choice of entrance. Their useful reward is knowledge of the three different challenge structures. The user explicitly said there is nothing useful to gather here as tribute.

The party needs enough information to prefer payment, combat, or environmental traversal for practical reasons. Exact observations and how they are obtained are not chosen. Extra scouting might reveal more useful details, but there is no adopted requirement to inspect every approach in a fixed order.

The outside should feel inhabited and dangerous rather than being only an explanatory panorama. Elementals and chaos are part of that direction, but there is no accepted keyed map, monster list, encounter order, or required fight. Any fight constructed here must receive the same additional-objective treatment as the entrance fights.

The rejected elemental proposal must not be reinstated to fill this gap. The last assistant proposal involving an elemental recycling a relief was not a user-selected encounter. Keep the general inhabited-landscape requirement separate from that specific rejected idea.

## Setting rules and constraints

- Attention can stabilize one existing possibility within a Limbo-altered creation. It can affect form or function, but does not grant unrestricted creation or world reshaping.
- Opportunities to shape events through will are primarily mediated through Vraedhul's creations.
- Transformation should remain learnable. Clear warning signs, recognizable terrain, and useful observation support informed decisions.
- Stable landmarks and coherent passages coexist with mutable areas and unformed zones.
- Larger deterioration matters across delays and absences; the adventure should allow patient exploration during a visit.
- Binding still holds the maker after Vraedhul's death. An anchor in the entrance rather than her living will is a possible explanation, not a finalized mechanism.
- Dangerous and cursed magic is part of Vraedhul's legacy. Lasting effects imposed on PCs must have a clear path to removal.
- No particular outside object or cache is available as useful tribute.
- No continuing scavenger, magical feeding process, or portal-fuel economy is part of the current background.

## Design ingredients

### Formal entrance

An intimidating manifestation of Vraedhul's authority, with a dangerous captive behind or within it. The Wizard of Oz influence is theatrical scale, voice, and representation. The creation has real power.

A mouth, claw, and eye can be coordinated parts of one entrance encounter. Specific effects, body vulnerabilities, binding anchors, and routes behind the spectacle remain construction questions.

### Krauzeth's entrance

A combat-focused route whose outer warning changes the inner defenders' preparedness and progress in sealing the passage. Preserving access, rather than merely killing defenders, matters.

### Zaurmath's entrance

One substantial ruined chamber with a visible onward tunnel and a gradient toward an unforming breach on one side:

- Relatively stable edges permit observation and investigation.
- Changing ground supplies the main traversal hazards, with recognizable precursors and recoverable mistakes.
- The unforming breach is unmistakably more dangerous and can be avoided entirely.

Possible crossings use outer ledges, with climbing and exposed gaps, or a more direct route through changing material. Skills, equipment, and suitable magic should genuinely help. Exact physical transformations and tells are not yet fixed.

#### Purpose of the gradient

The user accepted one substantial ruined space but required both recognizable Limbo character and clearly graduated danger. The bands describe how firmly matter retains its form; they are not merely harder checks or higher damage in otherwise identical terrain.

Relatively stable areas provide a place to look, experiment, and choose a route. The main changing-ground area rewards reading transformations and preparing crossings. The extreme breach demonstrates unmaking without forcing level 9 PCs into the full dangers of Limbo.

Examples considered were stone softening into water, hardening into glass, shedding burning dust, or air becoming solid. Example precursors included a wet sheen before liquefaction or dust collecting before air hardened. These illustrate a design requirement for intelligible warnings; no exact cycle, effect list, duration, damage, or check difficulty has been adopted.

The outer ledges and direct crossing offer different environmental demands. Suitable spells and equipment should genuinely help. Do not add an arbitrary universal anti-flight or anti-magic rule simply to preserve a planned obstacle; actual air hazards and spell interactions need to be established through the fiction.

The scene ends on reaching the onward tunnel, not after clearing a sequence of unrelated rooms. Most of Zaurmath's lair is gone; this should remain a compact surviving challenge. The extreme breach is not a required stage, and failure should have useful recovery possibilities before severe consequences become plausible.

### Shared discovery and later knowledge

The clear view confirms death and a Limbo eruption. Consequences and details are reserved for further exploration. Attention and existing possibilities recur through the creations; their specific applications in guest rooms and the later capstone are unresolved.

## Questions, conflicts, and provisional ideas

### Source boundaries

The original [[Dragon Arc - Backstory]] leaves death uncertain and offers several possible causes. This adventure adopts that a Limbo eruption killed Vraedhul and that Zaurmath died defending her. The exact event sequence remains open; the old suggestion of a predator or scavenger causing the destruction is not adopted.

[[Arborea]] establishes wild growth, not a general species of magical craft spirits. This maker, its captivity, and its changed perspectives are adventure-specific worldbuilding. Its relationship to deva metaphysics is optional and unresolved.

Later Dunmari experiences and DR 1727 research provide DM inspiration rather than automatically available DR 1720 testimony or objects.

### Rejected proposal

The earlier elemental proposal is rejected and superseded by the bound Arborean maker as the formal entrance guardian. The stair-maintaining elemental and associated relief encounter are not retained as outside options. The general intention to include elementals and other dangers in the landscape remains; no specific outside encounter is selected.

### Unchosen construction examples

- Specific maker forms such as branches confined in gold, amber eyes, living metal flowers, enchanted resins, bone, or living gemstones are illustrative.
- Specific attention cues such as showing tribute, engaging with its creations, or examining the binding are promising examples; no fixed trigger list or password mechanic is adopted.
- Gold around wrists, a heart or gem anchor, and physical maintenance mechanisms are possible binding details, not fixed facts.
- Specific Zaurmath-route warnings such as a wet sheen before stone liquefies, or dust gathering before air hardens, remain examples to construct and calibrate.
- A viewing crystal or device that must be solved before recognizing the remains is not an adopted extra obstacle.
- A personal-lair cavern versus mountain-top pavilion is unresolved.
- Simultaneously coordinating several creations in the audience chamber was proposed but deferred without adoption.

### Still to establish

- Concrete exterior geography and reconnaissance clues.
- The maker's actual original craft, appearance, speech, binding anchors, and possible release.
- What its truth-aware version knows about Vraedhul and the eruption.
- The loyalists' identities, motives, creature, alarm equipment, and destruction method.
- Exact warning signs and physical rules for the graded environmental route.
- The recognizable remains and view's physical framing.
- Actual guest-suite contents, connections, treasures, and lessons.
- The details of the eruption and the evidence explaining its wider consequences.
