# Vraedhul's Broken Lair Backstory

## Sources and anchors

- [[Dragon Arc - Backstory]] establishes Vraedhul's connection to Limbo, the planar weak point beneath her lair, the lair's destruction during the high tide of DR 1720, and the loss of much but not all of the hoard.
- [[Dragon Arc - Details]], [[Cleenseau - Session 29]], and [[Cleenseau - Session 30]] establish Krauzeth's surviving kobold factions, the party's alliance with Drubgrak and Vikka, Krauzeth's death, and the disposition of several named kobolds.
- [[Lair - Dragon - Revisit]] preserves earlier provisional ideas about a broken planar lair, Vraedhul's workshops and cursed objects, surviving vaults, and a magical scavenger.
- [[Vraedhul]] summarizes the current staging material about the dragon and the uncertainty surrounding her death.
- [[Limbo]], [[Research about Ancient Sources on Limbo]], [[Research about Limbo and Cha'mutte]], and [[Philosopher's Information Concerning Limbo]] establish the instability of Limbo, the role of will in imposing form, and the relationship between motion, transformation, and dissolution.
- [[Interlude (Preparations for Limbo)]] and the raw notes in [[Limbo Session 1 Notes]] preserve the Dunmari Fellowship's preparation for and first experience in Limbo. The latter introduces chaos beasts whose final form is a chaos storm, but it is not yet a finalized campaign session note.
- [[Historical Framework]] contains noncanonical development material connecting chaos beasts to ancient forces of chaos that became great storms in Limbo, growing and dividing in an endless cycle.

## Current situation

Vraedhul is dead. Her lair remains a damaged, spatially confused ruin shaped by the catastrophic Limbo incursion that killed her. Rooms, passages, and functional areas no longer occupy a straightforward relationship to one another, but the ruin does not rearrange itself while the PCs explore it.

Much of Vraedhul's hoard vanished or was destroyed during the catastrophe, but worthwhile mundane treasure, safe magical items, cursed creations, workshop materials, records, and sealed caches may remain.

A stranded creature associated with Limbo has recently gained access to the surviving magical stores. It consumes enchantment and is moving through the lair quickly enough that delay during the expedition can determine what treasure remains.

## The scavenger

The current working concept is a stranded chaos beast, perhaps a fragment or offspring of a larger chaos storm. In Limbo it was not necessarily a discrete animal with one persistent body. Crossing into Taelgar forced it into a fixed physical form.

The creature experiences stable matter as shocking and grotesque. Its body remains one body; wounds persist; stone refuses to become something else; distances and directions remain consistent; and objects retain rigid boundaries. It may smash or attack stable matter in panic, repeatedly testing whether the world can be made to change.

The scavenger is not evil. Its destructive behavior arises from terror, revulsion, and desperation rather than malice. It wants to return to Limbo.

Enchanted objects contain enough magical potential to let the creature briefly recover some mutability. By consuming sufficient magic, it can accumulate the power needed to open a route home. It does not care whether that magic comes from a useful object or a cursed one.

This makes cursed objects the best fuel from the PCs' perspective. If they can identify, handle, and deliver enough dangerous cursed items safely, they can help the scavenger leave while preserving more desirable treasure.

## Vraedhul's death

The adventure confirms that Vraedhul died in the destruction of her lair. The precise form of the proof and the complete account of her death remain to be designed.

Earlier material proposed that Vraedhul built up a reservoir of unstable planar energy, attempted to control the high-tide surge, and attracted something from Limbo that pulled stored energy, portions of the lair, and Vraedhul through the rupture. That account remains a useful proposal rather than an adopted fact.

## Setting rules and constraints

- The lair is spatially scrambled but static during play.
- Its disorder should be understandable enough to map, learn, and solve fairly.
- Vraedhul is truly dead; her survival is not the adventure's reversal.
- The scavenger consumes magic rather than treasure as wealth.
- Mundane valuables may be smashed, displaced, or ignored, but do not fuel its return.
- Cursed and noncursed enchantments both provide fuel.
- The scavenger's return to Limbo is a genuine possible resolution.
- Killing the scavenger is also a valid resolution.
- The adventure is not a rescue mission.
- Combat should be present but limited; exploration, puzzles, treasure decisions, and possible interaction should dominate play.
- The lair does not need to reveal a major future campaign truth.

## Design ingredients

### Clues and revelations

- definitive evidence that Vraedhul died;
- stripped or inert magic items showing how the scavenger feeds;
- evidence that mundane valuables are not its true target;
- signs that the creature is panicked by fixed form rather than motivated by cruelty;
- information about Limbo, chaos beasts, and the creature's desired return;
- records or apparatus explaining how Vraedhul contained, studied, or created cursed objects; and
- evidence that particular cursed objects can be used as portal fuel.

### Locations and landmarks

- the damaged exterior and entrance to the ruined lair;
- the place that proves Vraedhul's death;
- Vraedhul's workshop or another source of curse-handling knowledge;
- surviving portions of the broken hoard;
- the planar scar or heart of the old rupture; and
- displaced rooms and passages whose fixed relationships create navigational or spatial puzzles.

These are functional ingredients, not yet an adopted room list or map.

### Hazards and opposition

- cursed objects that are valuable as fuel but dangerous to identify, touch, move, or surrender;
- environmental consequences of the Limbo-damaged architecture;
- limited surviving guardians, warped servants, or other creatures if they serve a clear gameplay purpose;
- the scavenger itself, if opposed or frightened; and
- the loss of treasure as time passes.

## The possible kobold

A surviving kobold affected by the original Limbo catastrophe was discussed as a possible wandering NPC. One proposed version manifests three perspectives: the kobold before the catastrophe, the present kobold, and a fragment of the scavenger. The fragment may have used the kobold's body to release the larger creature.

This NPC is not adopted. The core dungeon should be designed without assuming the kobold exists. The kobold should be added only if the finished structure needs a mouthpiece for the scavenger, an unreliable guide, or another specific gameplay function that cannot be served more cleanly through the environment or the scavenger itself.

## Questions, conflicts, and provisional ideas

- The original Dragon Arc backstory left Vraedhul's fate uncertain, while this adventure adopts that she is dead. The adventure must provide convincing in-play confirmation.
- The cause and timing of the scavenger's recent release remain unresolved.
- The exact relationship between Taelgar's developing chaos-beast lore and the scavenger remains provisional because the Dunmari Limbo session is not yet finalized.
- The scavenger may be a fragment of a chaos storm forced into individuality, but its exact nature, appearance, name, and mechanics have not been chosen.
- A controlled portal may require feeding the scavenger a threshold of magic, using Vraedhul's planar apparatus, reaching the original weak point, or some combination. The exact process remains open.
- The role of a surviving kobold remains deliberately unresolved.
