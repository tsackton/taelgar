# Cleenseau Arc 2 Backstory

- [[Malach Timeline]] contains provisional and sometimes competing versions of Malach's early life, education in Tollen, activities in the highlands and Sentinels, research into immortality and chalyte, and alliance with mind flayers.
- [[Campaign Arc Overview]] previously proposed an Enford investigation, research into Malach's history in Tollen and the highlands, and an underground confrontation with mind flayers.
- [[Cleenseau Brainstorming Plot Notes]] proposed the mind flayers' possession of a separate soul jar and their imprisonment of Malach in a chalyte construct.
- [[Moon]] and [[2025-04-29 - Moon as Prison of Banished Gods]] preserve provisional setting ideas about the moon, its uncertain memories, and its possible relationship to banished powers.
- [[Three Despairs]] and [[Umidzka]] establish Umidzka as one of the Despairs, associated with the power that seeks to end all things, necromancy, and undeath.

## Current situation

Malach prepared a separate soul jar or similar contingency through an alliance with mind flayers. This survived the destruction of his divided phylactery and apparent death at the end of Arc 1.

A nihilistic cabal within or among the mind flayers defeated or displaced Malach's allies and corrupted the contingency. Instead of restoring Malach freely, it captured him in a chalyte ritual cage beneath the Sentinels.

The cabal believes that the moon regulates planar tides. It intends to use an approaching planar confluence to destroy or disable the moon and thereby produce a planar tsunami. The plan requires Malach's knowledge and soul: he knows how to manipulate planar structures, and his powerful self-persisting undead soul can anchor or sustain a ritual that the mind flayers cannot perform themselves. The cage imprisons and compels him while preserving the qualities they need.

The cabal does not necessarily know that a fragment of Umidzka is attached to Malach.

## Malach and Umidzka

At some point during Malach's research into immortality, he made contact with Umidzka or attempted to reach the place beyond the ordinary planar system where the Three Despairs exist. A fragment of Umidzka became attached to his soul, essence, or lichhood.

This creates the central problem after the cabal falls:

- Malach cannot safely be killed while the attachment persists.
- Killing him normally may release Umidzka, or allow the fragment to become an opening through which Umidzka can enter Taelgar.
- The chalyte cage contains Malach but cannot serve as a permanent answer because influence leaks through it.

Whether the fragment makes Malach literally unkillable or merely makes his death catastrophic remains open.

The attachment may have formed during his original research, during construction of the soul jar, or as part of his contingency after Arc 1. The current preference is that it grows out of Malach's own research and choices rather than making the mind flayers accidental saviors when the Arc 1 party defeated him.

## Malach's lunar conduit

A promising provisional explanation connects Malach's mysterious origin to the moon.

Malach encountered a fringe scholarly theory that the moon does more than govern planar tides: it stabilizes the wider planar system. He believed that suppressing this stabilizing influence during the correct lunar or planar conditions could open a path beyond the known planes and allow him to reach Umidzka.

The theory worked for Malach when it had failed for others. Malach likely attributed this to his own superiority. In fact, something about his forgotten origin may give him an otherwise impossible conduit to the moon.

One possible hidden truth is that Malach did not draw a foreign fragment of Umidzka through or past the moon. He tore loose a piece of the moon that had once been Umidzka; once separated from the moon's transmuting whole, it reverted to its original nature. In this version, the Umidzkan fragment is also literally a piece of the moon.

This explains both why the mind flayers need Malach and why research into his background and the moon converge. The mind flayers know that Malach successfully established a conduit capable of reaching the moon, even if they do not understand his origin or the true nature of the fragment.

## The mind flayer ritual

The ritual cage uses Malach as both expert and anchor:

- it imprisons him and prevents escape;
- it compels him to perform or sustain magic the cabal cannot execute;
- it preserves his soul across a prolonged or otherwise fatal working; and
- it directs the working through his unique lunar conduit.

At the planar confluence, the completed ritual will consume Malach and strike the moon. The cabal expects the moon's regulation of planar tides to fail, causing planar collision, overflow, or a planar tsunami.

The ritual's real result remains unresolved. One current possibility is that Malach dies but the Umidzkan fragment remains contained by the ritual structure or is taken into the moon. That containment is imperfect: the trapped presence can still project thoughts, influence susceptible beings, or attract agents to itself. This would be a serious outcome without immediately releasing Umidzka into Taelgar.

## The moon's possible nature

The moon should not be treated as an ordinary prison full of discrete, living prisoners. The stronger working model is a transmuting defense or immune system for creation.

It absorbs powers or fragments considered too dangerous to remain independently in creation or pass into the Land of the Dead. Those beings are petrified, crystallized, transformed, or melded until they become the moon. Their identities no longer persist in an ordinary sense, although traces of memory and personality may contribute to the moon's alien and confused consciousness.

The moon may have begun with a great sacrifice by an ancient hero who became the seed or directing consciousness of this defense. Its conflicting memories could therefore belong both to what it guards against and to those who created it.

This model remains provisional and should not resolve the moon's own uncertain history more clearly than necessary.

## Forces and motives

### The mind flayer cabal

The cabal seeks mass destruction through planar collapse. It understands the observed relationship between the moon and planar tides but is wrong or incomplete about the moon's deeper nature. It has captured Malach because his knowledge and immortal soul make its ritual possible. The cabal's use of a unique lunar conduit remains a provisional extension of this explanation.

### Malach

Malach is the cabal's prisoner and unwilling ritualist. He wants the cabal stopped because its success will consume him. This permits a narrow and temporary alignment with the PCs.

Once the cabal is defeated, Malach wants to be released. He may provide genuine information about his past or the ritual while concealing anything that would help the PCs destroy, transform, or permanently imprison him.

His influence through the cage is limited but sufficient to attract attention, communicate indirectly, or manipulate creatures in the deep.

### The PCs

The PCs initially save both the moon and Malach by stopping the ritual. They then inherit a dangerous stalemate: they cannot safely kill Malach, cannot responsibly free him, and cannot safely move or break the prison.

Their long-term task is to learn what Malach did, what the moon truly does, and whether the Umidzkan attachment can be undone.

## Setting rules and constraints

- The Arc 1 destruction of Malach's phylactery fragments was real. His survival depends on a separate contingency.
- The cabal captured that contingency and imprisoned Malach rather than freely restoring him.
- Malach cannot release himself from the chalyte cage.
- The cage is stable in its passive state after the cabal and active ritual apparatus are defeated.
- Moving, opening, or damaging the cage risks freeing Malach or disrupting the containment of Umidzka.
- Malach can exert limited influence through the cage, so leaving him there is a reprieve rather than a permanent solution.
- The cabal believes destroying the moon will cause a planar tsunami.
- The cabal's understanding of the moon is substantially incomplete.
- Malach cannot be safely killed until the Umidzkan problem has been resolved.
- The moon's exact origin and consciousness should remain uncertain.

## Two live permanent solutions

### Option A: Unbind and kill Malach

The recovered ritual knowledge makes it possible to distinguish Malach from the Umidzkan fragment, sever their connection, and dispel or return the fragment without releasing Umidzka. The same ritual opens the chalyte cage. Malach emerges as a vulnerable lich and must be killed in a climactic combat.

This ending treats the travel quest as the recovery of a genuine unbinding ritual and preserves the final execution of Malach.

### Option B: Transform or imprison Malach within the moon

The research reveals that the Umidzkan fragment cannot be safely separated from Malach. The moon is the only known structure capable of absorbing and transforming them together. The ritual opens the chalyte cage, defeats or restrains Malach, and admits him to the moon.

This ending makes the moon central to the solution and reverses the cabal's plan: the being intended to destroy the moon instead becomes part of it.

## Research path and destinations

### Tharwa Fëa and Tollen

[[Tharwa Fea|Tharwa Fëa]] preserves evidence of Malach's research into immortality, souls, undeath, and Umidzka. [[Tollen]] provides his scholarly context and access to fringe work on planar tides and lunar stabilization. Together they should establish the major questions and point toward the deeper expeditions.

[[Ishara Venn]] provides a possible connection to post-Great War work on planar vortices. Another provisional scholar, Emyr of Tafolwen, was suggested as someone who may have written about planar connections or tides and could conceivably still be alive in 1725.

### The highlands and Zimkova

The highlands reveal who Malach truly is and why the lunar theory worked for him. The adventure should culminate at the temple of a dead kestavo. Malach's lost childhood and unknown origin may conceal the source of his conduit to the moon.

The suggestion that Malach might be Avatus was raised but remains highly tentative and may conflict with future Addermarch campaign development.

### Drankor and the Occulta Ludum

Drankor currently fits the binding and lunar research better than Ursk. The Occulta Ludum studied the multiverse by binding and examining powerful magical creatures. Its surviving work could explain the chalyte cage, lunar conduits, or the transmutation needed for either final ritual.

The route could pass through another Occulta Ludum site before leading to Drankor. Drankor also offers existing maps, encounters, institutions, and room for new development in 1725. Any use of Hralgar or the binding stones must avoid disrupting intended future history unless that interaction is deliberately designed.

### Flexible intermediate destination

An additional destination can provide the desired breadth of travel and bridge the highlands to Drankor. It remains undecided. Earlier possibilities included the Stoneborn or dwarven realms in the Sentinels, Maseau or Aurbez, the Plaguelands, and the devastation south of Tyrwingha.

Ursk remains possible but is less favored because unrevealed material there may matter to Campaign 3. The Green Sea is deliberately avoided as a newly developed quest region for the same reason.
