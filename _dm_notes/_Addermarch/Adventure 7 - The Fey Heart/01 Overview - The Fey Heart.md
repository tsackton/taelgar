# Adventure Overview Template

Overall context: [True Scenario Designery Index](https://theangrygm.com/true-scenario-designery/).

## Brief overview

The PCs have destroyed the Serpentine Axe in the previous adventure, and recovered the fey heart that Duncan stole. Now they need to return the fey heart to the Singing Tree. This is the final arc of the long-running Fey in Haldrenn campaign arc, and should probably take the characters to level 6 (currently need 2,700 XP to level up). 

## Design statement

The PCs must `find a way into the Feywild and back to the Singing Tree` because `they need to return the heart before the tree awakes again`, but `they closed the Nightsong Roots portal and thus must find their way across Morlaith's domain from another portal`.

## Starting point

Reference: [Designing on Purpose](https://theangrygm.com/designing-on-purpose/).

We'll pick up at Caradoc's. The premise is obvious, though the players don't know exactly how much time they have before the Singing Tree reawakens, and are unaware of other challenges that will make this difficult. 

## Adventure goal

References: [Designing on Purpose](https://theangrygm.com/designing-on-purpose/); [Challenge Or Goal?](https://theangrygm.com/challenge-or-goal/).

The goal of the adventure is to return the stolen heart to the Singing Tree, thereby calming Morlaith and ending the threat to Haldrenn and the rest of the Highmoor. 

## Central challenge

References: [Designing on Purpose](https://theangrygm.com/designing-on-purpose/); [Challenge Or Goal?](https://theangrygm.com/challenge-or-goal/).

Morlaith's domain is an untamed fey forest with the feeling of an ancient European or Germanic dark fairy tale. It does not submit to ordinary geography: the PCs cannot simply pick a direction and walk in a straight line to the Singing Tree. They must discover how to continue through clues and guides, while overcoming the obstacles that bar each part of the route. Shifting paths may be part of this challenge.

## X-factor

Reference: [Momentous and Inertial Adventure Design](https://theangrygm.com/momentous-and-inertial-adventure-design/).

The X-factor is exploration and navigation. The main gameplay loop is finding the path through Morlaith's domain.

## Inertia

References: [Inertia: Fighting Failure](https://theangrygm.com/inertia-fighting-failure/); [Momentous and Inertial Adventure Design](https://theangrygm.com/momentous-and-inertial-adventure-design/).

The primary inertia is the existing, time-based Singing Tree clock in [[Fey Timeline]]. The tree's chance of awakening increases with elapsed time. If it awakens before the heart is returned, the final approach becomes harder as its corruption produces new hazards, such as animals running amok. Once the portal to Haldrenn opens, destruction and avoidable NPC deaths increase the later the party returns the heart.

Failed navigation costs time and brings the party into contact with broadly interchangeable bands of Umbraeth's hunters, forcing a combat before they can continue. The hunters do not need a finite roster; they are an ongoing danger in the domain. Even after a navigation failure, the party should eventually find a way forward, but the delay, combat, resource loss, and possible need to rest all compound the time pressure. It should take several failures in succession to make the adventure unrecoverable.

## Momentum

References: [Momentum: Building Victory](https://theangrygm.com/momentum-building-victory/); [Momentous and Inertial Adventure Design](https://theangrygm.com/momentous-and-inertial-adventure-design/).

The heart wants to be reunited with the Singing Tree. Early in the adventure its pull is weak, so the PCs must rely on clues and guides to determine where to go next. As they approach the tree, the pull grows stronger. Later it becomes a reliable compass, removing uncertainty about direction and leaving the PCs to overcome the obstacles in their path. This is momentum because successful progress makes subsequent navigation easier. The heart might also hinder the forces of Umbraeth, but that remains an open possibility rather than an adopted part of the design.

## Outcomes

References: [Quality Wins and Losses](https://theangrygm.com/quality-wins-and-losses/); [Designing on Purpose](https://theangrygm.com/designing-on-purpose/).

This adventure should not have a harsh binary failure state. Its main outcomes form a spectrum determined by time:

- The best outcome is returning the heart before the tree awakens, sparing Haldrenn and facing the normal final approach.
- If the tree awakens before the heart is returned, the final approach gains additional corrupted-natural hazards.
- Once the portal opens, Haldrenn begins to suffer. The longer the delay, the greater the destruction and the more likely avoidable NPC deaths become.
- If Umbraeth's hunters defeat the party, they may take the heart. A possible escape from this outcome is intervention by a fey lord whom the PCs can call upon, but who will demand a future service in exchange.
- No single navigation failure should make the adventure unrecoverable; severe outcomes should follow only from several compounding failures.

## Open design questions

- The exact clues, guides, rules, and obstacles that define the route through Morlaith's domain.
- The exact dated consequences for Haldrenn after the portal opens.
- The specific hazards added to the final approach after the tree awakens.
- Whether Umbraeth's hunters provide the final confrontation.
- The adventure's structure and intended number of sessions.
