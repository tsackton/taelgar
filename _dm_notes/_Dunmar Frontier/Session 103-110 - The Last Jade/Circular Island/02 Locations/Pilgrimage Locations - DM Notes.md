# Remains of Pilgrimage Locations 

There are a number of pilgrimage locations that remain intact, primarily:
- The Pilgrim's Path
- The Divine Statues
- The Temple of the Beacon
- The Elven Memorials

In general, traveling on the Pilgrim's Path is safe, and the magic of the Eight Divines still resonates, meaning that travelers are hidden from prying eyes while on the road. 

All pilgrimage locations are safe places for a Short or Long Rest.

## The Pilgrim's Path

The pilgrim's path is a remarkably smooth and well-maintained road, wide enough for four to walk abreast, paved with large marble tiles of grays, blues, and greens that shine against the darkness and rubble of the black/brown dirt and stone of the surface. Even just stepping on the road makes one feel light and easy still. 

For random encounters on the road, see: [[Table - Pilgrim's Path  - Last Jade]]

## Divine Statues

Eight Statues, starting from the one adjacent to the Port and going counterclockwise to the north:
1. [[The Wanderer]]
2. [[The Mother]]
3. [[The Father]]
4. [[The Sibyl]]
5. [[The Warlord]]
6. [[The Wildling]]
7. [[The Wyrdling]]
8. [[The Night Queen]]

Each is maybe 150 feet tall. Strangely perfect, undamaged. Standing almost as if their backs are against the island, holding it up. 

Pilgrim's Path starts from each statue.  A small distance inland is the "pilgrim complex", with a temple of the divine associated with the statue, barracks, kitchens, hostel-type environment. 

Each pilgrim complex has a well, drinking from this well functions as an [Enchanted Spring](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/tcoe/dungeon-masters-tools#EnchantedSpringEffects)

Otherwise, these are abandoned but clues might be found.

### Possible Clues

**a. Stone Relief of the Moving Island**
- **Location**: Above the entrance of a waystation.
- **Description**: A carved relief showing an island with root-like structures extending into the ocean, moving towards a distant shore.
- **Interpretation**: Depicts the island's journey from Hkar, hinting at its magical origins.

**b. Abandoned Offering Bowls**
- **Observation**: Bowls filled with petrified flowers and coins, untouched due to their sacred nature.
- **Interpretation**: Indicates the pilgrims' reverence and may suggest that offerings were made to seek favor or protection.

**c. Worn Prayer Location**
- **Observation**: A small statue at the entrance to a pilgrim complex, worn with time as people touched it for luck and perhaps dropped small prayers on sealed scrolls in it. Could still read some scrolls, which might reveal prayers for healing, thanksgiving, all the normal things.

## Summit Temple

- The summit complex is circular, with eight distinct buildings, each devoted to one of the Eight Divines, surrounding a central courtyard.
- Each temple is unique and reflects the character of its divine patron:
  - One temple may be made of shimmering marble with open-air columns.
  - Another may be dark and somber, constructed from volcanic stone etched with images of storms and fire.
- Though partially ruined, the structures retain much of their original grandeur, including:
  - Statues of divine beings.
  - Intricate mosaics
  - Altars that still pulse with faint, residual magic.
- Pilgrims followed a clockwise path around the complex, entering each building to pray, receive blessings, and reflect on their journey.
- The grand courtyard is at the heart of the summit, dominated by:
  - A massive, weathered beacon made of light-infused crystal and stone, though its light has faded.
  - A towering bell that marked the start and end of each pilgrim’s journey, still intact and radiating a powerful presence.
- Surrounding the courtyard are remnants of rest facilities and administrative buildings that once supported pilgrims and clerics.

## Elf Specific Memorials

From Mike: *The first thought that comes to mind is something like an elaborate and enormous flowering tree. I think the vibe would very much be about memorializing the connection to Aldanor. Perhaps even something spontaneous- like the elven forest expressing its own loss of these elves who were still entangled with the mortal world*

These elven memorials may function like [Primal Fruit](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/tcoe/dungeon-masters-tools#PrimalFruit).

### **The Grove of Eternal Leaves**

- **Description**: Hidden among the island’s natural forests, this sacred grove is dedicated to the Living Tree, the elven god of life and nature. At its center stands a towering, ancient tree with bark like silver and leaves that shimmer in shades of gold, green, and blue. Elven pilgrims would come here to weave memorial wreaths of enchanted leaves, each representing a fallen elf. Even after centuries, some wreaths still hang on the branches, glowing faintly at night, and the tree’s roots whisper ancient elven prayers when touched, sharing echoes of those who were honored here.

### **The Starshard Archway**

- **Description**: This elegant archway, made from a rare, star-like crystal found only in elven lands, stands at a high point overlooking the sea, dedicated to the Wild Star. The arch is adorned with intricate carvings of constellations, elven warriors, and celestial beings, with small, delicate star-shaped flowers growing around its base. When moonlight or starlight hits the arch, the carvings light up, casting ethereal patterns onto the ground below. Elven pilgrims would gather under the arch to gaze at the sky, believing they could commune with the souls of fallen comrades and receive guidance from the Wild Star.

### **The Veil of Silent Echoes**

- **Description**: Nestled within a secluded glade is a stone veil—a finely carved, translucent wall of pale marble that seems to hum with a gentle, otherworldly melody. The wall is inscribed with the names of elven warriors who fell in the Downfall Wars, written in a script that seems to shift and change, becoming almost unreadable as if the words are not meant for mortal eyes. Elven pilgrims would press their foreheads against the veil and whisper their thoughts, sending messages to the departed. Occasionally, faint whispers of elven voices, songs, or battle cries echo softly from the stone, offering both comfort and a reminder of the sacrifices made.
