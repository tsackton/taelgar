# Adventure Overview - Tawir Forest

## How to use this note

Use this note for premise-level pre-design before outlining scenes. Work iteratively rather than treating the sections as a form to complete in order. Revisit earlier answers as the design develops, and keep provisional decisions visibly provisional.

This note should establish why the adventure exists, what the PCs are trying to accomplish, what outcomes are possible, what makes the best outcome difficult, what the players will mostly do, and how inertia, momentum, and failure will shape play. A blank difficult section is an unresolved design question, not a reason to skip the subject.

Overall context: [True Scenario Designery Index](https://theangrygm.com/true-scenario-designery/).

## Design context

Why does this adventure belong here in the campaign? What purpose should it serve, what experience or tone should it provide, and roughly how large should it be?

### Working design

The opening adventure of the next Great Library arc concerns [[Airion]]'s hidden work in [[Tawir Forest]] and leads into a power struggle on the [[Elemental Plane of Air]]. The broader arc should move away from Material Plane, Chardon, and Voltara concerns toward elemental conflict.

The initial [[Arc 6 - Initial Brainstorming|brainstorming]] proposes three connected mini-adventures over roughly ten sessions, with an approximate 3/3/4 split and a final boss fight. This remains a provisional scale.

The Tawir Forest adventure is a combat gauntlet: a classic D&D dungeon crawl into an old wizard's lair. This group enjoys tactical challenges, and the adventure should begin the arc with that experience. The intended length is three to four sessions.

## Brief overview

Summarize the adventure in a few plain sentences. What is happening, what do the PCs get drawn into, and what kind of adventure is this?

### Working premise

Airion hid an artifact or spell in an old lair in Tawir Forest that enables control of stable, large-scale elemental gates. A faction on the Plane of Air wants that capability to enable an invasion aimed at wiping out humans. The full quest is to recover Airion's hidden work and bring it to a contact of the opposing faction on the Plane of Air. See [[02 Backstory - Tawir Forest]] for the fictional background.

## Starting point

Reference: [Designing on Purpose](https://theangrygm.com/designing-on-purpose/).

Where does the adventure begin? How does the starting situation reveal the goal, make it worth pursuing, and give the players a clear way to begin?

### Adopted hook

[[Silverstorm]], the roc already known to the party, is an ally of the faction opposing Zadkai. He asks the party to recover Airion's hidden gate work from the old lair in Tawir Forest and deliver it to a contact of that faction on the Plane of Air.

The existing relationship supplies the connection: the party recovered a stolen roc egg, and Silverstorm aided them in the defense of Voltara. See [[Great Library Session Notes - Arc 2]]. The precise meeting circumstances and delivery contact remain to be filled in.

## Adventure goal

References: [Designing on Purpose](https://theangrygm.com/designing-on-purpose/); [Challenge Or Goal?](https://theangrygm.com/challenge-or-goal/).

What concrete, finishable state represents complete success? Distinguish the goal from a motivation, activity, plot development, or general desire.

### Adopted goal

Retrieve Airion's hidden gate artifact or spell and emerge from the lair with it: this is complete success for the dungeon crawl. The full quest continues with delivery to a contact of the opposing faction on the Plane of Air.

## Likely outcomes

References: [Quality Wins and Losses](https://theangrygm.com/quality-wins-and-losses/); [Scenario Design That Makes a Statement](https://theangrygm.com/scenario-design-that-makes-a-statement/).

Identify the foreseeable ways the adventure can end. Do the outcomes form discrete alternatives, thresholds, a continuous spectrum, or some combination?

- What is the best outcome?
- What lesser, costly, or incomplete outcomes are likely?
- What would the players reasonably perceive as a loss?
- Can play continue after a bad outcome, and how does the situation change?
- How will the players perceive and evaluate the outcome they achieved?

Do not invent an exhaustive possibility tree. Record only outcomes likely enough to affect the design.

### Adopted outcomes

- **Complete success:** the PCs retrieve Airion's hidden gate artifact or spell and emerge from the lair with it. They bring it to their delivery contact on the Plane of Air, who provides the next adventure.
- **Failure:** Zadkai's faction recovers the artifact or spell first. The party must pursue them to the Plane of Air and stop them from opening the gate.

### Design constraint

The outcome creates a simple branch: success leads to delivery and a new adventure from the contact; failure leads to pursuit to stop the gate opening. Additional outcome distinctions and other local failure cases remain open; a failed guardian assault followed by retreat gives Zadkai that guardian's key.

## Central challenge

References: [Designing on Purpose](https://theangrygm.com/designing-on-purpose/); [What Makes a Challenge](https://theangrygm.com/what-makes-a-challenge/); [Challenge Or Goal?](https://theangrygm.com/challenge-or-goal/).

What directly makes the best outcome difficult to achieve? Distinguish the central or major challenge from incidental encounters, ordinary resource attrition, and opposition that does not determine the outcome.

### Adopted opposition

The PCs must overcome Airion's traps and recover three keys held by three guardians. All three keys are needed to open the portal to Airion's hidden treasury. Airion could command the guardians to admit him; intruders must defeat or outwit them to obtain access.

Zadkai has a base camp in the forest and sends expeditions to discover how to gain access to the treasury and recover the keys. His minions function like wandering monsters and provide tension during the party's exploration. The intended dynamic is exploring and overcoming the lair's defenses while rival expeditions are active; Zadkai is not simply pursuing the party through the lair.

## X-factor

Reference: [Momentous and Inertial Adventure Design](https://theangrygm.com/momentous-and-inertial-adventure-design/).

What will the players mostly do to win? Name the primary activity and describe its repeating gameplay loop. Exploration, investigation, interaction, travel, acquisition, survival, killing, defense, escape, and overcoming are possibilities, not a fixed taxonomy.

Check that the goal, central challenge, outcomes, inertia, and momentum are principally about this activity. Individual scenes may have different immediate activities, and the X-factor does not determine whether the adventure must be linear or open.

### Working activity

Tactical combat is the primary activity in this classic dungeon crawl. The repeating activity is finding and overcoming the guardians to recover their keys, while dealing with the lair's traps and Zadkai's roaming expeditions. Recovering all three keys permits access to the treasury portal.

## Inertia

References: [Inertia: Fighting Failure](https://theangrygm.com/inertia-fighting-failure/); [Momentous and Inertial Adventure Design](https://theangrygm.com/momentous-and-inertial-adventure-design/).

What happens if the PCs make no effective progress? What continually resists victory or pushes the adventure toward worse outcomes?

- Is the inertia weak resistance, movement toward loss, or both?
- What advances or intensifies it: time, delay, retreat, failure, progress, or something else?
- How does it manifest in play, and how can the players perceive it?
- What outcomes does it make more likely?
- Does an existing timeline, rule, or fictional process already provide it?

Inertia is a gameplay dynamic, not necessarily a new clock or mechanic.

### Adopted primary inertia

The lair responds to being invaded. Its defenses become harder and adapt to the party's tactics as the adventure develops.

The specific triggers, limits, and manifestations of this adaptation remain to be designed, including how the players perceive it and respond.

### Additional tension

Zadkai's expeditions provide recurring opposition and tension, functioning like wandering monsters. A failed guardian assault followed by the party's retreat lets Zadkai obtain that guardian's key. His expeditions' other progress, responses to the PCs, and the effects of delay or rest remain undefined. No timed race or rule that resting automatically advances Zadkai has been adopted.

## Momentum

References: [Momentum: Building Victory](https://theangrygm.com/momentum-building-victory/); [Momentous and Inertial Adventure Design](https://theangrygm.com/momentous-and-inertial-adventure-design/).

What counts as progress, and how does that progress make later success more likely? Distinguish moving closer to the end from acquiring a persistent advantage against the remaining challenge.

- What advantages accumulate as the PCs succeed?
- Which later challenges become easier, safer, or more intelligible?
- How will the players perceive that their position has improved?

Momentum is a gameplay dynamic, not necessarily a new point system or mechanic.

### Adopted momentum

Discoveries in the lair equip the party to overcome later opposition. Research, knowledge, and useful items, including one-use items, make it easier to defeat Zadkai and the lair's guardians. As the party succeeds and explores, these discoveries give them advantages against the remaining challenges.

The specific discoveries and items remain to be designed.

## Failure handling

What happens when the PCs fail a local challenge or make a poor choice?

- Does play continue, and by what route?
- What immediate cost or changed situation follows?
- How do repeated failures compound?
- How many failures should be required before severe or unrecoverable consequences become plausible?
- Can the PCs recover, and what does recovery cost?

Do not default to stalled play, character death, or an improvised rescue. If a fallback or bargain exists, establish what it preserves and what meaningful cost it imposes.

### Adopted local setback

If the party fails to overcome a guardian and retreats alive, Zadkai obtains that guardian's key. This is a local setback; the adventure-level failure remains Zadkai's faction obtaining the artifact. To recover a key held by Zadkai, the party must raid his base camp in the forest. Recovery therefore requires an additional dangerous undertaking. Further failures, including a failed camp raid, trend toward losing the adventure to Zadkai's faction. The GM will improvise the exact sequence and consequences in play; no fixed number of failures or exhaustive branching sequence is required.

## Design statement

Reference: [Scenario Design That Makes a Statement](https://theangrygm.com/scenario-design-that-makes-a-statement/).

After working through the design, summarize the assignment in whatever concise form is useful. One possible form is:

The PCs must `_____` because `_____`, but `_____`.

This is a summary of the pre-design, not a substitute for it.

### Synthesis of adopted design

A three-to-four-session tactical dungeon crawl opens the next Great Library arc. The PCs must recover Airion's hidden work on controlling stable, large-scale elemental gates and deliver it to a contact on the Plane of Air, keeping it from Zadkai's faction, which wants to enable an invasion. Access to the treasury requires three keys held by guardians Airion could command. The PCs face traps, guardians, and Zadkai's expeditions from a forest base camp.

The lair escalates and adapts to the party's tactics, while discoveries of research, knowledge, and useful items give the party advantages against the remaining opposition. A failed guardian assault followed by retreat gives Zadkai a key; recovering it requires raiding his camp. Further failures trend toward adventure loss, with the details improvised in play. Success leads to delivery and a new adventure from the contact; failure leads to pursuit of Zadkai's faction to prevent it opening the gate.

Silverstorm, a familiar roc allied with the faction opposing Zadkai, brings the recovery-and-delivery request to the party.

## Dramatic question

State the central uncertainty that should sustain the adventure. For example: Will the PCs `_____` despite or before `_____`?

### Working dramatic question

Can the PCs overcome Airion's adaptive defenses and recover his hidden gate work despite Zadkai's competing expeditions?

## Alignment check

Before outlining scenes, check the complete design together:

- Does the goal define the best outcome?
- Does the central challenge directly obstruct that goal?
- Does the X-factor describe how the players primarily engage with that challenge?
- Does inertia push play toward the worse outcomes?
- Does momentum make progress improve the PCs' odds against the remaining challenge?
- Do local failures change the situation without accidentally ending play?
- Can the players perceive enough of these dynamics to make meaningful decisions?

### Premise review

The goal, guardian-held keys, and tactical combat emphasis align. Adaptive defenses supply the main resistance, while useful discoveries improve the party's ability to overcome later opposition. Lost keys change the situation and create a recovery objective; repeated failure can lead to a continuing campaign branch on the Plane of Air.

Silverstorm's request supplies the hook and the full recovery-and-delivery goal; the exact meeting circumstances remain open. Later construction must make the adaptive defenses, useful discoveries, and loss of a key intelligible to the players. The specific handling of repeated failures is intentionally left to GM improvisation.

## Open design questions

List unresolved premise-level decisions that must be answered before or during structural design. Do not silently convert possibilities into settled design.

### Current open decisions

- How Zadkai's expeditions interact with the guardians, keys, and PCs; how the faction can reach the artifact first.
- How the lair's defenses escalate and adapt to the party's tactics, and how the players perceive and respond to those changes.
- The research, knowledge, and items that help the party defeat the remaining guardians and Zadkai.
- Additional outcome distinctions.
- The identity of the delivery contact and the next adventure they provide.
- Whether the opposing faction wants the recovered artifact destroyed; this is a provisional motive.
- The precise circumstances of the opening meeting with Silverstorm.
- The artifact-versus-spell identity, name, and mechanics, deferred; its function is already established.
