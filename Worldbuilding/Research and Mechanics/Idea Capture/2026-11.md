## November 9 — Coins as propaganda and silver as wealth

*Inspiration: Britain's Most Historic Towns, “Viking York” (episode 2), presented by Alice Roberts; the discussion of the Viking hoard near Harrogate. [Episode information](https://www.channel4.com/press/news/ep2-britains-most-historic-townsviking-york-clips-embeds-available).*

> British most historic towns about York. Talking about a Viking hoard found outside (harragate?). A few ideas - coins as propaganda which suggests could use diverse coins more to make world feel alive. Eg St Peter’s coin. Also consider D&D economy and the idea of just shifting everything to silver and/or dropping less treasure. Like a hoard of a few hundred silver coins is extreme wealth. Bracelets as stored wealth too seems relevant

**Research:**

- **The hoard near Harrogate:** The Vale of York Hoard, found in 2007, contained 617 coins alongside silver objects, a gold arm-ring, and a gilded silver cup, wrapped in lead. Its contents included a dirham minted in Samarkand and a French-made vessel: a small cache could embody an enormous trading world. The museum describes its owner as exceptionally wealthy, based on the whole assemblage, including its substantial bullion and fine metalwork. This is a useful model for a compact, consequential treasure rather than a direct conversion from coin count to wealth. [York Museums Trust](https://www.yorkmuseumstrust.org.uk/blog/beyond-jorvik-the-vale-of-york-viking-hoard-andrew-woods/).
- **Religious and political claims in a purse:** A St Peter penny combines the saint's name and a sword usually interpreted as his with a hammer often interpreted as Thor's. The combination gives a concrete model for coins addressing overlapping religious communities. [University of Nottingham, Northumbrian Viking Penny](https://emidsvikings.ac.uk/items/northumbrian-viking-penny/). The Vale of York Hoard also contains an almost-new coin proclaiming Athelstan “king of all Britons,” a claim tied to his expanding authority in 927. A new title or emblem appearing in ordinary change could make a political transition visible during play. [York Museums Trust](https://www.yorkmuseumstrust.org.uk/blog/beyond-jorvik-the-vale-of-york-viking-hoard-andrew-woods/).
- **Wearable, divisible wealth:** The Galloway Hoard's broad-band arm-rings were made from recycled silver, mainly coins, and could be cut up and weighed as payment. Coin, ornament, and bullion were different forms the same silver could take. A bracelet could therefore serve as adornment, savings, and a source of change; a partly cut band could show that its wearer had already needed to spend some of it. [National Museums Scotland](https://www.nms.ac.uk/discover-catalogue/how-viking-is-the-galloway-hoard).

**Economy design:** Two separate choices are worth keeping open: make silver the usual unit of prices and treasure, and reduce how much purchasing power adventuring delivers. Relabeling every gold price and reward as silver changes the feel but preserves affordability. Making a few hundred silver coins transformative wealth also requires choosing what they buy: months of household expenses, livestock, a boat, or the means to establish a workshop. Those benchmarks could guide treasure awards without requiring a detailed economic simulation.

**Taelgar connections:**

- [[Coin Ideas]] already proposes Chardonian coins bearing the magistros's portrait, and hobgoblin coins bearing Cha'mutte's mark or, more recently, clan marks. These older brainstorming ideas are a direct starting point for propaganda in circulation: an old imperial mark and a new clan mark in the same purse could suggest changing allegiances.
- [[Coinage of Sembara]] establishes common silver denominations, historical debasement, and mistrust of unfamiliar hoard coins. A discovery could have a recognizable ruler, a problematic purity, and a local nickname. The earlier [[2024-01-10 - Coins|coin discussion]] proposes using names and debased hoards as flavor while keeping accounting abstract; that offers a way to add this detail without tracking every mint separately.
- [[Dwarven Coins]] establishes trusted trade bars stamped with known weights of silver or gold. Wearable bullion could provide a regional counterpart: merchants weigh a bracelet beside a dwarven bar, while its owner also sees it as something to display or give away.

### November 9 follow-up — The Horn of Ulf and stranger treasures

*Inspiration: The same Britain's Most Historic Towns episode on York; the Horn of Ulf at York Minster.*

> Follow up. Horn of Ulf. Unique treasure - elephant horn! More exotic things in treasure hoards. Sometime too staid - the real world
> Has wild things like elephant tusk horns in York

**Research:** The Horn of Ulf is an eleventh-century *oliphant*: a horn carved from an elephant's tusk, about 75 cm long. York Minster places its probable manufacture around Salerno or Amalfi in southern Italy, whose trade with Africa and the Islamic world influenced its decoration. A lion attacks a deer or antelope, while a unicorn and other mythical creatures circle the rim. The material, carving, and eventual home in York bring distant places together in one extraordinary object. [York Minster's object description](https://yorkminster.org/app/uploads/2023/02/20230302-Horn-of-Ulf.pdf).

It also carried a claim to land. Tradition holds that Ulf presented it around 1030 as a “horn of tenure,” filling it with wine and placing it on the altar to dedicate his lands to the church. That suggests treasure whose significance survives spending or sale: a spectacular foreign object could also embody a grant, an oath, or a family's contested inheritance. [York Minster](https://yorkminster.org/app/uploads/2023/02/20230302-Horn-of-Ulf.pdf).

**Taelgar connections:** [[Grimbaskal's Hoard]] already includes a giant-sized hunting horn carved from a mammoth tusk and covered in scrimshaw, alongside a stuffed owlbear and an alchemical treatise carved on metal cylinders. These are useful precedents to draw on more often. The Horn of Ulf adds another possibility: give a striking treasure a journey and a social purpose, so its unusual material, foreign workmanship, and local significance each offer something to discover.
