## October 26 — Telling everything ends a life

*Inspiration: Lost Cities with Albert Lin, Nan Madol episode.*

> Idea - anthropologist says so little known about Nan Madol because no writing and oral histories limited because the people believed if anyone told the whole story they would die because there life was no longer useful. 
>
> Could be a curse, a culture, or an actual species or fey realm or something where this is real

**Research:**

- Pohnpeian historian Rufino Mauricio describes historical knowledge as essential to a historian's physical existence: “Once they reveal everything they know, they will die.” The dangerous threshold is revealing everything one knows—an interesting variation on finishing one particular story. [Paper, p. 17](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED422011.pdf#page=24).
- Suzanne Falgout describes knowledge as the life force animating the body. Important knowledge is closely tied to particular people, restricted in circulation, and edited during transmission. That adds a social dimension: withholding part of a story can be part of how knowledge is properly passed on. [Study, p. 93](https://micronesica.org/sites/default/files/supp02-10ed_0.pdf).

**Taelgar connections:**

- [[Feywild]] realms take on the character of their rulers. A realm organized around stories, secrets, or unfinished lives gives this idea a natural home.
- [[Elven Cycle of Generations#Aging and Leaving|Older elves]] gradually lose memories and their pull toward the world; the ancients who remain have a strong reason to stay. A story still needing to be told could be a compelling reason for one to linger.

## October 26 — Ritual drugs, protection, and permission

*Inspiration: Lost Cities with Albert Lin, Nan Madol episode.*

> Pounding Sakau - narcotic ritual drug. Used in lots of ways but one seems to be keeping spirits away and blessing expeditions to the ruins. 
>
> Could be a common thing - a ritual drug to protect you from the dead or as part of a permission or blessing ceremony. Taelgar probably doesn’t have enough ritual drugs

**Research:**

- Sakau is a psychoactive drink made from kava roots. On Pohnpei it has ceremonial and everyday social uses: asking forgiveness, requesting a marriage, and sharing at feasts. A prospective father-in-law accepting the offered cup can signal agreement to the marriage; serving order at feasts expresses social rank. [Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9272527/).
- Preparation is itself ceremonial. Roots are cleaned with coconut husk and pounded on stone with basalt pounders, with particular tasks assigned to particular people and pounding begun at the master of ceremonies' direction. The roots are then squeezed through hibiscus bark into coconut-shell cups; the hibiscus gives Pohnpeian sakau its distinctive thick consistency. [Illustrated ceremony](https://www.comfsm.fm/~dleeling/ethnobotany/e63sakau.html), [study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9272527/).
- *Sakau en Olu* is an offering to offended spirits believed to cause illness or misfortune. Presenting it acknowledges the respect owed to the spirit and restores the relationship. [Cultural account, pp. 122–123](https://library.sprep.org/sites/default/files/traditional-knowledge-wisdom-themes-pacific-islands.pdf). Lin's crew brought sakau to the Nahnmwarki to request permission and blessings to film Nan Madol. [Local report, p. 11](https://kpress.fm/issues/KPress-Vol19-Iss23-28Oct19-WEB.pdf#page=11).

**Taelgar connections:**

- [[Gatza]] began as a lizardfolk ritual drug for speaking with the dead, harvested from the livers of large predatory fish. Its controlled communal use and dangerous solitary use offer two different ways to build customs around a substance.
- [[Pyravela]] ends with communal bonfires where incense and drugs bring visions and prophecy from the Wyrdling. This already places ritual drugs in a public festival, woven into feasts, prayers, and the turning of the year.
- [[Tanshi Worship]] makes the right offering essential to getting a spirit's attention. People carry offering bags, and Godcallers preserve the knowledge of which substances and rituals suit each tanshi—a useful pattern for everyday preparations and specialist-led ceremonies.

## October 27 — A sacred city built in a lagoon

*Inspiration: Lost Cities with Albert Lin, Nan Madol episode.*

> the basic layout of nan madol is also worth thinking about - a massive city of basalt megaliths built in the water around a coral atoll / in a coral lagoon. gives a different vibe than normal port cities, and also has a possibly interesting religious / spiritual dimension if the city itself is kinda a sacred space or something, or a palace, not a normal city

**Research:**

- Nan Madol occupies a reef flat beside Temwen Island in Pohnpei's lagoon. Its roughly hundred artificial islets are raised platforms edged with huge basalt walls, with waterways filling the spaces between them. The long basalt columns are stacked in alternating courses—a striking, timber-like construction in massive dark stone. [Archaeological description and photographs](https://iarii.org/research/monumental-architecture-of-nan-madol/), [wall construction](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1503/).
- The palace-and-sacred-space idea fits its function closely: the ruler's compound, priestly residences, ritual spaces, and royal tombs occupied distinct clusters of islets, with a massive seawall enclosing the complex. Nandowas, the royal burial complex, could also serve as a fort during war. [Spatial study and site plan](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/3/10/pgae399/7795949). The Saudeleur rulers also required potential rivals to reside there, keeping other chiefs close to the seat of power. [Metropolitan Museum essay](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/nan-madol).
- Waterways had religious uses too: oral traditions describe small canals admitting sacred eels from the sea, honored with offerings of sacrificed turtles. Excavations found traces of the canals and turtle remains. That gives the water threading through the settlement a ritual role alongside transport. [Metropolitan Museum essay](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/nan-madol).

**Taelgar connections:**

- [[Omi]] combines coral-and-stone architecture with a sacred kelp and coral garden at the heart of the city. A lagoon sanctuary could explore a surface counterpart: boat approaches and stone courtyards above, with sacred gardens and spirit spaces beneath the water.
- [[Eastern Isles]] sailors tell of islands moving and channels changing. A monumental lagoon complex among those islands could make knowing the proper approach part of ritual expertise—pilots, priests, and permission ceremonies all intertwined.

## October 31 — Pigment mines, forts, and the color trade

*Inspiration: Britain's Most Historic Towns, Roman Chester episode.*

> I’m watching Chester Roman British on Britain’s most historic towns.  Talking about cerulean blue mined from copper mine outside Chester. Should consider places where pigments are mined and the idea that forts and such can protect pigment mines and general idea of dyes and pigments as major trade goods. Have Tollen but could use more

**Research:**

- A close Cheshire parallel is Alderley Edge: archaeologists suggest its Roman Pot Shaft may have been worked for blue azurite nodules. Azurite is a copper mineral, often found alongside green malachite, which also supplies pigment. A single mining district can therefore produce both metal and colors. [Mining archaeology framework, p. 14](https://www.namho.org/documents/MINING_FRAMEWORK_Pt_one.pdf#page=33), [Columbia's Making and Knowing Project](https://teaching640.makingandknowing.org/resources/activity-sheets/azurite-assignment/).
- Useful naming distinction: modern artists' **cerulean blue** is manufactured cobalt stannate, available to artists from the 1860s; **azurite** supplies the mined copper-blue version of the idea. Another mineral color is **cinnabar red**, mined at Almadén in Spain since antiquity; its manufactured equivalent is vermilion. This offers different regional industries built around particular mineral deposits. [Fitzwilliam Museum pigment guide](https://unlocking-miniatures.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/terminology/).
- Processing creates grades and specialist trades. Azurite becomes paler as it is ground more finely; washing and settling separate the valuable deep-blue grains from finer, duller particles. Pigment washers and graders could matter as much as miners, with merchants buying sealed samples or disputing adulterated batches. [Preparation and reconstruction](https://teaching640.makingandknowing.org/resources/activity-sheets/azurite-assignment/).
- **Alum makes a second strategic resource:** it fixes dyes in textiles. Yorkshire's historical industry quarried shale, burned it in heaps for nine months, leached out liquor, and added human urine before crystallizing the alum. It connected quarries, fuel, processing works, coastal shipping, and urban urine collection. For worldbuilding, a fort could protect a dye industry's essential fixative supply, while raids on processing works could destroy months of work. [National Trust history](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/yorkshire-coast/history-on-the-yorkshire-coast).

**Taelgar connections:**

- [[Tollen Dyes]] already establishes brilliant, colorfast Tollen red, while [[Tollen Magical Tattoo]] ties magical inks to the secrets of the [[Dyer's Guild]]. This could expand into a network of suppliers: distant pigment mines or alum quarries, specialist refiners, and guild agents securing contracts and armed protection. The city's expertise and its suppliers' control of deposits could be separate sources of power.
- The [[Copper Hills]], between Tokra and Darba, were once famous for copper mining and now contain abandoned mining settlements such as [[Vandar]]. They offer a natural place to consider blue and green mineral pigments: an old fort guarding a particularly valuable color-bearing seam, or merchants financing a return to abandoned workings because the pigment is worth recovering.
