## October 26 — Telling everything ends a life

*Inspiration: Lost Cities with Albert Lin, Nan Madol episode.*

> Idea - anthropologist says so little known about Nan Madol because no writing and oral histories limited because the people believed if anyone told the whole story they would die because there life was no longer useful. 
>
> Could be a curse, a culture, or an actual species or fey realm or something where this is real

**Research:**

- Pohnpeian historian Rufino Mauricio describes historical knowledge as essential to a historian's physical existence: “Once they reveal everything they know, they will die.” The dangerous threshold is revealing everything one knows—an interesting variation on finishing one particular story. [Paper, p. 17](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED422011.pdf#page=24).
- Suzanne Falgout describes knowledge as the life force animating the body. Important knowledge is closely tied to particular people, restricted in circulation, and edited during transmission. That adds a social dimension: withholding part of a story can be part of how knowledge is properly passed on. [Study, p. 93](https://micronesica.org/sites/default/files/supp02-10ed_0.pdf).

**Taelgar connections:**

- [[Feywild]] realms take on the character of their rulers. A realm organized around stories, secrets, or unfinished lives gives this idea a natural home.
- [[Elven Cycle of Generations#Aging and Leaving|Older elves]] gradually lose memories and their pull toward the world; the ancients who remain have a strong reason to stay. A story still needing to be told could be a compelling reason for one to linger.

## October 26 — Ritual drugs, protection, and permission

*Inspiration: Lost Cities with Albert Lin, Nan Madol episode.*

> Pounding Sakau - narcotic ritual drug. Used in lots of ways but one seems to be keeping spirits away and blessing expeditions to the ruins. 
>
> Could be a common thing - a ritual drug to protect you from the dead or as part of a permission or blessing ceremony. Taelgar probably doesn’t have enough ritual drugs

**Research:**

- Sakau is a psychoactive drink made from kava roots. On Pohnpei it has ceremonial and everyday social uses: asking forgiveness, requesting a marriage, and sharing at feasts. A prospective father-in-law accepting the offered cup can signal agreement to the marriage; serving order at feasts expresses social rank. [Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9272527/).
- Preparation is itself ceremonial. Roots are cleaned with coconut husk and pounded on stone with basalt pounders, with particular tasks assigned to particular people and pounding begun at the master of ceremonies' direction. The roots are then squeezed through hibiscus bark into coconut-shell cups; the hibiscus gives Pohnpeian sakau its distinctive thick consistency. [Illustrated ceremony](https://www.comfsm.fm/~dleeling/ethnobotany/e63sakau.html), [study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9272527/).
- *Sakau en Olu* is an offering to offended spirits believed to cause illness or misfortune. Presenting it acknowledges the respect owed to the spirit and restores the relationship. [Cultural account, pp. 122–123](https://library.sprep.org/sites/default/files/traditional-knowledge-wisdom-themes-pacific-islands.pdf). Lin's crew brought sakau to the Nahnmwarki to request permission and blessings to film Nan Madol. [Local report, p. 11](https://kpress.fm/issues/KPress-Vol19-Iss23-28Oct19-WEB.pdf#page=11).

**Taelgar connections:**

- [[Gatza]] began as a lizardfolk ritual drug for speaking with the dead, harvested from the livers of large predatory fish. Its controlled communal use and dangerous solitary use offer two different ways to build customs around a substance.
- [[Pyravela]] ends with communal bonfires where incense and drugs bring visions and prophecy from the Wyrdling. This already places ritual drugs in a public festival, woven into feasts, prayers, and the turning of the year.
- [[Tanshi Worship]] makes the right offering essential to getting a spirit's attention. People carry offering bags, and Godcallers preserve the knowledge of which substances and rituals suit each tanshi—a useful pattern for everyday preparations and specialist-led ceremonies.
