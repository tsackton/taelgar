## Historical Perspective

Many dwarves can recite family histories going back thousands of years, to the early days of Taelgar and the triumphs and defeats of the [[Downfall Wars]]. This long memory gives dwarven society a tendency to always be looking backwards. Indeed, dwarven historians reckon years from the beginning of predictable time, at the end of the [[Riving]], and their [[Calendar Eras#Dwarven Count of Years|Count of Years]] is widely used by scholars of other peoples. Though not widely known outside of dwarven communities, behind this reckoning lies a continuous count of hours from the beginning of time, with years marking intervals in an unbroken sequence. For some dwarves, this becomes a deep nostalgia for the glory days of yore.

Among the defining events of ancient dwarven history was the Sundering, a schism over how to respond to dangers gathering beneath the mountains in the long quiet years before the [[The Downfall|Downfall]]. Some held that the dwarves must remain and defend their underground homes, honoring their obligations to their ancestors and the Bahrâzel. Others argued for leaving their ancestral halls, fleeing the dangers that threatened them, and building new lives far way, aboveground. The division led many dwarves to depart, placing the claims of ancestral obligation and the freedom to choose another life in lasting tension.

Much later, the [[Great War]] transformed the dwarven kingdoms of the western mountains. [[Ardith]] was destroyed, and its refugees crossed Dunmar to establish [[Nardith]] in the [[Yuvanti Mountains]]. [[Khatridun]], devastated by the war, turned inward toward its history, while [[Nidzahar]] grew wealthy through trade and technological innovation.
