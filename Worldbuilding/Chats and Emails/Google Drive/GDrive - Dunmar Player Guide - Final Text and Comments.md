## Dunmar: A Guide

In the 191st year of the Nayan Dynasty, during the rule of Samraat Nayan Karnas, reckoned as the year 1748 after the founding of Drankor by the Chardonian counting.

## Geography, Climate, and Ecology

Dunmar in 1748 DR is a dramatically different place than it was 250 years ago, before the Great War. Then, it was a land of rolling grassland and winding rivers, stretching from the fertile Mahar river valley to the wide Nevos Sea, from the ~~CentralMountains~~ in the north to the Sea of Storms in the south. Regular summer thunderstorms raced across the plains, feeding the major rivers of the Hara, Kharja, and Mahar, along which riparian forests grew. In the south and east, regular flooding refreshed the soil and provided fertile land for farming, while in the north and west vast plains on the central plateau provided grazing and pasture for the famed horses of the Dunmari. Only the Mahar river delta, where the ruins of Drankor lurked in perpetual mists, was to be avoided.

Now, Dunmar is a land of contrasts. At the end of the Great War, the Second Plague and the Desolation of Cha’mutte spread from the Mahar river valley west and north, upending the land, while in the west of Dunmar, the Yuvanti Mountains were suddenly upthrust by the chaos, forming the high desert of the Garamjala.

### Climatic Overview

The climate of Dunmar is shaped by five major factors:

- The seasonal shift in prevailing winds, from easterly in the winter to westerly in the summer, which bring monsoon rains off the Nevos Sea and the Sea of Storms;
- The Yuvanti mountains, which rise sharply out of the flat coastal plain in the south, becoming gentler in the north;
- The central plateau, the high interior land behind the Yuvanti mountains, that rises from the confluence of the Hara and the Mahar;
- The southern cliffs, a series of steep cliffs and rocky terrain that separates the central plateau from the coastal plains to the south; and
- The ~~CentralMountains~~, soaring high above the northern portions of the interior plateau.

### Western Dunmar

- Southwest of the Yuvanti Mountains, on the Nevos coast
- Three major rivers -- from north to south, the Naya, the Shrev, and the Chaudi -- drain from the Yuvanti Mountains to the Nevos Sea.
- Coastal climate with year round rain in the far north, nestled against the coastal Apurnian Range
- Heavily monsoon-based climate with dramatic rainy and dry seasons supporting a savanna from north of the Naya to the land between the Shrev and the Chaudi
- More tropical with year round rain and coastal jungles south of the Chaudi
- Rocky coasts with harbors in the far north, otherwise swampy marshland transitioning to extensive mangroves in the Chaudi river delta

### Eastern Dunmar

- Northeast of the Yuvanti mountains
- Hard, rocky terrain, with semi-arid grasslands blending to canyonlands and scrubland further south; highly seasonal summer rain from the monsoons supports grassland but few trees
- Home to herds of antelope, axe beaks, and wild horses, packs of hyenas, lions, and giant wolf spiders, buzzards and giant vultures, and the occasional giant scorpion, along with many smaller mammals and birds
- Further south, in the rain shadow of the highest peaks of the Yuvanti, the extremely dry Garamjala Desert

### The Desolation of Cha’mutte

- The eastern border of Dunmar is dominated by the desolation of Cha’mutte, the general term that refers to the lands from the Sea of Storms north along the Mahar river valley to the Plaguelands, all cursed by Cha’mutte during the Great War.
- The Blasted Plains, called the Nashtkar, is the name given to the lands east of Dunmar’s borders. This is a land of strange magic, wild monstrosities, and other unknown dangers, where all but the bravest refuse to tread.
- To the south, the Nashtkar joins the vast Garamjala desert, deadly dry and desolate.
- Approaching the coast, great cliffs cascade down to a coastal plain along the shores of the Sea of Storms. A vast, rapidly growing jungle now stretches from the Mahar delta and the ruins of Drankor west to the River Chaudi in southern Dunmar.

### The Coasts of Dunmar

- To the west, the Nevos sea, a broad, generally calm bay, protected from the storm of the Endless Ocean by a major peninsula to the west, dotted with small islands and trading ships.
- To the south, the Sea of Storms, a place of great danger and mystery, where ships are regularly lost and storms appear suddenly with no warning

## People and Places

The region was once dominated by the mighty empire of Dunmar, a people skilled at horseback and warfare, hunting and herding, the chosen people of the goddess Jeevali and her divine siblings. However, Dunmar born the brunt of the fallout from Cha’mutte Plague, and the people were decimated in the battles during and after the Great War.

Now, Dunmar is a shadow of its former self. The formal borders of Dunmar, the land claimed by the Samraat Nayan Karnas, the current ruler of the Dunmari, stretches from the Myraeni Gap in the northwest, the ~~CentralMountains~~ in the north, the edge of the Nashtkar and Garamjala desert in the east, the River Chaudi to the south, and the Nevos coast to the west. In practice, only a fraction of this territory is effectively controlled by the Dunmari.

Traditionally, Dunmar had been ruled by the Samraat, the ruler who is acknowledged as the embodied representation of the divine will of Jeevali and the Divine Family, and thus ruled with complete authority, commanding the absolute allegiance of the heads of clans and small villages, as well as total authority over the priestly hierarchy that runs the temples. The current ruler, Samraat Nayan Karnas, claims this divine authority, although somel, especially in the east have their doubts.

### Western Dunmar

- Heart of the Nayan dynasty; relatively densely settled
- Productive land that supports a variety of agriculture, dominated by cattle and horse ranching on the broad pasturelands north of the Nayan river valley.
- Most settlements inland and along the Nayan river valley; the coast is swampy and little inhabited
- Nomadic ways largely abandoned, and the people now live in small villages and market towns; though some still travel to winter pastures after the rainy season with their livestock
- **Nayahar**, at the head of navigation on the Nayan river, is the largest city of western Dunmar, the home of the Samraat court. It is city of grand architecture, with numerous temples and monuments giving thanks to the gods and praising the wisdom and might of the Nayan dynasty.

### Coastal Dunmar

- In the coastal foothills of the Yuvanti and the Apurnian Range, between rocky headlines overlooking scattered islands and the rising mountains of the interior
- Rougher land with a mild coastal climate and the occasional sheltered harbor that connects this area of Dunmar to the sea.
- Densely settled
- Trade, especially with Chardon, is significant here, as is fishing, timber production, and mining in the Apurnian Range.
- Market towns, some growing to be small cities in their own right, are common here; this land was the least disrupted by the upheavals after the Great War, so many settlements are old and well-established
- Largest populations of non-Dunmari in Dunmar, including Illorians, Chardonians, and non-humans, primarily dwarves and halflings; a few halfling fishing villages dot the hinterlands and the largest population of dwarves outside Yuvanti lives in the Apurinian foothills
- **Darba**, the largest city, is among the oldest and most cosmopolitan cities in present-day Dunmar, and predates the Great War. Chardonian trade missions, a dwarven quarter, temples to the Eight Divine, and halfling inns all thrive here, and languages other than Dunmari are regularly heard in the markets and harbors.

### Central Dunmar

- Expansive plains stretching from the Myraeni Gap east to the Hara River
- The place where the old ways of the Dunmari are the strongest.
- The seasonal monsoons bring thunderstorms that support vast grasslands, while the high elevation of the central plateau moderates the hot summer temperatures
- Nomadic pastoralism is a way of life, with many family groups and small bands making regular circuits around the land, moving towards the higher plateaus in the hot spring and summer, and returning to small villages during the monsoon, when grasses grow everywhere. Although changing climate and patterns of rainfall since the great upheaval have disrupted traditional rhythms of life, the central importance of horses and seasonal migrations to new grazing lands have remained. Many families here have traveled this land since before the Great War, and many new families have settled here from the east.
- Notable towns include:
  - **Songara**, to the west, a large trading center, the site of massive horse markets to supply the Chardon trade
  - **Tokra,** along the river Hara, an ancient city with the major monastery of the Lakan Mysteries nearby and an important role the ceremonial life of the Samraat; nearly destroyed in DR 1553 by an attack of orcs lead by a fire giant; rebuilt in part by dwarves, which still marks its architecture
  - **Askandi**, to the south near the Yuvanti mountains, a center of trade with the dwarves

### Eastern Dunmar

- Arid plains and deserts from the Hara river in the west to the Nashtkar in the east and the Garamjala Desert in the south
- Sparsely populated, mostly by pastoralist herders, trying to keep their flocks of horse, goat, and sheep alive through the long, dry winters to be refreshed in the brief summer rainstorms.
- Four notable oasis in the southeast provide refuge and support for more settled lifestyles: **Karawa**, **Bas Udda, Gomat,** and **Niteni**.
  - Karawa Oasis, on the northern side of the Great Red Mesa, is the central focus of the village of Karawa
  - Bas Udda Oasis, on the southeastern side of the Great Red Mesa, is the central focus of the village of Bas Udda
  - Gomat Oasis, east of Karawa on the Old Trade Road, is too close to the Nashtkar to be settled, but provides a convenient watering hole on the eastern border of Dunmar
  - Niteni Oasis, to the southwest towards Askandi, is the largest oasis and supports a small agricultural and market town, Niteni.
- The Great Red Mesa rises from sharp cliff walls thousands of feet into the sky, its massive bulk of deep ochre, rich oranges, and muted browns dominating the horizon for miles around.

### Dwarves of the Yuvanti

- The Yuvanti mountains rise steeply from the coastal plains, a line of sharp peaks freshly uplifting in the chaos after the Great War, higher to south and more jumbled, rolling hills to the north
- Few Dunmari live in the steep mountains or the rough, arid foothills on the eastern slopes of the Yuvanti
- The large dwarven kingdom of ~~DK1~~ rules beneath and among the peaks.
  - Founded and is still largely populated by the dwarven clans -- the Boulderbeards, the Brawnanvils, the Strongaxes, the Redpeaks, and the Deepcaverns -- that fled the destruction of their homes and kingdoms in the southern part of the ~~CentralMountains~~ at the end of the Great War.
  - Grown comfortable on profits of trade in ore, precious metals, and metalwork from the mountains
  - The growing “Long Road” connecting ~~DwarvenCity1~~ in the south, through Tharn Todor, to ~~DwarvenCity2~~ in the north near Darba, when complete, will provide means of rapid transportation of goods along the 800 mile stretch of the Yuvanti.

### Further Afield

Outside of Dunmar itself, a number of notable places are described briefly below.

#### Chardon

- West and north of Dunmar, on the coast of the Endless Sea
- Heart of a vast empire stretching far into the north
- Home of world-renowned scholars, powerful magic, and a devastatingly effective military
- Rumors of clean water flowing by magic from every fountain and lights that dance above the streets give Chardon an air of mystery and wonder.

#### Iloria

- Two large islands, Burganos and Silanus, and scattered smaller islands in the Nevos Sea
- A mixing pot, a land of refugees and runaways, misfits and outcasts, one of the few places whose population grew during the Blood Years
- A collection of small free ports, minor kingdoms, and the occasional pirate autocracy.
- In the last decade, a new Magistros in Chardon made a name for himself by crushing the Illorian pirates, and the islands of Burganos and Silanus are now Protectorates of Chardon.

#### Sembara and the Green Sea

- North and east of Dunmar, across the mountains and the Plaguelands
- A land of forests and river valleys, fertile land, and old kingdoms
- While for many years Dunmar was cut off from the Green Sea by the terrors of the Plaguelands and the Desolation of Cha’mutte, over the past several generations trade has begun to trickle in, led by halfling caravans making the long circuit from Sembara to Chardon and back.

#### Myraeni Gap

- The land north and west of Songara, between Dunmar and the borders of the Chardonian Empire
- Rough, broken terrain, full of cliffs and gullies, as the land tumbles down from the high plateaus of Dunmar to wide lowlands stretching to Chardon and the sea.
- Claimed by the Myraeni, a mysterious people, and a very old civilization, largely unconquered even at the height of the Drankorian empire, living isolated in the hills from the Myraeni Gap north towards the ~~CentralMountains~~
- Since the Blood Years the Myraeni have been rarely seen; in their absence kobolds have become a nuisance, exacting heavy tolls on trade and often turning to banditry.

#### The Desolation of Cha’mutte

- East of Dunmar, a broken wasteland stretching nearly 1000 miles east to the dead forests of ~~DeadForest~~, 500 miles north to the barren ashy Plaguelands south of Sembara, and 900 miles south through the vast Garamjala desert to the haunted mires of Drankor.
- Rumors persist of vast orc hordes, warlike hobgoblin nations, and stranger evils hidden in the depths of the desolation.

#### Chaudi Jungles

- South of the Shrev in the west lie the dense forests of the Chaudi jungles
- Centered around the Chaudi river valley and stretching along the coast of the Sea of Storms towards Drankor.
- Rapidly growing with almost unnatural speed, and now thick with strange plants and animals.
- While formally claimed by Dunmar, the few people that live in these jungles acknowledge no king or overlord.

### Notable Settlements

Below, a few notable settlements are described in more narrative detail.

Karawa

In eastern Dunmar, nestled around an oasis in the shelter of the Great Red Mesa lies the small town of Karawa, about a week’s journey on foot from the wild frontiers of the Nashtkar. Surrounded by wide open expanses of dry, arid scrublands, broken only by the occasional rocky hill, the town has only a small permanent population of a few hundred people. These people are mostly dedicated to the Temple of Shakun, or are tradespeople living in mostly small houses of adobe and mud, facing narrow streets, with shade provided by colorful awnings, although some eke out a living growing crops that require little water and lots of heat, particularly dates and honey.

The small oasis is the central feature and lifeblood of the town. Over the years, a marble pool and short wall have been constructed around the oasis, allowing water to be drawn off at need. Palm trees provide shade and make the oasis square a place where people linger.

Adjacent to the oasis square sits the largest of the permanent structures in Karawa, the imposing structure of the Temple of Shakun, home to a mystery cult dedicated to the goddess Shakun. A large, imposing structure carved out of the side of the Great Red Mesa, the temple itself stretches partially into the cliff, but non-initiates are not allowed access to anything beyond a few outbuildings, a small market where the Mystai of Shakun trade dusts and powders with magical properties, and the chapel to Shakun facing the oasis. The chapel is beautiful - polished sandstone floors, and tile mosaics abound - but is a place primarily for prayer and does not seem to hold any secrets.

A few other public buildings cluster near the oasis: the main Temple of the Five in town, dedicated to the five divine siblings of the Dunmari pantheon; a pentagonal, domed building that serves as the meeting space for the council of elders, who regularly meet to deliberate, and a small public square where the judgements and pronouncements of the council are issued. On the northern outskirts of town, a caravanserai, a large square building with a courtyard, rooms for travelers, and space for merchants and animals, welcomes visitors as well.

Karawa is notable as the a central point of celebration of the Festival of Rebirth, the ceremony marking the sacrifice and return of Jeevali that takes place in spring, before the brief rains of summer return. Around the time of the festival, the population swells, as nomadic herders gather to attend the Festival, trade, and exchange news. A large market square on the northwestern edge of town, paved with vast tiles of polished sandstone, stands empty most of the year, but fills with shouts of commerce and greeting during the week of the Festival. Beyond, an open expanse lies, where the camps of nomads sprout as the festival crowds gather.

#### Bas Udda

Southeast of the Great Red Mesa lies the small community of Bas Udda. Like Karawa, Bas Udda is an oasis town, able to exist and thrive in the hot, arid climate only due to the existence of a significant, permanent water source. Unlike Karawa, in Bas Udda the oasis sits atop of substantial subsurface aquifer, and this has allowed a small agricultural community to develop.

Bas Udda is in many ways similar to Karawa: a cluster of adobe houses on narrow streets form the center of the village, facing the oasis, with a caravanserai and a few craftspeople rounding out the town. The agricultural hinterlands of Bas Udda stretch further, towards the Red Mesa, where irrigation channels bring life to the small holdings growing cereal grains and fruit. Much of the grain grown here is turned to beer, and the local caravanserai produces and trades barrels to much of eastern Karawa.

## A Chronicle of Dunmari History

### The Land Before The Dunmari

- More than a thousand years ago, the land was the center of the great Drankorian Empire.
- The city of Drankor, founded near the mouth of the Mahar river, grew rapidly, and the nascent Drankorian Empire quickly spread up the river valley, and across the highlands of Dunmar to the Myraeni Gap.
- The land of Dunmar and the city of Drankor at the mouth of the Mahar river remained the heart of the Drankorian Empire for more than a millennia, prospering under the growing knowledge and power of Drankorian magic. Many tombs, monuments, libraries, temples, cities, and fortifications from this era dot the land, although many more were destroyed during the fall of Drankor.
- A little more than 1000 years after the founding of Drankor, the Empire fell, as a devasting plague of magical origin swept through the heartland of Dunmar. The worst of the devastation happened in the region that is now Dunmar. The mouth of the Mahar and the ruins of Drankor itself were left a haunted, cursed desolation, and millions of people died as the Empire fell apart.

### The Founding of Dunmar

- After the fall of Drankor and the First Plague in DR 1059, death and destruction spread across the land, and the rains failed and the plains dried to dust.
- A few generations later, the Dunmari rode north from their ancestral homelands in the southeast of Taelgar, driven by a great cataclysm
- While some turned east to settle on the coast of the Green Sea, the bulk of this migration settled in what is now Dunmar, reaching the Mahar river, formerly the eastern border of Dunmar, in 1171 DR

*What follows is the story of the founding of Dunmar told by the Dunmari.*

When the great exodus of the Dunmari from the south reached the River Kharja, Bhishma, already a leader of her people, stopped and ordered the construction of a great temple, and began to pray. Begging Jeevali to save her people, she prayed without food or water or sleep for five days and five nights, until Jeevali answered her prayers. As Bhishma prayed, the rains began. Jeevali wept for her people, crying tears that poured from the heavens, cleansing the land and purifying the soil. But as she cried, she put so much of her power and spirit into her tears that she began to fade away. She diminished, and was gone.

The people wept, but Aagir appeared to them in a vision, angry, saying "My sister has sacrificed herself for you. To you, I say this. Her sacrifice was for you alone. This land is made whole and new, and a great kingdom can grow from the ashes. But it must start with bloodshed. Take this land in my name. You will be blessed, but you are not special, you are no different from other kingdoms. I do not give you the land, but tell you to take it with violence. Your empire will grow from a seed of destruction, as all things must, for death is necessary for new life to begin."

Asuddha, the brother of Bhishma, then took up arms, and called an army, and the people rode across the land, conquering and killing all who would stand before them, until the new Kingdom of Dunmar stretched from the Mahar river to Nevos Bay. But Bhishma and many of the people of Dunmar stayed at Temple, and wept for Jeevali and for the people slaughtered in the name of the gods. After nearly a year of battle and conquest, and tears and prayers, Asuddha returned to the temple and stood before his sister, saying, "I have made you the ruler of a great land, a land of plenty."

As he spoke, he was amazed, for his sister Bhishma stood and the spirit of Jeevali was beside her, and she said, speaking, "I have not been idle either, my brother, for as you conquered we prayed, and made sacrifices in the name of Jeevali, and lo she is returned."

And then Jeevali rose, and stepped forward to the people of Dunmar gathered at the Temple, and they rejoiced in her name and praised her and Bhishma, and Jeevali admonished the people to forget not this day, and every year hence the Festival of Rebirth has been celebrated at the driest time of the year in the spring, before the seasonal rains come, to mark the death and life of Jeevali and her cleansing of the land for the Dunmari.

It is said that Laka was there as well to witness the rebirth and renewal of his sister. Bhishma and Laka spent the night together, and from that union two children were born, twins, one divine and one mortal. The Divine, Shakun, looked to dangers and needs of the people that were not visible; the mortal, Kotana, looked outward to the physical needs of the people and the land, and would become the next ruler of Dunmar.

### The Aatmaji Dynasty

- The first dynasty of the Dunmari, founded by the Samraat Aatmaji Bhishma after the short and swift conquest of Dunmar, though Bhishma’s rule was short
- Fully established by Bhishma’s son Kotana, said to be fathered by Laka himself; during Samraat Aatmaji Kotana’s reign, the kingdom spread from the Myraeni Gap to the Mahar river
- During this time the kingdom was troubled by frequent dangers stirring in the swamps of Drankor; three times the riders of Dunmar rode forth to drive back evil, but each time the losses were greater and the cost harder to bear.
- In DR 1392, the last Aatmaji ruler, Samraat Aatmaji Dasa, decided to end the Drankorian threat once and for all, riding arrogantly to battle to cleanse the haunted swamps and fetid ruins of Drankor, and in his folly was utterly destroyed, ending the Aatmaji line.
- Despite Dasa’s failures, the 212 years of the Aatmaji Dynasty were ones of prosperity, and it is said during these years the gods themselves chose wise rulers to be Samraat, ensuring the Dunmari would thrive under skilled leadership

### The Dharajun Dynasty

- From the disastrous assault of Drankor in DR 1392, few returned, and for some time no new Samraat was chosen
- In 1399 DR, still without a Samraat, the Dunmari were attacked by the Thundering Axe Confederation, a group of orc tribes out of the east; by 1401 DR, the eastern third of Dunmar was overrun.
- Into this disaster rode Jita, a young woman from the far northwest of Dunmar, on a horse that seemed to move like the wind and radiate divine power. Winning several decisive victories again the orcs and unifying the country behind her, Jita was acclaimed Samraat Dharajun Jita in 1403 DR, founding the Dharajun Dynasty.
- It is said that they gods spoke through their divine presence in JIta’s horse, who was reborn generation after generation to guide the choice of rulers
- The rule of the Dharajun Dynasty became known as the Dynasty of the Horse.
- By 1500 DR, after a hundred years of peace, rumors of evil began to trouble the Dunmari; hobgoblin raids from the north increased; and much of the population retreated south of the Old Trade Road, which started to be fortified
- Raven’s Hold was established in the north to protect the northern passes to ~~Irebor~~

### The Great War

- In DR 1542, an army of hobgoblins, a coalition of tribes led by the Death Moon clan, invaded Dunmar from the north, but this initial attack was crushed by the Dunmari
- The northern border along the Old Trade Road was further fortified during DR 1543 in the face of rumors of a vast underground army led by terrifying, brain-eating monsters
- In the winter of DR 1544, Cha’mutte established a presence in Drankor, unwatched, and small armies of undead minions began emerging from the south to threaten Dunmar
- While the first attacks caught Dunmar unprepared, by DR 1544 the country was fully on war footing, holding the defense of the north and the Drankorian border, until the Killing Mist, the Red Death, emerges from the south, the red Plague of Cha’mutte that kills all in its path
- Despite desperate acts of heroism that prevent vastly more death in Chardon and Sembara, the Samraat Dharajun Ajala falls in the winter of DR 1545, and the Divine horse is lost, ending the Dharajun Dynasty after 142 years

### The Red Death

- In the aftermath of the Great War, the Dunmari are left leaderless as the land itself begins to shake and change; many died in the war, and many more die in the next year of chaos
- The twelve years of death and destruction immediately following the Great War are reckoned by the Dunmari as the years of Red Death, twelve years of famine, disease, and drought that reshaped the country

#### The West

- Considerably less upheaval than further east; more survivors, supported by grain from Chardon
- In DR 1546, a consensus ruler, Alal, is chosen by the Mulaak, but she claims no divine right, does not take a dynastic name, and does not assume leadership of the temple hierarchy, which remains leaderless
- The land from Tokra in the west to Darba in the east stabilizes under Alal’s competent rule, as refugees stream in from the east
- In DR 1552, the fragile peace was shattered by a large band of orcs, trolls, and ogres from the east, led by the fire giant Mavdyr; Tokra was overrun, with many surviving only by the magical defenses of the Lakan Mystai; Mavdyr was defeated by dwarves and stoneborn traveling south, the last of the great dwarven migration to the Yuvanti, who then stayed for a year to rebuild Tokra
- After the death of Alal, no Samraat emerged and central Dunmar was left without a ruler
- To the west, in Darba, the vanguard of the orc horde had been driven back by a motley group of Dunmari and Illorians, led by a charismatic Illorian captain named Chire, who then claimed right of sovereignty over the coastal plains around Darba, fortifying the city and establishing a small administration, which mostly served to collect tax to enrich himself.
- By DR 1557, the interior east of Darba was collapsing into a collection of independent clan leaders and city-states, while the coastal area around Darba was the small kingdom fo Chire

#### The New Lands

- West of the newly risen Yuvanti mountains, a fertile, empty land stretched, initially settled by the first cautious travellers in DR 1548, who met isolated families who had survived the upheavals, the cracking earth, the floods, the shifting ground.
- In DR 1549, the last remnants of Cha’mutte’s army of the undead emerged from the growing jungles around the Chaudi river, moving north in a mindless desire to slaughter the living; the Dunmari were driven back to the Shrev, where they held their defenses
- By early DR 1550, this undead army was defeated, but afterward few dared journey into the jungles to the south, for fear of what may still lurk there, and the Shrev became, at least in practice, the southern border of settlement.
- After the death of Alal in DR 1553, the people of the coastal plains refused to acknowledge the rule of Chire, who had claimed Darba and all the lands south to the Shrev for himself; no major battles were fought, but border tensions and banditry grew
- In DR 1556, Kundar, a respected captain who had made a name for himself in the undead wars nine years ago, began traveling around the plains, preaching that a sign from the gods was needed, and recruiting aid to construct a massive monument on the central river to the Five Divines. During this time, he often traveled with a priest of Aagir known as Guri, and it is said that she counseled much of the actions he took.
- In DR 1557, the monument complete, during the Festival of Rebirth Kundar is said to have prayed alone for a day and a night; when he emerged the next day, he gathered all the people who would listen, and told them he had received a vision, that the death of Cha’mutte was the destiny fulfilled of the Dunmari, and that this new land was the gift of the gods to reward the people for their suffering.
- A Mulaak was called, and Kundar was chosen ruler, choosing the name Nayan to be that of this new dynasty; the Nayan Dynasty was thus founded in DR 1557.

#### The East

- East of Tokra, the devastation of Cha’mutte’s death was greatest; the red mist of the deadly plague drifted west, and frequent storms of deadly winds and searing rain flew out of the east, turning much of the land to desert; many, many families died alone, starving and thirsty, unmarked in the wastes of eastern Dunmar.
- In DR 1546, a great many beasts and monstrosities begin to emerge from the deserts to the east, many gaunt and hungry, fleeing the horrors of the red mists and the desolation of Cha’mutte. The Dunmari survivors were saved by a large march of dwarves, many thousands all told, fleeing south for the safety of the new Yuvanti mountains. Many dwarven warriors, in honor of the Dunmari sacrifices during the Great War, helped defend the border for the next three years.
- In DR 1548, the survivors began to gather around the sheltered oasis near the Great Red Mesa, including Karawa and Bas Udda
- After the departure of the dwarven armies in DR 1549, the Mystai of Shakun began to work the magic that would eventually become the illusion wall, the barrier protecting the eastern frontier of Dunmar.
  - By  DR 1553, when the orc horde passed by, the whole Red Mesa area was protected
  - By DR 1556, the illusion wall ran 100 miles north and south from Karawa, allowing Bas Udda and nearby settlements to grow.

### The Nayan Dynasty

#### Unification and the Bitter Knife War

- In DR 1557, as the Nayan Dynasty was being established in the west, the Bitter Knife hobgoblin clan launched an attack across the Kharja River; the eastern Dunmari retreated to safety in Karawa, as they did not have enough warriors to mount a counter
- The Bitter Knife clan establishes a small but militarily effective slaver nation on the eastern border, surviving by plunder and forced labor
- In DR 1558, the Samraat Nayan Kundar in Nayanhar launched an attack against the Illorian Chire, ruling Darba and the islands of the coast, and won a decisive victory, reclaiming coastal Dunmar
- After the end of the monsoons in DR 1559, with a large army, Kundar rides east to cleanse the land of orcs amd hobgoblins
- While the orcs are easily defeated, wars against the Bitter Knife clan continue for 27 years
- During these wars, Kundar is killed in battle; his son Bhara becomes Samraat against the wishes of the eastern Dunmari
- Samraat Nayan Bhara is the first bad Dumari ruler that anyone can remember, perhaps ever; though skilled in battle, he is a poor administrator and a heavy handed ruler
- During his reign many in the east begin to doubt whether the gods still inhabit the Samraat rulers, and many in the west begin to wonder if the heavy price in blood and treasure to defend the sparsely settled lands east of the Hara is worthwhile
- By DR 1576, the Bitter Knife tribe is finally defeated, but Dunmar is more divided than ever before

#### The Second Hobgoblin War

- Over the next 25 years a series of uninspired Western rulers allow the country to further splinter, as the west turns increasingly to farming and Chardonian trade, while  the east clings to the traditional ways
- In DR 1589, the Dunmari renege on joint construction with the dwarves of new fortifications, roads, and a trading outpost on the eastern slopes of the Yuvanti, designed to revitalize dwarven trade east to Tokra, and including a grand monument to honor the dwarven sacrifices to protect the Dunmari a generation ago; the failure to honor these commitments sours relationships with the dwarves
- In DR 1599, a new Samraat comes to power, Samraat Nayan Parmasa; she becomes a cultural unifier and the idea of a Greater Dunmar, merging the east and the west, the new and the traditional, begins to gain popularity
- In DR 1602, the roads and fortifications in the Yuvanti is finished, and Askandi is founded on the river; relations with the dwarves slowly improve
- In DR 1603, Parmasa is killed in a hobgoblin ambush in the upper reaches of the Hara river; this launches a costly but effective war against the Blackened Claw tribe in the north
- By DR 1610, the Blackened Claw have been driven east over the mountains, and decisively defeated

#### The Uneasy Peace

- The past 150 years have been a time of unsettled peace: with the hobgoblin threat seemingly permanently defeated in the east and north, and a combination of Shakun’s magical defenses, the endless sands of the Garamjala desert, and the prolific jungles of Chaudi protecting the eastern border, no major invasions or existential threats to Dunmar emerge. But a series of weak rulers, unlucky breaks, and minor troubles keep squandering any momentum towards a Greater Dunmar.
- A series of Samraats become obsessed with minor but costly border wars against insignificant threats in the dense jungles to the south
- In DR 1714, the Summer of Red Storms, when gnolls stormed out of the Nashtkar, raiding and killing for six months before retreated, briefly called into question the power of the mystical defenses to the east
- The west turns more and more towards Chardon

#### The Current Day

- In DR 1736, a succession crisis occurred; the heir of the previous Samraat refused to take the throne, saying that he has been praying for weeks, and has felt no divine spark, no calling of the gods, no sign at all that he is destined to rule; Samraat Nayan Marathu, from a prominent eastern clan wais chosen instead
- Samraat Nayan Marathu’s reign began with great promise: he proposed a new capitol and a great monument between Darba and Tokra, brought the Samraat court east for the first time in a generation, brought balance to relations with Chardon, and formalized the defense of the southern border
- In DR 1740, he was murdered in mysterious circumstances, by a member of his household guard who spoke under magical compulsion that he could not remember committing the act
- Samraat Nayan Karnas, the current ruler of Dunmar, took over in DR 1740 to much contention, as many in the east supported his sister, Sura, who as a child had been dedicated to Shakun due to the magical power she showed at a young age

## Life in Dunmar

### Political Organization

#### The Samraat Court

- Historically, and largely still in the current day, Dunmar is an absolute divine monarchy, ruled by the Samraat, the divine representative of the gods on Taelgar; in the past most people assume the Samraat was chosen by the gods themselves, although many are less certain of this today, especially in the east
- The Samraat is the formal head of the temple hierarchy, through which much of the state administration occurs
- Technically, the Samraat also speaks for all the land in Dunmar; the land is owned by the gods, and cannot be alienated from them; in practice, local temples exert more direct control over land ownership

#### Clans and Temples

- The people of Dunmar are organized along two lines: clan and temple, and every Dunmari belongs to both a clan and a temple
- Clan is an expansive word in Dunmari, meaning something more like community or fellowship; while many clans are small family groups of 20-100 nomads, others may represent whole villages, or parts of cities
- Each clan chooses its own leader by any means acceptable to the group; many are hereditary, others are democratic, but there is a lot of variation
- Temples are the center of every settlement, and many also dot the landscape serving the nomadic populations; to be Dunmari is to be part of a temple, and all Dunmari have a temple they call home
- Temples serve both religious and administrative functions

#### Taxes and Obligations

- Each temple receives tithes from the people, in the form of gifts given at major festivals; temples also receive rents from land which they have obligations over
- A fraction of the tithe is kept for temple upkeep, while the rest is passed on as a tithe to the Samraat court; profits from rents are traditionally reinvested in the temple and the community, and sometimes gifted to local clan heads
- This tithe is due from all who live in Dunmar, whether Dunmari, or not, human or not; in more prosperous times, border temples collected tithes for safe passage from travelers; these days only at Songara is this tradition still maintained in some for
- Rents are primarily collected from land on which buildings have been constructed, or active farmland; pastures and commons are available to all without rent
- Mystai, the initiates of mystery cults, are freed from the obligation to tithe, as are mystery cults themselves; they have their own relationship with the gods. Mystery cults fund themselves largely through monopolies, often on the provision of specific magical goods, and rents from land they have obligations over
- Clan leaders owe specific obligations of service, traditionally military service, to the Samraat, based on the number of riders in their clan, so excluding the very young and very old; since the early days of the Nayan Dynasty, payment in lieu of service is accepted, and the revenue from these payments now funds much of the professional western army
- To meet these obligations, clan leaders have wide latitude to require service or impose tax; many towns impose market and sales taxes, or maintain monopolies on certain goods to raise revenue
- Traditionally, road and bridge tolls are frowned upon, and travelers are expected to support road infrastructure via tithes instead
- A census has not been taken in the east in many years, so while temples in the east still tithe the Samraat, the obligations of service have ceased to be collected from the Tokra and the lands east of it

#### The Mulaak

- The Samraat is the divine ruler of Dunmar, but the position is not hereditary
- All clan heads in Dunmar have the right to participate in the selection of the new Samraat, in an assembly called the Mulaak; this assembly has few formal rules but broad agreement is required to appoint a new ruler

### Religion

#### Key Tenets

- The gods created the Dunmari as their particular people, and thus the Dunmari religion and the Dunmari people are not separable; those who are chosen are Dunmari, even if they turn their backs on their homes. There is no clear sense of a Dunmari religious identity that is apart from just a Dunmari identity, and vice versa. Part of this is seeing the particular Dunmari pantheon as the special protectors of the Dunmari as a people; while of course other gods exist, and may even listen to individual Dunmari, those other gods do not have the interests of the Dunmari as a community in their hearts.
- The Five Siblings and the Dunmari people are in community with each other, and much of Dunmari religious practice emphasizes this community. The gods are the protectors, guardians, and guides of the people, and the people are the instruments of the gods.
- Anyone in covenant with the Five Siblings can be Dunmari, so immigration/conversion is not frowned upon, but it also isn’t encouraged. The communal side of Dunmari worship is a reflection of being part of the community, so even outsiders who live among the Dunmari and aren’t Dunmari themselves often participate in the communal rituals while worshipping their own gods in private (e.g., many dwarves in Askandi and Tokra follow this path, as do many Chardonians and others in Darba). But there is no sense that conversion glorifies the gods or increases their power or anything.
- The Five Siblings have a purpose for the Dunmari, and the Dunmari exist as a people to fulfill this purpose. The exact nature of this higher purpose isn’t always clear, and communication from the gods is rarely straightforward or direct. Mystery cults seeking esoteric knowledge to explore the god’s desires and intentions are a major part of Dunmari religion. But this knowledge is seen as secret, protected, because if the gods wanted all the people to know exactly their purpose they would just share it directly.

#### The Gods of Dunmar

##### Shurat

The Sky Mother, one of the two creator gods. Not directly worshipped; seen as distant and uninvolved in the world, having gifted it to her children and moved on. Associated with creation and birth, though. The Dunmari name for Ahra.

##### Sampa

The Earth Father, the other of the two creator gods. Not directly worshipped; like Shurat, seen as distant and uninvolved. Associated with death and endings. The Dunmari name for Malik.

##### Jeevali

The goddess of life, rebirth, home, and family. Often seen as the eldest and most important of the Five Siblings, a first among equals. The peacemaker between the sometimes fractious relationships among the four other siblings. In the west, seen as the patron god of Dunmar for her role in making the land what it was before the Great War. Generally looked to in times of trouble. Seen as the mother of Dunmar, as the protector of the people, as the one who chose the Dunmari for their great purpose. She especially watches over births, the home and family, and the harvest, and is the one who is seen as ensuring the monsoon rains come when they should.

##### Laka

The god of knowledge, history, time, memories, of the seasons, of the ancestors, and the passage of the year. Often seen as the keeper of traditions, as the one who determines the right moment for things, of the god of beginnings and endings. He is seen as the “cool head” among the Divine Siblings, the one counseling for caution and certainly and less risky courses of action. He is often seen as the second most important of the Divines, after Jeevali, and the one who is the keeper of Dunmar’s “soul”. He especially watches over funerals, and the memory of ancestors, and is the protector of the ruler of Dunmar and the government.

##### Aagir

The god of war, fire, and death. He is the protector of warriors, the fierce defender of the honor of Dunmari and of his siblings, the god of battle, and also the god of redemption, the one who helps the souls of those who were not deemed worthy in life redeem themselves. He is seen as the “rash” brother, the one urging action, the one prone to righteous fury and a demand to make things better. He watches over warriors most of all.

##### Sonkar

The goddess of beauty, artisans, truth, and justice. She is the dispassionate one, the one who weighs the deeds of souls coming to the realm of the Five Siblings. She is also the protector of artisans, smiths, and other craftspeople, and is seen as the one who watches over commerce and trade, punishing cheaters and ensuring fairness. She is the guardian of judgement in life as well, and watches over trials and the law.

##### Chidya

The goddess of horses, animals, but also nature, the land, weather, love, outcasts, and wanderers. She is the wild spirit, usually seen as the youngest and most carefree of the Five Siblings. She protects those who travel, and especially horses whom she loves most of all. She is seen as the bringer of unseasonable weather, of things out of time, of chance encounters on the road. She is the patron of outcasts and those in love. Although not the most central to Dunmari history, she is often seen as the most approachable of the Five Siblings and is the focus of much popular worship for this reason.

##### Shakun

A demigod, the patron of illusion, magic, prophecy, and dreams; often said to be the mystical protector of the Dunmari, looking to unseen dangers and magical threats. She is the Divine twin sister of Kotana, the second Samraat of the Aatmaji dynasty; Shakun and Kotana are said to be the children of Bhishma, the founder of Dunmar, and the god Laka. The mystai of Shakun have always been associated with omens and dreams, and interpreting the signs of dangers lurking for the Dunmari.

##### Adra

Another demigod, the patron of secrets, the quiet night, and darkness, a loveable trickster and patron of outcasts and misfits and lovers. He is the child of Chidya and a mortal, although who the mortal is no one can seemingly agree on.

##### Kotana and Bhishma

Although not technically gods, Kotana, child of Bhishma and Laka, as well as Bhishma herself, the founder of Dunmar, are honored in many parts of Dunmar, and have their own mystery cults dedicated to their worship and preserving their memories. Many believe that Bhishma and Kotana sit at the right hand of the gods now and can reach out to grant small miracles themselves, but the metaphysical truth of the matter is not know.

#### Temples and the Priesthood

- Temples are the center of the community, and rituals that involve joining the community, such as coming of age, or leaving the community, such as funerals, are celebrated at temples
- Temples are also the center of large festivals, of which there are several a year; these festivals are not just religious but also communal, with games, markets, and the coming together of widely dispersed people; the Festival of Rebirth is one example
- Temples are primarily designed around the worship of the Five Siblings, although shrines to individual gods do exist, typically as parts of buildings with other purposes
- Monuments, commemorating significant moments to the Dunmari, and often the life of notable Samraats, are common as well
- Each temple is run by a head priest, with a number of assistants and acolytes
- Outside of a temple, there is no official hierarchy; all priests are equal, and the Samraat is the formal head of the temple hierarchy

#### Worship

- Most worship in Dunmari society actually occurs outside of temples
- Individuals and families typically possess a set of icons or small statutes of the Five Siblings, which are the focus of private worship and prayer; many are passed down from generation to generation and sometimes are even seen as part of the family

#### Mystery Cults

- Exist outside the temple hierarchy
- For major gods (the Five Siblings), the mystery cults are a small fraction of worship; for the minor gods, they are often the main significant worship community around that god
- While the exact secrets they teach are shared only with initiates, many focus on esoteric knowledge, and some on the ecstatic sense of being in closer community with the Divine
- Rumors of magical secrets persist; for example, some say that initiates of the Mysteries of Chidya can learn to change themselves into animals

### Justice and the Law

- Law is religious law in Dunmar, and reflects the obligations of the community to the gods. Justice flows from the gods to the people, and all who are on the god’s land (that is, Dunmar) are subject to the law of the gods.
- The temple priests are the arbiters of the gods’ justice
- A bunch of traditions have grown up to ease the burden of requiring justice to be done via settled priests in fixed locations for a largely nomadic population, including some aspects of justice being delegated to clan heads, and acolytes of the peace that travel and provide traveling courts
- Punishments rarely involve humiliation or pain, but rather some form of penance. Often large fines due to the temple serving the judgement, other times service obligations to the temple, occasionally some kind of ritual humiliation. More serious punishments include excommunication and banishment, and death. Oftentimes the gods are directly involved in serious punishment; excommunication, for example, is often accompanied by permanent mark placed by a miracle of the divine on the perpetrator.
- Sheriffs and prosecutors don’t really exist; to the extent they do, these are usually the responsibility of the clan heads, who are charged with bringing wrongdoers to the temple for judgement. The victim(s) or their families are usually involved as well.
- Guilt or innocence is solely up to the priest; most medium and large temples have specific priest dedicated to Sonkar who has this as their responsibility. Appeals can be made up the temple hierarchy -- first to the head priest, then to the Samraat directly. Generally speaking interpretation of the will of the gods is key to justice; for many people this is one of their experiences of minor miracles, as the gods do take an active role when they can. Magic, such as powers like the Zone of Truth spell, is commonly used in determining justice as well.
- The Samraat has the power to overrule any decision of justice, and is formally, although not always practically, above the law, as the representative of the gods on Taelgar and the formal head of the temple hierarchy.
- Disputes between clans are usually resolved by a tribunal of priests, typically 3 or 5, with one from each of the clan territories, and the rest supposed to be impartial. Claims of justice against priests are resolved in a similar way, although often brought directly to the Samraat if possible. The outcomes of these disputes are much more likely to be appealed directly to the Samraat than typical disputes. Fines imposed against temples or clans from these disputes are due directly to the Samraat, rather than the local temple.

### Magic

- Magic in Dunmar is considered the provenance of the gods, like all things. The Dunmari make little distinction between arcane, divine, and primal magic, and arcane magic is considered part of the divine as well.
- While the details are not revealed to outsiders, most of the mystery cults have associated magical traditions as well. For example, rumors persist that some initiates of the Chidyan mysteries can turn into animals.
- Dunmari who display unusual magical talents usually can only find training in the mystery cults; the public practice of magic outside of mystery cults is looked at as slightly suspicious, except for miracles of the gods delivered via priests.
  - Foreigners, including non-humans, are an exception, and it is not a hard and fast rule, more a cultural trend in that Dunmari who show magical talent early in life are strongly encouraged to join the appropriate mystery cult
  - However, magic that seems to go against the gods is usually assumed to come from fiends or curses, and is considered quite evil

### Trade and the Economy

- Dunmar is largely agrarian, with an economy dominated by animal production -- horses, most notably, but also leather products, wool, hides, and fur.
- In the west, fertile land means that crop agriculture is becoming more important for the economy
- Historically, the economy was supported in part by trade, both as caravans/guards/merchants but also running a high speed message service that could move information very quickly across Dunmar on fast horses. But now, this is a minor part of the economy.
  - What is left of this trade economy mostly involves the Dwarf/Chardon trade, which passes through western Dunmar to the coast, or through central Dunmar to Songara.
  - The message system is largely extinct now, but fast messages still run from Askandi - Tokra - Songara, and Nayahar - Darba - Tokra; the latter is more efficient as it still has full official support and stocked waystations
  - Trade that comes from the east usually runs via halfling caravans out of Sembara, who trade for Dunmari goods to bring on to Chardon
  - Songara is a major horse trading center
- Timber is becoming an important product of the far south, south of the Shrev, and the trade in tropical hardwoods north to Chardon is a major source of wealth in the current dynasty
- A lot of food, especially water-intensive crops, is imported from Chardon, although less so as agricultural develops in the west, and the traditional Dunmari diet is almost all forage and animal products, with a lot of yogurt and cheese and such, plus meat’

#### Coins

Coins are not commonly used in the current day in Eastern Dunmar, as much trade is conducted in barter and in kind. The king in Nayahar mints coins, however, and these are in occasional use in the east. Gold coins are known as steeds (2 gp); silver coins as rupas (4 sp), and copper coins as taams (5 cp). Half-taams and quarter-taams also exist.

Older coins from pre-Blood Years uses the same names, but tended to be larger and more pure, and so can trade for more than their 'face value'

Dwarven coins are in common usage in Dunmar as well, especially iron trading coins, known as nibs, that are backed by a gold standard but not otherwise containing any intrinsic worth. Nibs in 1 cp, 5 cp, 1 sp, 5 sp, 1 gp, 10 gp, and 50 gp are known. Dwarven trade bars, stamped with a known weight of silver or gold, are commonly used for large transactions in Dunmar as well.
