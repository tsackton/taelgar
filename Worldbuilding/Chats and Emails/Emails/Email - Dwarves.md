Here is the latest Taelgar dwarf update. Guessing you probably won't ever read this but who knows. If you do have time I would be very interested in feedback.

https://www.gmbinder.com/share/-LtFg6GJLYThfAVQlu80

The design motivation here is that, at least as played by Isaac, Marco, and Nathaniel there really isn't anything very 'dwarven racial magic' about dwarves right now. Mechanically, they function like being able to cast a cantrip, and that is how it seems in play (they all do sometimes use their Dwarven abilities, so it isn't a matter of forgetting to do things). While a better role-player more invested in reskining cantrips as Dwarven rune magic could probably make it work, I wanted to try to make this easier and give more flavor mechanically.

The main approach I took is to invest more physicality into the actual runes. At the moment this is manifesting as i) focusing on self/touch effects, sometimes with a radius making them more like aura effects (although there are some exceptions), and b) having runes give both passive and active effects, with the active effects varying in power and often including some options that are only unlocked at higher levels. There is probably e ven further I could go with this, but for a first pass I didn't want to be too finicky (although this is still probably a bit overdesigned).

As far as balance, the goal is for all the major runes to be relatively balanced against each other, and for the castes to be roughly balanced against each other. So for major runes aiming for something on par with tool+skill+feat+cantrip (from traveler) or +1 ASI plus cantrip (from warrior), assuming the other parts balance out with the non-rune parts of the castes that get major runes (if that made sense). Or put another way, to keep dwarves balanced with the PHB dwarves, the major runes should be roughly as good as resistance + advantage to poison, which Taelgar dwarves don't get in their base package (this makes warrior dwarves not quite as good as mountain dwarves because a cantrip is probably not equal to poison resistance + advantage).

The other issue right now is that currently dwarves end up with a lot of things like advantage or expertise on a very situational roll, both from runes and from castes. Isaac likes this but to me it feels a little overdesigned -- it does add some flavor if you remember you have those abilities in the rare times it comes up. It is not bad as far as it goes but flies against the simplicity of the 5E design philosophy a bit (probably my time playing GURPS is showing a bit here). So I'm going back and forth on what to do here to keep dwarves flavorful but not too complex (especially with the complexity of the runes on top of everything). Probably will dump all the minor bits from the castes and just leave them as passive effects in the runes....

Anyway, there will be another version I'm sure but if you have comments would be quite interested (on lore, too).

## Mike Sackton — November 10, 2019 at 10:42 PM

A few quick thoughts.

- I like the flavor text
- I like the general mechanic
- agree that the stacking sets of advantage and expertise in rare situations gets a little complex and over designed

In general I’d try to push the “minor” and passive stuff a little bit away from advantage expertise toward minor non combat abilities.

For example instead of Stonecunning giving advantage on history checks, how about the ability to find weak points and strong points in stonework or masonry, allowing double damage when attacking masonry or stonework as well as some benefit when shoring up a wall.

Also I think the Mass healing is pretty powerful on the rune of heal although I guess with the 10 min casting time it’s relatively balanced.

Still thinking about it.

Thanks for feedback. Here is updated version: https://www.gmbinder.com/share/-LtFg6GJLYThfAVQlu80/-LtSqF5jgh3j1XpNic0k

Probably will give it one more round of changes / edits before playtesting.

Changes from version 1:

- toned down expertise / advantage mechanic, especially around skills. Kept a few expertise mechanics that seemed notable but in general tried to come up with related minor effects for passive options of runes.
- tweaked a few major runes for potential power issues, but may have overcorrected in some cases

Still could use a little refinement I'm sure, especially around details re: physicality of runes (can you activate a rune and then pass it around to different people? or even just one person? can you activate at a distance?). Could potentially also use one or two more major runes, and a bit of tweaking of minor rune selection, and refinement of minor rune descriptions (right now mostly just cantrips). Overall though feeling fairly satisfied; won't really get to see it in play until next time I play with Isaac's friend's campaign, but there are two dwarves in that group so should get some action.

Still interested in whatever additional feedback or ideas you may have.
