# Karawa

## Tim Sackton — May 28, 2020 at 7:03 PM

### People

Elder Kisa: the speaker of the village council and the unofficial 'leader' of the village of Karawa, she is an older woman, bent back and graying hair, weathered skin. She speaks slowly and deliberately, and her first thought is always the safety of the village. She was suspicious of you when you first arrived, but has warmed to you lately.

Candrosa, Speaker of Shakun: The public face of the otherwise secretive Shakun mystery cult. He is a tall, handsome man, with dark skin and short, curly black hair. He always wears an ochre robe, trimmed in silver, and carries a staff.

Suma, Priestess of Jeevali: Although the Mystery of Shakun is the more powerful religious institution, Karawa has a small temple to the five siblings as well. Suma, the priestess of Jeevali, serves the people of Karawa. She is a kind, generous person, welcoming of all, and genuinely interested in people's stories and lives. She has collected numerous tales over the years and is a good source of companionship. She is a middle aged woman with dark, curly hair and soft, friendly features.

Ikram: The owner of the caravanserai in town, a friendly, sociable fellow, prone to gossip and particularly fond of travelers. He is a chatty and always ready with a drink, these days typically either a sweet, chilled honey wine or a spiced beer (less traditional), although he also serves sharba, a popular Dunmari vodka-style drink fermented from milk. Food tends towards grilled meat (goat or sheep), kebabs, and stews, with some quick breads and the occasional noodle. He is proud of the reputation of his establishment and keen to see guests happy. He often has a line on merchants and goods for sale as well, as the caravanserai serves as the main marketplace in town.

Jasu: An older man, bulky and solidly built, with gray eyes, short black hair, and a beard. He is the town's blacksmith, and you often see him at the caravanserai drinking in the courtyard in the coolness of evening.

Badya: A younger woman, a server and general helper-out at the tea house Delwath lives above. She was a survivor of the first hyena attack on the town, and loves to tell the story for attention.

### Locations

The Temple of Shakun: a large, imposing structure carved out of the side of the red standstone mesa on the southern part of town. The temple itself stretches at least partially into the cliff itself, but you have never been in that part of the structure. Non-initiates are only allowed access to a few outbuildings, and to the chapel to Shakun facing the oasis. The chapel is beautiful - polished sandstone floors, and tile mosaics abound - but is a place primarily for prayer and does not seem to hold any secrets. The Mystai of Shakun do, however, sell and barter dusts and powders that are known to possess magical properties.

The Oasis: The central feature of the town is a small oasis. Over the years a marble pool and short wall has been built around it, allowing water to be drawn off for various uses. The reliability and purity of the water here has established Karawa as a waypoint and gathering place for the Dunmari. Palm trees provide shade and shelter and make the oasis square a place where people linger.

The market square: On the northwestern edge of town, there is a large market square, which stands empty most of the year, filling to capacity only during the large markets after the Festival of Rebirth, at least this is what you've been told. Beyond it, cleared scrublands and pastures serve as a temporary home for hundreds of herders who return for the Festival of Rebirth, or so you've heard.

The Temple of the Five Siblings: Although the Temple of Shakun is the larger draw in Karawa, a small temple to the Five Siblings stands on the eastern side of town, facing a small public square. The temple itself is plain, but well-maintained.

The Council of Elders: Standing across a small public square from the Temple of the Five Siblings is a circular, dome shaped building that is the main public building in Karawa. This space serves as the council chambers where the council of elders meets to deliberate, and the square in front is a place for public debate. Judgements are issued here by the council.

The caravanserai: On the northern outskirts of town stands a caravanserai, a large square building with a open courtyard, rooms for travelers, and space for merchants and animals. This is a center of commence in the village when the market is deserted, and many things can be acquired from Ikram, the proprietor.

The village: The village itself is mostly small houses of adobe and mud, facing on narrow streets, with shade provided by colorful awnings. A few artisans and scattered shops line the streets facing the caravanserai, including a tea house which you live above, and a blacksmith.

The fields: To the east of town, some small scale agriculture provides a bit of extra food, beyond what the herds can provide and what can be traded with pastoralists passing through. Crops that require little water and lots of heat do best, although the oasis feeds irrigation channels here that allows crops to thrive. Dates and honey are a particular delicacy, being used to make wine and desserts.

### The Mystery Cult of Shakun

The Mystery Cult of Shakun, at least as far as its public facing activities are concerned, seems from what you've learned and observed in the past few months to function like a monastery. Acolytes and laity pray, do menial tasks, and maintain the temple life. Several outbuildings function as dormitories, a cafeteria, and a chapel that is open to all.

Shakun herself seems to have a strong presence in the village. Most people you talk to attest to personal experience with Shakun, and you have noted with your fey sight that magic seems to pool around the oasis - something is keeping it pure and clear and reliable, and the people tell you it is the protection of the goddess Shakun.

Some people say that is you spend the night in meditation on the edge of the oasis, Shakun herself will come to you in a vision and open your eyes to one true thing about your future. While people refer to her as the Goddess of Magic, she seems to be connected to dreams and prophecies as well.

Although most people are content with the protections the goddess provides, there is obviously more to the Shakunian Mystery, and no non-initiate is allowed into the inner temple. Few villagers are interested in questioning too much, for fear that Shakun will withdraw her protection. Still, many people will gossip and speculate, and many rumors abound about the place: the words of the Speaker of Shakun are always true; the mystai of Shakun know the secret language of things and can change matter with a word; the temple itself is imbued with magic and all who step inside are changed. There is even a rumor that the Shakun mystai grow rock and sand and dust in gardens, and harvest them like plants to produce magical powders. While this sounds farfetched, the Shakun mystai do often paint there faces with ochre patterns, and sometimes will sell or share magical healing powders that, when sprinkled on a wound, are absorbed into the skin, healing the person.

Of course.

Lots of people try it -- it is almost a rite of passage for village youth -- but most of them drift off to sleep, or lose focus, or get distracted by bugs or the cold of the night air, or the sounds of scorpions running in the rocks, and see nothing.

As an elf, however, Delwath easily falls into a trance, and lets his consciousness drift deeper into meditation. As the night is almost over, in the predawn light, a presence grows in his minds eye, more clearly visible to his fey sight than his normal eyes. The village fades, and in front of Delwath stands a towering bronze door, carved with a multitude of scenes: celebrations, daily life, prayer, battle, death -- although he is not close enough to see the details clearly. A voice begins to grow, whispering in a distorted voice (think Galadriel in the mirror of Galadriel scene), "Your truth is you must choose to step forward into ----" and the first voice is cut off, as a familiar voice, the one Delwath has heard before, whispering to him, since he returned, cries out "Who are you? Begone!" as Delwath snaps to alertness, adrenaline rushing and his trance broken, the village quiet in the predawn light.
