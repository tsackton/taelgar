## Mike Sackton — February 4, 2021 at 5:43 PM

I was messing around with cities / towns / population density and I made a spreadsheet: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1BdTQmPSFR7LJluyIwFBvLD_036536qHJBSLuhAGjYak/edit#gid=885250069

If you have an easy way of getting accurate data on square miles for various regions, it would make it more accurate, I mostly just roughly eyeballed.

The calculators tab will use the Medieval Demographics Made Easy random numbers system to generate cities and towns, if you enter a starting population. The "size factor" increases the size of the first city, and the town # factor increases the number of towns.

The main tab should be mostly self-explanatory. The "Food per sq mi" is used to calc the cultivated and uncultivated land. Making it higher means less cultivated land per same density. Making it lower means more per density.

Historical medieval number is 180.

## Tim Sackton — February 4, 2021 at 6:14 PM

It's super easy in Photoshop. I did a few, underlined them.

For the Sembara map, there should be ~2.82 square miles per pixel, so if you can measure the area in pixels of a region you can easily convert to square miles.

This is simple to do in Photoshop if you lasso the area you want to measure and then use the histogram view which will show the number of pixels in the selected region. But there are probably other ways to do that as well. It is a bit of a pain to trace the borders exactly so I rounded to the nearest hundred, and even that may be off a little but not enough to matter for a fantasy roleplaying game.
