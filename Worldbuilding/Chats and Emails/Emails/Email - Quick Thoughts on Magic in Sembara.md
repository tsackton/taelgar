# quick thoughts on magic in Sembara

## Mike Sackton — February 10, 2021 at 5:54 PM

Basic non-magical numbers:

* 15 million population
* About 15 powerful nobles (dukes, earls, etc)
* About 150 or 200 barons (many of them in small clusters in the duchies)
* 94% of population is rural, so between 30,000 - 40,000 villages/manors
* About 10,000 lords of the manor (some control multiple estates; in other cases a manor is controlled by a castellan for a noble, hence the smaller number)
* About 300 towns
* About 20 cities

If magic users are about one in a thousand:

* Magic users cluster a bit, so say 25% or urban/army for low level, 75% for high level
* 40% of these are divine (clerics), 25% wizards, 25% druids, and the remaining 10% other
* 80% are level 2 or less; 19% are 3rd-6th, 1% 7th or higher

You'd have something like:

Population: 15,000,000

| Category | Share | All | Cleric | Wizard | Druid | Other |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Magic | 0.10% | 15000 | 6000 | 3750 | 3750 | 1500 |
| Rural | 75.00% | 11250 | 4500 | 2813 | 2813 | 1125 |
| Urban | 15.00% | 2250 | 900 | 563 | 563 | 225 |
| Army | 10.00% | 1500 | 600 | 375 | 375 | 150 |
| 1st level spells | 80.00% | 12000 | 4800 | 3000 | 3000 | 1200 |
| 2nd level spells | 12.00% | 1800 | 720 | 450 | 450 | 180 |
| 3rd level spells | 7.00% | 1050 | 420 | 263 | 263 | 105 |
| 4th level spells | 0.75% | 113 | 45 | 28 | 28 | 11 |
| 5th or higher | 0.25% | 38 | 15 | 9 | 9 | 4 |

That gives you enough spell casters that you'd likely have a few associated with most nobles (i.e. barons and above), plus a few clusters in schools, army, whatever (a cluster of 50 or 100 spellcasters recruited into an army would be reasonable).

You'd have 4,500 rural priests which means say 15% of villages are likely to have a spellcaster as a priest (and somewhere between 1 in 3 and 1 in 4 villages would have a spellcaster of some sort).

The 10,000 manorial lords would not regularly have access to magic, but it wouldn't exactly be unusual. You might imagine half the rural wizards are in some kind of employ with lords (so again, about 1 in 10).

Every town and city would have a couple of spellcasters for sure, unless there was a really good reason why not.

The 300 or so elders, probably about 2/5ths would be high level spellcasters and maybe another 2/5ths or a bit more mid-level spellcasters, and the remaining 1/5th mostly lower level spellcasters.

Does that seem about right?

Mike

I think more clerics and fewer wizards. I'd say about 50% clerics, 20% bards, 10% druids, 10% wizards, and 10% others (mostly fey warlocks in Tyrwingha) in Sembara.

Most of the wizards would be in cities and towns, often associated with nobles. Rural wizards would be rare, but bards less so as far as arcane casters go.

Otherwise, though, yes, this seems right.

I think in Tollen, you'd have more like 30-40% wizards, with the balance coming from clerics and bards, while in Tyrwingha the "others" may be a bit higher, and in Cymea things would switch again. But I do think it is nice to have wizards specifically be rare in Sembara, and have magic feel a bit different, much less a learned thing, more spontaneous/prepared. Most wizards in Sembara would probably have gone elsewhere -- Tollen or Cymea -- for training.

## Tim Sackton — February 10, 2021 at 8:56 PM

Yeah, this feels about right to me. A random peasant would almost for sure know, or know someone who knows, a cleric who can perform minor miracles, but almost certainly nothing above 1st level.

Most towns of substantial size would have a wizard, and cities could have many, but a peasant living in a small village in the central heartlands would almost certainly not know a wizard, or even know someone who knows one. But a bard or druid may have passed through a few years ago, and most peasants would have seen arcane magic at some point in their lives, but not regularly.

In towns and especially cities, magic would be uncommon, but not unusual. This isn't Chardon -- there aren't magic lights in the streets, for example, but in larger cities spells like identify could be paid for as a service without much trouble, and even in small villages it wouldn't be uncommon to find a priest who could at least cure wounds (lesser restoration, and especially raise dead, would be a different matter entirely).
