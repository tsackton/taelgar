## Tim Sackton — January 1, 2023 at 10:17 PM

I like both your other backgrounds.

In Izgil's background, I guess I still think it is a little weird to have
the second thuhr and especially the shadowy figure - the Bahrazel of course
work in mysterious ways and often do not reveal their purpose, but don't
usually hide their identity, and the second thuhr feels kind of disruptive
to me (but if the goal is to feel disruptive then I guess it works). I like
the idea that the Bahrazel want someone to learn about the moon and I like
the vague implication that this is payment of the debt of keeping his
family safe in the Great War. I think the idea that you can inherit debts
to the gods from your family is extremely dwarven and works well.

I guess though if you want to keep the thuhr changing, I would make it
explicit, have the shadowy figure be Karthel (runecrafter) and have Izgil
basically be traded from Maganna to Karthel. The purpose is mysterious but
the fact that this is the gods doing is clear.

In Viepuck's background, I think the Embry details are fine and make sense,
and the weird Far Realm stuff is great.

## Tim Sackton — January 1, 2023 at 10:58 PM

On the "getting a thuhr for the first time at 145", I think that probably
doesn't work as well if the goal is for the player to have something in his
past that shakes up his character arc.

A binthuhr dwarf is, in many circles, not considered a dwarf at all. There
are of course some of these people around, usually living among humans or
other races, but I don't think it is the trajectory your player probably
wants - rather than a relatively straightforward if slightly ill-fitting
dwarven life upended by this moon-related event, you have a very unusual
and non-dwarven background that reorients in a dwarven fashion late in
life.

It could be a fun character hook, but I think not really in keeping with
the background from your player.
