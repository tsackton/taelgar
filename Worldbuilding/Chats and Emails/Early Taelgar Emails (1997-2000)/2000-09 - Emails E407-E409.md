## E407 — “Re: Taelgar -- cosmology”

On Tue, 26 Sep 2000, Tim Sackton wrote:

> Basic overview:  Taelgar is at the center of a multiverse consisting of:
> the realm of Taelgar itself, with associated lunar, solar, planetary, and
> stellar spheres (question: to what extent is astrology an important
> force?);

I like astrology -- but I would prefer it on the fringes rather than in
the center. Here's a few random ideas/questions: What is Taelgar, exactly?
A round planet, I presume, but perhaps not. If so, how big is it?
Earth-sized? (I'd prefer so, but I could see reasons for making it
otherwise). Perhaps rather than an actual planet, it should be a bent
flat-world, like Middle-Earth after the Second Age. I think that all of
the celestial objects should revolve around Taelgar, but from your use of
the term spheres, I assume you agree. How about 2 stellar spheres, the
Zodiac (i.e. the near stars) and the heavenly sphere (the far stars, i.e.
those based on beings -- defeated dragons, relics from the previous
incarnation of the world, heroes like Beryl, etc). As far as the planets,
I like the idea of relating them to the races, in size, color and distance
from the sun. The solar sphere I have no paticularly creative ideas about,
and perhaps it should be simple and unexciting, just a big bright light
revolving around Taelgar. Although what exactly the sun is should be
defined. Same with the lunar object(s?). Certainly one moon, what is it?
Maybe two, perhaps a dark moon or secret moon of some sort? I'm not sure I
like a secret moon just because it seems like throwing in every random
cool celestial idea, and I think selectivity is good. Also, I'd say that
neither the sun nor the moon should be physical objects. If they aren't
magical or supernatural in some way, they should just be energy. Although
perhaps the moon has to be physical so that features are percievable on
it. But is that necessary? Do we need a moon that isn't just a featureless
globe of light?

>the realm of the Deity, which fades from “pure” good at the very
> top to “pure” evil at the very bottom (symbolism: the gray areas in the
> center have by far the most “area”) (question: is this better or worse than
> the previous two sharply divided realms, which is still what virtually all
> scholars would think in cultures that perceive Araht and Melchior as
> independent entities);

I prefer a spectrum to divided realms. Although perhaps there should be
some metaphysical representation of the two endpoints: like two lights at
each end giving off white and black light to form a continuum, although
not in those terms obviously.

>the realm of magic; the realm of consciousness,
> which includes the plane of consciousness, the dreamworld, and the astral
> plane; and the realm of nothingness (the “neutral column”).  The realm of
> the Deity is essentially inaccessible to living mortals of Taelgar.  The
> realm of magic makes occasional contact points with the realm of Taelgar,
> and is in very special cases directly accessible.  The realm of
> consciousness makes direct contact with individuals in Taelgar, and can be
> accessed with special and rare methods.  The realm of nothingness can be
> sought from either the realm of magic or the realm of consciousness, and it
> intersects the realm of Taelgar at or near the Temple (question: yes/no?).

I basically like that. I'm not sure about the Temple thing. It should be
some sort of focus, but it might be better explained as the center of
everything -- all the planes could intersect there. Or maybe the current
pseudo-explanation as the place of creation is sufficient.

> Questions: elemental realms; lunar, solar, planetary realms and there
> correspondences to the races; fixed stars and constellations and the myths
> behind them (Beryl, for example); movement of celestial bodies;
> relationship of dragons to cosmology

I think Taelgar can forgo elemental realms. I'm not sure its a good idea
to try to cram ever single neat idea into one world. Elemental beings
could still exist, they'd just live in the deep earth, or volcanoes, or
the deep ocean, rather than some special realm. I addressed the lunar,etc
realms above. The celestial sphere shouldn't move, I don't think. The
planets rotate around Taelgar, as does the sun and moon.

Dragons. Hmmm. To relate them, I think some idea of how this was all
created would be useful. Dragons should physically exist in (probably) the
realm of the Diety, to the extent that they have a physical existence. The
creation story should be worked out though, especially ideas about, if
Melchiaor and Araht are basically one, then the original ideas about
good/evil races don't really work. Also, explaining why hobgoblins (new
name?) don't have a firstborn, and how the kenku were created if Vorshatal
doesn't exist, and if he does exist, who is he, exactly? I was never
satisfied with the aspects thing. One solution (which slightly changes the
feel of Taelgar) is to reduce the numbers of non-firstborn, non-human
races and make them essentially rare. The hobgoblin kingdoms could be
reworked as human kingdoms infested and ruled by hobgoblins. Then, you
increase the power of dragons slightly, and make them be the source of
everything that wasn't created by the Deity. Kenku could have originally
been created by a dragon, but when Aerin went back in time it created a
paradox or something that allowed the kenku to rise themselves to "real"
race status.

A related question: where do pocket universes fit into the cosmology? It
seems like they should exist, but they need a place to be. Bubbles in the
Taelgarian multiverse? Perhaps any of the realms can have folds and
bubbles, so that one could create a pocket universe out of the bubble of
Taelgar (the Talydras dimension) or out of the realm of the Deity, or the
realm of magic (not sure what these would be, exactly, but it seems like a
cool idea -- perhaps all the dragons have their own little universes where
the actually live, created out of pockets in the realm of the Deity).
Folds would be what one utilizes to travel the way Rai did, and folds in
the other realms could be utilized in similarly powerful ways.

One of the things I'd like to do is try to move Taelgar away from a good /
evil orientation. Some races should certainly seem more essentially good
(like the halflings) than others (like the hobgoblins), but humans should
be just as varied as humans today, and the various kingdoms should be less
essentially benevolent.

One of the main things I'd like to do is work out a realistic political
system for the three major kingdoms, and likely work in a host of minor
new kingdoms. This raises the question of when should "now" be in all of
this? It seems like either just before or just after the campaign are the
right times to set it. The advantage of just after is that is when a lot
of the stuff we've already made up is, the disadvantage is that I don't
necessarily want to be working on a post-major disaster world. I'd rather
see Dunmar and Sembara healthy countries rather than devastated hulks.
Also, I'd like to mess around with things like crops and climate,
economies and roads and how goods move around and where they move from,
and peasant life and demographics, and what sort of population trends are
occuring or have occured, and, on top of all that, how does magic change
everything?

The biggest problem with the map of Taelgar is the deceptiveness of the
mountains. Switzerland is essentially in the middle of the Alps. Much of
Spain is highlands. The map of Taelgar makes it seem like the mountains
are uninhabitable. Same goes for forests and other geographic features.
To what degree are we willing to fudge geography to fix up problems with
the map? The map is based on the Tolkien paradigm of sparse human
inhabitation, and a few kingdoms whose political relations are mostly
internal or with non-human or poorly defined "evil" countries of humans.
To what degree do we want to make up a world within that paradigm, and to
what degree do we want the world to move to a more generally realistic
paradigm?

Okay, done procrastinating :-)

Taelgar is a round, stationary, earth-sized (approx.) world at the center of the universe.  There is no "atmosphere" in the modern sense, however, nor is the world suspended in the middle of space or anything like that.  The universe is divided into the sublunar and the superlunar (extralunar?) realms.  Taelgar occupies the sublunar realm, where normal physics operates (that is, Newtonian physics -- modern physics do not operate on Taelgar).  In the sublunar realm, things go from heaviest to lightest roughly, moving from earth that conglomerates in the center to form the world, up to the upper atmosphere (should this be Aristotelan, with earth, water, air, and fire occupying positions in the sublunar realms, or not? I think the planetary spheres should literally be spheres that rotate in which the planet is embedded.  Or something like that, to get away from the idea of empty space in which things orbit. the map, of the political realities of Taelgar, in 208 F.A.:

First, this is assuming that the ocean to the south of Dunmar is stormy
and hard to cross, so the lands on the southern parts of the map are not
easily accessible. We start with Chardon. It is a land of several major
thriving, rival cities, each based on mercantile empires, streching up and
down the coast, out to Shantu, and daringly to the South. Neither Shantu

sparously populated and somewhat poor kingdom, that peters out into a land
of barbarians and native tribes. To the east, something, I'm not sure
what.

but currently more territorial rulers who all owe allegiance to a Great

soverignity. Some of these kings are heriditary, some elective. Think
about the early United States, for example. To the south of Dunmar is

Chardon should shrink/vanish, although it should certainly be
passable). East of Dunmar is a lot of abandoned, perhaps magically ruined
land, including Arabor/Irebor and Drankor. Not to many people travel that
way or go that way. Economically and politically, Dunmar is fairly
isolationist. Lots of grazing, lots of independent communities, no
serfdom, that sort of thing. Think a combination of the medieval Spanish
mesta with a place like pre-Alfred Anglo-Saxon England (although I realize
you likely no nothing about that period :), all dumped in a terrian
similar to say, Ukraine, and with hints of Mongol Horde/Rohan added in for
good measure.
