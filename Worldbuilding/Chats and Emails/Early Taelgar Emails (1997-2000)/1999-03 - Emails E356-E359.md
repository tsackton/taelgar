its own sort of unique magic (excepting dragons of course). Gnomes would
probably use a heavily, heavily modified sort of runic magic. Giants and
liontaurs might have some sort of innate magic in the way faeries of

don't need any sort of ritual to do whatever it is they do magically. The

source is from the caster. So the firstborn races would need little in the

non-ritualistic form, and a more powerful, long acting ritualistic form.

Nope.  You are wrong here.  Runic magic has _always_ been a magic of creation, primarily.  This doesn't mean that a dwarven rune caster, when alone and naked is _totally_ helpless, but he certainly won't be as powerful.  The dwarves have a unique power of creation.  They are the only race that can change the very fundamental nature of something and make it magical.  Elves can come the closest, but only by tying of a weave around an item -- it still could conceivably be undone without destroying the item.  Dwarven magical items cannot be made unmagical without also destroying the item completely.  So the dwarves are incredibly powerful, it's just that their power is focused on a different area.  Plus, I think creation of a magical item should inherently be a difficult, time consuming, ritualistic process.

For the dwarves, there are two main sources of power; the first-born and the plane of magic.  The firstborn primarily serve to link the rune-caster to the plane of magic, not really to provide any inherent power themselves.  With the dwarven caste system, they already to that to an extent for the race as a whole.  So, here's a quick example of a dwarven rune-item creation process, and a spontaneous casting.

Say a dwarven rune-mage wants to create a shield that gives extra protection against missle weapons.  They would find some relevant materials, craft the shield incorporating some of the revelant materials and with the proper rituals to connect the proceedings to the plane of magic.  Once the thing is almost done, the runes would be engraved in the appropriate manner, and then the shield and runes would be transformed and merged by drawing on the power of the firstborn, again with an appropriate ritual.  Once that is done, the shield would become irreversibly magical.

For a spontaneous casting without any props, the rune-mage would have to sketch the runes in the air with his finger, probably chanting while doing it, and then invoke the firstborn to call forth the power of the runes.

What do you think of something along these basic lines.  This is one spot where I'm probably going to claim executive priviledge, but I still want to know what you think.
