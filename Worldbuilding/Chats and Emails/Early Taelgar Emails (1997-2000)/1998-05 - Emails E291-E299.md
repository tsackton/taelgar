now, the country to the east of it will be swallowed up (I think) by
chardon inside 25 years.  Maybe much sooner.  Chardon is changing from
city-state into state.

Disagree with the statement about Chardon. Sure, Chardon is turning into a
state, but why try to swallow up another country? I *really* dislike too
much realism. The three big countries acting to some degree as good guys I
really like. Sembia and Chardon are too far away for either to dominate
the other, I think. I really think that the most important currencies
should be accepted universally, because that makes bookkeeping about
wealth vaguely possible. Maybe the gov'ts don't accept foriegn cion, but
merchants do. I never really thought of the countries east of Sembia as
countries at all, rather as loose collections of city states. Would they
have the resources to mint a significant amount of coin? You do need gold
to mint, and silver to mint, before you can make coins. The country east
of Chardon I think is in no way about to be assimilated. Since Sembia
isn't interested in expanding, a country there could only benefit Chardon,
as it would make trade with sembia *much* safer. Civiliaztion is safer to
travel through than wilderness. And why would Chardon want to expand that
much? They don't necessarily have the resouces, and I'm sure they have no
desire for a war. They probably extert a strong influence on that country
(new volkraad, or did we change the name?) but I'm not going to make it
assimilated by Chardon in my campaign without some really good arguments
why it should be. Sometimes I think that you people drift too far from the
high fantasy of Taelgar to the nitty-gritty realism of the real world. Why
can't Chardon, Sembia, and Rohan be on friendly terms, run by basically
good people, patriots but not extreme nationalists? Why shouldn't they
essentially cooperate and like each other, rather than fight, or dislike,
or fear each other? I certainly prefer the Rohan-Gondor relationship from
LotR than any other as a model for how these three countries interact.
Also, making currencies not accepted universally (at least the coomon
ones-- I agree that the unnamed countries to the east and west of Chardon
might not have the same sort of universally acceptance, assuming they
manage to mint currency at all).

Sorry for the ramble, but now I'm curious about something: What are your
visions for the flavor and feel and mood of Taelgar? I like the basically
LotR/Eye of the World/"high fantasy" model, with considerably less black
in white in terms of villians and such. But there should definetly be some
*good* and some *evil* in the world, and a fair amount in between.

Mike
