A few thoughts on currency: I think that there should be 2 primary methonds of paying for things (well, plus barter, but that is probably somewhat less common, particularly in big cities, and it doesn't always work).  Large transactions are usually done in bars of gold or precious gems.  Smaller transactions are usually done in coin, some of which are worth no more than there metal content, others of which are worth more than there metal content.  Dwarven currency is very stable, although often rare, and it is the only form of currency that dwarves will accept or use (thoughts?  is that silly?).  It is made of pure metal, but because it is backed by what is definately the richest race per capita (humans probably have more accumulated wealth, but only because there's far more of them), it is worth somewhat more than the metal content alone.  Chardonian currency is also very stable, and is the primary currency used west of the mountains and in Rohan.  It's worth more than the pure metal content of the coins, I'd guess.  Sembian currency used to be the most commonly used, but it has lost that honor to Chardonian currency.  Currently, Sembia mints coins worth exactly what they would be worth in pure metal.  Rohan also mints currency, but it is currently nearly worthless.  Some of the nations to the east of Sembia also mint currency, but there currency is 1) not accepted in Sembia (usually) for political reasons, and 2) not backed by a stable or powerful government, and so not often used.  People will accept it (outside of Sembia), but other coins are preferred.

Sorry for the incoherence.  I've been been studying for so long that I'm getting the feeling nothing I say makes sense.

-Tim

I think the idea that currency is valued by the power of a government
(beyond the worth of the metal) is too modern for Taelgar. The U.S. Gov't
backed it's currency with a gold/silver equivelant until like, 1930's,
right? Also, currency stablitiy won't mean anything unless there is a fair
amount of currency trading. The main thing that makes the value of various
currencies change is the currency market. Exchange rates won't change
without good global communication, right? Large transactions could be done
with bars of gold, but based on something in one of the GURPS books about
medieval england, a lb. of gold was worth the equivalent of $20,000. (a
silver piece, weight .001 lb, is worth a $1, converstaion rate of silver
to gold is 20 to 1). I like that. So large transactions would happen via
sacks of gold coin, most likely.

outside of the country east of Chardon, and parts of Sembia. Should be
worth lots, but only because of high metal content (a dwarven gold coin
could be worth $1000, because it weighs and ounce (although that is
somewhat heavy, but you get the idea).

> It is made of pure metal, but because it is backed by what
> is definately the richest race per capita (humans probably have more
> accumulated wealth, but only because there's far more of them), it is worth

Why would anyone care about per capita? Chardon+Sebmia, if they have, say,
$100mil are richer than the dwarves, if the dwarves only have $50mil, even
if there are only 3 dwarves left.

> somewhat more than the metal content alone.  Chardonian currency is also
> very stable, and is the primary currency used west of the mountains and in
> Rohan.  It's worth more than the pure metal content of the coins, I'd
> guess.

Again, I disagree with the whole idea of money being worth more than the
metal content of the coins. Make Chardon and Sembian coins worth be based
on weight in silver/gold/copper, and dwarven coins be something cool
(maybe worth more for some reason, maybe just big, or some unusual metal
(mithril coins anyone?)).

> exactly what they would be worth in pure metal.  Rohan also mints currency,
> but it is currently nearly worthless.  Some of the nations to the east of
> Sembia also mint currency, but there currency is 1) not accepted in Sembia
> (usually) for political reasons, and 2) not backed by a stable or powerful
> government, and so not often used.  People will accept it (outside of
> Sembia), but other coins are preferred.

I don't think Rohan should mint coins at all. Why bother? Most of the
day-to-day trading of th3e Rohirrim would be barter, I imagine, and horse
herds would be the main way to judge large scale wealth. If they had need
for coinage, just use Sembia's or Chardon's, I'd say (although, I could
see the nationalistic argument for them minting there own coins, if anyone
thinks that should be so). The countries east of sembia, especially if
they are only loosing governments, also would be unlikely to bother to
mint there own coins, espeically if they raid sembia a lot. Why bother
minting your own coins if everyone uses Sembian coins?

Thoughts? Disagreements? Agreements?
