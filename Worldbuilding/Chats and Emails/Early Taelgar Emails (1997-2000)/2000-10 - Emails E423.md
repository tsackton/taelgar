On Thu, 12 Oct 2000, Tim Sackton wrote:

>What I mean is that the primary religious institutions should be
>residential monasteries, with temples only showing up in areas of large
>population density.  In other words, the religious locus of the community
>should not be a sacred space primarily used as a gathering place at
>particular times, but rather a sacred space that is set aside continually
>as a residential dwelling place for the guardians of sacred life, the
>monks.

I basically like this.

>As for how the common people obtain religion: 1) major ceremonies,
>2) wandering religious orders, 3) alms-rounds by local monks, and 4)
>teaching sermons at monasteries ("dharma-lectures," to use Buddhist term,
>basically expounding of doctrine for a lay audience, far less ritualized
>than, say, a mass or some other religious ceremony like that).

How would it be different from catechism or sermons in the Western
tradition? What sort of people (economic / social class) would attend
these lectures? How many monks are there on Taelgar? If a lot, where do
they all get their education -- something which isn't going to be
cheap. It is hard to imagine an organization of well-educated monks living
off the voluntary contributions of peasants that is widespread enough that
every village has a local monastery. The resources of most medieval
villages were barely enough to support a parish church, and tithing was
often enforced by law. Voluntary contributions are going to be less. Also,
this raises a related issue of peasant life and land ownership.

>To me, the
>following picture emerges: most communities have a monastery or a monastic
>community within a reasonable travel time from them, ranging from small
>groups of monks making their home in huts in the woods to small monasteries
>with 5-10 monks and a few buildings, to massive monasteries with 100s of
>monks, large monastic communities with attendent temples, meditation halls,
>lecture halls, etc.  In any case, most/all communities would have a sacred
>space set apart from the community proper in which rituals and devotions
>took place, tended by religious officials (monks) who lived within the
>sacred space and were a required element of the sacredness of the space.

This picture is nice, but seems to rely a lot of unclearly defined things
such as "communinity". Or, to put it another way, its enforcing a
homoegenous style of village life on Taelgar. In a place like Sembara, I
could see each manor (assuming manor's exist, which I think is a
fundamentally important idea to support the idea of a feudal
nation) support a monk or a small community of monks, perhaps endowing
them with land which is voluntarily worked by the people of the manor,
with perhaps some additional food supplied by the lord. In Chardon, urban
settings, wandering orders make a lot of sense. To get away from detail
for a moment, I like the basic idea of the monk defining the sacredness of
a space, and of each community (for some definition of the word) having
some sort of access to these sacred spaces. And I like the idea of the
monks being supported at a basically sustience level by voluntary
contributions. Perhaps it is generally accepted the monks cannot
acculumate possessions, so everything is either owned by the monastic
community (books, buildings, land, furniture) or is consumable (food,
drink, clothing). Furthermore, I don't really like the idea of some sort
of heirarchy above the level of monk. There would have to be some network
of client-patron relationships, as larger monasteries would have more
monks and resources, and would often be the sources of learning for the
monks in the smaller monasteries. But nothing would be formalized, and
anyone could in theory decide to become a monk, and perhaps all it takes
to be a monk is true calling. So in one sense of the word, anyone could
stand up and decide that all of sudden they are a monk, and then they are
one. Training would have to come, but as soon as one is called one is
religious, and one can begin to define sacred spaces.

Another question: what about women? That's actually a general question
worth considering for Taelgar as a whole. How egalitarian should various
places be?

about the essential potrayal of Melchaior as "evil" and Araht as
"good". Should we elimanate that potrayal? I don't want to start creating
societies with a philosophically eastern bent, or at least not a lot of
them. More on this below.

>Okay, now a few comments on the nature of "The Church" -- I agree that
>there isn't a Church.  Instead, there is a collection of monastic or
>ascetic or "spiritual" orders, some of which are welcome to be "firebrand"
>types, that are all doctrinally unified by the simple fact of Divine
>revelation of Truth.  There could also be "evil" cults that deny the Divine

I think we should introduce a fundamental conflict within Araht/Melchaior
which allows certain aspects to deny other aspects, or certain saints to
fight against other saints. The implication of this might be to add a
fourth level of understanding, between 2 and 3, where one seems all divine
beings as in some sense dervived from Araht, Melchaior, or a
combination. Araht is seen as creative/good, Melchaior is seen as
destructive/bad. The higher truth would be to realize that A/M are both
necessary, and the higher truth would be the realize they are one entity.

>Divine Truth:
[snip]

Short rephrase with my idea above added:

1) Ultimate Truth: The Divinity is seen as The Divinity.
2) Harmonious Duality: The Divinity is seen as two beings, Araht and
Melchaior, who are fundmentally both necessary for the universe to
function. One is the creative force, the other the destructive (as you
described)
3) Duality: The Divinity is seen as two beings in opposition, Araht and
Melchaior. One is benevolent, the other malevolent.
4) Aspects: Various diverse beings are seen as being independent spiritual
entities, associated with either Araht or Melchaior, who would be seen as
uberaspects.

I think this simplifies a lot of the problems of
conflict/harmony. Although it may be possible to eliminate 3, and merge it
into 4 in some way. But I think there needs to be a way to allow conflict
between Araht and Melchaior.

>Religious Diversity:
>Within this framework of Divine truth, the primary level of relgious
>diversity is the aspect level.  Thus most of the religious diversity of
>Taelgar is determined by what aspects and monastic orders are powerful in
>which regions, not by fundamental differences in doctrine, which is
>established as a Divine given.

Except there could be orders that see other orders who see higher levels
then they do as wrong. Within each level of understanding, divine truth is
clear, but once one crosses level boundaries it is less certain whether it
is necessary to accept the other levels as true. Or rather, the levels
above. I could see a cult of Melchaior, the evil one, as refusing to
accept that he is part of Araht. Or vice versa. And the Divinity
"allows" this because conflict between its parts is in some sense
necessary -- otherwise why the firstborn imperfections?
