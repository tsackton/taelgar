1st age:

destruction after 2nd age, take dunmar, parts of sembara, south at this

northerners stay in the north, though, weird, uneasy mix in dunmar.

dunmar, chardon, etc are generally less organized than the north or the

talydras show up, found drankor, and spread. think rome. after fge falls,

age, but several strong leaders found workable countries (sembara and
dunmar, among others).

Church--did it do anything? Magery--any mage-rule countries, that sort of
thing? Most of what I just wrote is based on medieval history and ancient
greek stuff, both of which I am taking classes on.

Yes. The history of the other races doesn't matter much except when it
"interferes" with the history of human--for example, a bare bones
hobgoblin history would be useful in working out how the humans fought
them and where the unified in defense and such... In the early middle ages
many of the strong kingdoms grew up because of the necessity of defending
the land from Vikings, Sacerens, Scots, Danes, Picts, or whatever the
local "barbarian" group was (the Anglo-saxon kings of England (Alfred the
Great and his decendents) are a good example of this). I think the
hobgoblins would have had a similar effect, and thus it would be useful to
map out a basic history... something like:

Great unified "hordes" during the first age, broke apart in defeat,
scattered to the north, and vasan-area, maybe elsewhere. During the late
second age, the tribes began to grow more significantly in strength, and
some moved to the current bastion of hobgoblin tribal power, the area
north of Dunmar. The First Great Empire (would anyone object if I renamed
it the Drankorian Empire? it seems to make more sense) to a large degree
drove the hobgoblins northwards, and the areas on the fringes of the
Drankorian Empire (north inner sea, north west coast, pandar area) see
large influxes of hobgoblin power. After the fall of the Drankorian
Empire, the hobgoblins (who aren't affected by the plague, right?) spread
south in droves, reclaimed the mountainous area north of Dunmar, and
caused a bloody century of chaos. Several powerful human leaders checked
the hobgoblin push to a large degree, and the hobgoblins didn't make a
major comeback until the end of the 3rd age. Then it becomes current
events and recent history.

Moving on to my ideas for human history (and taking Tim's comments into
account):

1st age:
Details are very unimportant at this distance in the past. The only
interesting things are what myths would come down through the ages, and
what the situation looked like at the end of the 1st Age. A few ideas of
countries that existed would be useful for *ancient* ruins. So...

Humans "awaken" near Drankor, and a great leader emerges and builds the
first human cities... upon his death, the humans begin to scatter, some
carrying on near Drankor, and expanding upon the Drankor civilization
(which would be the most important one of the age--the Drankorians would
have been the humans who actually fought with the elves, and left for
Hkar). Those who scatter are either nomadic, or settle in various fertile
areas (Sembara, Comor, up the entire river that runs into Drankor, the
Arabor area, the South, to some lesser degree the area where Chardon is,
and of course, elsewhere). This slow spreading of humanity sets the stage
for later kingdoms, and of course, peoples much of the land. Non-human
races would be more tightly clustered in various enclaves, except for the
elves who were scattered everywhere. Perhaps some tension between elves
and humans, as the humans would want to deforest the land, and the elves
to a large degree would not let them. Human civilizations rise and fall
during this time, but none reach the height of the Drankorian civilization
(by one figure the first age was 3672 years long--is that a good general
number? I think ~2500 would be better, and more in line with the 2nd and
3rd ages). Here is where wild and crazy ruins could be created--not sure
it is truely worth developing entire cultures just so they can leave
ruins. Also, various myths of great human leaders and such can be set
against this backdrop. Suffice to say, that as the battles to end the
first age end, the Drankorian civilization, being allies with the elves,
is mostly destroyed, and those who are not leave for Hkar, leaving Drankor
and the environs abandoned. The elves die in great numbers, and retreat
northwards, to the big surviving forest up there, leaving much land for
human populations to clear and farm. I imagine most of the human
settlements at this point are on the east of the mountains, clustered
around the Inner Sea or the big river (needs a name) that leads from Inner
Sea to Drankor, or in Dunmar, or as far north west of the mountains as the
horizontal river running from the South Chardon area to the moutains. Also
the South.

2nd and 3rd ages in next email...

Second age: (with thoughts of Tim's incoporated):

At the end of the First Age, the major significant areas of human
civilization would have been clustered around the Inner Sea, or in
Drankor. During the Second Age, the richest human civilizations were the
ones with close ties to the dwarves, and again, they were devasted at the
end of the Age. I don't have many ideas about the second age at the
moment, but basically I'm thinking there would have been a fair amount of
flux in the human civilizations. Drankor, although still settled, has
probably become considerably less powerful than it was in the First Age,
and perhaps commands only some small portion of it's original "territory".
I don't think any lasting traditions or empires really emerged from the
Second Age. Emerging from the ruins of the Second Age would be many small
warring lands, and a small human population, probably less than it had
been during any point other than the beginnings of the First Age--many had
ended up at Hkar, and many had died in the wars at the end of the Age.

Third Age:

At the beginning of the Third Age, Drankor was a small settlement,
probably a focus point for somewhat destitue humans. North of Drankor,
Dunmar, with it's rich fields, was a hotly contested mosaic of warring
dukes and barons. The Sembaran area and Arabor area were also a patchwork
of small kingdoms, trying to rebuild after most of the major cities
against the mountains had been sacked, even completely destroyed. West of
the mountains, the major settlements had been even more heavily hit then
the Inner Sea countries, and most of the population was small rural
villages, with a local lord. [This bit needs some work, but to a large
degree is probably mostly irrelevant in light of the Drankorian Empire's
coming domiance]. In the year 166 T.A., the Talyrdas landed at Drankor,
the site of the ancenstral human lands. It is quickly and swiftly
conquered, under the able leadership of Darius, and they begin to rebuild.
Soon Drankor is complete, and the Talyrdas spread northwards along the
river, and along the coast. By the year 200 T.A., the population of the
Drankorian city-state is growing rapidly, and Darius begins looking to the
rich lands of Dunmar to expand his Empire. He took the Barony of Nieper in
the year 204 T.A, through a lightning military strike. A year later, an
unsuccessful attempt by the late Baron's son to reclaim the land from him
(supported by several nobles in eastern Dunmar) lead to the confusication
of the supporting nobles land, and more than doubled the size of Darius'
empire. Drankor at this point, less than 50 years after the landing of the
Talydras, was the major power in the area. During this time many of the
other Talydras had been settling along the river, and the entire area
between the two rivers (Drankor and the one originting in a big lake in a
forest) was firmly under the control of Drankor, but only as far north  as
the more northern mountain range. Over the next 20 years (204-234), Darius
expanded his Empire to the north, west, and east. By 250 T.A., the
Drankorian Empire was worthy of it's name, and spread over most of the
territory of modern Dunmar (although a few Dukes in the north marches held
out for longer, as did the Baron of the Gap), northwards as far as the
ruins of Arabor, and eastwards as far as the old trade road, and perhaps
even a bit further. After stabilizing this chunk of land, Darius began a
series of Southern expeditions, which all meet with various degrees of
resistance, but ended with Darius' control of most of the Southern settled
lands. [There needs to be something further south than the south--whatever

in a position to be not terribly greatly affected by the Plague, and
therefore conqueor/assimilate much of the imperial South]. By the year 500
T.A., Darius had fully assimilated the South, and many of the old,
reluctant Dunmarian baron's have recognized Darius as Emperor. Darius
begins his northward push around this time. The area to the north of the
Gap is settled by [need ideas here. In fact, I don't have any good ideas
for any parts of the northward push, other than that at the end of it,
probably 200 years later (so circe 700 T.A.), Darius would control the
area from the Chardon area or even a bit north, to the northern Sembara
river valley, to the south of the map, and as far west as the desert and
that area--the only areas of significant political important and
civilization other than the Drankorian Empire would be the aforementioned
south of the South area, some city states on the eastern edges of the
Inner Sea, the nomadic northerns--probably on both sides of the mountains,
although also probably fairly different on each side, and Comor area,
maybe some other Inner Sea settlements, but I'm not sure exactly what

Even the best historians have little knowledge of the next 200 years. To
term them a dark age would be a gross understatement. In many places
literacy is lost--if not for Chardon and the Church, the vast majority of
