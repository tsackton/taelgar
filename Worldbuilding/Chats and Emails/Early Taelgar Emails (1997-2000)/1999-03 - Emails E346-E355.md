A few quick things.  I have 20 minutes before class in which I'm not going to get any work done, so I'm going to give you a _very_ brief overview of what I've been thinking about magic.  I'd like magic to feel more magical.  Historically, magic draws on the power of supernatural forces.  I like that.  That's how magic should feel in Taelgar; it is too simple the way it is now.  This will require slightly revamping religion, I think, or really just adding more detail.  I like the Jewish Kabbalistic stuff with all the secret names of God and things like that....I was thinking of incoporating a similar idea, with different names of Gods having different attributes.  I don't really have time to go into detail about the stuff I want to make up now...expect that in a few days, once my life settles down a little.  Certainly by next weekend.  Basically, historical magic comes in a large variety of flavors:
Alchemy
Astrology
Ritualistic Magic (many types of this -- there is the Medieval Christian stuff, Jewish things like the Key of Solomon, Late Antique Synchrenistic Magic, which is sort of a combination of Greek, Roman, Egyptian, Persian, etc magical influences, and Norse/Anglo-Saxon magic)
Runic Magic (Norse/Scandinavian/Anglo-Saxon type magic, using the Futhark runes)

Now, obviously, some of Taelgar magic will fall outside of anything here -- True magic, for one, clearly is outside the realm of these magics.  Dwarven racial magic is similar to Runic Magic.  I think I'd like Ogre magic to have a large ritualistic component.  Halfling and Kenku obviously falls outside of these things.  Lizard Man probably has a large ritualistic component too.

Okay, so far this is just random rambling.

I'd like to develop a Taelgar version of Alchemy, which I think you've already done some stuff on, but I'd like to make it a lot more detailed.  Also, I'm thinking about a Taelgar version of astrology, if not so much for divination as for magical purposes.

I guess my main point is that I'm starting to not really like the idea that really powerful effects can be done really quickly with no real prepartion or work.  I would like there to be a much greater ritualistic component to most magic, including the standard human spellcasting.  Also, I don't want spellcasting to be so uniform.  Oh, and finally, I think the Academy is going to have to be revamped slightly.  I don't like the idea of public classes on magic.  I'd prefer a historical, more mystical model to the D&D/Eye of the World model.

So anyway, that's what I have so far.  To give you a taste of what magic should feel like, I'm attaching a spell I wrote for Magic in the Middle Ages that I think is pretty good.  It's based of Norse mythology; install the rune font before you open my spell, since I use runes in it.

I have to go now, but send me comments.

-Tim

Okay, here's my basic idea. I still need to flesh it out/work out the
mechanics, but is this anything like what you were thinking?

There are two components to a success arcane magic spell. The first (and
most important) is the ritual. This determines the nature and effect of
the spell. The second is the "channeling" (or something) of magical
energy. This makes the effect real, and separates mages from nonmages
(represented by Magery, obviously). The magery part of spelling casting is
much less diverse than the rituals. I'm thinking that once a certain kind
of magic is learned, it is easy to use it in other spells. So learning the
second fire spell would be much easier than the first (I'm thinking of the
true magic stuff). The ritual bit would also be fairly easy to learn, in a
sense. The rituals in grimores would take much much much much longer than
the effective casting time of the spell (a fireball would take 30 minutes
of ritual or something). Yet, any mage who knows how to manipulate the
magic behind the spell can cast that spell (taking the 30 minutes, or 2
months, or whatever). Once a spell is learned, the ritual is shortened in
a different way for every mage. I'm not sure about the mechanics of this,
but I'd probably make spells learned at a low level (1 point in them) take
longer than normal. Also, preqs for spells could go away (not sure about
this one). Finally, each mage would cast the spell in a slightly different
way--not only different ritual, but different effect. A mage would have a
good idea of what their own spells did, but not a very good idea of what
other mages spells did (I'd make my players write out each of their
spells, with modified effects, based on the gurps magic version--maybe
write out rituals too, although this would only work if the number of
spells that can be cast by a mage was reduced.)

Is this vaguely, basically, what you were thinking? I'm starting to really
like the possiblities of a magic system based on something like this, but
I'd like some more refined input from you, especially on the learning
spells bit (and who would teach spell casting?). And of course the
cultural aspects need to be worked out.

Oh, you might want to check the webpage again sometime soon. I've gotten a

## E355 — “Re: More on Magic”

- **From:** Tim Sackton

Okay, I like that basic idea.  First, a few somewhat non-related comments, but still about magic.  I was thinking that the two main aspects to any magical effect are 1) the source of power and 2) the means by which the caster draws on the power and converts it to his desired effect.  So, we can make a chart listing each race, the available sources of power to each race, and the available mechanisms for each race and each source of power.

First, a little bit about each source of power.  The three I can think of are the plane of magic, the plane of spirits (equivalent to the astral plane/dreamworld/plane of consciousness) and the deities.  The plane of magic is very poorly characterized.  Perhaps relating it to the elements, and maybe to some sort of Taelgarian astrology would work.  I'll work on that; hopefully this weekend I'll be able to come up with a rough draft.  The plane of spirits is fairly well characterized by Kyr, but a lot of the general sense we have of the place has to be codified, and the relationship between consciousness, the lizard man first born, dead people, dreams, the representation of the physical world, time, and using all this to do magic also has to be worked on (which is probably not as daunting a task as one might think).

To talk about the deities as a source of magic requires a digression into religion.  I like what we made up already about the priesthood, temples, and that sort of thing.  The basic point is that the Taelgar religion doesn't really require worship in church, and it doesn't involve going to mass every week or things like that.  The world wasn't created in seven days; there is no Sabbath or anything like that.  Yet religion is still a powerful force, because the inner circle priests are _powerful_.  Although Aru/Melkor never descend to Taelgar, or directly interfere, or even indirectly interfere, their power is still available for use by those who know how to access it.  What I'm thinking right now is that this type of magic is far and away the most powerful kind, at least equally true magic.  However, and this is a big however, accessing the divine power source is very, very difficult, and can only be done through ritual and prayer.  I don't know where this leaves holy knights; perhaps they have a lesser, but less time consuming, form of the same thing.  Maybe a 20 minute ritual before expecting battle to enhance strength or something.  I kind of like it, but tell me what you think.

Plus, maybe another source of magic that might work is the earth itself.  The resonances from the creation of the earth could contain some remnant of divine magic that could be tapped with the appropriate means.  Let's tentatively consider that a fourth source of power.

So that is sources of magic.  That leaves mechanisms.  Obviously one mechanism is ritual.  Another, I guess, could be just thought.  I can't really think of any other way of drawing magical power other than those two, and the second one is really weak, since it is really just a very, very shortened ritual.  Well, anyway, let's then say that for now, all magic works by ritual, broadly defined.  It is the type of ritual that distinguishes.

This leaves out one kind of magic -- the totally mental things like the telepathy of the halflings or kenku, and the shape shifting of kenku, and things like that.  I guess we'll have to worry about where to fit them in later.  Halfling magic needs to be more fleshed out as it is, so I'll leave halfling and kenku magic out of the following, leaving the following races to deal with magically: humans, dwarves, ogre, elves, lizard men; plus the esoteric races: dragons, giants, liontaurs, gnomes; plus the "evil" races: hobgoblins, orcs, mind flayers.  Any race I'm forgetting?  Esoteric and "evil" races, I don't know magically what they should be like, so like kenku and halflings, I'll leave them out.  Any thoughts?

So, then, starting with elves.  Elves draw power from the plane of magic exclusively, and are good enough at it that they can do an awful lot of stuff with just thought.  For the really, really powerful stuff, like plane-shifting, even elves must use rituals.  The character of these rituals is rather unclean at this time.

Next, orge.  Ogre have two related-yet-different types of magic.  The first is weather magic, agriculture magic, stone magic, mountain magic.  It comes from the plane of spirits (and the resonances in the mountains), and to draw on it requires rituals that involve a lot of mediation.  The orge must contact the spirits of the mountains.  How that is done I'm not quite sure, but that would be the essence of this branch of ogre magic.  The second branch, also from the plane of spirits (and the resonances of the stone) involves battle-magic.
