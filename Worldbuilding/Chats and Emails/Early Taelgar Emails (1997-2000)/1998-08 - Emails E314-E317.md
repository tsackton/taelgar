The Chardon national government is a council made of reps from several different provences -- me and Schwartz made up a lot of this in NH; it's written down somewhere.  Basically, the council is weighted towards the Chardon city provence getting want it wants -- it is one short of a majority, so all the outlying provences have to gang up against it in order to do something Chardon doesn't want.  The head of state is selected by the council members from among there ranks, and so far has always been one of the Chardon proper delegates.  It seems likely that in the next fifty years or so the outlying provences will agitate for more representation, and probably get it, although the Chardon provence will remain the wealthiest and most influential for some time to come.

In Chardon, the reps are selected in the following way: a certain number of people are picked by the merchant houses, a certain number by the academy, a certain number by the military, and certain number by the trades guilds.  Chardon is heavily guild organized, with basically everybody who works in the city belonging to a guild of some sort.

This could certainly be fleshed out some more, but you get the basic idea, I hope.  A pseudo-democracy, heavily guild based, ruled by councils.

In terms of nation directives, Chardon's main goal right now is expansion.  That is the focus and driving force behind all their policies, basically.  Think of U.S. manifest destiny.

>2) Sembara, Dunmar, various Inner Sea countires: Any notes on
>strange/unusual things going on there, any notes on the Inner Sea
>countries. I can mostly fake this though.

One idea about the Inner Sea -- as you go further east, the Inner Sea becomes increasingly littered with small islands, shoals, and dangerous rip tides, making navigation difficult to impossible.  No one knows what lies on the other side of the islands, but the westernmost of them serve as pirate bases, and are the home to many strange things.  Perhaps one of the bad guys behind the recent events in Chardon during Harvestfest makes his base out there.

Inner Sea politics were fairly well developed in NH, I think.  I'll leave you to flesh that out some more.  Email me whatever you come up with, of course.

>3) The Academy as a political/powerful organization, as rather than a
>school.

The Academy has a lot of clout in Chardonian politics as outlined above.  Also, Academy mages are present as advisors in most courts/governments in Taelgar.  They can subtly influence policy that way.  Finally, the Academy has such overwhelming power (the Academy alone, given some time to prepare, could probably successfully invade any country in Taelgar) that when they make a policy statement, something they almost never do, it is usually at least considered.  In some ways, the Academy functions as a U.N. with teeth, but it rarely if ever actually interferes.  In some ways, they see themselves as the guardians of Taelgar.  Does this make sense?

(Oh, also, like the Mockers, they are very concerned with information, and spend a lot of time gathering it.)

>4) History through 153 F.A. Most of this is done. I can fill in the FA

Right.  Mostly done -- if you actually type up a finalized timeline, I'll look it over and comment.  I think that's pretty well done.  More detail could be added to the 2nd and 3rd ages, especially to the 3rd age during the F.G.E and right afterwards.

>5) Magic. What does the Academy know about how it works. What should my
>players know.

Taelisin probably knows about as much as Rai knew.  The Academy higher ups know about gemstone magic, and a little about true magic.  Your players should probably know almost none of this.  They would know that ley lines exist, but not how to draw on them or even necessarily that you can draw on them.  They would probably be ignorant about nodes (which although related to ley lines, are not the same -- where two ley lines crossed, they would see nothing special at all).  They would maybe know that true magic exists, and would definately know that gemstone magic exists, but nothing at all about them.  They would have probably heard the word chalyte in connection which gemstone magic, but not know anything about it.  That's the academy players.

The general public knows nothing about the different kinds of magic.  Some of the general public might have heard of ley lines, but they would have wildly different ways of interpretting what they knew, and would have varying and usually wrong ideas about them.

Does that answer your questions?
