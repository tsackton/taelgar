## E250 — “Taelgar stuff (long)”

- **From:** David Schwartz
- **Date:** May 5, 1998, 8:33 PM EDT
- **To:** Mike Sackton
- **Cc:** Tim Sackton, Eric Rosenbaum, Dave Kong

More on mockers, kenku, giants, a hobgoblin nation and international
politics still to come.

Ok, first, can I drop by the next session and play an npc or someone like
that? Second, are the people in the campaign local, and are you playing
over the summer.  Third, I think that we should do some one shots or short
adventures with tim GM-ing.  I think that these are the sort of things out
of which a whole hell of a lot of information will arise, kind of like
improving a scene and sticking it into a movie.  That aside, here's a
little bit of information.
I have finals tomorrow and the day after, so this is really just a form of
procrastination, so i make no claims about the quality of what i write,
all I'm thinking about now is IR.
That said.  I deffinitely think that there should be an established
hobgoblin nation taking up much of the territory that formery was alm and
southern volkraad.  The reasons for this are many, but mostly I think that
it would be a falacy to think that only thge so-called good races are the
ones that form cities and industrialize and stuff.  I think the whole idea
of hobgoblin tribes roaming around with shoddy weapons and armor is a
stupid one, they couldn't pose as large a threat as they do with that
being the case.  So I really think that there should be a hobgoblin
nation.  Now, I further propose that this nation not be currently hostile
towards the south.  Hobgoblins aren't stupid, they know that if they
attacked Chardon big academy types, as well as a large army, would kick
the shit out of them.  So they are content to expand northward and
eastward towards the mountains, and that is what they have been doing for
the last 75 years, after having been beaten back northwards in the
hobgoblin wars.
The present is an interesting time, hobgoblins are beinginning to settle
what used to be volkraad, and the people of Sembia, especially thge ones
who guard the pass are starting to be afraid.  Sembia has enjoyed a sort
of millitary laxness during the past several generation- the navy is
sufficient to drive off the neighbors to the east, and the no longer have
a need to man the great wall of sembia, in fact, the wall is probably 150
miles south of what sembia would claim as its northern borders.  There
also hasn't been anybody from the west that sembia has had to worry about,
save for the occasional raiding party trying to make it through the pass.
Consequently, Sembia is starting to get very worried at these rumors of
settlement in the former volkraad.
The hobgoblin nation _actually_ has no wish to go to war with sembia at
the present time.  the lesson learned from the orignal volkraad was that
there is no way to adequately defend a frontier country.  Just as the
humans were destroyed by the hobgoblins, the hobgoblins would be destroyed
by the humans at this point.  I have to stress that that is only the short
erm goal, in another fifty years, if things progress as planned, there
will be a large, strong hoblgoblin nation with a well developed
infrastructure and large domestic economy.  It is at that point that far
sighted hobgoblins plan to sweep in from the west and take down sembia.
Essentially, this would be the same scenario as the barbarians from the
north sweeping down and conquoring a decandant and largely defenseless
china in whatever century that happened (12th?  8th?  my history is rusty)
So, the hobgoblins aren't planning anything right now.  And are actually
trying to keep a fairly low profile, they are at the poin that a large and
concentrated millitary effort would be able to break the nation and send
them back into tribalism again, and they really don't want that to happen
and don't even want to have to fight that war or any form of it.
So we have what is basically a growing hobgoblin nation.

Now, there are several things that the countries of the world could do to
stop the hobgoblins
1.  They could fight.  A fight would be prolonged, costly and mostly end
up with door to door fighting in the streets of the new city.  every block
would be a difficulty to take and every citzen would become a terrorist
once it was taken.  It is doable, but the cost in lives and money to do
something like this would be enormous.

2.  Increasing trade ties with the hobgoblin nation would mean that the
hobgoblins would become increasingly depenadant on external trade for
their existence.  the hobgoblins would find that they got goods from the
rest of he wrold that they could get nowhere else.  They couldn't fight a
war because fighting a war would mean the end of those sources of trade.
Domination would not be economically feasable in this case.  In other
words, if strong ties to the hobgoblin nation were created, a situtation
of interdependance would arise, the nation would be unable to exist
without the other cpuntries around it- just as Chardon would be unable to
exist as itis  without, for example, the islands to the west and Rohan.

Those are just two possibilities, but i really want to make the case the
utter destruction of the hobgoblin nation is not necessary.  True,
hobgoblins really hate humans a whole lot, but that doesn't mean that they
would plundge headlong into a war that they were unable to win, the
hobgoblins are going to sit tight and wait until everything is in place to
attack.

Ok, I'm sure you have comments and thoughts on the above, and I may have
plunged just a little bit too much into liberal IR theory, but I want to
point out that the traditional notions of constant war between humans and
the "bad guys" shouldn't necessarily apply.

On the other hand, there probably still are a whole bunch of hobgoblin
tribes wandering around, maybe even some immigrating to the nation and
having to wander en mass across other countries, which might not make
those countries too happy.  Also, hobgoblin tribes like those in the
foothills of the big mountains, or in the hills north of rohan, or in the
hills to the east of where the plague used to be, are all still around, so
it's not like all hobgoblins are civilized.

Upwards and onwards.

---------

I like liontaurs.  I really like them.  Pop them in some deserts or some
grasslands, what's east of Rohan?

---------

Kenku, um, if I haven't said this before, perhaps I should clarify it now.
There are nine tribes of kenku, each numbering about one hundred
thousdand, the tenth tribe is virtually non-existent, in fact, entirely
non-existent, except for rumors.  The entire bloodline was killed during
the war ending the third age.  A chief controls each of the clans, which
are faily loose organizations, and once a year, all the chiefs get
together to discuss, relax, drink and generally have fun.  Once every
generation, maybe every twenty years or so, all the kenku everywhere get
together to commemorate their role in the passing of the age and their
status as a nation, which most ribes don't think about except at that
time.
The overwhelming majority of kenbku population in in Vasa, the plains of
vasa are home to immense herds, (semi-cultivated) of plains animals-
buffalo thingies, antelope, bnasically the same sort of thing you see in
the great plains of the west in like 1700 before anyone was there.  The
kenku themselves live in the mountains surrounding the plains, both to the
east and the west and hunt across the territory between them.  Each tribe
has its own hunting area, a sort of loose border, and occassionally fights
break out.  Each tribe sort of manages its own herds- stampede them to
high ground when floods might come, that sort of general thing, but mostly
they are just left to roam free.
There is a small group of kenku living in the newly rebuilt urlich
monestary, which has been vastly enlarged.  There is space for about
20,000 kenku at absolute capacity, but only about 5 to 7 thousand live
there at any given time.  This is the political capital of the kenku
nation, due to its centrality, as well as the centeral point of the
messanger service, all cross continental flights go through the monestary.
The official seat of kenku government is at the monestary, but, if one has
not already realized, the idea of a "kenku government" is a bit of an
oxymoron. Basically, the government speaks for three of the tribes, and
these are the three that one sees in human civilization- the rest pretty
much spend their time up north in Vasa.  This can provide a bit of a
problem from time to time.  The racial telepathy skills of someof the
elders allow them to communicate important information to all of the
kneku, i.e., we are at _peace_ don't go killing people, but the degree to
which those words are heeded varies in inverse proportion to how far north
you go.  The kenku tribe living at the top of the world, who exist live in
family groups of five or ten, and are seasonally change from brown to
white to match the snow, and hibernate for three months don't really
listen to a damn thing that those southern kenku say- the northern tribe
tends to be a fiercely individual lot- because they had lived in the
western extremes of pandar most of them were left behind, as a
consequence, they don;t feel that they owe the kneku race as a whole much
of anything at all.

I was thinking about the description of kenku in the fiend folio from
first edition d+d.  It says that they are always looking for adventure,
and i think that this could be brought into a campaign sometimes.  kenku
are out wandering the world, mostly in groups of kenku, but i wouldn't be
surpirsed if shape changed kenku joined human adventuring parties from
time to time just for kicks.  Really just for kicks, not to kill or steal
anything, just for the thrill of wandering around the continent getting
into trouble.

Uh, so that's it for kenku for the time being.  More will come in responce
to specific questions.  really, just as a genberal note, it is much easier
to answer specific questions or lines of inquiry about stuff than it is
just to start talking at random.

giants.  I see the giants as inhabiting the forests at the north end of
vasa and perhaps in some protions of the countries to the east of vasa,
whose names I forget. The giants are big, like real big, standing over
fifteen feet tall.  Similarly, the forests they inhabit are real big, huge
trees that go way way up and have stood as is since before time started.
The campaign uses for giants are all over the place.  First, it would be
fun to play a giant, second, giants could have all kinds
of uses as plot
tools for someone who's in sembia or vasa.  Why are giants just now coming
out of the forests after 5,000 years of sitting there?  What is the
sembian reaction to them?  What skills do they have to offer the rest of
the world?  They could be used for a countless variety of plot hooks, and
awhole campaign could be based simply around a journey towards the forests
and what happens along the way.

A side note- wouldn't it be cool if there were little pockets in the world
where time still hadn't started?  I mean, tim started for the whole world,
and things started growing and dying and having kids and all, but what if
there were still a few places where that had not happened?  What about
people from the past three ages who went into those places just to sit
down for a mintues, and have been preserved, imagining that only a moment
had gone by since they sat down. . .

More forthcoming on giants.  I really think we should do some role playing
this summer to get some of these idea crystalized.  I mean, it's one thing
to talk about them, but it's really using them in a campaign that is going
to make them workable.  For example, talking about giants is just neat-o,
but I don't think that they are really going o take off until they are
actually incorporated into a campaign.  this is the same idea as the
hobgoblin nation- most everything that we have done has been based off
what happened and what was created during our years of playing taelgar, i
think that any big new information and ideas need to actually be
incorporated into a campaign to see if they work.
