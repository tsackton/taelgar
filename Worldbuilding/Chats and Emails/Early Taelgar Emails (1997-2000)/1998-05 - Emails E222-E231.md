At 01:26 AM 4/7/98 +0000, you wrote:
>2) Magic. What do the people of Taelgar know? A few more specific

>magic... how common? Who can use it? Is there any sort of

True magic is known about in detail only by the real elites of the Academy and the people associated with the new Council.  The only ones who can use it are Tayledras and Elves, although I'm thinking of letting the occasional _very_ rare human (1 in every 10,000 with Magery 3) also have the talent.  I'm not quite sure how I would explain that though.  Gemstone magic, same deal with knowledge, but anyone with magery 3 can use it.  For both kinds of magic (but more so for gemstone magic), the common people will have heard rumors, but nothing substantial or substantiated.  Almost all, if not all, of the rumors about true magic will be derived somehow from Rai.  There is no natural/drudic magic, except so much as lizard man psionics fill that role.  Real magic comes from one of two places: Eru/Melkor, or the plane of Magic.  Arcane magic is a very crude form of true magic, and thus derives from the plane of magic.  Nodes/ley lines are probably at least generally known about by most mages, but it takes a great deal of training and skill for non-true magic users to be able to draw from them; there are probably only 2 or 3 people who can.  I haven't thought much about mana areas -- presumably there are some high mana areas, and some low mana areas.  Powerstones are fairly common, and function essentially exactly as described in GURPS Magic.  Magic items, other than powerstones, are probably somewhat rare, but not so rare for it to be really surprising to find one.  Just remember that every magic item has to have some story behind it.

>One other question: Any suggestions on cool, unique ways to
>eventually get the people in my campaign having uniquish but really
>cool powers? I liked the high power flavor of the last 2 years of
>Taelgar, and would like to duplicate some of it, but would like some
>suggestions.

The best way to do this is to give people secretive backgrounds, that they don't fully understand (best example from my campaign is probably Rai on this one).  One warning though -- I would stay away from True Magic as one of the cool powers that your players eventually get.  It will be impossible for any character to ever come anywhere close to Rai's level of coolness, and you'll probably be dissapointed.  On that note, you should also probably avoid making any of your players the firstborn of a race too.  :-)
From a GM's point of view, the trick is to leave yourself lots of hooks you can later latch on to.  Also, when you give players cool stuff, make the abilities of the cool stuff somewhat mysterious, giving them the ability to change and grow with time (like what I did with Analladin/Willow Wind).  Reveal powers slowly.  And be very careful of unconsciously trying to duplicate my campaign, because it won't work.  :-)

-Tim
