## E387 — “Taelgar & library”

- **From:** Brynn Kessler
- **Date:** June 25, 1999, 1:27 PM EDT
- **To:** Mike Sackton

First, the library. Dan wasn't there, so I couldn't use your card to check
out books. I got Walters to check them out for me, although he had to go
on a tirade about how we destroy books (specifically his GURPS basic set).
Incredibly annoying.

Secondly, Taelgar. I wrote down a few things about magic that I thought
I'd throw at you. Take it or leave it.

he primary aspect of Tir'resh (magic) is Tir'tzeh (change). All of the
power inherent within magic is the power to change Taelgar to a greater or
lesser degree. The greatest magic creates a permanent change; these are
called Tir'resh Olam (enchantments; Olam means world).
Equally as important as Tir'tzeh is I'resh (wisdom, knowlege, ability).
The true power of Tir'resh lies not within Tir'tzeh, for anyone can use a
Tir'tzehrit (ritual; hedge-wizardry is often refered to as Tir'tzehran)
to cause Tir'tzeh Olam (best translated as a magical effect; note the
difference between this term and Tir'resh Olam, which goes further to
explaining the difference between Tir'resh and Tir'tzeh). A Tir'reshet
(mage) must possess I'resh, for that is what shapes Tir'resh in the
fashion the Tir'reshet desires.
All Tir'resh is performed by a Tir'reshet contacting Malcom Tir'resh
(Magicsphere; note that malcom does not denote a physical location and
would normally be interpreted as an abstraction). This contact allows the
Tri'reshet to reshape the world, to cause a Tir'tzeh Olam.

Brynn

## E388 — “Re: Taelgar & library”

- **From:** Tim Sackton
- **Date:** June 25, 1999, 7:49 PM (archive timestamp; no Date header)
- **To:** brynn@brandeis.edu
- **Cc:** Mike Sackton

That's the type of thing you would learn, definitely, although I'll have to think about the new words.  I'm not sure I like the words...they all sound very similar and very hebrew (understandably), which makes it a little confusing.  But in general, good.  That's the type of thing I was trying to convey about how your character would think about magic.
Remember the aspect of responsibility.  Using your words (which I'm still not sure what I think of), I'resh aslo implies not just wisdom, but a great deal of responsibility.  Tir'tzehrit also implies responsibility, and it is the ability to use I'resh and Tir'tzehrit responsibly that makes a great mage, and distinguishes the wise from the foolish or naive...at least that would be the lesson drilled into you as an apprentice.
Also remember the purpose of the time you spend as a journeyman...to discover what it means to be a mage.  That is something that should be on your character's mind a lot, especially if you want to play yourself as a serious student of magic.  Some journeyman are happy to just be journeymen forever, but it doesn't seem like that would be your character, from what I know.

Hope that helps...

Tim
