## E276 — “Taelgar--Economy”

- **From:** Mike Sackton
- **Date:** May 7, 1998, 10:15 AM EDT
- **To:** Taelgar Discussion Group, Dave Schwartz, Eric Rosenbaum, Mike Sackton, Tim Sackton

Don't know if anyone cares, but how does this sound for monetary units:
half-penny, quarter-penny? ($.50, $0.25)?
$1 = 1 copper farthing (penny? what should it be called?)
$4 = 1 silver mark
$80 = 1 gold crown
therefore
1 penny = 4 marks = 80 crowns

What do people think about having currency (money w/ value beyond the
metal) that is based on certain governments, and not accepted
everywhere... or maybe 2 major sources of coins, Chardon and Sembia, each
of which have slightly (or vastly) different coins, but each of which are
accepted everywhere... for example:

1 Sembian copper  = 25 sembian commons  = 2500 sembian nobles

common = $1, copper = $0.04, noble = $100
there could of course, be multiple denominations
copper = small, about the size of a dime, 1000 to a lb., made of copper
common = small, about 2/3s dime size, 1350 to a lb. made of silver
noble = about the size of an english pound, 200(?) to a lb., made of gold

1 Chardon penny = 4 Chardon marks = 80 Chardon crowns

penny = $1, mark = $4, crown = $80

penny = about the size of a quarter, made of copper, 800 or so to a lb.
mark = about 150% the size of a dime, 900 or so to a lb., made of silver
crown = about the size of a $0.50 piece, 500(?) to a lb, made of gold

No exchange rate necessarily, but you could buy a $50 item for, say 10
marks and 10 commons.  What do people think? Also, should there even
bother existing a coin for &lt;$1? No one is going to buy anything with a
copper, say. Maybe have a "half" and "quarter" version of the lowest
valued coin? So change Chardon to copper = $1, 1 copper = 10 commons = 100
nobles.

What do you guys think?

Mike

A few thoughts on currency: I think that there should be 2 primary methonds
of paying for things (well, plus barter, but that is probably somewhat less
common, particularly in big cities, and it doesn't always work).  Large
transactions are usually done in bars of gold or precious gems.  Smaller
transactions are usually done in coin, some of which are worth no more than
there metal content, others of which are worth more than there metal
content.  Dwarven currency is very stable, although often rare, and it is
the only form of currency that dwarves will accept or use (thoughts?  is
that silly?).  It is made of pure metal, but because it is backed by what
is definately the richest race per capita (humans probably have more
accumulated wealth, but only because there's far more of them), it is worth
somewhat more than the metal content alone.  Chardonian currency is also
very stable, and is the primary currency used west of the mountains and in
Rohan.  It's worth more than the pure metal content of the coins, I'd
guess.  Sembian currency used to be the most commonly used, but it has lost
that honor to Chardonian currency.  Currently, Sembia mints coins worth
exactly what they would be worth in pure metal.  Rohan also mints currency,
but it is currently nearly worthless.  Some of the nations to the east of
Sembia also mint currency, but there currency is 1) not accepted in Sembia
(usually) for political reasons, and 2) not backed by a stable or powerful
government, and so not often used.  People will accept it (outside of
Sembia), but other coins are preferred.

Sorry for the incoherence.  I've been been studying for so long that I'm
getting the feeling nothing I say makes sense.

-Tim
