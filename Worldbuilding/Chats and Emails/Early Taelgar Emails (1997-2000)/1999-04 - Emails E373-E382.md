## E378 — “[WORLD] The Magic Systems of Taelgar”

[Taelgar is a world in which I run a GURPS campaign. This is the magic
system of that world. Written from the perspective of me--someone in the
world would not have all of this knowledge]

Magic is the manipulation of energy to cause an effect. There are
essentially three components to any use of magic: the source of the
energy, the conduit for the energy to get within reach of the mage, and
the method of manipulating the energy to create the desired effect.

The sources of magic in Taelgar are either the Celestial Spheres, or the
Earthly Spheres. The Celestial Spheres are:

* The Heavens (Essentially, the kingdom of Am (the Creator))
* The Starry Void (Home to Dragons, and other esoteric powers)
* The Five "Planets" (not actually balls of rock in space--these represent
	the essence of the five non-human True Races, and are called
	planets because they move about in the sky like earthly planets)

[The Planets are in need of names, BTW. Suggestions would be nice.]

The Earthly Spheres are the Plane of Magic and the Plane of Spirits.

A small divergence is necessary here to explain creation and the races of
Taelgar. Am is a being from a universe, that was destroyed in a
holocaust of magical backlash caused by overuse of magic. Only Am, the
Dragons, and a few items survived from the old universe. Also, the essence
of existance, the "Grey Column" which is the ulimate source of everything
survived. Am drew a small amount of power from the Grey Column, and
created the Heavens, the Starry Void, Taelgar, and the Earthly Spheres.
Then, Am created various races. To create a race, first a Firstborn (or
group of Firstborn) was created that would epitomize the race. Then, from
that Firstborn the rest of the race grew. The Firstborn are:

* The Ent and the Unicorn of the Elves. The First created.
* The Mountain Being of the Ogre. The Second created.
* The Seven Clan Leaders of the Dwarves. The Third created.
* The Fire, Earth, Water, and Air Essences of the Lizard Men. The Fourth
	created.
* The Traveller, Sailor, and Merchant of the Halflings. The Fifth created.

The order of creation determines lifespan, relative power and many other
things. The disadvantage to being created through Firstborn is that the
race becomes homogenous. The Firstborn is what the race is like. And so Am
created the humans directly, without a Firstborn, to create a race of
diversity. So humans were the Sixth True race. There are other races that
were created (hobgoblins, kenku, orcs, giants, liontaurs, gnomes, mind
flayers) [some of these names are in desparate need of changing, but I
always have difficulty coming up with good names] but they were not
created by Am directly, but rather through Dragons, generally, or possibly
through other esoteric magic, such as that contained in the items
enchanted in the previous universe that survived. Hence, they are not true
races. So each of the planets is in reality, the essence in some sense of
the Firstborn of the race.

Another thing to note here is what happens to a soul after it dies. If the
race is True Race (excepting humans) then the soul somehow joins with the
other souls of the race. The earlier the creation of the race the greater
indiviualism the soul retains--each elf has a real afterlife, whereas each
halfling joins with a larger whole and all traces of identity are lost.
The other races fall in between. Human and non-True race's souls vanish
when they die. I haven't decided whether the non-True race's even have
souls. The souls of races where the souls survive go to the Celestial
Sphere of the race.

A note about Dragons. They are extremely powerful creatures who use
Taelgar as an idealogical battleground for how to best prevent the
collapse of the new universe. Each "Age" is a battle between Dragons, but,
like the Illumanti, they can only manipulate pawns, not fight themselves.
Strict codes of conduct exist for these battles, and they last thousands
of years. Many of the political events on Taelgar are really part of one
dragon or another's scheming. No one in the world knows this however.
There have only been 2 dragon conflicts so far--the first was about
whether or not non-True races should exist (the side that thought they
should won) and the second was about whether or not Firstborn races should
exist (the side that thought they should won).

Back to magic.

If the source of power for the spell is the Celestial Spheres, there are
only two possible conduits for the power. One is a Firstborn, which can
channel magical energy to beings of their race. This creates the racial
magic of the non-human true races. It is varied in nature, but always
limited to a certain race. Basically, elven magic is the free manipulation
of magical energy, unlimited in scope; dwarven magic is the use of runes
inscribed in objects or drawn in the air; ogre magic a meditative magic
with effects similar to weather, or a cinematic martial arts style magic;
halfling magic is that of communication; and lizard man magic is that of
healing and the mind. If anyone wants more detail I can sketch some
further thoughts, but no one in my game is non-human, so this magic isn't
that well developed.

The other possible way to draw from the Celestial Spheres is via names.
This is a rather complicated an less well fleshed out system of drawing
power. I'll write up some thoughts in another email. I'm interested in
hearing about other people's name-based magic systems.

The other possible source of power in the Earthly Spheres. The Plane of
Spirit is drawn upon in very indiviual ways. In each case, the conduit is
usually deep faith or belief in something, or some connection to the world
of spirits. However, it varies greatly from indivual to indiviual. The
mechanism for control of the ritual is almost invariably meditation and
faith, however, although there are exceptions.

The Plane of Magic is drawn upon through Ley Lines, which are places where
the plane of magic touches Taelgar. A small percentage of humans are born
with the ability to see Ley Lines--these people can then learn to
manipulate the energy in a direct, as opposed to indirect, fashion. Those
who have not learned to manipulate the energy must rely on rituals to
manipulate it for them. Those who have learned how to manipulate the
energy can avoid time consuming rituals and quickly form the request
energy patterns to create the desired effect. This form of magic is by far
the most common form among humans.

I could write more about both the plane of magic system and the naming
system. Would anyone be interested? Does anyone have criticisms or
suggestions about the above material?

Okay. Some more detail. Knowing the True Name of a being gives power of
the being and everything that being created. But, only until the being
dies--the non-human True Races have True Names, but the true names loose
most of there power upon the death of the person. The True Name of God is
clearly not knowable--whoever knew that name would control everything.
Similarly, the true names of the Firstborn and the Dragons are unknowable.
This group of entities, however, also have Names of Power (loosely based
of Jewish tradition here). Invoking the name of power gives the invoker a
certain degree of control, over some aspect of the name-owner's creation.

have some control over a small aspect of creation. Furthermore, the True
Names of the dead do not lose all there power--they can be used to draw
the attention of the dead soul. This can be good, if the soul is likely to
help you, or bad if the soul is not. And some of the Names of Power of
either God or the Firstborn can control the souls of their creations, so
even if the soul is unlikely to help you, it is possible to bind it to
your will, so to speak. Hopefully this was somewhat coherent. The power of
names is still very fuzzy in my mind. Plus it is very very early in the

Okay. Here's what I think Sembara should be like:

History
-------

During the heyday of Drankorian power, the northern streches (from old
Arabor to the Wall, mostly] were organized into small provinces, ruled by
appointed counts. When Drankor fell, these counts generally took power
into themselves, and a large number of small kingdoms were created, out of
the people culturally Sembaran (which in turn was an ancient mix of
various Inner Sea cultures). The most notable two were the kingdom of
Velen, which had traditionally been the seat of the Drankorian governer
for most of the north (and was the only city of significant size--around 10,000
people--left in Sembara), and the kingdom of Souzal on the south coast of
the Inner Sea, where many refugees from further south had fled (although
the city of Souzal itself was not really a city, yet).

In this climate of constant power struggles, warfare, and raiding a great
man was born. He founded the city of Marsember, and later, the country of
Sembara. At his death, in 1642 T.A., Sembara was a loose alliance of
states, each with a indenpedent minded ruler. Many of the smaller kingdoms
had been wholely destroyed, and House Marsemb controlled much of the
kingdom of Souzal personally. But it wasn't until the time of his grandson
(around 1710 or so) that the last Baron stopped using the title King.

Okay, so by Derick III's time (say the 5th Sembaran dynasty) the throne
has gained a lot of power, and the signficant reforms made by Derick and
his predecessors last through the reign of Ancor II (a good king who made
one bad decision--more on this when I get to the civil war reasoning).

So government during the time of Ancor II (I'm not going to try to detail
who made what changes--I think a lot of them should be the result of
Derick III however):

First, the important groups:

1) The King. The King is a powerful monarch who directly controls the
Marsember penisula (which, along with Marsember itself, it the both the
royal demense, and some of the richest land in Sembara), and controls
through the control of various Baron's the rest of the land of Sembara.

2) The Baron's Court. The is the organization of the Barons, who are the
ten most important noble families in Sembara, and often control rather
directly significant tracts of land in Sembara.

3) The minor nobles. These nobles are not very influential on a national
scale, but play an important role in the local government.

4) The army. Derick III created a standing army, not composed of vassal's
or mercanaries, but rather paid soliders. The king is the Supreme
Commander of this army.

5) The navy. See the army. But the navy existed before Derick III.

6) The Rangers, and the Knightly Orders, etc. These play an interesting
role in Sembaran culture, etc, but don't tend to have much influence on
politics. Maybe more on them later.

7) The civil bueracracy. Headed by a council and such generally appointed
by the king, the growth in power of the bueracracy has been fairly recent
and at the expense of the power of the nobles.

Next, how things work:

On an national scale, the king controls fairly directly all foriegn
relations. Treaties and such can only be signed by him, the king is
completely in control of such matters. The civil bueracracy has begun
recently to expand to a foriegn service of sorts, but the civil bueracracy
is generally under the king's control--at least, he clearly has the power
to hire or fire anyone for any position within it, and that means it tends
to support him. Domestic policy is more complicated. The Barons
originally controlled their own land fairly completely: the owed military
service, some small one-time taxes (inheritance, mostly) to the king, and
loyalty and fealty to the king, and could pretty much whatever they
pleased within that framework. The lesser nobles owed similar things to
the baron's, and nothing to the king. In modern times, the duties and
rights of barons have changed somewhat:

Barons must pay a flat land tax to the king, either in coinage or goods,
depending on the size of the barony. This amount has been modified over
the years by various deals between Baron's and kings, but the Barons owe
taxes to the king. The Barons no longer owe military service, but they
are no longer allowed to have significant private armies. Many of the
one-time taxes have been elimanted, in favor of a yearly tax. The King is
always the ward of a underage Baron. The Barons must maintain royal
castles and roads on their own land--whether they change tolls is
immaterial to the king, as long as the roads are maintained. The Barons do
not have the right of High Justice.

The lesser nobles owe similar things to Barons--a typical Baron will
control about 25% of the land in his Barony directly, and the rest will
either be land owned freely by peasents, or land owned by a lesser noble.
In general, the lesser nobles control their local stretch of land, but the
cannot due very much. The can appoint magistrates and conduct low justice.
These nobles are the vassals of some Baron, and generally owe him
something--often a small land tax. The nobles collect rent from the
peasents who work on their land, and pay it to the Baron, who uses this
income to pay the king's tax. There is also a class of landed gentry, who
are not nobles but own significant amounts of land. They tend to mix
freely with the lesser nobles, and rent there land out to peasents and
such.

Cities are an exception to all of the above. Generally, the city controls
it's own land, and has powers based on some sort of city charter (which
only the king can grant). This is often similar to the powers of a baron,
although cities can invariably maintain miltia and such. It was from the
Marsember and Marstenport civic miltia's that Derick III built the
national army.

The military is under control of the king, and consists of all those who
wish to join. No lord may prevent someone on his land from joining the
military, unless that someone would be foreswearing an oath. The soliders
are payed wages and housed in barricks, and fight where and who the king
tells them to fight. They have largely replaced the old system of feudal
vassals, although many nobles still maintain fighting skills, and
tournaments and such are not unpopular. Although technically Barons are
not allowed to maintain private armies, many small house guards (from
lesser noble vassals) can be combined to form a significant force should
the need arise. However, the oaths of fealty no longer include a bit about
military service--that has been replaced with money. So no lesser noble is
required to provide troops for his lord. Which greatly limits the power of
the Barons, since few lesser nobles will risk the King's wrath.

The Knightly orders are generally formed from nobles, however some of them
allow anyone to join. They are generally loyal to the Order first--which
means in most cases, they would side with the king in (or avoid
altogether) a military conflict.

The civil beuracracy has recently been growing in power--even before
Derick, various councillors were invested with broad powers to investigate
certain happens or act in the King's name in certain matters. Derick began
an explosive expansion of the civil beuarcracy, and the Barons resent
this. The cb (civl bue...)  . . .
[actually, I don't like the idea of much of a cb at all, the more I think
about it. Certainly there are people in charge of certain things in the
