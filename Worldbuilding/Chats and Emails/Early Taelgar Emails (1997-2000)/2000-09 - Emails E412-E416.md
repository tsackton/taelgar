Okay, now for a brief summary of everything:

To start with, we have several rough outlines of ethnic types, that need further detail:
--Imperials: these are any group that was partially assimiliated by the Drankorian empire; need lots of fleshing out because few groups would have been totally assimilated, and so would have retained some of their old character
--Dunmari: these are the broad group of people that immigrated north after the fall of Drankor
--W. Northerners: these are the people that were indigenous to the northlands on the west side of the mountains; while they were never fully under Drankonian rule, they did come into contact with the empire
--E. Northerners: these are the northlanders on the east side of the mountains who had very little contact with the Drankorian empire
--Basques: a temporary name for the group of people that live on the mountainous coast in between Dunmar and the pennisula south of Chardon; never under Drankorian rule
--Desert people: the constellation of ethnicities that lived on the fringes of the desert during Drankorian rule, and eventually migrated north as that area fell apart

Now, basic ideas for polities:

Chardon -- pseudo-Athens/classical Greek model, with Taifa and Renaissance (particularly Italy) influences; ethnically homogenous imperial stock; competing city-state-like entities; Academy, etc

Sembara -- pseudo-medieval France model, with classical high fantasy influences; ethnically homogenous now, composed of E. Northerners and Imperials primarily, but blending has been extreme; monarchy

Dunmar -- pseudo-Rohan model, with Mongol and tribal influences, plus historical stuff you mentioned; Great King rules by power of horse selecting him; ethnically Dunmari; note presence of underground mystery cult

In the southern mountains, north of Dunmar, running towards Sembara and across to where it meets with Chardon in the west -- medieval Swiss model? ethnically modern W. Northerners (New Volkradians and other refugees?) with some Imperial stock, some E. Northerner stock though Arabor refugees

In the region between Dunmar and Chardon -- pseudo-Bjornling model? mix of Basques, Dunmari, Imperial; very sparse population

North of Chardon -- medieval German model with Taifa influences; Imperial and W. Northerner

Mountains on coast between Dunmar and Chardon -- Basques; linguistically different; need more detail

Around Inner Sea -- countries on the south coast tend to have a desert people influence, on the north coast a E. Northerner influence, with a strong imperial theme

How does that sound?  Too many, too few?  Many need a lot more fleshing out, and I'd like to look at the map for a little while (yes, I will be home on Sat but I'm not staying the night, so we couldn't spend too long).

## E414 — “Races of Taelgar”

- **From:** Tim Sackton

Here is something else to think about -- some ideas I've had about races.  I'd prefer to get the human picture sketched out in broad strokes before worrying about this too much, but the comments about hobgoblins in the last email inspired me.  Note that in Taelgarian terminology, race is approximately the same as species, and ethnicity or subrace is what we mean by race/ethnic group.

I'm going to organize this in a bunch of lists -- first, the races that an average educated person living in a relatively large urban center in Taelgar would have heard of, starring the ones that they _might_ have met.  In other words, things PCs would know about.  Then, a list of all races of significance.

Average Person:
--human, many ethnicities*
--halfling*
--hobgoblin, "tribal" subrace and "urban" subrace (basically divided into pawns of mind flayers ("tribal") and pawns of Cha'mutte ("urban"); the average person might have met an "urban" hobgoblin
--kenku (* in Sembara)
--orge (* in pseudo-Switzerland/New Volkrad, maybe in Chardon/Sembara)
--dwarf (* in Sembara, maybe Chardon)
--lizard man (* in Chardon, maybe Sembara)
--mythical: elf, giant, maybe mind flayers, maybe dragons, etc
Basically, the average person will know about the races listed here, and besides humans and halflings, may have met ~2 others.

All Races:
(* races are significant enough to play a factor, sometimes minor, in human politics)
(+ races actually care about human politics to some extent)
--firstborn races, in order: elves, orge*, dwarves* (and gnomes), lizard men*, halflings*+, (kenku*+)
--other races created by Aru/Melchior before time: humans*+, hobgoblins*+ (logic: hobgoblins are a mirror image of humans: whereas most humans are basically nice, most hobgoblins are basically nasty -- which is not to say they are constantly fighting or anything, since they have to get along to survive as a civ, but that they have a lot of relatively nasty tendencies like extreme arrogance and other such things -- also, hobgoblins are the only other race that has ethnicities and large variation within it -- all other races are either firstborn limited or too rare to have really any variation)
--races that exist, method of creation uncertain: mind flayers, goblins/orcs (two names for same thing), giants, liontaurs, Tayledras (essentially extinct)
--mythical/very rare creatures of intelligence known to exist: giant eagles, dragons, faeries

Any suggestions for additions, subtractions, rearrangements?  The big change is including hobgoblins in the category that humans fall into -- perhaps they should be down one category.  Races are actually in pretty good shape -- well, at least the firstborn races (except kenku) and humans are in pretty good shape.  The last two categories, and hobgoblins are very bare bones right now, but only hobgoblins are really important to the larger socio-political framework, the others are flavor (since they are so rare, or so localized to a particular region) and so do not need to be heavily worked on now.

Really, I think 1) compilation of firstborn race data, 2) serious fleshing out of hobgoblins and kenku (since our only model for kenku -- Aerin -- is obviously not a good one for the race as a whole, being the firstborn and all), and 3) a vague sense of how much variety there should be in the "creation uncertain" and "mythical/very rare" categories are the top priorities in races right now.

Any thoughts on these would be appreciated at your leisure.  :-)

Tim

## E416 — “Taelgar -- religion”

- **From:** Tim Sackton
- **Date:** September 28, 2000, 6:51 PM (archive timestamp; no Date header)
- **To:** Mike Sackton

I had a realization today that part of the reason religion hasn't really seemed to gel is probably because we're trying to force it into a pseudo-Western, Christian model that it doesn't want to fit into.  I think Buddhism would be the perfect model for the bulk of religion in the known areas of Taelgar.  Perhaps one of the things that could be very different about the South is a tendency towards more Christianized worship.

Outline/ideas (some good, some bad):
--primary religious personnel are monks and nuns
--no weekly religious service
--alms-rounds
--occasional large rituals in which most of the community participates
--distinction of practice between laity and monks
--introduce mystical elements, varieties of sects
--possiblity of including rebirth, karma-like concepts?
--if karma: devotion towards Aru would be seen as good karma, devotion to Melchior bad karma (but possible ways to subvert karma?)
--leaves open possibility of higher level monks recognizing that there is no difference between the two
--aspects become "compassionate Buddhas" - enlightened beings that help out the laity

The theology I think needs some serious work, especially to incoporate the concept of aspects, the firstborn, and the nature of Aru, Melchior, and Aru/Melchior.  These could, of course, be debates within the religous community in Taelgar.  However, the basic idea of a primarily monastery based clergy, dependent on lay practicioners, but without a weekly religious service, and with de-emphasized devotional aspects seems to fit really well with what we've worked out.  Eventually I'd like to flesh out in more detail different sects that emphasize different things, and perhaps typically regional variants as well; also, scriptures would not be in the model of one canonical, divinely revealed book, but rather the collections of teachings or something along those lines.  Plus, indigenous religious aspects that get incoporated in certain areas, places of different religions (Dunmar? "Basques"?).  Finally, parts of the South should have radically different religious ideology (heavy emphasis on devotional worship, perhaps, other differences).

I'll flesh it out in more detail later, of course -- but do you have any preliminary comments?
Particularly, do you have any interest at all having something vaguely related to past lives, rebirth, karma, etc?

Tim
