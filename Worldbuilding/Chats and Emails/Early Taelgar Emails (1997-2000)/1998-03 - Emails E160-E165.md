## E160 — “Re: Priests”

- **From:** Mike Sackton (Reply-To; From header absent)
- **Date:** March 24, 1998, 1:50 AM UTC
- **Cc:** Dave Kong, Eric Rosenbaum, Tim Sackton

> 	I like the way that we worked me being a priest- the whole thing
> with the reaction roll.

I agree here. That is how I am working it in my campaign, and I think
that works well as a game mechanic.

>The essential thing top understand is that all
> aspects will need to be approach and dealt with in different ways.  When I
> first started trying to get Vorshitaal to do stuff for me, I would offer
> him people I had killed- make blood sacrifices.

There should definetly be some amount of favor granted in exchange
for nothing but prayer.  I am allowing the paladin character to heal
people, by simply praying to Eru.

> That sort of thing just
> isn't going to work for the other aspects.  When I got to be more
> developed in my relationship with Vorshitaal, I gave up on the whole
> sacrifice thing, but other things took its place, "I'll do this for you if
> you do three things that I find funny."

This only works when you have a personal relationship with your God.
I don't think everyone should.

>That sort of thing took its
> place.  Direct sacrifice or offerings were replaced by deed which were
> done for the aspect.  And this, I think is the thing which should be
> stressed when dealing with priests who are adventurers- their reaction
> adjustments for trying to get something to happen will be based on their
> deeds as seen by their aspect.  If the aspect thinks that they have been
> doing good, the person will be rewareded with the result that they wish.
> If the peron has been straying from the tenets held by the aspect, then
> the aspects may admonish the practitioner.

What about people who simply worship Eru or Melkor? I don't think
that every priest needs to be a priest of an aspect.  Granted,
neither Eru nor Melkor can directly influence Taelgar, even to the
extent of granting divine favor.  But most of the people who worship
a god worship Eru or Melkor, and are in realitly worshiping an aspect
taloired to them, right?  Thus, the above starts to make less sense.

> 	One advantage of there being a relatively small amount of priests
> is that the aspects don't have to deal with thousands of people at once.
> Each aspect has a few hundred people to whom it has granted its favor.
> This is important, because, in practicality, aspects are essentially minor
> dieties, they aren't all that powerful, they have definite limitations to
> their power.

But what about priests of Eru or Melkor?

> 	Priests in cities, though few in number, wield great but limited
> power, they heal the sick, provide guidance and council to those who seek
> it.  Besides just the people who have divine favor, there are others who
> have given their lives into the service of eru (and melkor, though no one
> really admits this in civilized societies) these people provide council,
> advice, help through traumatic times and sort of screen the people who
> come through- if they have serious problems that need a priests' spells to
> fix, then these underling guys send the people on in to see the priest.
> In major cities, where there is a temple of eru, there is usually one
> priest and two priests in training.  These are the only people who have he
> divine favor advantage.  There are also one to two hundred people who act
> as support staff- clergy who don't have the ability to cast.

I disagree about Melkor.  I think he would be worshiped, or at least
briefly prayed to, whenever a loved one dies, whenever a their is
famine to get rid of, etc.  I like the basic idea of the organization
of the temple, but it feels to bueraractic.  I think that the priests
would be slightly greater in number (maybe 3-5 per city), and some
villages and random small towns would have a real honest to god
priest.  The religious, but non-priest folk would number in the 20-60
range, and would act as support staff, and would generally have first
aid, etc, and would help the sick, injured in mundane ways, as well
as give mundane counciling.  I don't really like the screening idea.
I think the priesthood should be based somewhat on the idea of the
Rohirrim and their horse---uncorruptable and inherently noble.

> 	The head priests wield incredible power- a very high level of
> divine favor, say 20 or even above.

Head priest? I'm not sure I like that idea either.  I don't think the
priesthood of Taelgar should have a hiearchy.  A small village priest
would have the same influence as a priest from the temple in Chardon,
provided both were equal in terms of ability.

>I like all of the
> theology as it stands now.  The pages and pages of it in the players guide
> could really stand to be condensed into a single account of creation
> (though it is nice to have the myth for the players).  basically, we need
> to get it organized and classified and all that shit.

Agreed. I think it is pretty close to final form, except for a true
account of creation, and creating mythology behind the
religion--creation myths for other races (maybe other cultures?),
work on holidays, work on the Scrolls of the Divine (for example,
many medieval names came from names in the Bible-- a similar effect
would be very cool in Taelgar).  But the reality behind the religion
in the cosmic, not cultural sense feels pretty complete to me.

> give me a call about it at 617 627 7751 and figure out where we're going
> to do it, and how we're going to work getting a ride and all that stuff.
> 	Ok, later.

Aerin did, but nontheless, I think that they should, from time to time,
speak directly with their gods.  Eru and melkor are very impersonal.  They

aspects.  For instance:  None of the kenku (or mockers) deny Eru in any

worships him, they all worship Vortishaal.  Simlarly, i would argue that
the halflings, and all sailors do the same for that aspect of the sea
thingy.  The elves and rangers for the aspect of the forest the list could
go on.  My point is that I think the idea of divine favor works much
better with a personal god- you have a reaction roll with the aspect you
worship- it's a personal relationship.

earth have branches, large branches, that emphasize your personal
relationship with god.  That's what it is really all about- a one to one
relationship, man to God, and I don't see why people on Taelgar who
concern themseleves very deeply with the aspects should have any different
