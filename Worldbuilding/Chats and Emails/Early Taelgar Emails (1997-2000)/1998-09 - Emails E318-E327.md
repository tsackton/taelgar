Legal system -- there is a central judiciary consisting of a certain number of judges selected by the Council for terms of life, sort of like the Supreme Court.  The main force of law is the regular judges.  Many things are based just on common law, some of which has been codified in a document.  Basically the important thing about Chardon law is that the judge hearing the case makes a decision based on his knowledge of the law, which can be appealed to the central judiciary in much the same manner as appealing to the Supreme Court.

As for the ruins, here's what I know to be true.  The land became barren sometime around the fall of the F.G.E..  It has something to do with a strange magical field, which is why the ley lines are odd.  It could have been many things, including the aftereffects of a magical battle, or of a plague drop site, or of a mage trying to draw too much power and killing himself, or something like that.  Something involving magic gone wrong.  Also, spells cast inside the barren area should do funny things sometimes.  Here's an idea that would work though, if you like it:  the first time Cha'mutte created the plague, instead of using the other dimension he used drop points.  That is, he magically gated the plague causing elements to certain designated locations, and let it spread from there.  The ruins could have been the focal point of a drop zone.  Also, about the ruins: The F.G.E. could have easily built extensive tunnel systems underneath, leading to food, water, secret exits, and all sorts of other things that might be useful.  These tunnels could now be used by some bad guy who is interested in recreating the plague for his own purposes.  That is, if you want to run a semi-traditional dungeon adventure for your players, which I think could be kind of cool.

Does that all make sense?

Any other questions?  If you have questions or things you want made up, I can usually find time to answer emails, but don't count on me to volunteer info that is not asked for -- I'm going to have a very, very busy semester.
