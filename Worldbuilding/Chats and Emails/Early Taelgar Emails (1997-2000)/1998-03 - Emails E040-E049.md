For the kenku, their telepathy is line of sight, they have to be able to
see the person they are communicating with.  This isn't so bad though, it

No, not saurians, I'll leave it to Kong to find a cool name, since he's
the name guy.

> and the kenku (kenku limited to
> gray man abilites-- (which are what, BTW?)).

Umm, gray man stuff.  Right.  See, the thing with gray man is that not all
kenku can do it.  In fact, very, every few can, there's probably under
fourty in the whole world.  The way you become a gray man is to undergo
this ritual ceremony thing, the details of which are a closely gaurded
secret (read:  the gm can make them up if anyone goes through it) in which
certain icons of vorshitaal are used to transform the person.  A couple of
things happen when you do this:  the gray man gets his conciousness in a
new and different spot on the astral plane.  The gray man also gets a
totally new body, a sort of proto-kenku with no true form.  See, most
kenku shapeshift and come back to their real form, most kenku find it
annoying to stay shapeshifted in one form that is not their own for more
than say a month or two.  But gray men have no true form, sure, they have
a few faces which they use out of habit, but they have no actuall form to
which they muyst return.  The form which they inhabit, as well as the
power to hide conciousnesses comes directly from eru- that's why it's not
discoverable by true or normal magical means- it's divine magic, like that
singing thing.  Right.  So that's gray men.

>  Lizard men would have
> traditional psionics, as well as "plane of conscious" KyrPowers(tm).
> Anything I left out?

>  What about psionics in humans?

um.  I say no psionics in humans.  None, no chance at all.

> Directions in
> which these races will progress?

Speaking for the kenku, the my pet race so to speak, I think that they are
gradually going to figure out how to use more and more of their psionic
capacity.  maybe learn how to communicate non-line of site, to set o
telepathic conversation wards and other stuff.  it must be stressed that
while kenku have tlelepathic powers _similar_ to halflings, they are not
the same.  it is much, much harder for kenku to learn to do this stuff.
Someone who spends a lifetime working on telepathy may be as good as your
average trained halfling, but the vast majority of kenku don;t hold a
finger to halflings.
Which reminds me, there are certain artifacts which allow kenku telepathy
to be modified.  The clan chiefs all have these icons in the shape of
eagle talons which allow them to communicate with any kenku.  The mocker
are also rumored to have that sort of thing.

have to say about all this stuff.  I kind of feel like I'm in your

## E047 — “Re: Taelgar”

- **From:** Tim Sackton
- **Date:** March 11, 1998, 7:56 PM (archive timestamp; no Date header)
- **To:** Mike Sackton, Dave Schwartz
- **Cc:** David Kong, Eric Rosenbaum

In response to Mike's questions about Chardon:

The key to understanding Chardon is understanding its history.  Chardon was initially just a road, connected the westernmost north-south trade road with the main body of the First Great Empire to the east.  As it's importance grew as a crossroads, a few buildings sprung up -- an inn or two, maybe a few farms to support the innkeeper, a blacksmith, a general store, not much more than a trade stop.  Virtually no permenant population, but lots of traffic.  A port grew out of this too, and it became an important stop on the sea routes too.  It grew into a small town, but it was still fairly far away from the center of the Empire.  It probably wouldn't have grown much bigger if not for a coicidence.  Many large nodes exist in the general vincity of the volcano.  When the Academy in Drankor decided to start a second branch, away from the bustle of everything where professors could go to take sabbiticals, do dangerous experiments, and so forth, Chardon seemed like the ideal location.  Lots of nodes available for use, extensive trade routes so that they could get anything they needed, but a small enough concentration of people so that they could get away from the pressures of crowded city living, and in particular the politics of Drankor.  So the (Second) Academy was built in Chardon.  Inevitably Chardon began to grow outward from the Academy.  However, it grew slowly, and mostly just as the Academy grew.  It also one of those places that the really rich build large estates in to go to when they want to get away from the city.  All this growth was taking place outward from the Academy.  At the same time, the port grew, and the few settlements around the main trade road became the outskirts of the Academy town.  Then, of course, the First Great Empire fell.  Chardon was far enough away not to be affected that much by the plague, so the population remained fairly stable.  Over the next 50 years or so, the Academy became a haven for knowledge (the original one having been destroyed, or at least rendered inaccessible, by the Plague).  Also, Chardon became a nucleus for population growth (kind of like a seed crystal).  Random wanderers following the roads would eventually end up in Chardon.  It started to grow, and eventually the Academy town merged with the old port town, and walls were built, mostly because at that time, Chardon was just barely not on the frontier.  In the past hundred years or so, Chardon experienced a renaissance.  As the areas to the north opened up, it became a major trade hub between Rohan and the north, and for that matter between Sembia and the north.  The old Academy town, which included the estates of First Great Empire nobles, became what Mike dubs "the rich district".  Although right near the Academy (to the west and northwest of it) it isn't rich so much as what could almost be described as a college town.  Lots of shops, places to eat, taverns, but very few inns.  The old port town is now known as the Old Port, and the area just south of it has become sort of an Artisian's district.  These two areas make up what Mike calls the Halfling Quarter, although it is by no means entirely halfling.  This is where the rich go when the want to go slumming (the real slums are too dangerous for them).  Its where most of the local culture is.  It is probably, in a way, the most interesting part of Chardon.  An analogy might be certain sections of Broadway in New York.  It certainly functions as a theater district.  In between this area of Chardon and the Academy/rich area are several things.  First, the governmental areas.  They line the western (oceanward) side of the Great Plaza, which borders on the old Academy town.  Second, to the south of the government buildings is a primarily upper middle class residential area.  A few shops, but mostly homes.  This is where a lot of the richer artisans live.  North of the government area, against the river, and where the main trade road enters from the north, are many, many, many inns.  On the other side of the river, you have the slums.  This is where the poor people live, the lucky ones in buildings, many on the street.  This area is pretty much ignored by the city government -- undesirables are thrown here to keep the city proper clean and pretty.  Mocker law is the rule here.  The areas lining the main roads are much nicer -- the city actually bothers with them -- but the further you go from either the road or the river, the worse the area gets.  Some parts of this area could be thought of as like Harlem or certain areas of the Bronx in NYC.  On the ocean is the New Port.  This area is less slummy, but it is like a fairly typical medieval port.  Lots of ruffians.  Lots of sailors.  Cheap inns, cheap alcohol, and cheap whores.  Again, mocker rule is pretty much the way here, and if you're a "pretty boy" don't hang around too long in New Port or you might get shanghaied into being a sailor.  Streetwise is definately the skill of the day here.  This is where the people who can't afford Old Port stay.  The docks are also overflow from Old Port, but the people who can afford it take there dingys over to Old Port to spend the night.  The city government was intiated a major campaign to clean up New Port, just when the war hit and their attention was forced elsewhere.  That's the main deal with the city itself.  One last thing -- government.  There is a mayor, who is the person officially in charge of the city.  He is a semi-elected position, in that it takes political clout more than anything else to get your name on the ballot, but once you do, its supposed to be a fair election.  I haven't decided if there are policies that are just ignored for getting your name on the ballot, or if there just aren't any policies.  There is also a legisative council.  Exactly what there powers are I haven't decided.  It is broken up into two parts -- the upper council, consisting of between 5 and 10 people, plus the major that is similar to the cabinet (there are also a few "secret" members of this council, who know one is supposed to know is on it, and a few truely secret members of this council who know one does know is on it -- an example of the latter would be our good friend the Traveller), and the lower council, consisting of a variable number of people, usually around 50 - 100.  All you need to become part of the lower council is a petition with enough signatures.  That's all I have for general government right now.  And some of it I'm not entirely sure of.  Anyway.  I have to do some real work now.  I'll do more taelgar stuff either tonight or tommorrow, hopefully.

-Tim
