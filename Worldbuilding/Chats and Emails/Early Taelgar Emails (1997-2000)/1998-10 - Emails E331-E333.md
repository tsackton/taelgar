# October 1998 — emails E331–E333

## E331 — “Re: Taelgar Stuff”

- **From:** David Schwartz
- **Date:** October 14, 1998, 11:30 PM EDT
- **To:** Mike Sackton
- **Cc:** Tim Sackton, Dave Kong, Eric Rosenbaum

Hey there yall.  I just put on Robin Hood, it's been sitting in a pile of
my cds unlistened-to for months and years.  Wow, it's so hard to think
that it's been more than a year since Taelgar ended.  This music is
brining back so many memories.  The smell of candle wax, Tim with his evil
little smile, Eric pondering the metaphysics of a multi-dimensional flat
universe, Kong being mysterious, and Mike's description of Beryl doing
forms with his staff in the firelight where we had camped one night.
The bookshelves behind Tim, the furnace that Kong would tell to shut up.
"Guys, it's ten of one, I _really_ have to go home now" steak and cheese,
chicken kabob, calzones.  "what kind of sub is that?" "uh. . . I don't
know, maybe "steak tip?"  peering out from my corner watching the rest of
you just as emerged in your characters as I was.
And I wonder how much of him is in me.  The music brings back memories,
cities on fire, frantic travel through the night, one piece of shattering
bad news after another.  Councils, realizations, when we finally would
figure things out, or, even better, when Tim would see how it all fit
together (far be it from him to just make things up) and would get really
excited and say, "oh wait, this is so cool."  And we'd ask what and he'd
just get real quiet with his evil smile.  Skelital armies fighting in
midair.  Assaults on citidels, chess, archmages and islands, armies and
emmisaries.  Death, then life, then death, again, oh wait, then life
again.  Tea-time chats with demi-gods.  Time travels, cities in the mist,
treasure rooms, ancient statues.  The vast caverns under the mountains,
empty.  Lava worms and contacts in dingy taverns.  negotiating for
poisons, for gems, for armies, for nations.

The music brings it back, and it was a hell of a time.

-dave

On Wed, 14 Oct 1998, David Schwartz wrote:

>The music brings it back, and it was a hell of a time.

It was at that. And, like many damn good stories, the sequel ain't as good
as the first one... Maybe there is something about playing in a lighted
dorm room, maybe its just the fact that it is hard to escape from college
life, I don't know. Maybe my expectations are just too high, or my
storytelling abilities not on par with Tim's :-). I dunno it just feels
like there is something lacking in my games. I don't want to blame my
players, they are cool people, my friends, but I don't see them sinking
into character the way we did. Becoming someone else. Someone with a
responsiblity for the world. Every time we played, I'd feel like there
was this crushing weight of responsiblity bearing down on me. Saving the
world is hard work, and Beryl felt it. And in some part of my mind, I knew
that it was all fake and that it didn't matter if we screwed up, but while
at that "table", it did, I think. Or something. I'm just rambling now.

And Taelgar's changed too. As much as Tim may not like it, there is just
no way I can consult with people on everything I make up. I'll eventually
have time to write out something talking about various things I've made
up, but I just don't right now. It's just not the same... But it was a
damn good story, and I'm glad its over, and I'm glad that it still lives
on.

If anyone wants to show up for a game, they take place on weekends,
generally Friday nights or Sunday afternoons, feel free. It'd be nice if
you told me though :-).

Oh, and if anyone wants to here about things I've been making up or what's
going on in the new campaign, let me know... if people bug me I'm more
inclinded to right it up. I have a midterm a week for the next 5 weeks
(including this one), but not a whole lot of other work, except a bit of
reading, and a problem set or two. So I can probably find the time...

If any of that made any sense at all :-)

-Mike

## E333 — “(no subject in export)”

- **From:** David S Kong
- **Date:** October 15, 1998, 6:01 AM EDT

All,

	Sometimes it's hard for me to actually believe that there was a time
when Rai and I were so close that the line between whatever realities we
perceived was ephemeral at best. I miss him, and Kyr, and Aerin, and Beryl, and
everything else. I suppose maybe it's because college hasn't worked out for me,
and that I realize now what friends and adventures I had and took for granted
in high school. It's so sad to me that it all seems so distant, and that where
I am is so far away from that freedom and imagination. We can make worlds-
so fantastic and comforting that maybe reality wouldn't be so bad, because at
least somewhere I would have the courage to do all of the things that I wanted
and dreamed of. At least somewhere I didn't resign myself or settle for what
everyone else settled for, because settling meant losing it all. Maybe there
were lessons about life in those castles in the sky and in those rolling
plains-because Rai was a hero. We were all heroes, and not because we killed a
dragon or some hobgoblins, but because we were fighting, godammit. I walk
through my life half-dead, barely able to recognize myself in the mirror
anymore. Sometimes when I wake up in the morning I wonder why that feeling of
everything just being right isn't there-why the fuck it is that I could give
two shits about the world around me. I remember when the Traveler asked all of
us why we were fighting, why it all mattered. And with every fiber of MY
being, I knew that it was because we were fighting for the ones we loved, for
our families, and for the families of those who couldn't fight for themselves.
I remember that feeling, so intense, that I was shaking. I don't suppose I
could tell you where Rai began and David Kong ended at that point-because for
once in my godamn life I was fighting for something I believed in. Even if
none of those things were real and if someday I woke up and it was all just a
faded memory, I would still find solace in a purple robe and a new skin-one
braver than my own. This is the first time in a while I've thought about Rai,
and I do it with some sadness and shame. I'd be letting him down, I suppose,
living life the way I do, barely awake to the people who love and care for me.
In retrospect I find that there was a lesson in it all, lost somewhere in the
tussle to save the world. Rai knew it and believed it, and when I think back
to that time I wonder if I will ever believe it as strongly as he did.
	Thank you all for changing my life, and giving me the chance to be the
hero. I suppose it all may seem melodramatic, but I speak with a gravity no
less than what Rai spoke of so long ago. He revelled each second and carried
his burden the best that he could, and that in the end was enough.

Dave
