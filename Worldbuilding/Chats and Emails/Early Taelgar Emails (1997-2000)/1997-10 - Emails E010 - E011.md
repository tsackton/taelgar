# October 1997 — emails E010–E011

## E010 — “Re: Taelgar”

- **From:** Tim Sackton
- **Date:** October 28, 1997, 3:41 PM (archive timestamp; no Date header)
- **To:** Dave Schwartz, Mike Sackton, Eric Rosenbaum
- **Cc:** Dave Kong

Okay guys, here it is.  My thoughts on Taelgar.  Mostly info on the current state of things, not real substance, but it's a start.  When you get a chance guys, give me feedback.

At 08:49 PM 10/26/97 -0500, Dave Schwartz wrote:

>
>
>Random Thoughts on Taelgar:
>
>    We need to determine which names are our own and which have been
>stolen from other worlds/ books, in order that for our final version we
>aren't running into copywrite stuff.

This is easy.  Pretty much all the original names of things are stolen.  Most names made up after the campaign got going are original.  I have lists of NPCs, and all the placenames are on the map, so it will be easy to figure out what is mine and what isn't.  When changing names, I'd like to make the names have some significance, or at least give each country consistent sounding names.  Part of this involves developing pseudolanguages for each country.  Anyone feel up to helping me with that?  The first thing to do would be to come up with linguistic evolutionary trees (based on real languages to make our lives easier), and then pseudolanguages that have a consistent feel.

>    In the meantime, we need to make/ find developed sections on:
>        The astral plane- rules description, functional uses

This is something that we need to spend some time writing down.  I've got pretty much nothing written, but I have some pretty good ideas as to how it works.  Near the end, Eric was getting to some good stuff with those maps of consiciounesses and stuff, although I'd like to make them a little less Freudian and more "fantastic."  The basic idea behind the astral plane is derived from a spirit world concept.  The biggest problem is that the idea of a concsiouness is very abstract, and power levels are very abstract too.  Eric, me and you will have to work on this a little.  Basically, if we try to codify exactly what Kyr could do, we can start writing real rules for the astral plane.  The other thing to remember though is astral plane manipulation is pretty much limited to powerful lizard men (or lizard men derivatives, like Kyr), so we don't need to worry about this so much at first (until the Taelgar: Lizard Men supplement comes out :-)  ).

>        True magic- rules, function

This is fortunately much better written up than the astral plane.  Kong and I actually made up a lot of rules, but we never really played by them that much, so they weren't that well play-tested.  Everyone take a look at the tentative (incomplete) stuff on the web page for true magic and tell me what you think so far, at least about the basic ideas behind the rules.  I think the core of the system is pretty sound (fatigue based, skill based, different sub-powers, weaves (speaking of which, I think we need a better name for weaves -- weaves are too Eye of the Worldish)), but it seems to me that the power levels and picky mechanics still need to be worked out.

>        Various races- packages, personality traits, where are they

I've actually got a lot of stuff on this, at least in my mind.  The concept of different races in Taelgar really started to solidify when you guys started looking for the firstborn.  In Taelgar, there are two kinds of life: created and evolved.  Created life conforms to Plato's *edios* concept (I think that's how you spell it), the idea that everything has a perfect form (the firstborn) and all members of the species are imperfect variants on the form.  Anything with a firstborn is created.  There is also a distinction between divine firstborn (elves, dwarves, halflings, lizard men, ogre) and non-divine firstborn (Aerin is the only current example).  Cha'mutte's basic argument was that everything should have been given the chance to evolve.  Puts it in a completely different light, doesn't it.  Unfortunately, his methods were a little draconian, to say the least.  Dragons don't think like mortals though.  To him, it probably seemed perfectly justified.  I have the packages for all the races in basically complete form somewhere.  I have a good idea of geographic range and personality of all the races too.  They might need a little more fleshing out, but for the most part, we have a good start here.

>        Rangers- who, what, where

Mike?  You're really the ranger expert.

>        Personalities- kings, dragons, etc.

Like races, I've got a lot of stuff on this.  I could write up good descriptions for most of the frequently appearing NPCs.  It's just something I have to sit down and do.

>        Marsember
>        Chardon
>        Rohan- roharic society, the king's horse
>        Points of Interest- the temple

In general places need to be developed more.  For the most part, all I have on various places is a sense of what they are like and maybe a map or some historical background jotted down in a notebook somewhere.

>        Ages- dragon warefare, ending an age

See above.  Applies to history too.  Plus, a lot of stuff needs to be reintepreted in light of dragons.

>        Eru/ Melkor- Aspects, religion

I've got a fair amount on Melkor and Eru themselves, but not on the sociological effects of religion.  Basically, it ended up as a fairly unimportant force.  I think there is a lot more that could be done with it.  Sects of Eru, competition between aspects, cultural tidbits like prayers to the aspect of luck, more variation in temple design, specific prayers, things like that.

>        The Dreamworld- how it works, rules, functional uses

This is currently very very very vague.  Eric, I think you had some ideas near the very end of the campaign, but I forget exactly what they were.  I know they're written down somewhere though.

>        Governments

See places.

>        Trade

Trading companies and currency could fall under this.

>        A set distance scale for the map, as nice as "one week's travel=
>one hand" is, we need something more exact.
>        Mockers

Schwartz, you're the leading Mocker expert.

In general, the things that were important to the campaign (races, major NPCs, true magic, Eru and Melkor, dragons, certain places and aspects of history) are very well fleshed out, but in turn things like trade, distances on the map, non-crucial places, specifics of governments other than Sembia, etc are not well fleshed out.  In most cases, I have a feel for what they are like, but I haven't put in the work.

Two big questions:

1) What is the fourth age like?  Obviously only I can answer this, but discussion might help me get some ideas.

2) In what time period do we want to set Taelgar when we type it up?  I'd vote for the beginning of the fourth age, which is what we'll almost definately end up doing (remember Taelgar is first and foremost my world, sorry guys, but it's true -- of course it couldn't exist without you guys, but I get to make the big executive decisions).  I'd like to here what you think though.

Okay, that was very long.  Comments would be greatly appreciated whenever you get around to it.

-Tim
