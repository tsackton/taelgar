I got home from the recording studio earlier than expected, and although I _should_ be writing my chem lab right now, I'm going to take some time to make a few changes here and there to what Mike said, and then make it official Taelgar stuff, assuming no one has any major complaints with anything I say. At 02:35 PM 3/14/98 +0000, Mike Sackton wrote:
>Taelgar Player's Guide
> General Knowledge of Religion, History, Geography, Politics, Races
> and Magic for New Players
>
>Religion:
>
>There are two gods: Eru and Melkor. There are also several entities
>called 'aspects'. These entities are things such as Vorshitaal, the
>"god" of thieves, or Mielikki, the "goddess" of nature.  If you
>imagine Eru and Melkor to have multiple personalities, aspects are
>specific personalities of Eru or Melkor. Some aspects are entirely
>composed of Melkor personalities, some entirely of Eru personalities,
>some a little of both.  Most humans tend to worship Eru, as opposed to
>aspects of Eru. The two mentioned above, plus Ulmo, the aspect of the
>ocean, are the only aspects that have any significant following.

Some comments.  First, all these names need to be changed.  I forget where Vorshitaal comes from, but Eru and Melkor are from Tolkien, Mielikki and Ulmo from Swedish? mythology.  The aspect of Luck is also important, as is the "earth mother" aspect (fertility, growth, and good harvest).  The aspect of oceans tends to cover weather, and travelers in general too.  Some other aspects go in and out of style according to the times.  The aspect of war is popular in times of war.  Melkor is not seen as evil, per se, in most cultures.  He is the necessary balance to Eru.  In times of hardship, he will often be appeased.  There are evil cults of Melkor, however.

>Religion is very understated. Worship is generally done at the home,
>by individuals in individual ways. There are temples to Eru, but not
>many.  Therefore the Church has little political power, because there
>is little in the way of organized religion. However, Eru is quite well
>respected by virtually everyone. Priests are not common, but they are
>holy. The consequence of this is that holy people are generally very
>hard to harm, since no one will be willing to do so.  However, because
>there are few benefits for being a priest other than respect, there is
>virtually no corruption within the priesthood.

This isn't strictly true.  Yes, in our campaign religion was downplayed.  However, the way it works is more like this: the organized priesthood is very small.  Since most people do tend to worship at home, only going to temple when they need solace or on important holidays (another thing that should be made up.  any ideas?), there is no need for lots of priests to "tend the flock," so to speak.  Therefore the only people who are allowed into the priesthood are those that have the spark, ie, can channel divine magic (how that works exactly is another thing that needs to be worked out -- I kind of liked the way we did it for Aerin, that is, roll a reaction roll and if you get a good enough reaction, ask for a favor and see if your god can grant it).  This makes the priesthood extremely small.  And the priests tend to be spread out over a great distance, one or two tending each temple, a few more in the really major ones (Chardon, Marsember, etc).  Since there isn't really a big organized Church, the political power of the priests tends to be small.  However, everyone listens to them, and they can weild great influence if necessary.  Sound good?  Schwartz, I'm particularly interested in your comments on this.

>There is a religious work: The Scrolls of the Divine.  This is similar
>to a modern day Bible, but it is passed almost entirely through oral
>tradition.  Although there are copies available in many major temples,
>they are not "required reading" of the religion, as the Bible is in
>modern day.

One correction here about the Scrolls of the Divine.  It was passed down through oral tradition until the Second Age, when it was transcribed by a priest on Hkar, and some new stuff was added.  It would definately be available in every temple, and probably sections of it are read by most people at some point or another, although for the most part the only people who read the whole thing are priests and scholars.

>Holy symbols: Mielikki: a crescent moon, a stylized tree (trunk and
>two branches), or a deer's antlers
>           Vorshitaal: unknown, at least by you
>           Ulmo:  breaking wave
>           Eru: simple star, the sun, owl
>           Melkor: a dark cloud, bleached bone

Vorshitaal: a stylized dagger, right Schwartz?  Speaking of which, what ever happened to Bloodtooth?
Eru: a circle with a cross in the middle, or sometimes a plain circle, or sometimes a circle with a cross in the middle, but the ends of the cross stick out a little bit.  Very occasionally a stylized serpant swallowing it's tail
Melkor: I don't like either of the ones you suggest, but I can't think of anything really good.  Ideas?

The creation myth (cut for brevity) might change a bit, but that's a good rough draft.  I don't think there are any major problems with it.

>History:
>
>First Age:  The Eldar Days. The time of the Elves. Cataclysmic wars
>end the Age and change the face of the continent.
>
>Second Age:  The time of the Dwarves and Ogre.  Great underground
>tunnels are built.  The Age ends with the Dwarf-Orc Wars, decimating
>the Dwarves and the Ogre.

For the second age, what stands is a good thing to tell your players, but what actually ended the age was the sinking of Hkar.

>Fourth Age:
>
>The 4th Age began in chaos. Some of the more important events of the
>early fourth age. (Current date: 103 F.A.) (all dates F.A.)
>
>1 The Red Plague spreads across Rohan, killing many.  Rohirrim king
>briefly recovers, but fails to prevent the spread of the Plague.
>Bralta I is chosen as the new king.

Rohirrim king recovered in time for the final battle, I think.  Taelisin got the horse, didn't he?  With Cha'mutte's death, the plague begins to spread again.  However, there is nothing keeping it from being dispersed by the wind.  (Cha'mutte had his limits, after all.  It was more important to him to use the plague as a barrier between Rohan and Sembia than to kill lots of people with it, so he expanded it to the largest size he could hold stable, and then stopped.)  After spreading in all directions for several weeks, it slowly begins to disperse.  It spreads into southern Sembia and eastern Rohan before beginning to blow away.  Both countries suffer heavy losses, although Rohan suffers more.  General panic ensues in Rohan.  By the middle of the first year after Cha'mutte's death, the plague has blown west, decimated Turmish and also further cutting off the north from the south, as all the existing trade stops are destroyed.  The Turmish government completely collapses.  Rohan can only maintain order west of the river.  The capital is relocated.  The plague, fortunately, missed Marsember, but barely.  Much of the population flees to the northern strongholds.

A side effect of the dispersal of the plague is toxic rain, as it gets into the upper atmosphere.  For the next 5 years, sporadic deaths, and a great deal of property destruction occurs due to this effect.

Also in this year, the hobgoblins that survived begin to attempt to spread in all directions.

The dwarves and ogre, outnumbered perhaps 15 to 1, fight a prolonged retreat over the course of several months, finally arriving back in the mountains exhausted, and suffering greatly.

The elves also fight a prolonged retreat, outnumbered maybe 50 to 1, although this doesn't bother them so much.  Many elves die, however, further reducing the population, almost below sustainable levels.

>3-17 The Hobgoblin Wars.  All of the major forces of hobgoblins are
>pushed North of the old Volkraad border. Pandar still overrun with
>hobgoblins and other nasties.

1-52 The Hobgoblin Wars.  The number of hobgoblins and other nasties in the north was greatly underestimated by all.  Cha'mutte had kept huge reserves in the other dimension, whom he had magically altered to be plague resistant.  The plan was to drop them in the plague on the borders of Sembia and Rohan as soon as the massive armies sent north by the "good guys" had been destroyed.  He died before he could do that.  After several days of not getting the "go-ahead" order, the hobgoblins decided to leave.  Perhaps 500,000 hungry, desparate hobgoblins, wanting territory for themselves, began fighting their way south.  For fifty years, wars raged across the north, fought primarily by Chardonians and those in the Rohirrim and Sembian armies who, because of the plague, no longer had families to go home to.  The kenku also played a major role in these battles.  However, the plague circulating in the upper atmosphere caused many problems for the kenku -- many died before the cause was figured out.  Eventually, at great cost, the hobgoblins were reduced in number sufficiently so they could not put up a fight.  They fled to the foothills of the mountains, some settling the northern areas of what used to be Volkraad, others settling in Pandar, or south along the mountains.  Everything north of Chardon is effectively abandoned to the wilderness.

>9 Kenku begin to move out of the mountains, into the old nation of
>Volkraad and Vasa. They make there homes on the edges of the
>mountains.

10-12 Kenku begin to establish Vasa as their homeland (primarily Vasa, since Volkraad is still overrun by hobgoblins).

>14 Chardon sends exploratory vessels northward, finding
>only wreckage where the country of Amn used to be.  No attempt to
>resettle is made.

This could also take the form of scouting during the war.  Either way, Amn is found to be totally ruined.

16 Current king of Rohan dies.  The new horse is born, but, in an unprecedented move, does not pick a king.  Instead, rides north and west, towards the area of Rohan that has been abanonded the longest.  Rohan sinks further into chaos.  Effectively stops existing as a country.

> 33 Derik III of Sembia dies. Derik IV takes the throne.

Derik III lives to the ripe old age of 74, and later becomes known as one of the great kings of Sembia.  His son is not named Derik IV, however.  I know I made up a name, but I forget it.

66 With the wars over, Chardon begins to grow.  It sends exploratory vessels southward, but they never return.

74 The horse of Rohan returns, rode by a young King.  He begins to gather people, and rebuild his country.  Eventually becomes known as one of the greatest kings of Rohan ever.

At 103 F.A., Sembia is beginning to return to its former prosperity, but much of its former glory is gone.  Most of the Sembian army did not return from the north -- a serious blow to Sembia.  Rohan is just starting to re-emerge as a country.  Turmish, Cormyr and Dhamara, for all intents and purposes, are wilderness.  Pirate raiders are becoming a problem on the Inner Sea, and barbarians attacks on Sembia are increasing.  The dwarves and the kenku have both offered aid in hunting down the problem at the source, presumably Turmish or Cormyr, but Sembia has not sufficiently recovered.  Vasa has been settled by kenku.  The dwarves are beginning to re-emerge from the mountains, in particular lending support to Sembia.  Chardon, however, has massively expanded.  It is now a country in its own right (borders undetermined until I can get my hands on my map, which is at home).  It is just beginning to start re-exploring the North.  Little has been heard from the elves, although several messengers have been dispatched.  Around 3 F.A. a message was recieved from the elves saying "we are in dire trouble.  send aid."  Unfortunately, at this point, no one was in any shape to send aid.  What has happened to the elves is a major mystery.  (I'll tell you guys now, they're not dead.  I don't know what happened to them, but they're around.)  I could keep going.  Does that give you some sort of idea what's happening?  I'm trying to give you several good campaign hooks (now I want to run a fourth age campaign too).  I'd like feedback on all this.  Does anyone think it's too harsh?

Obviously politics need to be modified to relfect this situation.

That's it for now.  I'll deal with the addendums in another email.  I'll also try to mop up remaining questions from earlier emails.

-Tim
