## E022 — “Kenku”

- **From:** David Schwartz
- **Date:** February 23, 1998, 8:53 PM EST
- **To:** Mike Sackton

Hmmm, good to know that ou are still alive, had my doubts for a moment
back there after the total radio silence.

Uh, kenku.  Well, they are these winged bird thingies.  No, wait, you
already know that part.

Ok, the meat of what I want to say.

Tim summarized kenku very well in the racial description:  Kenku are
sneaky, sly, deceitful users of people.  Kenkus are not fun and happy
people.  They are always on the lookout for their own best good.  They
don't really care about the means they use.  That really summarizes kenku
philosophy:  the means always justify the ends.  Which, incidentally, was
one of Aerin's big problems.  Since Cha'mutte's end was ok with him, it
was a question of the means which he used.  Since Aerin, as a typicall
kenku, didn't care about means, he fund it very hard to condemn the big
dragon 'o' doom.
	Really, the thing to stress is that kenku are not fun and happy
all the time.  They have abig superiiority complex (which they shouldn't)
lie whenever they feel it is right, and in general have no morals.  There
is some loyalty to the clan, and an even vaguer sense of loyalty to the
race as a whole, but nothing too signifigant.
	But this is really all just about kenku character traits, most
of which I am sure that you picked up on over the past four years.  What
you are probably more interested is the role that kneku play in the fourth
age.  Some of this I have thought through well and thoroughly, some of
this I am just sort of writing on the fly so here goes:
	Kenku have started to play a major role in world affairs.  The few
lasting things that managed to come out of the wars ending the third age
was that Kenku can be very useful.  So there is a sort of kenku UPS-
kenku messengers are employed by most trading companies, governments,
wealthy indiviuals and people who want a messenger.  This greatly
facilitates communication between everyone- races, governments and all the
rest of stuff like that.  The major centers of kenku population are vasa-
which is a 75% kenku nation, there is also a large population in volkraad
(or the former volkraad)  The othr major population center, and the center
of kenku operations- the kenku capital so to speak- is on the sight of the
former ulrich monestary.  The complex has been vastly enlarged, with the
addition of enormous residence areas, huge warehouses, and large training
grounds.  Probaly about 100,000 Kenku call the monestary home, although
only about half that number is actually present at any given time.  The
monestary is the center of the messenger service operations too.
	Other governments really aren't too fond of Kenku, there is this
sort of linger suspicion that one day, all the kenku are going to get
together and destroy the rest of the world.  I outlined Kenku world
domination to you last spring, but the truth of the matter is that the
kenku are much to disorganized to actually ever do something like that.
There is enough dispute between rival clans even without trying to put
together some sort of cohesive millitary campaign.  Plus, those who really
know what they are doing know that any sort of millitary campaign would
ultimately be a failure- kenku don't want most of the territory that
exists in the world.  Plus, everybody remembers what t was like to live in
slavery in Pandar, and nobody wants that to appen to any other race.
	Basically, in the fourth age kenku are essentially what they were
in the third age- just much more common- every city has kenku circling
above it, coming or going.  And the sight of a kenku on a city street
isn't really much cause for alarm.  Kenku are still mistrusted, but not to
the point of violence- a series of very bloody reprisals for the killing
of kenkus (along with much exadurated rumors of these killings) pretty
much stopped anti-kenku violence.  It is pretty much confined to whispers
of feared kenku plotting, and a general unease at the highest levels of
government that Kenku might do something rash.
	I don;t know if you are planning to do a little people campaign,
where nobody cares about high levels of government, or a big people
campaign, where that's what the story is.
	Anyhow, as for mockers.  Imagine the mockers like this:  all of
the mafia houses throughout the entire US start working together,
organizaing their activities and systematically go about their buisiness
together.  That is essentially what the mockers are.  At the highest
level, the mockers are run by three mind-linked kenku, essentially three
guys thinking as one mind.  There's a whole big old chain of command, with
plenty of people not relly knowing what thir position.  Nobody really
knows how big the organization is because the public doesn't realize that
the mockers are a continent wide organization- everyone likes to think
that their local mockers are either a) not the real thing- just people
using the name, or b) all that there is.  For instance, people living in
Chardon don;t think that the mockers extend outside the city.  Most
members of the organization (the lower level people) also don;t realize
the extent of the thing.  Because of the multiple wings- theivery,
assasination, robbery- people working one job will only hear rumors of the
people working another job- at a lower level, each wing of the mockers is
a separate organization, with different contacts, safe houses and fences.
	Also, nobody, *nobody* _NOBODY_ knows who the head of the mockers
is, so I am counting on you not to reveal this to your players under any
conditions, even if they are just curious after the campaign has ended.
It's a mocker thing- nobody knows.  The people who the three guys contact
are communicated with telepathically, so nobody really knows who they are,
just a voice in the head.
	Mockers run all crime.  Name a crime, and it was done by the
mockers.  There is however, some truth to rumors of different facgtinos in
the following sense:  There is a large degree of regional autonomy to the
mockers.  The guy who's essentially in charge of Marsember doesn;t really
give a damn about Chardon.  Not all of the mockers are Kenku, but a large
porportion are kenku, or are kenku who go around shape changed into humans
most of the time.  And if you are a Kenku, you re not nessesarily a
mocker- not every Kenku is one.  But the mockers have come to rely heaily
on the kenku informationnetwork, including the continental overflights,
which are another major kenku thingy.  The constant surveilance of
everything did not end with the end of the third age, quite to the
contrary, the flights continue with some regularity, allowing mockers to
track, among other things, troop movement, caravan movement and shipping.
	People at large know that a lot of kenku are thieves- that in
part contributes to the general mistrust of kenku, but most people simply
do not suspect or even imagine possible the size and number of the mocker
organization.
	I could go on and on about the mockers, but before I do, I would
be interested to know what informatgion you are specifically looking for,
ie, how many point characters?  What profession?  What race?  If someone
wants to play a kenku mocker, I'd be happy to supply lots of infomation at
whatever level they know to be the truth.
	Well, I really could go on and on about all this, I love th
mokcers and the kenku- they are sort of my Taelgar pet projects.  And it's
good to hear that you are still doing roleplaying- starting a campaign is
good news.  When do you guys play?  I would love to stop by some time and
play a mocker or a kenku for a session, or even make a guest appearance as
aerin.  It's been far too lon since I have been on Taelgar.

-Dave

## E026 — “Aerin”

- **From:** David Schwartz
- **Date:** February 24, 1998, 1:20 AM EST
- **To:** Mike Sackton
- **Cc:** Tim Sackton

In the aftermath of the final battle at the temple, events proceeded
slowly.  It was a hell of a fight, and all of us gave it everything we
had.  Aerin was really the only only of the four to come out of it with a
genuine corporeal form- not a constellation, or astral ethereal contruct
or statue.  As the three remaining of us stood in the temple, gazing at
the night sky, we made our farewells- each of us had places to go and
debts to honor.
I telepathically (switching to first person) contacted the mockers, and
explained that I was stepping down as head mocker, from that point on, it
would be run by the three mind-linked kenku who were really handling
everything anyway.  I contacted the kenku clan heads and explained I was
going on a trip, and to contact me when they figured out how to break
through telepathic barriers.  Then I set one up.
After parting with Rai and Kyr, I headed southwest back to Chardon, to
meet with the halfling elders and master telepathic communication.  I met
with all the other firstborn, and we talked long and deeply about what I
was and what that meant.  Then, when I felt I had gained everything I
could from the other races, I left that gathering place.
I spent years in retreat high in the mountains, fasting, communing with
Vorshitaal and perfecting the art of shapeshifting.  When I felt I had
learned all that I could from him, I left the mountains and wandered the
world.   travaled to the west, to see Volkraad as it was being rebuilt, I
traveled along the mountains, to see the newly rebuilt Ulrich Monestary.
I travelled south to the areas where the plague had existed, and witnessed
the rebirth of nations. I went west through Rohan and to
Chardon, meeting with the friends I had made there and talking with the
new halfling elder.
Then I flew across the ocean to the south, to the Island where Rai lives
(or lived) and spents months reading the histories and studying the nature
of the "gray man."
After that, a few decades into the new age, I woke up one summer morning
just as the sun came over the edge of the horizon.  I packed my few
possesions into a smack sack, tucked my walking stick between my wings,
and took off, flying upwards.  When the air started to go thin, I closed
my eyes and let the wind carry me where it would.  Northward across the
sea, back to the continent, and where the wind finally put me down, I
started to walk, in no particular direction, with no particular purpose,
just wandering the face of Taelgar, dealing with whatever comes my way.

And that is Aerin's new life- the life of an unmade firstborn, waling
Taelgar in search of adventure, new people and delving inside himself to
discover the history of the Kenku race.

----

Hope that clears things up for everybody.  Info on mockers in Chardon is
forthcoming.

-Dave
