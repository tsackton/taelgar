## Magic Rules, notes

Each “school” of magic (Chardon academy would be one, other examples could include Dunmari teaching lineages, something among the Khesan, any Sembaran mage schools, or just independent teaching lineages – but the Chardon academy school is the most prominent and the most related to ancient Hkar) has set of ritual magic skills, all of which are M/VH: ritual magic itself, which is the skill of casting rituals, thamaturgy, which is the skill that involves knowledge of the theory of magic, and mage sight, which is the skill that allows you to identify other rituals and other forms of magic. Ritual magic has a maximum level of Thamaturgy +2, and mage sight has a maximum level of Ritual magic +1. (Perhaps it would be better to use manuevers??) Various schools default to each other at certain levels, based upon the similarity between them – for example, two schools derived from Drankor would have a closer default that two schools derived from Hkar (but not Drankor), which would have a closer default that two schools that were completely independent and never copenetrated (which would probably have no default).

Additionally, each school is broken down into “colleges” of magic, that are the categories that spells themselves fall into. These are always based off of the true magic weave types: fire, earth, water, air, life, death, magic, body, mind, soul. In a typical system, the four elements types might default to each other, life and death might default to each other, body, mind, and soul might default to each other, and magic probably has no default. Each of these is another skill, but a different skill for each school. Each of these skills also defaults to ritual magic at some level. Finally, within each “college” there are the rituals themselves, each of which is a skill (or possibly a maneuver) that defaults off of the skill in the “college.”

Casting spells involves rolling against your skill in the ritual. Many spells cast will be rituals that are memorized – these have no penalty. Rituals which you have a written source for in front of you typically take longer, but get a bonus. Improvised rituals are extremely flexible, but typically are cast at a penalty off of a particular “college” skill. In order to learn a ritual, you typically need some base level of power and knowledge of some other rituals.

The ability to use magic in the first place is an advantage that must be bought at character creation – it cannot be learned, although it can be improved once in play. It is a 5 pt advantage to be a magic talent, which gives you no benefit other than the ability to learn spells. Additionally, there is a mage sense advantage, which represents levels of attunement to the Realm of Magic and ability to sense its presence in Taelgar. This is a 10 pt/level advantage, and is the bulk of the basis for a mage’s power. Typical journeyman mages of the Academy have this at level 2, mages 3, and the most powerful wizards as high as level 5. Some of the more difficult and powerful rituals require a certain level of attunment to cast spells. Your level of attunment is added to your IQ for the purpose of calculating your skill level with all magic related skills. Finally, you get an IQ+attunment level roll to detect magic, sense magic, or identify magic from the magical energy rather than the ritual elements (which is the identify magic skill above). Additionally, each level of attunement allows you to buy certain advantages, and gives you certain minor abilities. At level 1, you can cast rituals without a text, and you gain the ability to temporarily tie off rituals. At level 2, you can sense and utilize ley lines/nodes, and your ability to temporarily tie off rituals increases. So on at levels, 3, 4, and 5 (more detail to come).

Tieing off rituals can only be done on a ritual you know (not an improvised ritual), and it requires a material focus for the ritual (at least at low levels of attunment: 1-2). The number of rituals that you can maintain in this state is equal to at a minimum twice your attunement level, and can potentially be increased. Note that this counts in some ways like maintaining a spell – if you are knocked unconscious or your ability to utilize magic is cut off, the spell will dissipate or potentially go off.

Finally, certain schools of magic have non-generic abilities and skills that they teach their members upon reaching a certain level of attunement: these are typically the “secret society like” schools of magic, such as the Academy or the Dunmari underground.
