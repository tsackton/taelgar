# Dating Systems and the Chardonian Academy — June 1999

## Dating systems

- GM—First, Second, Third, Fourth Age (Third Age = 2244, Fourth Age currently 211)
- Chardonian dating: from founding of the Academy (946 T.A.), so current year is 1509
- Sembaran dating: from founding of Sembara (1649 T.A.), so current year is 806
- Dunmari dating: the traditional dating system of the old people (from a significant event in their distant past, occurring at 122 T.A.). Dunmar founded 1406, current year is 2333
- Drankorian dating: counted from founding of Drankor, taken as 213 T.A.; therefore end of T.A. was 2031, current date is 2242. Drankorian dating is, however, rarely if ever still used, as most dates were disrupted by the plague
- Kenku: date from Aerin, so 212
- Comor uses a recent date based on new independence, so 195
- Areas to the west tend to use Sembaran or Chardonian dating for convenience, as there is no clear founding date or anything
- South has a completely different system

## The Chardonian Academy

The Chardon Academy has a dual existence—as a place of learning and political power, and as a place of secret knowledge and magical teaching. It is the Inner Academy, the place of magical teaching, that we are concerned with.

A student entering the Academy will be watched carefully, and if he demonstrates ability of the right sort, the way to enter the Inner Academy is made known to the candidate, and should the candidate figure out the way (like the Master Doorkeeper on Roke), the candidate may enter—a ritual takes place, and the candidate is officially an Apprentice Mage of the Academy. Apprentice Mages stay at the Academy, learn, and do menial chores for a very long time, never really being taught anything magical directly, but learning a lot of history, literature, art, math, geology, natural history, and everything else that an “educated” person should know. They also have to do all the dishwashing, sweeping, cleaning, cooking, and everything else for the Inner Academy. Generally, students remain apprentices for at least 2–3 years, often more. The main point, of course, is to teach patience, respect for the importance of “menial” chores, and other things like that. After some time as an Apprentice, the mages let more and more magical teaching out, until by the 4th year usually as an Apprentice, the student is learning almost nothing but magical knowledge, and magical ethics, and “how to be a mage.” This includes such things as respect for the power of magic—magic is not to be used lightly, ever. Any time you channel magical energy, especially without ritual, you tinker with the balance of energy on Taelgar. And although this usually doesn't cause any problems, it is always something to keep in mind. This is not to say you should never use magic—just that it should not be used trivially. Eventually, when the Masters feel the Apprentice has matured enough, and learned a sufficient mastery of magic, there is a ceremony, and the student is promoted to Journeyman Mage.

As a Journeyman, the student is expected to embark on a journey to discover what it means to be a Master Mage. This can take many forms—some Journeymen choose study and contemplation, others physical journeys, or something else. Some Journeymen never achieve mastery; the rest, when they feel they are ready, and when they feel that they know what it means to be a Master Mage, return to the Academy to be tested, and take part in a ritual to become a Master Mage.
