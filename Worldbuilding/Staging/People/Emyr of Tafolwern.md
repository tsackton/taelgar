# Emyr of Tafolwern

Emyr of Tafolwern was a Tyrwinghan planar geographer and cosmological writer of the DR 1600s. He is best known for [[On Hidden Doors]], a polemic about temporary planar connections.

Emyr rejected some orthodox accounts of [[Echo Realms]] and argued that elemental vortexes, fey crossings, and similar events belong to a wider class of temporary planar connections shaped by planar drift.

%% Known facts
- Source: [[On Hidden Doors]].
- Emyr wrote [[On Hidden Doors]], a Tyrwinghan cosmological treatise from the DR 1600s.
- [[On Hidden Doors]] is described as a forceful polemic on temporary planar connections.
- Emyr argued that elemental vortexes are part of a broader class of temporary planar connections.
- He included uncontrolled or unpredictable fey crossings in that class.
- He argued that planar drift helps explain why permanent portals sometimes appear or disappear.
- Source: [[Elemental Vortex]].
- Emyr argued that [[Ishara Venn]]'s work on elemental vortexes did not go far enough.
- He associated temporary planar proximity with phenomena such as unexpected volcanoes, violent hurricanes, and strange fey melodies.
- Source: [[Research about Planar Connections]].
- Emyr described this class of connection as planar upwellings.
- The research note attributes three properties to Emyr's model: temporary or unpredictable nature, material passage between planes, and unstable boundaries with extraplanar influence.
- Source: [[Echo Realms]].
- Emyr criticized the idea that an Echo Realm requires a primary source plane and a secondary reflected plane.
- He argued for four primary realms: the [[Material Plane]], the [[Feywild]], the [[Plane of Souls]], and the [[Dreamworld]].
- He treated other so-called Echo Realms as limited in time, space, or both.
- Source: [[Dreamworld]].
- Emyr described the Dreamworld as the fundamental plane of imagination.
- Source: [[Shadowfolds]].
- Emyr proposed that the Shadowfolds are prisons for those who commit vast sins.
- Source: [[Planar Tides in Relation to the Dangers of the Plaguelands]].
- Harbek Ferrystone's planar tides work built on earlier work by scholars such as Emyr.
