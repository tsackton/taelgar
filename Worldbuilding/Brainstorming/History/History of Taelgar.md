# History of Taelgar

> [!warning] Work in progress — noncanonical
> This is a worldbuilding development document. Do not treat any part of it as established Taelgar canon unless it has been separately adopted into a canonical note.

## Overview
Taelgar's history is shaped by four great cataclysms. Each disrupted the world and gave rise to a different order:

* [[Riving|The Riving]], when time began
* [[The Downfall]], when [[Hkar]] sunk and the world was reshaped
* The [[First Plague]] and subsequent fall of the [[Drankorian Empire]]
* The [[Great War]] and death of [[Cha'mutte]]

This document is meant to be fully and minimally canonical. Ideas and explorations are in [[History of Taelgar - Non-Canonical Ideas]] and open questions are in [[History of Taelgar - Open Questions]].

History is more developed the closer to the present day it is, and some regions have seen more detailed development than others, so this document is necessarily uneven. 
## The Riving

The [[Riving]] was both an event and an act of creation: a moment and an eternity, through which linear time began and Taelgar became a fixed place. Its primordial setting and acts of creation belong to a mythic, transitory existence, not an ordinary sequence of before, during, and after.
### Primordial existence

The primordial world was unfixed: powerful beings could reshape reality with a thought or gesture, and neither time nor causality followed the patterns familiar to mortal minds. Even a single, stable reality was uncertain. This mythic, transitory existence was not an earlier version of later lands and kingdoms, and remains in some respects unknowable.

The [[Primordial Cosmos]] was simpler and less differentiated than the present [[Multiverse]]. Its actual form remains undefined: it may have comprised one realm or several, without the separations familiar in later cosmology. The [[Plane of Magic]] was intertwined with this world, but whether it existed as a distinct plane is not established.

### Creation and separation

Through the Riving, the unfixed primordial cosmos gave way to a world whose history could unfold through successive years. Taelgar became a fixed place, but its landscape was not immutable: later cataclysms )in particular the [[The Downfall|Downfall]] and the [[Great War]]) would reshape the lands themselves. The separation of the planes accompanied this creation, although the exact nature of the transition remains undefined.

During the Riving, the [[Divine Presence]] gave the [[Embodied Gods]] physical form, permanence, consciousness, and the power to create. They in turn created the [[Elves]], [[Dwarves]], [[Halflings]], [[Lizardfolk]], and [[Stoneborn]], although whether this was in the moment of the Riving or after the beginning of linear time is not established, nor is it clear if all of these creations were simultaneous.

### Surviving echoes

Echoes of ancient power survive in the later world. [[Dragons]] and [[Giants]] retain a connection to the extraordinary magic of the primordial cosmos, although whether they are descendants, transformed survivors, or creations of its powers remains undefined. The giants' ancient history includes mastery of weaving magic and reality together, rune-supported skyships, and artifacts of unparalleled beauty and power, although whether this is a story of the [[Primordial Cosmos]] is not clearly established.

Some [[Fey|fey]], and perhaps elementals, may likewise preserve a continuity with the magical powers of primordial existence. The legends and stories of these beings, along with those of giants and dragons, may preserve fragments of the universe before the Riving, although their relationship to primordial reality is not clearly understood.

Fragments of the Riving's ancient power also survive in the world. The [[Crown of Purity]] and [[Heartroot]] are known examples; other remnants may survive in forms not yet recognized.

### Canonical facts

* There were no mortals before the Riving. Primordial beings were highly magical, with an undefined variety of forms, including at least some predecessor form of [[Dragons]] and [[Giants]].
* These primordial beings were not gods in the sense of the later [[Embodied Gods]] or [[Incorporeal Gods]]. The [[Divine Presence]] and [[Void Mind]] belong to a different, primordial category; both came to be in some fashion, but their origins remain undefined, and pre-date the Riving
* The [[Energy Realms]] were created in some way by the Riving. This connection does not establish a creation sequence for every plane of the present Multiverse.
* The [[Istaros]] took form during the separation of the planes. The [[Heartroot]], created as a byproduct of that separation, sustained a fertile ribbon along the upper river through the ensuing ages.
* In an ancient war, the forces of chaos were defeated and exiled to [[Limbo]]. Whether this occurred before time began or during the Riving, and the identity of their opponents, remain unknown.

## Before Humanity

*CY 1 – circa CY 1550*

The first years of linear time were an age of learning, peace, and growth. The [[Elves]], [[Dwarves]], [[Halflings]], [[Lizardfolk]], and [[Stoneborn]], created by the [[Embodied Gods]], developed their societies in a world that was broadly safe and secure. Vast nonhuman civilizations grew, and creation itself receded into distant memory. Whatever the larger opposition between the [[Divine Presence]] and [[Void Mind]], it did not dominate their lives throughout this period; danger was small and isolated rather than pervasive.

Among the great civilizations of these years were the dwarven realms beneath Labkhan, the elven realm of [[Alcarinque|Alcarinquë]], and the lizardfolk empire of [[Urtzabala]]. Each was a vast single-species empire, but they were not necessarily the only great realms of their peoples. Their existence does not exclude other empires, independent settlements, or communities elsewhere where different peoples lived together.

Under the [[Sentinel Range|Labkhan]], a great dwarven civilization grew. Delving and craftsmanship shaped its underground realms: great halls, worked stone and metal, and the magic of runes. Dwarven traditions remember the first dwarves made from solid stone deep beneath the mountains, and the [[Bahrazel]] making their mountain homes.

The great elven realm of [[Alcarinque|Alcarinquë]] occupied vast northern forests across the [[Istaros Watershed]] and [[Hara Basin]], reaching the [[Mostreve Hills]] and the Cymean foothills. It was a realm of ancient forest magic, whose surviving character can still be felt in [[Orenlas]], where the great dreaming trees echo the song of [[Aldanor]]. Orenlas survives as a remnant of this ancient realm. Alcarinquë's possible extension as far west as [[Ainumarya]] remains undetermined.

The lizardfolk empire of [[Urtzabala]], the Great Reed Sea, was a civilization of vast wetlands and many large communities. Its swamps occupied the area of the present [[Western Gulf]] and neighboring lowlands, including lands that later became the [[Enst]] and [[Wistel]] valleys. [[Ozabal]] and [[Latazaro]] survive as remnants of those ancient swamps. Their full extent is undetermined, and they may have been very large, stretching as far east as present day [[Medju]]. Urtzabala was not necessarily the only major lizardfolk settlement or realm.

Halflings were widespread in the early world. Their ancient histories survive in songs and shared memory rather than a single dated chronicle. Their tales remember wandering when the world was new, when songs could sing sustenance into existence, and later encounters with humanity as another change in a continuing history of journeys. Many halflings claim to have been the first to sail the [[Green Sea]].

The stoneborn, created by [[Entamba]], also belonged to this early world, though little of their history in these years is established. The extent of the early civilizations, their relationships with other nonhuman peoples, and the histories of lands beyond them remain uncertain.

Humanity entered a world with a long history already behind it. Its arrival did not end an age of stagnation, nor did it necessarily mark an immediate transition from peace to conflict.

### Canonical facts

* [[Hobgoblins]] were created by the [[Void Mind]] at the same time as humanity or later -- not before. Their exact creation date remains undetermined.
* [[Thark]] came to be by the machinations of the [[Void Mind]] and creates the [[Orcs]] -- whether this was during the Riving, or later is not established

## Humanity and the Downfall

*Circa CY 1550 – CY 4122*

### Humanity on Hkar

The [[Divine Presence]] created humans on [[Hkar]], humanity's first homeland, endowed with the [[Divine Spark]]: the power through which human belief could create and sustain the [[Incorporeal Gods]]. Humanity was something new, but entered a world already old with nonhuman civilizations. Its early life on Hkar was marked by rapid growth, a rich experience of miracles, and experimentation in belief and practice. Over many generations, human communities and religious traditions became more varied. The details of those changes, and the pace at which they unfolded, remain open.

Human religious traditions changed over this long era. The ancestors of the Northerners carried older traditions of small gods and intercession; later emigrants carried pantheonic religions, while Hkar itself developed the urban culture that would eventually give rise to Drankor.

The [[Chainshroud]] belongs to this early human history on Hkar, although its place in the religious sequence is not precisely dated. Its followers preached slavery and domination, empowering [[Sarqon]], a god of slavery. The cult was defeated and Sarqon imprisoned by the Divine Presence, preventing gods of slavery from arising again.

The circumstances and timing of humanity's first contact with the older peoples remain undefined, including whether any humans had already left Hkar. The cultures that eventually left Hkar carried different languages and religious traditions, and developed distinct histories in their new homes.

### Migrations into an older world

The earliest major departures from Hkar were those of the peoples later called [[Northerners]]. Over time they settled along the western coast of Taelgar, spread northward on the western side of the [[Sentinel Range|Sentinels]], and eventually crossed the mountains and spread east into the northern highlands and forests. These departures need not have been one brief, uniform wave. The migrants were settlers rather than an enduring extension of Hkar: sustained contact with their ancestral homeland dwindled, and their cultures developed independently. Their descendants include the [[Deno'qai]], older highland strands of the [[Zimka]], and the [[Vargaldi]], although the later forms of those cultures should not all be assumed to have existed at the time of migration.

The Northerners carried traditions of small, local gods who interceded with the Divine Presence. Their languages gave rise to the family later called [[Languages#Northros Language Family|Northros]]. At this point, the forests of [[Alcarinque|Alcarinquë]], the dwarven realms beneath Labkhan, and the lizardfolk wetlands of [[Urtzabala]] remained major centers of civilization. Human settlement did not simply replace them: much of the early northern settlement lay on the margins of the older nonhuman realms.

The southern migration was another major departure from Hkar, after pantheonic religion had begun to develop there. The migrants' cultures and histories remain largely undefined. Other, smaller departures also occurred, including the migration of the ancestors of the Dunmari; their departure and early settlement cannot yet be assigned a precise place in this chronology.

The [[Mariners]] undertook another great migration in the years before the Downfall. They settled first around the [[Eastern Green Sea]] and then spread westward along its shores. Their seafaring culture carried the ancestor of the [[Languages#Eastros Language Family|Eastros languages]] and a pantheonic religion distinct from [[Mos Numena]]. Cultural and religious divisions among their descendants produced different traditions across the Green Sea; the [[Skaer]] descend from these ancient migrants, but how they emerged as a distinct people is not established.

In the lands later called [[Greater Sembara]], northern migrants arriving over the mountains encountered mariners arriving from the east. These lands were still dominated by dwarves in the Sentinels, lizardfolk in the great wetlands of the Enst and Wistel lowlands, and elves in the forests south of the later Mostreve Hills. [[Tyrwingha|Tyrwinghans]] also belonged to the region's early human history, but do not record their lineage. At [[Tollen]], a mariner settlement was founded in CY 3902, before the Downfall; its ancient founding provides one of the few fixed dates within this long era of migration.

### Change on Hkar and the approach of war

While emigrants established new homes, Hkar continued to change. By the eve of the Downfall, the island had a highly magical urban culture, and worship of the [[Mos Numena Pantheon|Eight Divines]] had become the dominant pantheonic tradition among its urban elite. The emigrant cultures were not simply copies of this later Hkar: the older northern traditions and the Mariners' distinct religious inheritance had already followed their own paths.

The approach of the Downfall Wars was not a clean break from peace into open conflict. For a long time, ordinary life and the growth of human and nonhuman societies continued under an increasing sense of danger. What different peoples foresaw, and when they acted on it, remains unsettled.

### The Downfall

The [[Downfall Wars]] shattered the older world. Human and elven chronicles remember extraplanar invasion and desperate alliances, with conflict reaching nearly every land. The wars' campaigns, beginning, and full duration remain undefined. In CY 4122 they culminated in [[The Downfall]]: Hkar sank, and much of Taelgar was reshaped.

The landscape of the older civilizations was transformed. Alcarinquë's forests were devastated; [[Orenlas]] preserves a surviving remnant, while the later Drankorian heartland grew in lands once occupied by the elven realm. The Downfall also reached the Green Sea, upending the world of the early settlers of Tollen. The once-vast wetlands of [[Urtzabala]] survive only in remnants such as [[Ozabal]] and [[Latazaro]], although their disappearance cannot yet be assigned a precise date or process.

Humanity's island homeland was lost, but many descendants of earlier emigrants already lived beyond it. The catastrophe reshaped lands and seas, without a settled account of the fate of every people around the Green Sea or farther south and east.

Reliefs and prayers on the [[Circular Island]] preserve the escape of Hkaran refugees on an island that walked through the sea, guided by priests, wizards, and the Eight Divines. Refugees from Hkar subsequently founded [[Drankor]] near the mouth of the Istaros in CY 4133. The founding belongs to the next era, but marks the immediate transition from the lost homeland to the civilization that inherited much of its magic and religion.
