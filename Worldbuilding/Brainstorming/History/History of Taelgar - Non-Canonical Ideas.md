# History of Taelgar - Non-Canonical Ideas

> [!warning] Work in progress — noncanonical
> This is a worldbuilding development document. Do not treat any part of it as established Taelgar canon unless it has been separately adopted into a canonical note.

This document collects developed, noncanonical candidate histories, restated here as self-contained accounts. They may offer mutually exclusive alternatives to one another, but none should contradict established canon. Source citations are preserved in hidden comments; the accounts do not require those source documents to remain in place.
## The Riving

These proposals concern the primordial setting and the Riving together. Their explanations of origins and transformations are not an established sequence in linear time; distinctions between primordial existence and creation during the Riving remain unresolved.
### Collective origin of the fey

Rather than being created by primordial archfey, the diverse fey could have emerged from the transformation of a primordial essence during the Riving. This essence, understood by later fey as something recognizably fey, became many kinds of creatures of widely varying power. A collective origin associated with the Riving is established, but transformation from a single primordial essence remains a proposed mechanism.

### Planes as containment

Some planes could have arisen through the containment of powerful primordial forces during the Riving. In this proposal, the Divine Presence created Limbo as a prison for chaos, while an order-associated realm—provisionally called Mechanus—served a comparable purpose for order. This same idea could extend to some other potential planes ([[Arborea]], in one telling).

### A possible planar separation account

In this possible account, the primordial cosmos was an unformed sea of unfettered magical power. During the Riving, the Divine Presence tore reality apart, separating raw magical and creative energy from the Material Plane and limiting the power to reshape the world.

In this account, the Ethereal Plane stretched between the Material Plane and Plane of Magic, while the newly created Land of the Dead separated the material/soul world from the Plane of Creation and raw divine energy. The separation unleashed energy that even the Divine Presence could not fully control; its continuing resonances fueled the Echo Realms.

The proposal distinguishes three kinds of planar origin:

- The Material Plane, Plane of Magic, Plane of Creation, Astral Plane, and Ethereal Plane existed in some form before the Riving; the transitive planes were subsequently stretched or reshaped.
- The Energy Realms, Plane of Souls, Land of the Dead, and Echo Realms arose during the Riving in recognizable forms. Echo Realms might subsequently collapse, merge, grow, or split.
- Spiritual Realms arose during or after the Riving, each with its own creation story, spun from soulstuff and the Plane of Creation by Embodied Gods, later Incorporeal Gods, and humanity's primal beliefs.

This is a proposed mechanism and chronology, not established canon. The primordial cosmos was simpler than the present Multiverse, but its actual number and arrangement of planes remain undefined. The Energy Realms' connection to the Riving and the intertwining of primordial magic with reality are established; the specific roster of primordial planes and the rest of this separation mechanism are not.

## Before Humanity

The following candidate accounts are noncanonical. They need not form one combined history; alternative models are identified explicitly.

### Humanity's creation opens the door

The Void Mind is inactive or unable to intervene in Taelgar throughout the period before humanity. Humanity's creation opens the door to its influence, beginning a conflict whose danger is not immediately recognized and which ends with the Downfall. Thark and the hobgoblins may arise around humanity's creation through this opening.

This candidate is incompatible with an account in which the Void Mind is already actively intervening before humanity.


### Dwarven expansion beneath and beyond the Sentinels

Dwarves awaken beneath the Sentinels, building great halls and delving deeper as their population grows. Their underground kingdoms gradually span the range while dwarves remain little seen outside it. By the time Hkar is raised, they begin moving into new mountains in the east, west, and north.


### Stoneborn retreats and the transmission of craft

Early stoneborn live in secluded retreats in high peaks, the frozen north, and deserts. Their influence reaches other peoples through masters of art and craft trained by them, even while their communities remain apart from the great realms.

### Two great elven realms

Two great elven realms dominate the early world: Alcarinquë in the north and Tinwerquë in the south. Tinwerquë extends through tropical and subtropical forests south of the interior savannas, potentially from sea to sea. Elven wisdom, long lives, and magic underpin their political predominance.


### Alternative models of contact around the Green Sea

**Model A — limited contact zones.** Substantial interaction is concentrated chiefly in three areas: dwarf–elf contact in the southern Sentinels, elf–lizardfolk contact around Tyrwingha and Isingue, and halfling–lizardfolk contact along the Green Sea coasts. Lizardfolk inhabit coastal swamps from north of the later desert to the Sembaran lowlands, while widespread halflings are especially concentrated around the Green Sea, southeastern coast, and interior plains.

**Model B — civilizations converging near Urlich Pass.** The Green Sea reaches farther inland, allowing major nonhuman civilizations to meet near the later Urlich Pass. This convergence is connected to a site associated with the creation of the Firstborn, where the final battle with Cha'mutte would much later take place.

These models are not necessarily mutually exclusive: convergence near Urlich Pass could coexist with other contact zones. The inland sea could belong to this period or to later pre-Downfall history.


## Humanity and the Downfall

These developed candidate histories are drawn from existing material. They are not new inventions, and need not form one combined account. The older Three Despairs hierarchy, settled divine-realm creation sequence, and dwarven Sundering are not reinstated by these proposals.

### From personal protectors to pantheons

In this proposed history, early humans manifest personal divine protectors, physically present while their creators live. At death, these protectors accompany their creators' souls, but cannot continue acting in Taelgar in the same way. Charismatic leaders then gain strength by persuading others to worship their personal gods. Continued devotion after a leader's death, including ancestor worship, gives rise to more enduring small gods tied to families, places, and communities.

Contact with other peoples introduces new magical knowledge and a conception of divinity beyond the small local protector. Human cultures develop diverse pantheons. Among Hkar's increasingly urban population, similar gods are gradually understood as aspects of shared archetypes; this religious consolidation becomes Mos Numena. The broad existence of earlier small-gods traditions and later pantheons is established, but these mechanisms and their precise sequence are not. This account does not require human gods to create or inhabit separate divine realms.

### First contact and the northern migrations

Three accounts remain possible. In the first, nonhumans discover Hkar before the main northern departures. Contact brings new magic and ways of understanding divinity; the ensuing upheaval helps drive emigration and a transition away from personal deities. Northern migrants carry the last traditions of that earlier religious phase into the mainland.

In the second account, humans begin leaving Hkar before contact. Northern settlements and their small-god traditions develop for some time while Hkar remains undiscovered by other peoples; contact and its changes to Hkar come later.

In a third account, departures begin as a trickle before nonhumans discover Hkar, then grow into a larger migration after contact. Some northern communities could thus predate contact while others leave amid the religious and magical changes that follow it. None of these accounts requires a single date, cause, or uniform culture for the northern migrations. Contact and migration remain undated beyond their broad placement between humanity's creation and the Downfall.

### Southern colonies and a continuing Hkaran trading world

In this proposed history, the southern migrants settle the central and southern mainland as Hkaran colonists rather than settlers cut off from their homeland. They retain trade and contact with Hkar, grow rich through exchange with nonhuman peoples, and develop powerful mainland societies. Their early pantheonic religions would distinguish them from the northern small-gods cultures without making them copies of later Mos Numena Hkar.

The southern migration itself is established; this settlement pattern, continued contact, colonial organization, wealth, and political power are not. Sustained trade with Hkar is one possible history, not a requirement: whether these migrants maintained contact with their homeland remains unresolved.

### An arcane hegemony on late Hkar

In one proposed account, after the Mariner Exodus Hkar becomes dominated by an educated urban elite, allied with elves known as the peronar who guide human magical development. Arcane experimentation creates new spells and increases magical power, while Mos Numena becomes the dominant religion. Smaller, culturally cohesive groups continue to emigrate in disagreement with this growing urban and magical order.

The magical urban culture and Mos Numena's importance before the Downfall are established in broad outline. The peronar's role on Hkar, specific inventions, the causes of later departures, and a distinct dated hegemony remain noncanonical.

### Skaer refugees from a Mariner schism

In this possible account, a group of eastern Mariners breaks with others over religion and sails west in search of a quieter place to live. Their communities become the ancestors of the Skaer. The timing and nature of the schism, the migrants' precise route, and whether they were already recognizably Skaer remain undefined. Mariner ancestry is established; this particular reason for their departure is not.

### Elven preparations that outlive their warning

In this proposed history, Elmerca warns the elves of a coming darkness without revealing precisely what threatens them. The elven empires begin preparing for war, but no visible danger materializes. As the years pass, the groups devoted to those warnings lose influence, even though the premonitions continue. A small, isolated group—provisionally called the Wardens of Elmerca—keeps warning of the danger while elven life moves on around it.

Elmerca's association with foresight is established. This particular warning, the preparations and their decline, and the Wardens' name, organization, and founding date remain unsettled.


### The defense and destruction of Hkar

One developed explanation places something of the Divine Presence's own power within Hkar at humanity's creation, strengthening the human Divine Spark. During the Downfall Wars, the Void Mind's forces discover that capturing and corrupting it could destroy humanity's creative power and weaken the Divine Presence. The defenders prevent immediate capture, but cannot remove the ultimate danger. The Divine Presence withdraws that power, sinking Hkar and ending the threat at the cost of the homeland.

In this account, the defenders' sacrifice and Hkar's destruction are both necessary: the homeland could not be saved, but the defense prevents its corruption before it can be destroyed. The withdrawal also explains a greater divine distance and more difficult god-creation afterward. These are proposed causes and metaphysical consequences, not established meanings of the Downfall.
