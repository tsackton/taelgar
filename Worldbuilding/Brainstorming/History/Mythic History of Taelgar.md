# Mythic History of Taelgar

> [!warning] Work in progress — noncanonical
> This is a worldbuilding development document. Do not treat any part of it as established Taelgar canon unless it has been separately adopted into a canonical note.

The age before the [[The Downfall|Downfall]] is known more clearly in its broad movements than in a sequence of dated events. This account establishes the shape and character of that age without making its unsettled peoples, migrations, and conflicts more definite than they are. Specific events with established dates and lasting consequences belong in [[History of Taelgar]] and the histories of the places and peoples involved.

## Work Plan

In my view, the best way to do this is to focus on improving specific key pages that capture core concepts in Mythic History, and then provide a word-light, link-heavy overview in a page like this. The core pages are:

One thing missing from the list is any place to capture discussions of "watchful guardians": the [[~Wardens of Elmerca~]] or the [[~Cult of the Warrior~]] for example. These might be best handled as properly developed tentative pages.

The idea is not to try to invent everything - or even anything - about what is canonically true about these places, but rather to frame like the current Riving page and a lot of the Cosmology pages. Invent what modern Taelgarians know, and provide DM Notes categorizing what is and is not canon. The other goal is to try to have a more interlinked web of pages than a single long narrative, while still maintaining a link-dense page that allows easy reading of the related pages.

This may potentially expand, although it depends on the creative energy and desire for invention: in particular, writing the Riving page raises a number of questions around creation stories, and the primoridial cosmos asks questions about how giants, fey, elementals, and dragons, in particular, might remember or recall or talk about the older, pre-Riving order. There are also some fundamental questions about the [[Divine Presence]] and the [[Void Mind]]: this project does not intend to answer them, but it might update those pages to be more explicit about ideas both canonical and not, and to capture questions, and perhaps a bit of what modern Taelgarians would know of these things.

## Purpose of Mythic History

There is no particular goal to develop a well-established 4000 year history of events that occurred during this long period before "developed" history began. The [[The Downfall|Downfall]] was a destructive cataclysm that overturned the world, and plausibly destroyed populations, memories, and records. The mythic history here serves to establish several important aspects of future history:

* The development of new religions after the Downfall is, if not impossible, very rare. Most or all religions on Taelgar should have a firm origin in this mythic era
* Humanity originated on Hkar: all human cultures on Taelgar should eventually have an explicable origin within the migrations from Hkar
* All non-human races (with potentially very rare exceptions) originated before the Downfall, and so this mythic history gives a home for origin stories
* Sometimes, it is useful to have a very ancient source of something (i.e. the [[Binding Stones]]), and this mythic framework provides a home for these events

This does not mean no events in this period are canonical -- the founding of Tollen is a clear canonical example -- but this document is focused on the broad strokes of deep history, rather than specific dates. 
## The Riving

The [[Riving]] was both an event and an act of creation: a moment and an eternity through which linear time began and Taelgar became a fixed place. It marks the [[Multiverse#The Creation of the Multiverse|transition]] from the [[Primordial Cosmos]] to the [[Multiverse]]; from unsettled time to linear time. What came before cannot be dated, and although events could still happen, for many events the distinction between happening "during" the Riving and "before" the Riving is not clear. In the [[Primordial Cosmos]], powerful beings could reshape reality with a thought or gesture; neither time nor causality followed the patterns familiar to mortal minds, and even the existence of one stable reality was uncertain. 

Little can be said about the specific nature of the [[Primordial Cosmos]] or what the [[Riving]] really was, but a few core truths are recorded here:

* The [[Primordial Cosmos]] was inhabited by powerful beings that could reshape reality with a thought or a gesture; these creatures were neither mortals nor gods. These beings were highly magical, and with an undefined variety of forms, including at least some predecessor form of [[Dragons]] and [[Giants]], as well as representatives of primordial forces such as chaos, order, growth, and death, among others
* The [[Material Plane]] was not part of the [[Primordial Cosmos]]. In a fundamental sense, there was no geography or fixed places
* Fragments of the [[Riving]] survive as powerful magic in Taelgar: the [[Crown of Purity]] and the [[Heartroot]] being two notable examples, and the [[Oracle of Hope]] a possible third
* Although [[Dragons]] and [[Giants]] are the only creatures with canonical predecessors before the Riving, it is likely that some other creatures, in particular [[Fey]] and [[Elementals]] have some relationship to the primordial beings, and the tales of these, and other stranger creatures, may contain echoes of the [[Primordial Cosmos]]
* [[Aerin]] visited this period via time travel, and in some fashion, this experience caused or created the kenku; although this should not be taken to imply the kenku existed before the Riving established the [[Material Plane]] nor necessarily imply that the kenku existed from the moment of the Riving

The origins of the [[Primordial Cosmos]], the [[Divine Presence]] and [[Void Mind]], and the stories of how each of the planes of the [[Multiverse]] came to be are primarily concerns of [[Cosmology]] rather than history, and are not explored or documented here. 

The [[Riving]] marked a transition, and when the transition was completed, a new universe existed. The transition included:

* The [[Multiverse#The Creation of the Multiverse|creation]] of the [[Multiverse]], and the separation of the planes
* The creation of the [[Material Plane]] and the initial fixed reality of Taelgar: the continents and oceans, mountains, and forests
* The creation of the [[Embodied Gods]], and their subsequent creation of [[Elves]], [[Dwarves]], [[Halflings]], [[Stoneborn]], and [[Lizardfolk]]
#### Source of Religions
The Riving is canonically the source of the gods and religion of the Firstborn species:
* [[Aldanor]] and [[Elmerca]] of the [[Elves]]
* the [[Bahrazel|Bahrâzel]] of the [[Dwarves]]
* the [[First Ones]] of the [[Halflings]]
* [[Entamba]] of the [[Stoneborn]]
* the [[Tharzen Anzinakoa]] of the [[Lizardfolk]]

It cannot be the source of any human religions, but could be the source of other divine-like, worshipable entities if needed.
#### Source of Species
The Riving is canonically the creation moment of the [[Elves]], [[Dwarves]], [[Halflings]], [[Stoneborn]], and [[Lizardfolk]], who were each created in some fashion by their gods. It is a rich source of creation for other species as well, especially strongly magic species. It is likely that [[Dragons]], [[Giants]], [[Fey]], and [[Elementals]] were all created during the Riving, and numerous other species could have been created in this momentous reshaping of the cosmos.
#### Source of Events
Very few events should be placed during this period. The lack of linear time makes it difficult to describe and pinpoint specific events, and events that occurred in this period should have a strongly mythic and magical flavor. The Riving is a good source of powerful magic for unique artifacts, but should be used sparingly.

## The long age without humanity

The first long age of linear time was one of learning, peace, and growth. The peoples created by the Embodied Gods developed in a world that was broadly safe and secure. Great civilizations arose, and the acts of creation receded into distant memory. Whatever opposition existed between the Divine Presence and the [[Void Mind]], it did not dominate the lives of these peoples throughout the age. Peace did not require every land to be free of danger.

Three vast, single-species realms give some shape to this world:
* Beneath the [[Sentinel Range|Labkhan]], dwarves built halls and kingdoms of worked stone, metal, and rune magic.
* The elves of [[Alcarinque|Alcarinquë]] inhabited the great northern forests; [[Orenlas]] preserves something of its ancient forest magic.
* The lizardfolk of [[Urtzabala]], the Great Reed Sea, lived in vast wetlands around the lands of the present [[Western Gulf]]. [[Ozabal]] and [[Latazaro]] are remnants of those swamps, whose full ancient extent remains unknown.

These empires are not meant to be the only empires, nor exclude the possibility of multi-species realms, but they are documented to give something of the flavor of this period, and because there are modern survivals of these ancient places.

## Humanity, belief, and dispersal

The Divine Presence created [[Humans|humanity]] on [[Hkar]], endowed with the [[Divine Spark]]: human belief could create and sustain the [[Incorporeal Gods]]. Early humanity grew rapidly in a world rich with miracles. Belief and practice were varied and experimental, and over many generations human communities and religious traditions became more diverse. The development of particular kinds of gods or rites need not be reduced to a single settled sequence.

Humanity eventually encountered the older peoples, but neither the first encounter nor its relationship to the earliest departures from Hkar is fixed. The first major emigrants were the peoples later called [[Northerners]]. They settled along the western coast, spread north along the [[Sentinel Range|Sentinels]], and eventually crossed into the northern highlands and forests. Their descendants preserved traditions of small local gods who interceded with the Divine Presence. These migrations need not have been one brief or uniform movement; their communities developed with little sustained contact with Hkar.

Other departures carried different inheritances into the wider world. A southern migration took place, though its destinations and histories remain largely undefined. Smaller migrations included the ancestors of the Dunmari. The [[Mariners]] settled first around the [[Eastern Green Sea]] and spread west along its shores, carrying a pantheonic tradition distinct from [[Mos Numena]]. Their descendants developed different cultures and religions; the [[Skaer]] are among those descended from Mariners, without a fixed account of how they became a distinct people. Across Taelgar, migrants encountered older civilizations and one another. Their descendants were not simply displaced copies of Hkar.

Meanwhile, Hkar itself continued to change. By the eve of the Downfall, it had a highly magical urban culture, and worship of the [[Mos Numena Pantheon|Eight Divines]] had become dominant among its urban elite. The older northern traditions, the Mariners' religious inheritance, and the diversity of other migrants show that no single late Hkaran culture stands for all early humanity.

## Gathering danger and the Downfall

The passage from the long age of growth into the [[Downfall Wars]] was not a sudden change from peace to open war. Ordinary life and the development of human and nonhuman societies continued as danger grew. Where that danger first appeared, what different peoples understood of it, and when they acted remain unsettled. The breadth of the coming conflict should not be projected backward onto every earlier generation.

The wars eventually became all-consuming. Human and elven chronicles remember extraplanar invasion, desperate alliances, and fighting that reached nearly every land. They culminated in [[The Downfall]]: [[Hkar]] sank and much of Taelgar was reshaped. [[Alcarinque|Alcarinquë's]] forests were devastated. The wetlands of [[Urtzabala]] survive in fragments, though neither their disappearance nor every change to the older world can be assigned a single settled cause or date. The fate of distant lands and many peoples remains obscure.

The Downfall brought this lost age to a close, but it did not erase every part of it. Human cultures already existed beyond Hkar; older peoples, places, beliefs, and powers survived in a changed world. Their later histories belong to the more securely dated [[History of Taelgar]], while their deeper origins often retain the uncertainty of this account.

## Established deep-history facts

These facts matter to the ancient world but cannot all be placed confidently within the four movements above.

- [[Thark]] came to be through the machinations of the Void Mind and created the [[Orcs]]. Whether this occurred during the Riving or later is not established.
- Cracks to the [[Far Realms]] appeared, and [[Ithilids]] and other creatures entered the deeper and more isolated parts of Taelgar. Whether these incursions began during the Riving or later is not established.
- The Void Mind created [[Hobgoblins]] no earlier than humanity's creation. Their exact creation date remains undetermined.
