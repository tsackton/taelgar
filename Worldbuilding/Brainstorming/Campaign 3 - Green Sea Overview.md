# Campaign 3 — Green Sea Overview

Player-facing brainstorming for a brief introduction to the Green Sea, with the Western Gulf, Tollen, and Sembara peripheral to the overview.

## Northern and Eastern Green Sea — Writing Reference

### Vostok

- **Overview:** Picture river mouths, scattered villages, and long stretches of wooded shore, with boats doing the work that roads do elsewhere.
- **People/Settlements:** Small communities cluster around chiefs and clans, with most people living near the coast or lower Kem and plenty of empty country between them.
- **Climate/Biome:** The coast is cool and forested, with slow springs and winter snow broken by thaws, while the inland plateaus are much drier and bitterly cold.

### Skaerhem

- **Overview:** This is volcanic fishing-island country, where rough crossings, rocky shores, rich seas, and the possibility of a lava flow shape everyday life.
- **People/Settlements:** Small villages gather around good harbors and geothermal warmth, organized into close communal groups that share work, boats, and devotion to [[Kaikkea]].
- **Climate/Biome:** Expect cool summers, wind and salt spray on exposed shores, and cold winters whose snowfall varies sharply between islands.

### Great Northern Forest Coast

- **Overview:** This should feel like the edge of an enormous wilderness: rocky shores, few safe places to put in, and forest stretching far inland.
- **People/Settlements:** Coastal inhabitants and settlements remain unspecified.
- **Climate/Biome:** Cold maritime shores give way to spruce and larch forest, bogs, and willow flats, with long winters, muddy spring thaws, and short summers.

### Ursk

- **Overview:** Think tightly controlled wizard-state: foreign ships arrive for valuable trade, but the docks are only a narrow opening into a closed, isolated society.
- **People/Settlements:** Foreign merchants are confined to designated docks and warehouses at ports such as [[Praznitsky]] and [[Zakat]], trading for furs, monster parts, and prized dragonhide without being allowed into the cities beyond.
- **Climate/Biome:** Snowy winters and muddy thaws give way to warm summers in the grain-growing valleys, while the coast stays cooler and wetter and the northern country becomes taiga.

### Northeast of Ursk
*Intentional blank, including the far northeastern peninsula.*

- **Predicted climate/biome:** Expect long cold winters and short summers, probably with boreal forest giving way to tundra farther north and colder, more exposed country at higher elevations.

### Eastern Isles

- **Overview:** This is a whole world of islands, strange weather, difficult passages, and sailors’ stories of land moving between voyages.
- **People/Settlements:** The clearest existing image is the [[Vermillion Isles]], where kenku build towns high on red sea cliffs and smaller human communities occupy sheltered valleys below.
- **Climate/Biome:** The chain stretches from cold, stormy northern waters toward warm southern seas, with rainy slopes and dry sheltered shores creating sharp contrasts even before local magic enters the picture.

## Cymea and Irrla — Writing Reference

### Cymea

- **Overview:** Think faded maritime grandeur: crumbling coastal cities, a long Drankorian heritage, accomplished sailors, and fleets that no longer command the sea.
- **People/Settlements:** Mostly human port cities and river towns trade, squabble, and make alliances with their neighbors, with no single power holding the peninsula together.
- **Climate/Biome:** Expect mild winters and warm to hot summers, with humid sea air and thunderstorms feeding wetter hills above sunnier, drier sheltered valleys.

### Irrla

- **Overview:** Picture a huge, sparsely settled island with treacherous shoals along its northern and western shores, and steep mountains, deep valleys, and occasional volcanoes making the interior difficult to cross.
- **People/Settlements:** The human [[Republic of Svolhas]] holds the fertile southeast behind a frontier of castles, hobgoblin pirate clans occupy the eastern tip, and the whole western shore remains undeveloped in terms of peoples and settlements.
- **Climate/Biome:** Think warm, humid summers and cool rainy winters, with lush forests on the exposed western slopes, sunnier woodland and open country in sheltered eastern valleys, and snow in the high mountains.

## South Cymea — Layout and Terrain Brainstorming

South Cymea could have two or three named cultures with broad, recognizable vibes, leaving plenty of room for other communities and player-created places. Their territories need not form tidy blocks, and the transition into Cymea proper remains open.

### Basic Layout

- **Northern estate country, oriented inland:** Old cotton estates, great houses turned into fortified communities, tenant villages, family rivalries, and local rulers whose grand inherited titles outlast the territory they once governed.
- **Middle coast:** Prosperous stone towns, sheltered courtyards, fishing villages, and busy harbors, where old local families have connections across the Green Sea and every town takes pride in its own ways.
- **Southern imperial society, just west of the mountains:** A living successor to Drankor, with a crowded capital, elaborate courts, public ceremonies, scholars, and officials who believe they still serve an empire with a future.

The middle coast could share a recognizable culture across independent towns, with boatyards, gardens, small farms, and coastal markets around the larger ports. Family connections, hospitality, and local reputation offer useful starting points for characters. Coastal towns could buy cotton from the inland estates, weave cloth, arrange shipping, and supply imported goods, tying the two regions together without making them the same society. This coastal culture could extend north beside the estates as well as occupy the country between them and the southern imperial center.

### Cotton Country and Proposed Terrain

The current physical model makes cotton plausible in the warm northern South Cymean lowlands: hot summers, relatively mild winters, maritime moisture, and sunny breaks between showers and thunderstorms. The estates fit lower plains and river valleys; cooler uplands would carry other crops or woodland. Irrigation could supplement rainfall in drier districts, while wet harvest weather remains a concern.

A proposed terrain addition would strengthen this picture:

- **A coastal ridge or belt of substantial hills** would intercept some moist sea winds, giving the inland plains relatively sunnier and drier conditions where the airflow crosses the ridge.
- **A river fed by wetter uplands** would supply water for irrigation during dry spells and connect the estate country to the coast through a gap in the hills.
- **Broad, gently sloping river terraces** would offer deep agricultural soils above the regularly flooded ground, with enough drainage to avoid waterlogging the cotton.

Picture wooded coastal hills, rivers cutting through toward the sea, and wide agricultural plains opening inland: estate houses above the fields, roads and irrigation channels crossing the plain, and trading towns near the river outlets. The ridge's orientation and height still need placement on the map; it would shelter the plains from some weather, not guarantee a dry autumn or block storms from every direction.

## Names

### Passage Islands

**Passage Islands** is the Common name for the archipelago off Cymea: one large offshore island, with a mix of large and small islands between it and the mainland. The name comes from the sailing passage between the mainland and the large island, giving the whole group a practical geographic name with a little maritime atmosphere.

The islands' people, settlements, and cultures are intentionally left open to develop together with the players; they need not be an extension of mainland Cymea.

### Largest Island — Cymean Name Options

The largest island needs a Cymean exonym; its local name can wait. These ten options remain unchosen, and the reasons describe sounds and possible associations rather than established translations.

1. **Alveira** (al-VAY-rah) — a softer Portuguese sound, geographically neutral.
2. **Talvera** (tal-VEH-rah) — a sturdy Spanish sound, substantial and easy to say.
3. **Cadrava** (kah-DRAH-vah) — harder consonants give it an old-chart, weathered feel.
4. **Montara** (mon-TAH-rah) — suggests high land, if the island is named from its skyline.
5. **Seravela** (seh-rah-VEH-lah) — a flowing maritime sound, with an echo of *vela* for a possible sailing association.
6. **Vardessa** (var-DESS-ah) — slightly Italian in sound, with enough weight for a major island.
7. **Orvessa** (or-VESS-ah) — compact and distinctive, with little implied meaning to constrain later invention.
8. **Caldria** (KAL-dree-ah) — a clipped, older sound that could survive language changes on charts.
9. **Ravona** (rah-VOH-nah) — rounded, simple, and easy to use at the table.
10. **Valdora** (val-DOR-ah) — suggests valleys and a gentler, more pastoral mood, if that direction appeals.

## Possible Shape of a Brief Overview

1. **Life on a connected sea.** Open with voyages, trade, seasonal departures, and the relationships between distant ports. Establish the sea as a place people live and travel.
2. **Different shores, different lives.** Offer a handful of short contrasts: Skaer volcanic islands; Cymean coastal cities; guarded northern ports; mountainous Irrla; the red cliffs and kenku towns of the eastern islands; and Medju's spice markets.
3. **Magic, danger, and rumor.** Close with uncertain channels, stories of wandering islands, piracy, and the difference between familiar home waters and distant places known through travelers' tales.
