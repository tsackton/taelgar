# Campaign 3 — Green Sea Overview

Player-facing brainstorming for a brief introduction to the Green Sea, with the Western Gulf, Tollen, and Sembara peripheral to the overview.

## Northern and Eastern Green Sea — Writing Reference

### Vostok

- **Overview:** Picture river mouths, scattered villages, and long stretches of wooded shore, with boats doing the work that roads do elsewhere.
- **People/Settlements:** Small communities cluster around chiefs and clans, with most people living near the coast or lower Kem and plenty of empty country between them.
- **Climate/Biome:** The coast is cool and forested, with slow springs and winter snow broken by thaws, while the inland plateaus are much drier and bitterly cold.

### Skaerhem

- **Overview:** This is volcanic fishing-island country, where rough crossings, rocky shores, rich seas, and the possibility of a lava flow shape everyday life.
- **People/Settlements:** Small villages gather around good harbors and geothermal warmth, organized into close communal groups that share work, boats, and devotion to [[Kaikkea]].
- **Climate/Biome:** Expect cool summers, wind and salt spray on exposed shores, and cold winters whose snowfall varies sharply between islands.

### Great Northern Forest Coast

- **Overview:** This should feel like the edge of an enormous wilderness: rocky shores, few safe places to put in, and forest stretching far inland.
- **People/Settlements:** Coastal inhabitants and settlements remain unspecified.
- **Climate/Biome:** Cold maritime shores give way to spruce and larch forest, bogs, and willow flats, with long winters, muddy spring thaws, and short summers.

### Ursk

- **Overview:** Think tightly controlled wizard-state: foreign ships arrive for valuable trade, but the docks are only a narrow opening into a closed, isolated society.
- **People/Settlements:** Foreign merchants are confined to designated docks and warehouses at ports such as [[Praznitsky]] and [[Zakat]], trading for furs, monster parts, and prized dragonhide without being allowed into the cities beyond.
- **Climate/Biome:** Snowy winters and muddy thaws give way to warm summers in the grain-growing valleys, while the coast stays cooler and wetter and the northern country becomes taiga.

### Northeast of Ursk
*Intentional blank, including the far northeastern peninsula.*

- **Predicted climate/biome:** Expect long cold winters and short summers, probably with boreal forest giving way to tundra farther north and colder, more exposed country at higher elevations.

### Eastern Isles

- **Overview:** This is a whole world of islands, strange weather, difficult passages, and sailors’ stories of land moving between voyages.
- **People/Settlements:** The clearest existing image is the [[Vermillion Isles]], where kenku build towns high on red sea cliffs and smaller human communities occupy sheltered valleys below.
- **Climate/Biome:** The chain stretches from cold, stormy northern waters toward warm southern seas, with rainy slopes and dry sheltered shores creating sharp contrasts even before local magic enters the picture.

## Cymea and Irrla — Writing Reference

### Cymea

- **Overview:** Think faded maritime grandeur: crumbling coastal cities, a long Drankorian heritage, accomplished sailors, and fleets that no longer command the sea.
- **People/Settlements:** Mostly human port cities and river towns trade, squabble, and make alliances with their neighbors, with no single power holding the peninsula together.
- **Climate/Biome:** Expect mild winters and warm to hot summers, with humid sea air and thunderstorms feeding wetter hills above sunnier, drier sheltered valleys.

### Irrla

- **Overview:** Picture a huge, sparsely settled island with treacherous shoals along its northern and western shores, and steep mountains, deep valleys, and occasional volcanoes making the interior difficult to cross.
- **People/Settlements:** The human [[Republic of Svolhas]] holds the fertile southeast behind a frontier of castles, hobgoblin pirate clans occupy the eastern tip, and the whole western shore remains undeveloped in terms of peoples and settlements.
- **Climate/Biome:** Think warm, humid summers and cool rainy winters, with lush forests on the exposed western slopes, sunnier woodland and open country in sheltered eastern valleys, and snow in the high mountains.

## South Cymea — Layout and Terrain Brainstorming

South Cymea could have two or three named cultures with broad, recognizable vibes, leaving plenty of room for other communities and player-created places. Their territories need not form tidy blocks, and the transition into Cymea proper remains open.

### Basic Layout

- **Northern estate country, oriented inland:** Old cotton estates, great houses turned into fortified communities, tenant villages, family rivalries, and local rulers whose grand inherited titles outlast the territory they once governed.
- **Middle coast:** Prosperous stone towns, sheltered courtyards, fishing villages, and busy harbors, where old local families have connections across the Green Sea and every town takes pride in its own ways.
- **Southern imperial society, just west of the mountains:** A living successor to Drankor, with a crowded capital, elaborate courts, public ceremonies, scholars, and officials who believe they still serve an empire with a future.

The middle coast could share a recognizable culture across independent towns, with boatyards, gardens, small farms, and coastal markets around the larger ports. Family connections, hospitality, and local reputation offer useful starting points for characters. Coastal towns could buy cotton from the inland estates, weave cloth, arrange shipping, and supply imported goods, tying the two regions together without making them the same society. This coastal culture could extend north beside the estates as well as occupy the country between them and the southern imperial center.

### Cotton Country and Proposed Terrain

The current physical model makes cotton plausible in the warm northern South Cymean lowlands: hot summers, relatively mild winters, maritime moisture, and sunny breaks between showers and thunderstorms. The estates fit lower plains and river valleys; cooler uplands would carry other crops or woodland. Irrigation could supplement rainfall in drier districts, while wet harvest weather remains a concern.

A proposed terrain addition would strengthen this picture:

- **A coastal ridge or belt of substantial hills** would intercept some moist sea winds, giving the inland plains relatively sunnier and drier conditions where the airflow crosses the ridge.
- **A river fed by wetter uplands** would supply water for irrigation during dry spells and connect the estate country to the coast through a gap in the hills.
- **Broad, gently sloping river terraces** would offer deep agricultural soils above the regularly flooded ground, with enough drainage to avoid waterlogging the cotton.

Picture wooded coastal hills, rivers cutting through toward the sea, and wide agricultural plains opening inland: estate houses above the fields, roads and irrigation channels crossing the plain, and trading towns near the river outlets. The ridge's orientation and height still need placement on the map; it would shelter the plains from some weather, not guarantee a dry autumn or block storms from every direction.

## Maritime Trade Peninsula — Layout and Terrain Brainstorming

### Writing Reference

- **Overview:** Picture a crowded harbor city climbing green hills, with steep streets, packed waterfronts, shipyards, and ships arriving from every corner of the Green Sea.
- **People/Settlements:** One major international port anchors the region, with generations of migration and trade shaping its neighborhoods; other ports, inland communities, and local cultures remain open.
- **Climate/Biome:** Warm subtropical shores and rainy mountain slopes surround a great wetland of flooded forest, shallow lakes, and winding channels, with mangroves along suitable coasts and increasingly dry country toward the desert.

### The Main Port

The Hong Kong/Macau touchstone is a dense, lively maritime city: warehouses beside crowded docks, workshops and markets climbing the hills, and families with connections across the sea. Cultural mixing should feel deeply rooted in the city’s everyday life.

The city provides a strong focal point while leaving plenty of room for players to invent neighborhoods, communities, and places elsewhere on the peninsula. Its government, local language, and exact harbor location remain open.

### The Great Swamp

A broad, low basin east of the mountains could gather water from several rivers before draining toward the coast. Picture flooded woodland, reed beds, shallow lakes, and branching channels, interrupted by wooded islands and low ridges of dry ground.

Summer rain could expand the flooded country, while deeper channels, lakes, and spring-fed forest remain wet through the drier season. Boat routes and overland paths would change with the water level.

The swamp can be explicitly magical, but the magic should strengthen a recognizable physical process. Four possibilities:

- **Rain-catching mountains:** Mountain forests draw in maritime cloud and trigger unusually dependable rain, feeding rivers while the desert receives little moisture.
- **A vast underground reservoir:** Rain sinks into the mountains and emerges slowly through springs across the lowlands; magic increases storage and sustains the flow through drought.
- **A forest that spreads the rivers:** Exceptionally vigorous roots dam channels and trap sediment, keeping water spread across the basin instead of letting a single deep river drain it.
- **Rain that keeps returning:** Magic encourages moisture evaporating from the wetland to fall locally again, producing frequent showers over the swamp while surrounding country stays dry.

These could combine. Rain-catching mountains and unusually persistent springs offer one promising foundation, but no mechanism is chosen.

The wetland’s edge could follow the height of the water table: flooded forest gives way to dry woodland and thorn scrub as the ground rises toward the desert. Older brainstorming also places lizardfolk in this region; their communities and relationship to the wetland remain open.

### Mangrove Shores

Mangroves fit the peninsula’s warm coastal setting wherever shallow, muddy tidal ground has sufficient shelter. Using the numbered reference sketch:

- **3 — western shore:** Mangrove-lined bays and tidal creeks, with firmer wooded ground behind them.
- **4 — eastern bay:** Mangroves around the shallow inner shore of the bay, with potentially rocky or sandy ground on the projecting point.
- **1 — southeastern shore:** A possible broad outlet for the inland swamp, with branching freshwater channels meeting mangrove islands and muddy estuaries; shoals or barrier islands could shelter the coast.

These are possible shoreline arrangements, not settled locations or boundaries.

## Medju and the Spice Cities — Regional Brainstorming

### Writing Reference

- **Overview:** Picture sun-baked harbor and oasis cities, shaded markets, caravan courtyards, and merchant houses whose private gardens stay green while the country beyond the walls turns brown.
- **People/Settlements:** Medju’s wealthy harbor connects a wider world of oasis cities, mountain gateways, smaller ports, and caravan communities scattered along the desert trade routes.
- **Climate/Biome:** Hot, dry coastal country with milder winters, sparse scrub, and cultivated pockets around dependable water, backed by desert and bare mountain slopes.

### Ports, Oases, and Caravan Routes

The Spice Cities are a network of coastal ports and inland oasis cities, with [[Medju]] as the great coastal meeting point. They need not share a government or a single culture; “the Spice Cities” is how outsiders group places connected by trade. The name describes what passes through their markets, without requiring the spices to grow on this dry coast.

Three useful anchors for the regional feel:

- **Medju:** Caravan courtyards beside harbor warehouses, crowded markets, and the conspicuous wealth of the spice lords.
- **An inland oasis city:** Dense gardens and orchards behind walls, shaded streets, and caravans gathering before the next desert crossing.
- **A city near the mountain passes:** Steep roads, crowded inns, and markets where goods arriving from beyond the mountains change hands.

The oasis and mountain cities are possibilities rather than fixed settlements. Other cities, smaller communities, cultures, and the farther destinations of the trade routes remain open for player backstories and later development. No additional city names are needed now.

## Names

### Passage Islands

**Passage Islands** is the Common name for the archipelago off Cymea: one large offshore island, with a mix of large and small islands between it and the mainland. The name comes from the sailing passage between the mainland and the large island, giving the whole group a practical geographic name with a little maritime atmosphere.

The islands' people, settlements, and cultures are intentionally left open to develop together with the players; they need not be an extension of mainland Cymea.

### Largest Island — Cymean Name Options

The largest island needs a Cymean exonym; its local name can wait. These ten options remain unchosen, and the reasons describe sounds and possible associations rather than established translations.

1. **Alveira** (al-VAY-rah) — a softer Portuguese sound, geographically neutral.
2. **Talvera** (tal-VEH-rah) — a sturdy Spanish sound, substantial and easy to say.
3. **Cadrava** (kah-DRAH-vah) — harder consonants give it an old-chart, weathered feel.
4. **Montara** (mon-TAH-rah) — suggests high land, if the island is named from its skyline.
5. **Seravela** (seh-rah-VEH-lah) — a flowing maritime sound, with an echo of *vela* for a possible sailing association.
6. **Vardessa** (var-DESS-ah) — slightly Italian in sound, with enough weight for a major island.
7. **Orvessa** (or-VESS-ah) — compact and distinctive, with little implied meaning to constrain later invention.
8. **Caldria** (KAL-dree-ah) — a clipped, older sound that could survive language changes on charts.
9. **Ravona** (rah-VOH-nah) — rounded, simple, and easy to use at the table.
10. **Valdora** (val-DOR-ah) — suggests valleys and a gentler, more pastoral mood, if that direction appeals.

### Middle Coast — Common Name Options

Common exonym candidates for the coast of stone towns and maritime communities; South Cymea remains the name for the northern estate country. These options remain unchosen, and their possible origins are brainstorming rather than established geography or trade.

1. **The Silver Coast** — could come from pale beaches, glittering water, or a commodity merchants once bought there.
2. **The Ochre Coast** — reddish-yellow cliffs, soils, or plaster on the towns would give it an earthy, specific visual identity.
3. **The White Coast** — pale stone towns or headlands become the defining sight from offshore, particularly fitting the stone-town imagery.
4. **The Red Coast** — exposed red earth or cliffs give it a straightforward geographic name that leaves culture open.
5. **The Pearl Coast** — suggests a coastal pearl fishery alongside the larger trading economy, with small settlements supplying wealthy ports.
6. **The Coral Coast** — ties the shore to reefs and potentially its building stone, if that geology fits.
7. **The Chalk Coast** — pale headlands visible from offshore give it a more rugged feel than the town-focused White Coast.
8. **The Painted Coast** — could refer to brightly plastered waterfronts or bands of colored cliffs, giving an evocative name a concrete origin.

### Southern Imperial Region — Cymean Name Options

Cymean exonym candidates for the southern imperial society just west of the mountains. Valentia, Velastra, and Vessara are the current favorites; no name is chosen yet. Reasons describe sounds and possible associations rather than established translations.

1. **Valentia** (vah-LEN-tee-ah) — a current favorite, strongly classical and confident, carrying a sense of civic and imperial continuity.
2. **Velastra** (veh-LAS-trah) — a current favorite, firmer and more imposing in sound, suited to ceremonial titles and declarations.
3. **Vessara** (veh-SAH-rah) — a current favorite, compact but stately, with enough weight for a powerful city and its surrounding territory.
4. **Aurentia** (ow-REN-tee-ah) — a formal, Latinate sound with a warm opening, suggesting an old provincial name preserved by its successor society.
5. **Castravia** (kas-TRAH-vee-ah) — harder consonants give it a more martial character, fitting fortified cities and an enduring imperial administration.
6. **Seressa** (seh-RESS-ah) — softer and more conversational, with the doubled consonant giving it some of Vessara's weight while remaining easy to use at the table.
7. **Talaris** (tah-LAH-riss) — a compact classical sound with a different ending from the other candidates, useful if the realm takes its name from an old capital.
8. **Ordelia** (or-DEH-lee-ah) — measured and dignified, fitting a settled regional identity with courts, scholars, and a long civic tradition.

### Maritime Trade Peninsula — Landform Options

Cymean exonyms or a Common geographic name; none chosen.

- **Sarona Peninsula** (*sah-ROH-nah*) — simple and neutral; “the Saronan coast” works naturally.
- **Calona Peninsula** (*kah-LOH-nah*) — softer, with room to encompass several cultures.
- **Belora Peninsula** (*beh-LOR-ah*) — compact and distinct from Cymea and Irrla.
- **Aramora Peninsula** (*ah-rah-MOR-ah*) — could sound like a local name adapted into Cymean.
- **The Long Cape** — a practical Common name for the land projecting north into the sea.

### Main Port — Local Name Options

The city should have a local name with a sound distinct from Cymean. Eastros-family names were explored first, followed by a more Turkic/Central Asian sound palette. The local language and its relationships remain open; these names have no assigned translations.

**Turkic/Central Asian direction:**

- **Tashan** (*tah-SHAHN*) — straightforward and substantial.
- **Sayrak** (*SIGH-rak*) — brisk and distinctive, with a strong ending.
- **Ordan** (*or-DAHN*) — understated, like an old name worn smooth by everyday use.
- **Talgan** (*tahl-GAHN*) — sturdy, fitting a working city of docks and workshops.
- **Keshar** (*keh-SHAR*) — softer, fitting a port connected to inland caravans.
- **Aruk** (*ah-ROOK*) — short and memorable.
- **Saryan** (*sah-RYAHN*) — flowing and gentler in sound.
- **Tazhir** (*tah-ZHEER*) — distinctive; *zh* as in “vision.”

**Earlier Balto-Slavic/Eastros options:** Neruva, Vezna, Kelmis, Rasek, Selava, Darsen, Lazava, Varnis, Odrava, Zhelan.

No city name is chosen.

### Great Swamp — Common Name Options

- **The Blackwater Marshes** — dark channels beneath flooded forest.
- **The Great Morass** — a broad outsider’s name for an enormous wetland.
- **The Drowned Woods** — emphasizes trees rising from standing water.
- **The Thousand Channels** — emphasizes travel through branching waterways.
- **The Spring Marshes** — fits the possibility of abundant groundwater welling up across the basin.

No swamp name is chosen.

## Possible Shape of a Brief Overview

1. **Life on a connected sea.** Open with voyages, trade, seasonal departures, and the relationships between distant ports. Establish the sea as a place people live and travel.
2. **Different shores, different lives.** Offer a handful of short contrasts: Skaer volcanic islands; Cymean coastal cities; guarded northern ports; mountainous Irrla; the red cliffs and kenku towns of the eastern islands; and Medju's spice markets.
3. **Magic, danger, and rumor.** Close with uncertain channels, stories of wandering islands, piracy, and the difference between familiar home waters and distant places known through travelers' tales.
