# The Trading Year in Talshan

Working model for trade around Cape Odrava. Talshan has a great many merchants and buyers and a relatively small fleet. A Talshan house can buy a cargo outright, hold it for a month, and sell it to a captain sailing in a different direction. The spring halfling fairs are the clearest example. Merchants from [[Cymea]] and [[Tollen]] arrive the previous autumn to buy from ships that have spent the winter in the eastern Green Sea.

Most of the regular regional carriers come from the imperial province and the Passage Islands. The Pearl Coast towns buy goods from their hinterland and sell them, along with pearls, to merchants who call at their ports. Some Pearl Coast merchants probably sail their own ships to Talshan, but a great deal of Pearl Coast cargo arrives on imperial or island ships. The [[Republic of Svolhas]] on southeastern [[Irrla]] is another important market served mostly by visiting ships.

The warm current runs west between Cape Odrava and southern Irrla, then turns north in the open water east of the Passage Islands. The Pearl Coast and the imperial ports lie well inside that bend. In the gulf west of the cape, a merchant cares chiefly about the angle of the wind on the outward and homeward crossings. Between Talshan and Irrla the current matters on both legs. ## Who trades in Talshan over the year

### Winter — merchants in residence

Talshan houses and the Cymean and Tollender merchants who arrived in autumn trade from the stocks already in town. The western buyers have come to stay for the spring halfling fairs. Imperial and Passage Islands merchants with goods or orders outstanding can leave buyers in Talshan too. A local wind may bring in a ship from the islands or allow an imperial captain to work along the Pearl Coast. With calms between such opportunities, neither fleet can count on making a full bay crossing in each direction on a regular schedule.

### Spring — the halfling fairs and the first regional voyages

The first ships coming west from [[Medju]] are halfling family ships on annual circuits with business still to do farther west. They leave on the first dependable easterlies. At Talshan they can sell to buyers who have been waiting all winter, reload, and continue west-northwest to the Passage Islands under the gulf’s south or south-southeast winds. Fair purchases bound for Tollen can travel with them as far as a Cymean exchange port.

Imperial merchants have a particularly good spring round trip. They can come directly from the province or call on a Pearl Coast town to buy pearls and goods from the hills for the fairs. The same south-southeast wind that brings them to Talshan can carry their purchases home. Passage Islands merchants trying to reach the fairs prefer a spell of wind from the south for the outward crossing. A house that already has a buyer in Talshan can secure cargo before its ship arrives. Once loaded, an island ship has a favorable course home across that range of spring winds.

The Medju merchants who intend to return east arrive later. They can leave after the first halfling ships, use the developing easterlies and the warm current to come west, and spend part of the summer in Talshan. They have until the autumn change of wind to put together a homeward cargo.

### Summer — repeated gulf crossings and westbound cargo from Irrla

The imperial and Passage Islands fleets supply the Medju traders through the summer. An imperial ship can make a second voyage after the fairs. An island ship can bring goods from the Passage Islands or buy on the Pearl Coast, cross east to Talshan under a southerly, and carry eastern goods back west. The Pearl Coast towns can sell to both fleets throughout the season. Some local ships probably make the crossing themselves.

A Medju ship can use the summer easterlies on two successive legs, **Medju → Svolhas → Talshan**. Svolhas lies north-northwest of Medju, so an easterly gives the ship wind over the side or aft of it on the way north. It can approach Svolhas from the south, sell there, and sail southwest to Talshan with the strong easterlies and westward drift. A ship at Svolhas with buyers in the Passage Islands can instead sail west south of Irrla, giving the western shoals plenty of room. Talshan thus sees cargo *from* Svolhas in summer, usually on a visiting merchant’s ship. Cargo bought in Talshan *for* Svolhas can wait in a warehouse for autumn freight.

### Late summer and early autumn — the uncertain weeks

Medju traders complete their buying and watch for a wind that will last long enough to carry them east of the cape. The first westerly spell may let some ships leave while others wait through a return of the easterlies. Imperial and Passage Islands ships can still bring late consignments, but a captain setting out now has less assurance of the wind for the homeward leg. Cargo already in a Talshan warehouse can catch a departure that goods still on the Pearl Coast will miss.

One imperial circuit takes advantage of the change. A ship can leave while the gulf southerlies still hold, sail **imperial province → Talshan → Passage Islands**, and spend the unsettled interval trading in the islands. Once northwesterlies develop it can work south **Passage Islands → Pearl Coast → imperial province**. Its Talshan call belongs to the end of summer even if the cargo it buys there only reaches the Pearl Coast in autumn.

### Autumn — eastern departures and western arrivals

Westerlies and northwesterlies carry Medju ships back southeast and give other carriers an eastward course to Svolhas. A Medju merchant who bought at Svolhas on the way to Talshan can now go straight home. Another can make the Svolhas call on the autumn leg, carrying Talshan purchases northeast to Irrla and then sailing south-southeast to Medju. Both itineraries bring the ship back to its home port before the winter calms if the change of wind comes in good time.

Merchants from [[Cymea]] and [[Tollen]] arrive from the west, often in time to sell directly to the departing Medju traders. Many stay for the following spring fairs. Passage Islands carriers have a favorable east-southeast crossing to Talshan and can carry some of these merchants and their goods on the last leg. An island ship arriving on a westerly can take freight onward to Svolhas. To get home it must allow for a later change of wind, winter away, or plan a longer circuit. One such circuit continues from Svolhas to Medju in autumn, winters there, and returns by Talshan and the Passage Islands with the spring trade.

Imperial merchants can bring goods east-northeast to Talshan under the same autumn winds. For the west-southwest return to the province, a northwesterly gives a better sailing angle than a westerly. Other imperial ships are already trading south down the Pearl Coast. Both fleets can therefore have merchants doing business in Talshan after some of their ships have left for home.

## Voyage timing

At the ordinary planning rate of 60 statute miles per sailing day in [[Trading in the Green Sea]], an imperial ship needs about 13 sailing days each way to Talshan. Eight days to trade and four to six days waiting for suitable weather put a spring round trip at five to six weeks. An island ship sailing directly to the fairs needs about nine sailing days each way. This leaves room for both fleets to make a fair voyage and return to Talshan while the Medju buyers are in residence.

The direct Talshan–Medju route is about 925 miles, or roughly 16 sailing days in favorable conditions. A ship arriving from Medju in late spring and leaving in autumn spends much more of its voyage trading than sailing. The 380-mile Talshan–Svolhas crossing takes only about seven favorable sailing days, but the direction matters more than the distance. A summer ship at Svolhas can reach the Talshan market promptly. A ship in Talshan promising delivery at Svolhas must price in the wait for an eastbound opportunity.
