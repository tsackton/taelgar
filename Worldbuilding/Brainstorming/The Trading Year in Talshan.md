# The Trading Year in Talshan

Working model for trade around Cape Odrava. Talshan has a great many merchants and buyers and a relatively small fleet. A Talshan house can buy a cargo outright, hold it for a month, and sell it to a captain sailing in a different direction. The spring halfling fairs are the clearest example. Merchants from [[Cymea]] and [[Tollen]] arrive the previous autumn to buy from ships that have spent the winter in the eastern Green Sea.

Most of the regular regional carriers come from the imperial province and the Passage Islands. The Pearl Coast towns buy goods from their hinterland and sell them, along with pearls, to merchants who call at their ports. Some Pearl Coast merchants probably sail their own ships to Talshan, but a great deal of Pearl Coast cargo arrives on imperial or island ships. The [[Republic of Svolhas]] on southeastern [[Irrla]] is another important market served mostly by visiting ships.

The warm current passes west between Cape Odrava and southern Irrla, then turns north outside the Passage Islands. Most of the bay between Talshan and the mainland lies inside that bend. The spring and summer southerlies can carry ships both ways across the bay, depending on the bearing. Farther east, a ship heading for Svolhas has to make good its easting while the current carries it west. See the maps in [[Green Sea Ocean Circulation]] and [[Green Sea Atmospheric Circulation]].

## Who trades in Talshan over the year

### Autumn — western merchants arrive, Medju ships sail home

Merchants from Cymea and Tollen arrive in the fall, often intending to stay until the spring fairs. For a time they can sell directly to [[Medju]] traders finishing their homeward cargoes. Westerly and northwesterly spells help the Medju ships make their way east against the current. A Tollender merchant can reach Talshan by changing ships at a Cymean port or in the Passage Islands.

Passage Islands ships can come southeast to Talshan after a front on a northwest wind, sell island or Pearl Coast cargo, and take western goods home on a subsequent southerly. Imperial merchants in Talshan can buy from the western arrivals for the province. Other imperial ships are already on their way home from a summer circuit, trading south from the Passage Islands through the Pearl Coast. Those ships may have made their Talshan call back in August.

### Winter — the western buyers stay

Talshan houses and the overwintering Cymean and Tollender merchants continue trading from their stocks. Imperial ships can make a bay crossing when the wind holds across their course, bringing goods from the province or from a recent Pearl Coast call. Passage Islands arrivals depend more on finding a wind for both the southeastward crossing and the return. An island house with regular business in Talshan has good reason to leave a buyer there over winter and send a ship when it has a paying cargo. The number of winter crossings is still open.

### Early spring — the halfling fairs

The first ships west from Medju are the halfling family ships that still have a western leg to make on their annual circuit. They take the first dependable easterlies, sell at the fairs, and continue toward the Passage Islands or Cymea. The western merchants who have spent the winter in Talshan are ready to buy as soon as those ships arrive. Fair purchases bound for Tollen can go west in stages through Cymean exchange ports.

Imperial merchants can time a round trip for the fairs, calling on the Pearl Coast on the way to collect pearls and hinterland goods, then buying eastern cargo in Talshan for sale at home. Passage Islands merchants can bring their own cargo to the fairs and carry purchases home for several buyers. For them the timing works particularly well if a south or southwest spell gets them to Talshan before the first halfling arrivals and a southeasterly follows for the westward voyage.

### Late spring and early summer — the Medju traders arrive

The Medju ships whose owners intend to return east can leave later than the westbound halflings. They still benefit from the westward current and spring easterlies. Their next deadline is the autumn change of wind, so they can spend part of the summer in Talshan selling their outward cargo and buying from successive regional arrivals.

Imperial merchants can make a second Talshan voyage in this interval, bringing goods bought on the Pearl Coast and taking Medju cargo back to the province. Passage Islands merchants have a useful three-market circuit. They can sail **Passage Islands → Talshan → Svolhas → Passage Islands**, buying in Talshan for Svolhas and buying in Svolhas for home. A June or early July departure from Talshan gives them a chance of southerlies for the northeastward crossing. On the long westward leg south of Irrla they can use the warm current. This puts ships in Talshan offering freight space to Svolhas even in a year when few Svolhas-owned ships call.

### High and late summer — buying for the autumn departures

The Medju merchants still in Talshan can assemble a homeward cargo over several weeks. Imperial ships on direct round trips and Passage Islands ships back from Irrla can both supply them. A Pearl Coast house might send a ship of its own if it has enough goods to sell. It can also sell the same goods to an imperial merchant at its home wharf and let that merchant make the crossing.

Some imperial merchants have a reason to leave home late in the season. An August departure allows a ship to sell in **Talshan**, buy there for the **Passage Islands**, and finish the voyage south through the **Pearl Coast** to the **imperial province** as autumn fronts bring northwesterlies. The captain gets three markets into one voyage and reaches the southbound coast when the summer headwinds begin to break. The goods bought in Talshan in August may already have changed hands in the islands by the time the autumn merchants from Tollen reach Cape Odrava.

## Voyage timing

At the ordinary planning rate of 60 statute miles per sailing day in [[Trading in the Green Sea]], an imperial ship needs about 13 sailing days each way to Talshan. Eight days to trade and another four to six waiting for weather put it home in five to six weeks. A spring fair voyage and a second voyage to trade with the Medju merchants fit comfortably in the same year.

The Passage Islands–Talshan–Svolhas circuit is roughly 1,720 miles, or about 30 sailing days for the three legs. A week to ten days in each market away from home and several days waiting for the right winds bring the whole voyage to about seven to nine weeks. An island ship leaving in late May could trade at Talshan in early June, reach Svolhas later that month, and be home in July. The relative frequency of southerly and easterly spells off southern Irrla will matter more to that timetable than the small difference between one anchorage and the next.
