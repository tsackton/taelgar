Relatively few reliable reports of planar travelers have come from the plane known as Limbo. Scattered fragments of lost poems and confused ramblings, collected by [[Gaius Devarro]] in his treatise [[On Minds and Chaos]], hint at a place of churning chaos, where nothing holds its shape for long; stone melts to water, which freezes to fire at a whim, before bursting into a diamond that blows away as snow. [[Marcion of Iridel]], quoting a fragment of a lost treatise called the [[The Unstable Sphere]], called it "the endless unmaking," suggesting a domain of constant, unceasing, chaotic transformation. More recently, [[Arryn|Arryn of Tollen]] complied a series of inferred principles of the nature of Limbo in [[Experiments upon the Planar Substance]]. 

The origins of Limbo defy any explanation, although [[Yendalo]] speculated that Limbo may in fact be a shifting wound in reality itself, where the fundamental essences of existence refuse to take any permanent shape.

%% an expanded description of Limbo relevant to the Dunmar campaign %%

Limbo, the fundamental plane of chaos, is a plane defined by constant, unceasing transformation. As recorded by [[Gaius Devarro]] in his treatise [[On Minds and Chaos]], nothing in Limbo holds its shape for long: stone melts to water, which freezes to fire at a whim, before bursting into diamond that blows away as snow. [[Marcion of Iridel]], quoting a fragment of a lost work known as [[The Unstable Sphere]], called it "the endless unmaking," suggesting a domain of constant, unceasing, chaotic transformation. The fundamentally shifting and chaotic nature of Limbo make travel and exploration of this plane particularly difficult. Marcion recorded that ancient scholars considered Limbo impossible to map in any meaningful way, though this does not mean it is featureless. Gaius Devarro collected many tales of seemingly impossible stable spaces: sanctuaries, of a kind, held solid by acts of impossible concentration by intellects of iron. He describes massive rocks floating like icebergs in empty space, never changing, hollowed out into laboratories of transmutation, and impossible towers that twist in space, stretching to pinnacles of elemental power. No tale collected by Gaius speaks with authority on the content of these sanctuaries, however.

## Travel and Survival in Limbo

Despite the challenges, reliable reports suggest it is possible for extraplanar explorers to survive, and even travel, in Limbo. Several reports, including the first-hand experience of the [[Dunmar Fellowship]], and the stories of the [[Order of the Awakened Soul|monks of Dunmar]] who survived in [[Limbo]] under [[Rai]]'s protection, indicate that fixing a direction in the mind causes a traveler to move in that direction, though this offers little protection against the chaos of Limbo, which can easily turn air to stone in an instant, entombing all who are unlucky enough to occupy that space. To actually survive, therefore, requires something more: a way to temporarily still the constant chaotic transformations of the plane. 

The most practical advice for how to achieve this comes from the reported experience of the [[Dunmar Fellowship]], and from the notes recorded in [[Arryn|Arryn of Tollen's]] experimental exploration of the nature of the chaos magic of Limbo, [[Experiments upon the Planar Substance]]. Both sources agree that to stabilize the material of Limbo into a constant form requires a powerful intellect. A firm, strong mind, fixed in purpose, can stabilize the space of a small camp for a day, and with greater effort and exceptional mental acuity, even sustain order for weeks or more. [[The Unstable Sphere]], and [[Rai]]'s experience reported by the [[Order of the Awakened Soul|Awakened Soul monks]], hints at regions of stability lasting decades or longer. However, strong minds can destroy stability as easily as create it: a thought can reshape nearby, unworn, non-living matter; move loose objects; and even seize control over a stabilized pocket of the plane from a weaker mind, though if this is done violently or carelessly the consequences are, like much of what happens in Limbo, dangerous and unpredictable. As Arryn reported, transformation encourages further transformation, eventually leading to dissolution into the pure primordial chaos of Limbo. 

Though scholarly arguments continue to this day about it, [[The Path of Unmaking]] purports to describe other ways to travel in Limbo: via hidden currents that flow through the chaos, or even via fissures that can connect two distant points, allowing nearly instantaneous, if quite dangerous, travel over great distances. 

## Cha'mutte and other Inhabitants of Limbo 

There are no recorded scholarly works that identify any native inhabitants of Limbo. Indeed, the general consensus is that native inhabitants of Limbo cannot exist.

Nonetheless, a provocative theory, emerging from the work of [[Paulina Nusinius]] shortly after the [[Great War]], claimed that while Limbo has no native inhabitants, [[Cha'mutte]] was in some sense part of Limbo itself, or at least had the power and ability to draw Limbo closer to the Material Plane, strengthening his power through control of planar tides. Paulina claimed that this association explained many otherwise hard-to-reconcile facts, including the substantial reshaping that occurred in regions around the world after Cha'mutte's death. While this theory remains controversial, the adventures of the [[Dunmar Fellowship]] provide some [[The Last Jade Vision|substantiation]]: the essence of Limbo was woven into and central to the prison that Cha'mutte built to hold Apollyon. 

Further, the connection between the Plaguelands and Limbo links Cha'mutte to this plane. Though [[Harbek Ferrystone]]'s predictions in [[Planar Tides in Relation to the Dangers of the Plaguelands]] failed to predict the [[War of the Ashen Horde]], discrediting a direct model of the [[Plaguelands]] as Limbo-tainted for several decades, this connection was directly confirmed by the [[Heartroot Vision|discoveries]] of the [[Dunmar Fellowship]] in the [[Session 134 (DuFr)|summer of DR 1749]], during and after the [[Battle of Heartroot Vale]].
