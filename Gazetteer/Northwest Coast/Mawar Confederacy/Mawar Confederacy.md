## Overview

North of [[Chardon]], hugging the coast of the rocky, inhospitable [[Mawakel Peninsula]], the Mawar people have lived symbiotically with the ocean for generations uncounted. They preserve an ancient culture and language, as well as a distinct Mawaran form of [[Mos Numena]] centered on saintly ancestors and local heroes.

## History
_See more: [[Timeline of the Mawar]]_

%% Notes: see: [[Time Skip - Bullet Points]] for important recent history, e.g. 1747-1749%%

 Mawaran tradition traces their ancestry to the first peoples to leave [[Hkar]] ages ago, and the Mawar once settled widely across the coastal plains and shores north of [[Chardon]], as far as the northern coast of the [[Slate Sea]]. But when the Drankorians spread north, they ceded the southern lands and settled largely on the [[Mawakel Peninsula]] and the near-shore islands, maintaining an uneasy peace within the Drankorian sphere of influence.

After the fall of [[Drankorian Empire|Drankor]], the Mawar became a significant coastal and trading power, and Mawar ships could be seen plying coastal trade routes as far south as [[Chardon]] and as far north as the far northern shores beyond the [[Slate Sea]]. All this changed with the expansion of [[Pandar]] and the coming of Cha'mutte's armies. The Mawar survived the [[Great War]] and the [[Blood Years|chaos afterward]] by retreating to their coastal villages, relying on the protection of the difficult terrain of the [[Mawakel Peninsula]] and the magic of the [[Fog of Mawar]]. 

After the [[Fog of Mawar]] lifted, the Mawar entered a renaissance of renewed trade and classical Mawaran institutions. The [[Timeline of the Mawar#The Mindknife Wars|Mindknife Wars]] ended that golden age, and the later expansion of the [[Chardonian Empire]] brought both stability and competition. The Mawar Confederacy remains one of the few northern civilizations to have survived the [[Blood Years]], but its recent history is marked by retrenchment, wary independence, and a struggle to maintain its place in the trade of the northern coast.

## Culture and Religion
_See more: [[Mawaran Religion]]_

The Mawar are an independent and democratic people, proud of their heritage and egalitarian and practical in outlook. The confederacy itself is governed by an elected conclave that meets yearly, with an administrative council and governance structure elected by the conclave to handle any necessary governmental tasks during the months of the year the conclave is not in session. Most villages are run by elected leaders, who are also sometimes the representative to the conclave, with some local variation. 

The Mawar depend entirely on the sea for food, travel, resources, and their livelihoods, and almost the entire population lives within a few miles of either the sea or the [[Sulqat]] river. Much of their culture and outlook is based around this relationship to the ocean. However, they are not known as long-distance mariners: few Mawar undertake long ocean journeys and most of their seafaring vessels are designed to remain within a few miles of the coast. 

During the Drankorian expansion, the Mawar adopted the [[Mos Numena]] tradition, but their local form of this religion developed as a distinct regional variant shaped by older [[Northern Folk Religions|northern intercessory traditions]], lizardfolk ritual practice, and the culture of the sea. In Mawaran religion, the [[Mos Numena|Eight Divines]] remain the ultimate source of miracles from the [[Divine Presence]], but prayers often pass through saintly ancestors and heroes who bring the attention of the Divines to the needs of the people. Figures such as [[Guzo the Mariner]] are treated as heroic intercessors rather than replacements for the Divines, and ancestor veneration remains an important part of Mawaran religious life.

## Gatza
_See more: [[Gatza]]_

A particular drug of the Mawakel peninsula is gatza, a drug harvested from the livers of great predatory fish of the Endless Ocean, and traditionally used by lizardfolk in controlled rituals to commune with ancestral spirits. Outside that ritual context, it can become addictive and dangerous, drawing grief-stricken users into obsessive visions that may be false or malevolent and can leave them wasting away in gatza dreams.
