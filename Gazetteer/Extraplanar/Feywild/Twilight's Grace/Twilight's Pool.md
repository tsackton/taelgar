# Twilight's Pool

> [!info]+ Information  
> `$=dv.view("_scripts/view/get_Affiliations")`
> 
> > `$=dv.view("_scripts/view/get_Whereabouts")`

![[twilights-pool-gate.png|400]]

The Twilight's Pool is the principal crossing between [[Twilight's Grace]] and [[Tafolwern]], and is sometimes described as the front door of the court of [[Archfey Ethlenn]]. Ambassadors, honored visitors, petitioners, and others traveling openly between Ethlenn’s realm and mortal [[Tyrwingha]] used to commonly pass through the Pool in the days of story and legend, and still rarely do, although travel is less common than it once was.
## In Twilight's Grace

On the Feywild side, the Pool is a large oval reflecting pool, roughly 50' in diameter, surrounded by elegant edges of polished black marble. The stone seems to retain the warmth of a summer evening, even though no direct sunlight falls upon it. Twinkling lights drift across and beneath the surface. Reflections of [[Tyrwingha]] mingle and dissolve in the water, always showing twilight: villages beneath evening skies, rivers shining beneath the stars, vineyard slopes, distant towers, moonlit roads, and lights moving behind cottage windows. No single scene remains for long. Although some reflections clearly reflect the present or recent past, others are more mysterious. Some say these reflect Tyrwingha as remembered by [[Archfey Ethlenn]]. Others say these are foretellings of the future or dreams of the Tyrwinghans. At times, ripples pass across the Pool carrying faint echoes of laughter, grief, relief, longing, or tears. 

Those who step into the Pool do not feel wet. Although the water appears infinitely deep, invisible steps can be felt beneath the feet, descending gently toward the Material Plane. With each step, the reflected scenes grow clearer until the traveler emerges in [[Tafolwern]].

## In Tafolwern

On the mortal side, the Pool is smaller, little larger than a substantial ornamental fountain. It lies within an old formal garden and is surrounded by ancient blocks of black obsidian inscribed with silver [[Sylvan Writing|sylvan]] writing. Unlike much of the Sylvan-inspired ornament found elsewhere in [[Tafolwern]], these inscriptions are genuine. They speak of welcome, memory, passage, and return. The Pool always reflects a twilight sky, regardless of the hour or weather above it. Stars, drifting lights, and distant glimmers sometimes appear upon its surface, but the Pool never shows people. Faint voices, music, or laughter can occasionally be heard from within it, though never distinctly enough to understand.

Stone steps descend through the cold water toward the center of the Pool. After three, the stairs simply end. Those accepted by the crossing step forward and emerge dry in Twilight's Grace. Others fall abruptly into the deeper water and are merely soaked. 

No reliable method has ever been discovered for determining which fate awaits a traveler. Tyrwinghan tradition holds that the Pool knows whether one has truly been invited.

- The mortal-side Pool should be located in an old garden connected to royal or civic grounds, though not  within the palace itself. There is likely a tower of the oracle of the riven nearby

# The Traveler's Road

> [!info]+ Information  
> `$=dv.view("_scripts/view/get_Affiliations")`
> 
> > `$=dv.view("_scripts/view/get_Whereabouts")`

The Traveler’s Road is an old and indirect crossing between [[Twilight's Grace]] and western [[Tyrwingha]]. On the Material Plane, it emerges in the countryside roughly one or two days’ walk from [[Eskbridge]].

The Road is used by messengers, wanderers, scouts, and travelers who wish to pass between the worlds without the ceremony or scrutiny of the [[Pool of Twilight]].

## In Tyrwingha

On the mortal side, the Traveler’s Road is a narrow, twisting path through lightly wooded country. It is overgrown with thorn, elder, hazel, and long grass, and in places moss covers the remnants of ancient paving stones.

The path does not appear to lead anywhere important. It bends frequently, doubles back upon itself, narrows between leaning trees, and occasionally vanishes beneath roots and undergrowth before reappearing a few yards farther on.

Travelers sometimes report:

- hearing footsteps behind them when no one is present
    
- seeing wagon ruts where no cart could have passed
    
- finding weathered milestones whose inscriptions have worn away
    
- smelling woodsmoke from houses that cannot be seen
    
- hearing the faint notes of an old traveling song somewhere ahead
    

Near the crossing, two overgrown tracks braid together beneath a stand of leaning trees. A worn stone marker stands nearby, sometimes said to be an ancient shrine to [[The Wanderer]].

The Road is not a main thoroughfare and is rarely encountered by accident. Those who do find it often assume it is merely an abandoned local track until the country begins to change around them.

## In Twilight's Grace

On the Feywild side, the Road appears clearer but no straighter. Low stone walls, silver-shadowed hedges, and lantern-lit puddles line its course beneath an unchanging dusk.

The puddles reflect stars that are not visible in the sky. Grass whispers with the sound of passing travelers, and small offerings can sometimes be found beside the road at bends and crossroads.

Signposts occasionally appear along the way. Their directions are often contradictory, though those familiar with the Feywild insist that all of them are true.

The Traveler’s Road is not closely watched. It belongs less to Ethlenn’s court than to the idea of travel itself, and those who use it are expected to know how to behave upon an old road.

## Crossing

A traveler who remains upon the Road and continues forward without looking back may pass between the Feywild and the Material Plane.

Once the Road begins to change, turning back or stepping from the path usually returns the traveler to the side from which they began. Some stories claim that those who leave the Road entirely may emerge somewhere else, or at some other time.

On reaching Tyrwingha, the change is subtle. The light becomes flatter, the air colder, and the sounds of ordinary insects and birds return. The path becomes muddy and narrow, and after some further walking eventually joins a local road or reaches a small hamlet.

%% notes

- This is the practical and comparatively discreet western crossing.
    
- It emerges one to two days from Eskbridge, not immediately beside the town.
    
- It should feel like an old road that remembers having once been important.

# The Starlight Grove

> [!info]+ Information  
> `$=dv.view("_scripts/view/get_Affiliations")`
> 
> > `$=dv.view("_scripts/view/get_Whereabouts")`

The Starlight Grove is a quiet crossing between [[Twilight's Grace]] and the wooded hills of southern [[Tyrwingha]]. It lies in country where vineyards, forest, and old roads meet, and offers access toward both the [[Mostreve Hills]] and the southern borderlands.

## In Tyrwingha

On the Material Plane, the Grove is a small clearing hidden within woodland. Wild grapevines sprawl untidily across fallen branches, old stones, and the lower trunks of trees.

It is always dusk within the Grove.

The light inside does not match the hour outside. The treetops remain murky and difficult to see, and the clearing is filled with muted shades of silver, violet, and dusky orange. Small lights drift slowly downward from the canopy like fireflies or falling stars.

The grapevines often grow leaves of silver or dark orange, and some bear both upon the same stem. Their fruit ripens at no predictable season.

The place feels both neglected and protected. Local travelers know it as somewhere easy to become lost and unexpectedly fortunate to find.

## In Twilight's Grace

On the Feywild side, the Starlight Grove opens into a larger and carefully maintained vineyard garden.

The same silver and orange vines grow there, but they are trained along elegant trellises and arranged in deliberate rows. Low walls, shallow channels of water, and narrow paths divide the vineyard into graceful patterns beneath the permanent dusk.

The drifting lights are brighter here, moving among the vines and gathering beneath the treetops. Distant pavilions and garden walks can sometimes be glimpsed through the rows.

The contrast between the two sides is striking. In Tyrwingha, the vines have escaped cultivation and become a wild tangle. In Twilight’s Grace, they remain part of an ordered and intentional landscape.

## Crossing

Those who pluck and eat a ripe grape may cross between the worlds.

The transition is usually gentle. The fruit tastes first sweet, then cool, and for a moment the traveler seems to stand within two twilights at once. The air shifts, the distant sounds change, and the other side of the Grove settles into place.

Tradition warns that only grapes which seem ready to be taken should be plucked. Unripe grapes, or those hidden beneath thorns, may lead nowhere at all.

Silver grapes are said to make the crossing feel cool, quiet, and clear. Orange grapes are said to taste warm and bittersweet. Both lead to the same place, although travelers frequently insist that the journey feels entirely different.

On emerging in Tyrwingha, travelers first notice the smell of damp earth, leaves, wild grapes, and distant woodsmoke. Depending on the wind, they may hear a sheep bell, a village bell, or someone singing far beyond the trees.
