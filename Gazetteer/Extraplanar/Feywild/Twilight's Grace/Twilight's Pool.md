# Twilight's Pool

The Twilight's Pool is the principal crossing between [[Twilight's Grace]] and [[Tafolwern]], and is sometimes described as the front door of the court of [[Archfey Ethlenn]]. Ambassadors, honored visitors, petitioners, and others traveling openly between Ethlenn’s realm and mortal [[Tyrwingha]] used to commonly pass through the Pool in the days of story and legend, and still rarely do, although travel is less common than it once was.
## In Twilight's Grace

On the Feywild side, the Pool is a large oval reflecting pool, roughly 50' in diameter, surrounded by elegant edges of polished black marble. The stone seems to retain the warmth of a summer evening, even though no direct sunlight falls upon it. Twinkling lights drift across and beneath the surface. Reflections of [[Tyrwingha]] mingle and dissolve in the water, always showing twilight: villages beneath evening skies, rivers shining beneath the stars, vineyard slopes, distant towers, moonlit roads, and lights moving behind cottage windows. No single scene remains for long. Although some reflections clearly reflect the present or recent past, others are more mysterious. Some say these reflect Tyrwingha as remembered by [[Archfey Ethlenn]]. Others say these are foretellings of the future or dreams of the Tyrwinghans. At times, ripples pass across the Pool carrying faint echoes of laughter, grief, relief, longing, or tears. 

Those who step into the Pool do not feel wet. Although the water appears infinitely deep, invisible steps can be felt beneath the feet, descending gently toward the Material Plane. With each step, the reflected scenes grow clearer until the traveler emerges in [[Tafolwern]].

## In Tafolwern

On the mortal side, the Pool is smaller, little larger than a substantial ornamental fountain. It lies within an old formal garden and is surrounded by ancient blocks of black obsidian inscribed with silver [[Sylvan Writing|sylvan]] writing. Unlike much of the Sylvan-inspired ornament found elsewhere in [[Tafolwern]], these inscriptions are genuine. They speak of welcome, memory, passage, and return. The Pool always reflects a twilight sky, regardless of the hour or weather above it. Stars, drifting lights, and distant glimmers sometimes appear upon its surface, but the Pool never shows people. Faint voices, music, or laughter can occasionally be heard from within it, though never distinctly enough to understand.

Stone steps descend through the cold water toward the center of the Pool. After three, the stairs simply end. Those accepted by the crossing step forward and emerge dry in Twilight's Grace. Others fall abruptly into the deeper water and are merely soaked. 

No reliable method has ever been discovered for determining which fate awaits a traveler. Tyrwinghan tradition holds that the Pool knows whether one has truly been invited.
