# Lost in the Feywild

![[lost-in-the-feywild-hero.png]]
*Five travelers watch a hundred souls go home beneath the first stars of evening.*

Lost in the Feywild is a seven-episode adventure about the [[Tollen Misfits]], a strange inheritance, a Fey bargain, an ancient magical society, and a prisoner at the center of a mysterious house. What begins with a seemingly ordinary journey through [[Varrow Forest]] to [[Dandelion House]] leads into the [[Feywild]], through an endlessly ending party, and into a house built to move its prisoner through space and time.

>[! abstract] Campaign Information  
>
> - The [[Tollen Misfits|PCs]], a found family band of adventurers from around Tollen: [[Edric]], [[Kaito Min]], [[Txarro]], [[Ayveen]], and [[Tarek]]

>[! note]+ *The Tale of the Dandelion Child, told by halfling storytellers around the taverns of Tollen*
>
>There are tales that begin with a war, and others with a wedding, but this one began with kindness, which, as any good storyteller will tell you, can cause more trouble than either. 
>
>Long ago, the story goes, a fey lord in a twilight realm, known as Lord Revel Beginning, found a lonely child beside a bottomless well. The child had no words and knew little of the world, so Revel taught it language, letters, games, and stories. They taught it about trees and roads and doors, and showed it how to stretch its roots and grow itself into houses. They taught it many things, but could not linger, for Lord Revel Beginning was bound to the beginnings of things. They returned, bound by their nature as the fey are, to their manor, to begin a magnificent revel, promising to come back when the party was over. 
>
>The child understood that they would not, for the child did not live in time as you or I. Left alone, it saw the lights of dreamers drift past, and became enchanted by their beauty, by the vibrant light of the divine in all of us, by the beauty of what the fey call dreams but we call the song of our ancestors. The child, though, did not understand these dreams, these lights, these tales. It held them tight, grasping them in its embrace. 
>
>This was long ago, in the time of the great empire of Drankor, when wizards and scholars ruled with secret knowledge. One order, the Arithrimos Lamperum, bound time and space with mathematics, making number maps that held the secrets of the Multiverse. They, it is said, discovered what the child could do and, being cleverer than they were wise, built a shifting house of 27 rooms around it. They did not just trap the child. They bound and trapped the souls it held, using their fervent desire to reach the Divine, to reach home, to power the machinery of the prison. For a prison it was, and the Child took on new names: the Dreamer, and the Thirst, and the Prisoner. 
>
>The prison at the center of the house was never still, for as the house moved the prison moved as well, through space and time at once. And thus the Prisoner could not escape. But with hubris comes the fall, and the fall came to the Arithrimos Lamperum as surely as it comes for all who thwart the gods. The order began to fail, but its leader, Thalestria, refused to accept the judgement of the gods, and devised a scheme. She spoke of Lord Revel Beginning and such was the power of her speaking that she spoke them a new name, Lord Endless Ending. She bound the party from beginning to ending and bound the lord to deliver a mortal soul to the shifting house, each time the clock struck zero. And such did the moving pieces of the house continue to move, as centuries passed and the ceaselessly ending revel of the Lord continued to end. 
>
>Centuries passed, until a fateful day when a lord of Sembara, Hulda, a scarred and bitter adventurer, found his way to the 27th House. Our story does not tell his tale, except to say that he passed through that room, the prison in the center of the moving house, and left. But not to return to his life, no. He was cast across time and found himself in the years before the rulers of Sembara called themselves kings, in the years before the rising of Cha'mutte and the great war that followed. Stranded, long before his own birth, he sought to rewrite his fate, and wrote a quest for his future self: a deed to a manor, granted only should he enter the Twilight Kingdom and destroy the bargain that had forced him into the 27th House. 
>
>Centuries passed, until a fateful day when a lord of Sembara, Hulda, a scarred and bitter adventurer, received an ancient deed, held in trust for hundreds of years, granting him ownership of a manor in Varrow Wood should he enter the Twilight Kingdom and make Lord Endless Ending break his bargain. Lord Hulda, a man of little patience, failed. 
>
>But this is not the end of the song. Five travelers enter the story, hired by Hulda to complete the task that he could not. Five unsuspecting friends, forced into a story not of their making, but who saw the tale to its end. They entered the Twilight Kingdom, and passed through the ending revel of Lord Endless Ending, and entered the moving house of the scholars of planar numbers. They wandered its rooms until they found the central prison, as many had before. But they did something none had done. They found a being, the Prisoner, the Child, a being that was dangerous, powerful, guilty, lonely, wronged and doing wrong in equal measure. And they told it stories. Stories about death, about love, about kindness, about loss and memory, about letting go. The Prisoner listened. The Child listened. And opened its hold, letting the souls it held go. They flew like milkweed seeds of light, home to the divine, and the prison, robbed of its power, broke apart. 
>
>The Child vanished, but songs say that if you go to the Twilight Kingdom, you will still find the Lord of the Ended Revel sitting and watching a strange bottomless well where his friend from long ago vanished out of the world.
