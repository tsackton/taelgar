# A Recurring Festival for Cleenseau

Exchange proposing a regional festival for the Cleenseau area. Authored turns appear in sent order; quoted reply history and mailing-list footers are omitted. Complete email archive: [[Recurring Festival Idea (Email)]].

### Dan Walters <arcadinal@protonmail.com> — Fri, 17 Oct 2025 12:05:40 +0000

Robin would like to propose to the lords of Cleenseau, Tavois, Aulout, Valit, Beury, AND Ganboa, in the interests of promoting continued cooperation for the purposes of defense, mutual economic prosperity, and general friendly relations, that we hold a monthly (or quarterly if monthly is too much) festival where everyone is invited to participate in a series of competitions.

Activities:
Contests for baking and barbequing (food is always a draw)

An archery tournament (England back in the day I believe actually required participation in stuff like this for the peasantry to keep a trained archer corps, but I'm looking for more 'let's make this fun' as opposed to something where you get fined if you don't show up)

A warlord tournament (essentially inter-town militia training, but more fun). A warlord tournament is people fight one on one duels, the winner of each match becomes the captain of a team of two, then the teams of two fight, the winner becomes a captain of a team of 4, and so on until you work up to a melee between two sides. The idea behind these it is one a test of skill, but also a test of teamwork and honor because you want your defeated opponents to be motivated to fight for you in the next round.

Round the day off with a bardic/entertainment contest.

Can also incorporate a pop-up market.

### Mike Sackton <msackton@gmail.com> — Tue, 21 Oct 2025 19:35:34 -0400

In general this idea is pretty well received, although in world both Robin
and the people in Cleenseau would want to tie it to the existing festival
and holy day calendar, which I have many ideas about but haven't properly
developed.

I'm using this as an excuse to work out a full holiday calendar - in
general, there are about 15-17 holidays in the region already.... some are
common (like Pyravela which came up in game), others are regional miracle
remembrances (like the festival of the bridge that was in session 2 or 3,
or some other local ones I've mentioned in passing), and others are bigger
miracles (i.e. that everyone in Sembara or even the wider region including
Tyrwingha and Tollen celebrate).

So basically I think the way this would work out in world is that some of
these local festivals, which are traditionally only celebrated in Asineau,
or Beury, or Valit, would be "thrown open" to the whole region, and some of
the other festivals, that are usually celebrated in each village
independently, would instead rotate village to village.

Some of the festivals and holidays are not well suited to this kind of
activity (i.e. Pyravela and Vitalae are these very specific ritual holidays
associated with the gods), but many of the festivals are.

So I think you'd probably end up with 5-6 festivals a year that used to be
more local/village specific that are now these regional fairs.

(You might want to up your budget for "temple upkeep" to generous to
support this though - the temples traditionally fund this)

### Mike Sackton <msackton@gmail.com> — Tue, 21 Oct 2025 21:35:18 -0400

The bastion rules basically let you pick whether your Garden:

(a) generates food
(b) generates income
(c) generates useful stuff (i.e. healing potions, healers kits, or poison)

So the first step is picking one of the three options.

The food option produces about 100 days of food per bastion turn, which is
currently a month, but which I might reduce to 7 or 14 days. Either way in
practice this would support between 3 and 5 people. It isn't a substantial
increase in the food available to the village, although it can be useful if
you want to outfit an expedition or similar. It could also be used to
support a feast day (although the food is only part of the cost).

The income option generates approximately 50 GP/turn, which I might tweak a
bit, but ultimately it is a pretty decent income generator. This would
probably be local luxury food items (mushrooms, honey, snails) and some
incense and similar things.

The useful stuff option generates healing potions or poison.

As a side note, remember that the village itself produces plenty of surplus
food. A significant portion of your income is selling excess barley and
wheat at market for 1 GP a bushel. And a significant reason the village
"feels" low on food is because Lord Valbert was appropriating more than his
fair share and selling it. If the village grows enough food for 400 people
but the lord takes 120 people's worth of food, and the population is 300
people, you are short. But that doesn't mean the village lands can't
support the population, it just means the lord was taking from the people
for his own benefit. Lords do that sometimes.

If you want to build up a food surplus simply reduce your income and avoid
selling all your wheat. That's much more effective than trying to build a
garden. The best use of a food-supplying garden would be a bit of a buffer
for feast days and such, but actually you'd be much better off with an
income generating garden and then increasing your food supply by selling
less of your surplus. Or a useful stuff generating garden, if that feels
more like Celyn's vibe (herbs and such). Although maybe the income
generating garden is also in part herbs and salves and such. You could
probably have a mixed garden that generated healers kits and salves and
other healing stuff, plus a bit less income, but no healing potions.
