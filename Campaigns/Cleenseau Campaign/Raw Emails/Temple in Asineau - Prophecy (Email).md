Timing on this would fall somewhere in the week and a half or so spent training the local army to fight monsters and undead.  Celyn has been in and out of the temple to the Wyrdling a few times, of course, to talk to El and Eleanor, but even though the temple is to his god he does not really mesh with *templeness* so even here he doesn’t seem at all inclined to figure out how the system works.  He is interested in checking in on the folks he has filed as his people, and the temple is where they are, so he goes there.

This time, though, he’s thoughtful, and after paying his respects to Eleanor he looks for El with a bit more purpose.  Once he finds them, he waits politely for a moment to speak and then asks, “Could I consult your expertise?  You are … better at the divination part of things than I am.  Interpretation of prophecy.  I just,” he shrugs, “fate is the thing I have to carry.  I _think_ I have a sense of some of what I have to do, but it’s probably easier to be more sure of it talking it through with someone else?"

Celyn waves Viepuck to join if he’s so inclined when he buzzes through, answering El’s ‘If you think it wise’ with “I suspect this will take both wisdom and cleverness, and Viepuck is cleverer than I am.”

Once they get sorted out to sit wherever they’re sitting, he renders his explanation.  “This is not precisely a prophecy, or I don’t think it is, but it came from the fey, and they’re fond of the same sort of riddling phrasings that prophecies come in sometimes, so I was thinking that some of the same sorts of thinking would be useful.”  He pauses.  “And it might be prophetic.  The one who gave it to the messenger who got it to _me_ clearly knew she would be seeing me, so he had some foreknowing power.”

He leans back, murmurs, “A little bird…” and concentrates, working on recalling things.  (If Viepuck spikes his brain this will help but he can eventually get to this on his own, he did *try* to fix it in his memory.)  Eventually: “The words I was given were: A little bird’s wings can’t reach from sunrise to sunset, nor even from the late afternoon shadow to the sunset gate. But sometimes a little bird can go where an eagle dares not fly.”

He pauses.  “The little bird is me.”  He gestures at his shield, propped up next to him, currently displaying a small grey-and-orange bird perched on a branch on the top right and a rather peculiarly-colored duck on the bottom left.  He does swap up the birds a lot; this is less stylised than usual, and really quite terrible as heraldry not that he cares about field and color rules or indeed battlefield comprehensibility.  “That’s the easy part.  After that it gets complicated.”

A pause.  “The Twilight Lady is my liege, ultimately, as a Tyrwinghan, so I wonder about the sunset gate and the connection to her realm, for example.  The eagle is a great power, obviously, compared to my finch, and so that could be saying that I can accomplish things that more … momentous beings cannot, by being small and clever and hidden.  It might also suggest someone specific.”  A little gesture.  “Mostly what I get is that I am small, I can’t mend everything, but there is some *specific* thing that I - possibly we,” he gestures to encompass Viepuck and expands it to other members of the party in principle, “can do that matters.  Some fate.”

He shrugs.  “So I’ve been thinking about this since it was given to me and on the one hand I trust that the path will be apparent in its due time and on the other, if this was given to me, perhaps it means I have a chance to *miss* it without guidance.  Or the fey may just be tryng to be encouraging and I’m thinking too much.  It’s hard to tell with fey."
