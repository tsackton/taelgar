## Sun, 12 Oct 2025 22:19:08 -0400 — Mike Sackton <msackton@gmail.com>

Subject: Taelgar: Asineau as Bastion

Hi all,

Please use this thread for discussion about your bastion and what you might
want to build in Asineau. I will have other threads for
roleplaying/news/plot.

*Mechanics*
I am working on the assumption that the entire party will contribute to the
Asineau bastion. This gives you 8 special facilities. You can trade two
facilities for a Stable (technically a level 9 special facility) if you
wish. The special facilities all have game mechanics, as described here:
Bastions.
<https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/bastions#SpecialFacilityDescriptions>
Each
facility requires either one or two hirelings - typically an NPC who is
loyal to the party, although each party member can serve as one hireling if
you prefer. You can select NPCs you know
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/campaigns/cleenseau-campaign/cleenseau-campaign-index-of-npcs/#important-people-in-asineau>,
make one up yourself, or ask me to make one up for you.

I am going to rule that the entire village is effectively your bastion, so
you don't need to worry about space (unless you want to), nor worry about
basic facilities.

*Resources*
Excluded magic items, and items you've donated or sold, the party has a
decent pile of treasure, mostly from the treasure room you found in the
same ruined fort that had the green-orb-of-making-zombies. I have a
detailed list of everything you've found, but the summary is in total you
have about 900 GP. Almost half (400 GP) is from an exquisite diamond ring
you took from Areschera. Viepuck also has 400 GP (some coin, some credit)
from the Garay family, as his share of the spider silk scheme. (I will send
the detailed list in a separate email).

The manor of Asineau has about another 100 GP in gold reserves, and
although if you want to get into the details of management, in general you
can assume the manor is self-supporting and generates about 200 GP a year
in income, mostly from the sale of fish and wheat in the winter and spring.

The manor also has a pool of 800 person-days of labor. About 400 days are
usually spent working the manorial land, but you can spend the other 400 as
you wish on construction projects. In GP terms this is mostly unskilled
labor but you can "spend" it like 100 GP of labor costs.

*Existing Facilities*
The current manor has the following special facilities already in place:

*Stable <https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/bastions#Stable>*(Enlarged
to Vast).
This is tended by Bertram Northwoods
<https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/people/sembarans/bertram-northwoods/>,
a somewhat shy and retiring man who seems overwhelmed by current events. He
had no particular loyalty to the old lord, but also has no particular
aptitude for animals. There are four riding horses here, returned to the
estate after service with the army, and four draft horses.

*Armory <https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/bastions#Armory>*
The armory needs an overseer. It is stocked with weapons and armor for 10
guards.

*Sanctuary
<https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/bastions#Sanctuary>*
The Temple of the Wyrdling serves as a sanctuary. It is tended by El and
Eleanor.

*Barrack <https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/bastions#Barrack>*
There is currently space for a number of guards both in the manor house and
housed in the village. This functions as a barracks with five defenders
already recruited. You do need a guard captain, there is a soldier left
behind by the Army Garrison to keep an eye on things but she intends to
return to her post as soon as the new lord (i.e. you) arrives and settles
in. You can expand your barracks to a proper barracks for 1,000 GP, which
allows you to treat your barracks as vast for game purposes. You can also
recruit up to 50% more defenders than your barracks holds, but they are
militia and less effective.

*Walls*
You can build a rough wooden palisade for 5 GP per hex. This is about the
same condition as the current walls and won't last more than a season. You
can build a proper reinforced wooden wall for 20 GP per hex, and a solid
stone wall for 150 GP per hex. The cost is about 1/2 labor and 1/2
materials. Any clever ideas you have can certainly reduce the cost either
of materials or labor. Improving the "good condition" rough walls to
properly reinforced wooden walls costs 15 GP per hex. The ruined walls, if
you scavenge for materials, gives you 15 GP total in reusable timber.

One note on the wall: you cannot block or impede the road. The villagers
will also be uncertain about anything that blocks their access to the river
although you can certainly build walls along the cliff.

Let me know what you want to do. I can also share some advice, if you want.

Mike

The village has two principal granaries. The largest, which can store about
2000 bushels of wheat (or ~1/2 the harvest) is above the mill. There is an
additional granary set amongst the houses near the Wyrdling temple, which
is communally owned and maintained by the village, and can store a bit more
1000 bushels of wheat. (A bushel is about 1.25 cubic feet, so these are not
particularly large structures, although storage efficiency isn't perfect -
a 1000 bushels could fit in a cylinder about 8' radius/8' tall), and the
manor has a small granary in the yard (which can hold about 600 bushels) as
well as some space in the hayloft above the stable dedicated to grain
storage (which holds around 600 bushels or so).  Additionally, dried or
brined fish is stored in barrels in many cellars, especially the
manorial cellars. Plus roots and legumes and other vegetables, and some
smoked or salted meats (pig, mostly).

Food consumption is about 4 bushels / person / year for "lean" diet and 7.5
bushels / person / year for an "average" diet. This assumes some
fish/meat/veggies/cheese/butter to supplement, which I haven't bothered to
track in detail.

In general, stored grain usually runs out between April and June, and in
fact late spring/early summer can be the worst time of year, when the old
grain supplies are gone and the new crop hasn't grown in yet. In Asineau,
this is when the shad is running in the river though, so typically the diet
in April and May would be extremely fish heavy, plus eggs/cheese/milk, and
some early vegetables, and the dregs of the stored grain. It would be
reasonable to assume there is, for practical purposes, no grain reserves.
The first harvest is usually the barley in late July/early August, followed
by millet a month late, and wheat by mid-September.

What a Stable does:

After a Beast that can serve as a mount spends at least 14 days in this facility, all Wisdom (Animal Handling) checks made with respect to it have Advantage.
Trade: Animals. When you issue the Trade order to this facility, you commission the facility’s hireling to buy or sell one or more mounts at normal cost, keeping the ones you buy in your Stable. The work takes 7 days, and the DM decides what types of animals are available for purchase—horses, ponies, and mules being the most common. The Mounts and Other Animals table in the Player’s Handbook gives standard prices for various mounts. You bear the total cost of any purchases.
When you sell a mount from your Stable, the buyer pays you 20 percent more than the standard price; this profit increases to 50 percent when you reach level 13 and 100 percent when you reach level 17.

In general, bastion facilities with special magic abilities can only be
used by someone with the right type of magic. So the Sanctuary of the
Wyrdling can only be used by Celyn, as the only cleric of the Wyrdling. If
Robin wanted similar powers, he would need a sanctuary to the Wanderer. In
some cases, I may rule that two characters are similar enough to share a
facility, but in general think of having your own facilities for magical
things. Other facilities - like Barracks, Storehouse, Garden, etc are
common. Although by the rules each facility needs a hireling or two (so an
NPC) that rule is a bit flexible. For example, Robin could set up a small
altar to the Wanderer in his bedroom and tend to it himself, and I'd count
that as a Sanctuary (if Dan wanted). But that would use one of Dan's two
special facility slots.


> (The Asineau map could go into its wiki entry, btw.)
>

On my list to update the wiki. I have to re-remember the scripts to do it
though :)


>
> Local resources are agricultural and local industry is limited; arguably
> the local industry is covered by half a workshop’s worth of tools at most
> (Carpenter, Tinker, and Woodcarver possible, and probably just the
> Carpenter).  Immediate potential I perceive for additional Workshop-type
> stuff that’s Watsonianly sensible include Potter (if there is a clay field
> nearby that can be worked) and a Weaver and Leatherworker (sheep have been
> noted).
>

There probably is some local pottery and weaving, although the village
isn't diversified enough to have a full time weaver - households generally
spin and sew their own clothes, although I'm sure there is some sharing of
resources and spinning circles and the like. There are definitely small
clay deposits along the Enst, but no kiln or major pottery production. Most
pottery would be imported (I think I made up a source, but not at the right
computer to check my notes). Anyway, outside the Cleenseau region.
Leatherworking is done in Cleenseau currently.

Mike
