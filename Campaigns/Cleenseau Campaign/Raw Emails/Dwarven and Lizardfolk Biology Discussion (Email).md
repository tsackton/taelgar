# Dwarven and Lizardfolk Biology Discussion (Email)

Local archive of 3 message bodies, including quoted reply history. Correspondence on possible species biology. The dwarven proposal was not adopted here; the lizardfolk idea was exploratory, not established canon.

## Mon, 12 Feb 2024 12:51:02 -0500 — Artan Eter <artan.eter@gmail.com>

Subject: A thought on dwarves

So I’ve had a background thought percolating on dwarves and classic fantasy dwarven culture for a while.

Standard depictions of dwarves include:
* obsession with stuff - material wealth or personal goods or just something like that
* obsession with story - of the clan, associations, family, myth, etc.
* obsession with personal acclaim - be it through becoming a true master of something, (craft, battle, etc), family acclaim, (bob son of bob son of bob is much more important than Ted, even if they have the same resume).
* tendency toward phenotypical male-dwarf presentation, women seem rarer.

So a couple interesting things can come up with this if you think about hormone influences on development.  Lots of species are environmental-sensitive for sex-development, so it’s entirely possible that dwarves turn out to be similar (though they may not know it).  But breed to 3 categories - F, standard reproductive capability, f-Dwarf phenotype; X - sterile, think natural eunuchs, but m-dwarf phenotype, probably composing 1/3-1/2 (depending on circumstances) the population but the average non-dwarf would never be able to tell, so it just looks like there are a lot more m-dwarves., and M - m-dwarf phenotype, standard male.

X-dwarves have the kind of standard racial tendency toward obsessiveness sometimes, but less personal drive, tend to be more workers and less Masters.  Think a natural eunuch class.

F-dwarves are deeply valued and respected, because dwarf-mothers as a core family point are deeply respected by all offspring.  And can be equally skilled craftsmen, politicians, farmers, etc.  Strength equal for m/f/x dwarves, with less species phenotype differentiation, though recognizable by anyone familiar with dwarves.  Tend to not do deep mining as much, that tends to be more x-dwarves, with a few m-dwarves in charge - deep mining is dangerous even if the rewards can be high.

M-dwarves are NOT by default fertile.  I saw some interesting things with testosterone levels, and they are heavily influenced by personal accomplishment and status.  So possibly m-dwarves tend to have a little more personal drive than x-dwarves, but otherwise present the same (too-low-T to differentiate), UNTIL they manage to gain some kind of personal acclaim (craft mastery, war prowess, political acclaim, a particularly good harvest, born to named lineage even (borrowed acclaim)),  Only after that are they fertile and can produce offspring.  So Tim-son-of-Ted is as much a big deal because that means Ted had specific prowess and regard.

This might also result in f-dwarves wanting children but bound to a yet-interfile m-dwarf or x-dwarf (or other f-dwarf, but that is an obvious seek-elsewhere problems) to seek a m-dwarf of renown, those children often coming away with Tim-son-of-Jane names (if Jane was herself a master-something or had particular acclaim), or just holding clan-name.

I would expect this wouldn’t be seen as negative in dwarven culture, just a thing people do, and such children would be readily embraced by x/m-dwarf partners anyway, as that child might turn out to have parallel interests, or bring fame, etc etc.  Just not get to have the extra-moniker of son-of-Ted, giving them the extra self-confidence/T-boost, making them more likely to be fertile themselves and carry that named-derivation line.

Also explains why you don’t get half-dwarves, with them working on an entirely different breeding/development system.  I suspect some amount of m-dwarves just never differentiate and remain x-dwarves, or may drop back if hit with some kind of deep personal shame.  F-dwarf fertility rate probably similar to humans, but have a much easier time of the process, being able to do heavy work probalby up until birth and soon after.

With the longer lifespan and moderate fertility, you do end up with a lot of die-off probably in the pre-60-range either from x-dwarves in accidents or m-dwarves doing something dangerous to try to attain glory.

Anyway, felt like an interesting way to keep within standard dwarf fantasy culture and yet completely differentiate the species function from other expectations.  I thought you might find it an interesting concept.  Feel free to grab bits if they make sense.

Chris

## Mon, 12 Feb 2024 18:15:26 -0500 — Mike Sackton <msackton@gmail.com>

Subject: Re: A thought on dwarves

I like the idea of messing with biology and family structures as part of a
way to make other species seem unique. For dwarves in Taelgar specifically
I'm not sure this fits (there is a lot of game lore already around dwarves)
which doesn't quite fit the "standard fantasy" bit, in particular there is
no pattern that suggest that male-phenotype (i.e. beards) is more common
than other phenotypes. And dwarves have this "sacred thuhr" thing that is
what drives mastery in a way that doesn't necessarily fit with sex
differentiation.

That said, I think the idea of creatures not being fertile by default and
messing around with sex differentiation and testosterone levels might be
something that fits lizardfolk better... The "mastery" thing isn't quite a
lizardfolk thing but I like the idea of a fantasy species where fertility
is not tied to a monogamous couple necessarily and where it comes and goes
based on specific actions, rather than how you were born.
