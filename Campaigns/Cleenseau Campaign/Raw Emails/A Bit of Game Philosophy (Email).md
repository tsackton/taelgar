## Mon, 08 Jan 2024 14:22:00 +0000 — Dan Walters <arcadinal@protonmail.com>

Subject: [Thantos] A Bit of Game Philosophy

So, your mileage may vary with this sort of thing, but here's my take on reactions and response/support type skills in general.

I love them and I wish there were more of them. Skills that replicate that moment in a movie where a bad guy is about to stab someone and another character dives in to deflect the blow at the last moment, or in an anime when a mage is trying to batter down an enemy's defenses and two other characters step to add their power and together they take the villain down (see like...half of Dragonball Z, plenty of super hero fights, combination attacks in Chronotrigger, etc.).

As examples for me...holding a doorway and being missed because of my armor is cool and laying into the wight from behind is also certainly cool. But WAY cooler (for me anyway) were moments like when that skeleton tried to go after Celyn and I smacked it down before it could get the hit in, or when I was about to get hit with the arrow curse and other party members started throwing defensive spells to deflect the attack. I love that sort of thing, that's exciting to me.

It's exciting to get a lucky crit roll to smack an opposing armored foe (like the wight), but even more-so would be a moment like if I took a hammer swing and bashed his shield out of the way and then Celyn put an arrow through its face while its defense was down. Think the battle against the cave troll in Fellowship where Legolas aims, Marry (or was it Pippin) who was still holding into the troll stabs it in the neck, and when the troll bellows in pain Legolas shoots it in the mouth. That sort of teamwork take down, at least for me, is really thrilling compared to just one person getting lucky on a dice roll...makes me feel the team love.

Also I agree with Mike on asymmetry of NPC vs. PC actions. I expect lieutenant and boss level monsters to be dishing out a lot of damage (and stuff like that arrow curse), and then the PCs get to do tactical heroic stuff to keep each other on their feet. As you Mike says it's not much fun from the PC side to have a success and a cool moment taken away, but it's immensely satisfying to foil an enemy attack that's about to bring to hurt to a friend. When I was staffing at Madrigal LARP, my question to myself when stating out a boss monster I was going out as was always how was I going to use those abilities to generate cool moments for PCs...I was always disappointed for instance if I actually managed to kill a PC before their buddies could jump to the rescue, but when they pulled off the rescue I'd be exhilarated, because that sort of cooperative rescue was exactly the sort of positive drama I was trying to generate.

Daniel Walters

## Mon, 8 Jan 2024 13:13:50 -0500 — Mike Sackton <msackton@gmail.com>

Subject: Re: [Thantos] A Bit of Game Philosophy

Dan,

I agree with all of that for sure. And actually, I thought the combat was
great, and I loved the moments when you all were able to deflect the bad
stuff.

But I also think that if the deflect is *too good*, you never have any
damage. If the wight had never hit anyone, I think that would have taken
something away from the drama, as there is nothing really to be scared of
-- it is a lot more satisfying to have a terrifying cursed arrow that's hit
you once, and to deflect the second time, so you don't die, then to just
walk through the fight with no damage at all.  Now, I can always just fudge
die rolls to generate the right amount of dramatic tension, but I find that
hard to do in the moment, as it feels very predetermined. So I find it
easier to just run the combat straight other than sometimes tweaking HP
levels if it feels like the bad guy needs more (or less) staying power.

I think a spell like Silvery Barbs is overpowered from a game design sense
-- and you might say, who cares? We are not in competition! And I'd agree
with you. The only two reasons I care about game design abstractions are:

1. It is a good way to make sure the party is balanced - I don't want
anyone to feel "left out" of the combat, or like they can't keep up with
everyone else's cool stuff, and it gets hard to fudge everything in game.
So for example if I am constantly boosting Izgil's spells but not
Viepuck's, we end up in a situation where Izgil is the dominant spellcaster
and Viepuck gets left behind. Paying attention to game rules helps to avoid
that.

2. It makes it easier for me to plan combats. Combats where there is no
danger at all are, I think, somewhat boring (although it can be fun every
once and a while to wallop the bad guys, it isn't fun *all the time*). The
D&D CR mechanism is a pretty good way to figure out what types of enemies
will typically present a reasonable challenge, and since I like
worldbuilding a lot more than combat planning I tend to like to lean on the
D&D CR mechanic to plan out the difficulty of combat. This starts to break
down the more powerful the party is compared to a "typical" party of your
level -- and you guys already have a number of advantages on a RAW party
(bonus stats + feats, to start).

That said, maybe tweaking the range of Silvery Barbs is not the right
change or even particularly necessary. The wight did end up managing to
come across as scary. I just prefer combats where (a) there is some
dramatic tension and (b) I don't have to do a ton of on the fly tweaking to
create the dramatic tension. At the same time, I don't like to arbitrarily
limit what characters can do, as that takes away agency. So I'm looking for
cases where I feel like the rules work against creating a sense of danger
in a big fight and whether there is something that would be better from a
rules perspective.

I also do like the abstract "game design" theory, so I get sucked into this
type of conversation in the abstract when it doesn't really matter. But
again, mostly it just really helps me when the party isn't unexpectedly way
more powerful than I was anticipating.

Unless maybe everyone just likes fights to be cakewalks. I can work with
that if it is what everyone prefers (again, honest question).

Mike

No one currently has Guidance, but it is a good spell.  Celyn has some
potentially duplicative cantrips, so if Kiya wants to swap for it, I'm
happy with that. If so, I'd suggest this version of Guidance below:

*Guidance*
> Casting Time:Reaction, which you take in response to you or an ally within
> 10 feet of you failing an Ability Check
> Range:10 feet
> Components:V, S
> Duration:Instantaneous
> You channel magical insight to the creature who failed the Ability Check.
> That creature can roll a d4 and add the number rolled to the check,
> potentially turning it into a success.


This is more fun than the regular Guidance because it doesn't use
concentration, so you don't have to worry about dropping some other spell,
and it is in reaction to a failure, so you are more likely to remember to
use it, and no one wastes time rolling dice when you would have succeeded
anyway.
Do note that you need to know you failed, on certain types of knowledge,
insight or perception checks you might not be able to use it.

Mike
