A few notes on this: the document was found in a merchant's house (who is
actually the grandson of Roland and Lizette Ausson, remember them?) and he
kept it basically because it was from the since-destroyed city of Isginue.
It's a bit confusing because there are three bits:
* A book of commentary, originally written 700 years ago, but translated
from the Drankorian to Isinguen about 300 years ago.
* Which is commentating on a play, also maybe a thousand years old
* Which is quoting, as part of the commentary, another book, of unknown age
(but obviously older than 700 year old book of reviews it is quoting)

Secondarily, some of the words are particularly badly translated, and might
not be accurate. I've pushed a new version to the website with those bits
in italics, but in particular the phrase *shootout amongst the mighty *was
scratched out and rewritten a bunch of times and Izgil felt like it was not
likely an accurate reflection of what the author was trying to say.

---

The main bit of clear knowledge Celyn or Robin would have is that
Twilight's Grace is Ethlenn's realm and is closely associated with
Tyrwingha. There are stories about the fey, and in particular, that any
Tyrwinghan who wants to can abandon the mortal realm for Twilight's Grace,
although how exactly that happens is a bit mysterious. Ethlenn is seen as
quite powerful, although most Tyrwingha's would believe or expect that her
fey realm and Tyrwingha are kinda-sorta-maybe the same, and that her power
outside her realm might not be quite so powerful.

Remember: and this is something Celyn would know. The Feywild is weird --
time passes strangely. But also, the sun doesn't move across the sky --
each place in the fey wild is always the same time of day. So you move
across the day by moving across the Feywild. So for example, there is some
place (Sunset Gate, maybe?) were it is always sunset. And Twilight's Grace
is east of the sunset, so it is always night there. So *anyone* who moves
to Twilight's Grace will never see the sun again, as the sun never moves in
the feywild.

I didn't write it up but the book would go into more details about each of
these misdeeds. Gloomshaper's wicked deeds were a series of tricks he
played on mortals: find someone on a floundering boat at sea, for example,
and offer to "take them to a dry place" in the Feywild, for example, as
long as they gave him something (their good looks, their strength, their
hair, whatever), and then it turns out that when he said "a dry place in
the Feywild" he meant some wild land in the part that is always night, or
an endless maze, or whatever -- not somewhere safe. Classic "dark" fey
trickery, basically.

None of the stories Celyn or Robin has heard suggest much relationship
between fey and undead -- the fey, whatever else they are, are *alive *and
mortal(ish). They aren't soulstealers like devils or necromancers. The
Shadowfell (a dark mirror of the feywild, in a way) is more related to
undeath, but nothing you've seen suggests that the Shadowfell is at play
here -- i.e. don't think of "fey wild" = "seelie", "shadowfell" =
"unseelie" or anything like that. The shadowfell isn't a place fey would be
found at all.
