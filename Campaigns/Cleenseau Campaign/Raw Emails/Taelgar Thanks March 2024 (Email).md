# Taelgar Thanks March 2024 (Email)

Local archive of all 5 message bodies in this correspondence, including quoted reply history. Player and DM reflections on the preceding session, including tactics actually used.

## Mon, 04 Mar 2024 21:59:56 +0000 — Dan Walters <arcadinal@protonmail.com>

Subject: [Thantos] Taelgar: Thanks

BTW, I just wanted to say thanks for last session.

I found the moving combat against the skeleton and hoard to be wonderfully exhilarating. It had a lot of elements I really like in a battle, particularly interesting terrain to make use of and changing situations (as we moved towards the town) done in a way that we had a chance to plan, react, and adjust. The pacing felt fluid, dynamic, and exciting.

I enjoyed orchestrating the cavalry raids (I love getting the chance to do more tactical stuff like that).

I really enjoyed how on point everyone was with cooperation and teamwork. There were a bunch of cool rescue moments...just in time heals, groups of enemies being held at bay, Caelyn popping in to stab one of the skeleton clerics, Izgul holding the far side of the bridge, Viepuck wearing the giant down until we could gang up on it (also getting four criticals was a nice change from my usual streaks of disappointing rolls)...I just was really into how that all came together, it felt like we went up against something the could have really wrecked the field (especially if it had gotten inside the town) and we just did a good job handling it as a team. I was feeling the group love and it was awesome.

So, good combat, good GMing, good PCing...y'all did amazing and I had an amazing time.

Daniel Walters

## Mon, 4 Mar 2024 17:55:55 -0500 — Mike Sackton <msackton@gmail.com>

Subject: Re: [Thantos] Taelgar: Thanks

Thanks, I also had a really good time and felt like that combat came
together well. I never know exactly how much behind the scenes stuff to
explain, but the ranged harassment especially from spell sniping eldritch
blast also made a huge difference. It might not have been obvious from how
things played out, but one of the key challenges was separating the zombies
from the skeleton - if you had been taking 5-10 hits from zombies each turn
while fighting the skeletal beast you would have been doomed. The ranged
harassment scared them, basically, and forced them to adopt a "move fast to
get into battle" tactic which naturally separated the skeletal and the
zombies. It also redirected the skeletal to fighting you guys rather than
the town.

So good job on that as well.

Mike

## Tue, 5 Mar 2024 07:49:55 -0500 — Artan Eter <artan.eter@gmail.com>

Subject: Re: [Thantos] Taelgar: Thanks

I don’t think we could have taken another 5-10 hits from zombies at all, let alone each round for a time.  Things were heroic, but we were also stretched thin at the end.

That was also one of the only times I’ve seem RPGs properly make use of “the party has horses and can move about”.  A lot of standard tabletop combat is go in, slug/skirmish small-scale, die-or-don’t.  The ability to actually be able to disengage and regroup and have it matter rather than immediately be chased down the next round was nice.
