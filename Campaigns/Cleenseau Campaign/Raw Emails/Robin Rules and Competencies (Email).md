Subject: Robin - Rules stuff

Dan,

Hey, I realize you are busy. Just trying to close off a few rules for
Robin. Do not feel stressed if you don't manage to respond to this, we can
just quickly use it as a checklist to talk through on Sunday.

-----------------------

*Fey Touched Spell*

*Option 1: Mark of the Fey. *
Bonus Action, Concentration Required. 1 Hour Duration.
You mystically mark your target within 90 feet with a fey mark. Until the
spell ends, you deal an extra 1d6 damage to the target whenever you hit it
with a weapon attack, and the target has disadvantage on Stealth or
Deception checks to deceive/hide from you.

[This is basically a fey-skinned hunters mark]

*Option 2: Fey Duel*
Bonus Action, Concentration Required. 1 minute duration.
You compel a creature to fight you, instead of others. One creature you can
see within 30 feet must make a Wisdom Saving throw. On a failed save, the
creature is locked in a duel with you. It has disadvantage on attack rolls
against creatures other than you, and must make an additional Wisdom Saving
throw each time it attempts to move away from you. If it succeeds on the
save, it can move freely that turn, but is still under the effect of the
spell. A target that is immune to charm cannot be so compelled.

The spell ends if you attack any other creature, if you cast a harmful
spell on another target, if an ally attacks and damages the target, if you
end your turn more than 30 feet away from the target, or if an ally casts a
harmful spell targeting the target. Incidental damage (i.e. from an area of
effect spell) does not end the spell.

[This is basically a fey themed Compelled Duel]

*Option 3: Free the Mind*
Action, Instantaneous
As an action, you target one creature within 30 feet of you that is charmed
or frightened. This effect ends on them.

[This is totally made up]

*Option 4: Truthseeker*
Action, 1 minute, no concentration required. Verbal component only, a
single spoken command.
As an action, you compel a single target to speak only the truth for the
next minute. The target makes a Charisma saving throw. On a failure, the
target must speak the truth, or be silent. On a success, the target is not
compelled but you have advantage on any Insight checks against the target
until the duration ends, and the target is immune to this compulsion for
the next 24 hours. A target that is immune to charm cannot be so compelled.

[This is totally made up]

If you don't like any of those, let me know, you could of course pick
something from the book. I actually think the Fey Duel (option 2) would be
a very good fit for Robin, as potentially would Option 4.

-------------------------

*Lay On Hands*

We originally made it a dice pool because it seemed easier to me to spend
dice rather than points. If you really want a precise healing pool though
we can change it. You'd have 5 * level healing, and all the powers would
cost current/2, rounded up, * 5 healing.

So you'd have 30 HP healing pool, and the various cures that cost 1 would
cost 5 points, the cures that cost 5/6 would also cost 15 points, and the
cures that cost 7 or 8 would cost 20 points. This is a slightly more
expensive cost for the special actions, and the benefit of more predictable
healing.

Totally up to you which you take.

----------------------------------

*Shield Master*

I wanted to clarify: I gave you 2 options on character creation for shield
abilities.
Option 1: Use reaction to impose disadvantage on an enemy attack.
Option 2: Creatures trying to get past you to attack allies provoke
opportunity attacks.

You picked Option 1 which is why you couldn't target the harpies with
opportunity attacks.

----------------------------------

*Spells*

Wanted to remind you of a few spells I added as always prepared:
* Calm Emotions https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2023-calm-emotions
* Warding Bond https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2296-warding-bond (but
without the material component)

Warding Bond in particular is very Robin-esque, so I wanted to make sure
you were aware of it.

-----------------------------------

*Competencies*

You have 6 unused competencies. A few ideas:

Tyrwinghan - currently Robin only knows Sembaran, quickest analogy on the
assumption you don't want my 1000 word dissertation on languages:
Tyrwinghan is like Welsh or Irish or Scots Gaelic in the 1800s. Sembaran is
like English. Lots of people speak both, but it is possible to only speak
Sembaran or only speak Tyrwinghan.

Soldier - basically knowledge of military life, battlefield tactics, basic
quartermastery, etc - comes from his background being trained at the Aben
garrison of the Tyrwinghan army, plus recent experience semi-leading troops.

Orator - this is the art of being a great public speaker. You don't need
this to be good at persuading people (you already have persuasion), and
your +3 Charisma means Robin has natural energy/inspiration. Being an
Orator would be specifically the ability to give good, rousing speeches.
The game effect would probably be if you roll well on Orator, you get
advantage on Persuasion.

Some random artisan skill: is there something Robin does in his spare time?
Sewing? Woodcarving? Painting? Poetry?

Military Strategist: this has come up a bunch recently and is plausibly
something Robin might have learned i.e. from Ida. Not sure when you'd roll
it, exactly, but it would be a mechanical marker that Robin is better than
Dan at military strategies. Might serve as me giving you a warning if I saw
a fatal flaw in some plan that Robin would logically also see.

An extra language or two is never crazy, especially say goblin, which Robin
might have learned for spy/military reason

----

That's it. Again, I don't really expect you to have read this far! But if
you do, great, if not we can run through anything here quickly in person.

Mike
