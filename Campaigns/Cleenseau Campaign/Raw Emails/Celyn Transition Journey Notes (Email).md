I figured some nattering might be of use, feel free to share with Dana and Charli if you think any of it would help.



It’s really damn hard to figure out how to approach medical transition possibilities when the information that one actually _has_ is “I’m definitely not this one”.  This is not sufficient information to define a vector.  I actually restarted therapy a bit more than a year ago to get _unstuck_ on this question, among other things.  While the stereotype is clear dysphoria and physical wrongness, there are plenty of people who don’t have that sort of clear understanding of what’s wrong.

I spent years trying to get an ontology of gender and sexuality sorted out— I used to be a blogger on the fringes of the feminist blogosphere and would get into extended discussions of the topic, partly because I felt very unsecure in my capacity to understand myself as a woman.  (I described it as the dancer on the end of the kickline who’s half in the curtains because they don’t know the steps.)

I went from “I guess I’m a woman because that’s the body I’ve got but enh” to “oh, definitely not a woman” when I was pregnant with Kat and got absolutely wrecked by social dysphoria.  The problem I had was of course that “I’m not that” doesn’t answer “what I actually am”.

I spent a long time stuck on the notion that I needed to be able to answer “what I actually am” conclusively before I could seek any sort of affirming care, even as my awareness of my issues got worse and more exhausting over time.  (There is a known phenomenon where acknowledging dysphoria makes it feel worse because it’s not ignorable anymore, and I am, historically, a _champion_ dissociator, I don’t remember most of a whole year of high school level champion dissociator.  I have a lot of stuff coming off ignore.)

Which means my big recommendation for Charli is: don’t assume you have to have a clear answer before trying things.  If you don’t like a hairstyle you can get a haircut.  Experiment, try things out, figure out what feels right and what feels nope.  And don’t get hung up on ‘is this the correct gender’ when what matters is ‘is this correct for me’.  You don’t have to know what you are to know what feels right or good.

This extends to HRT, by the way.  If a hormone palette is actively wrong then it will almost certainly show up faster than any significant physical changes.  Things that I did not know in my twenties when going on the Pill sent me into a savage multi-year depression that was only addressed when I clawed enough out of it to ask for a different formulation and got a high-testosterone version….  I have an ex who is on both supplemental T and supplemental E to try to figure out what is best for their system and psyche.  Several of my lingering “unrelated” health issues have cleared up since I started hormones, which makes me certainly feel a way.  (That was an _autoimmune_ disease….)

The thing about dysphoria is there are tons of things that one doesn’t know are dysphoria before they get fixed.  I was talking with Alec (a trans elder and now, to my immense bafflement and wonder, a friend) about my neck and shoulder tension and he said, “That’s dysphoria.”  I was very WTF about that, but … some of the issues with that have gone away post-surgery.  I am pretty sure some of how I am about music is actually a dysphoria thing.  The way I hate yelling ‘thank you’ up the driveway at the UPS guy who has just dropped off a box, definitely dysphoria.  It’s weird.  And because of fish not being able to identify water, there’s stuff that just won’t parse as such until after the fact.

The only silver lining about potentially having a couple of weeks off my hormones is being able to see what that does for me because it’s not fishwater anymore. :/


Anyway, that’s what I have for a start on thoughts.
