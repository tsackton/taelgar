> > 3 - Is Fellburn in the same Barrony as Clenseau?
>

No. Basically you have: in the west, the mountains and then the military
Barony of Dunfry. Next, to the east is Cleenseau, which is the *western *edge
of the Barony of Aveil. 60 miles east from Cleenseau is Rinburg, which is a
free city (not part of any barony), and also the *eastern *edge of the
Barony of Aveil. Another 100 miles east is Fellburn, which is a large city
of 5000-6000, and one of (but not the largest) city in the Duchy of Wisford.

(The map here might help:
https://msackton.github.io/taelgarverse1720/gazetteer/greater-sembara/sembara/sembara/
- the baronies and duchies are all labeled)

The Duchy of Wisford is an ancient, wealthy duchy (the duchy predates the
formation of Sembara). A few points about the Duchy of Wisford:

* The current Duke is Jacques Bellemont, but none of you know much about him
* The Dukes of the ancient duchies such as Wisford hold enormous sway in
their duchies, acting almost as the monarch. They don't control the army
though, which has a separate royal command structure
* Rosalind mentioned in one of her letters that she was stopping to visit
the Duke, whose father fought with her grandfather, and she called the Duke
"an astute political observer"

Certainly true, although one point to make: it seems from everything you've
heard that the *local dead* are rising but they seem poorly directed.
Rosalind mentioned in her letter that the skeletons seemed mindless, and
practically ignored their riverboat. Ida said they same about the zombies -
they didn't seem to have a goal, or any direction. It feels more like a
situation where some almost random number of dead arises in each village or
hamlet, not something where there is a large directed army. It seems like
where there are larger groups of undead, it is because there were larger
pools of dead - Fellburn is famous for being the site of several large
battles over the last 300 years, some of which the Sembarans lost. So the
area is likely dense with unconsecrated mass graves.
