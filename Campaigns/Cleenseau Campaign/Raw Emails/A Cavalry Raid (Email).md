I was also thinking in terms of how a cavalry raid might be useful, however, the question is whether we're the ones to do it given the needs locally.

Question for Mike, how many horses does Ida's garrison have, or how many could we reasonably requisition that are well trained enough not to spook in a fight? Does she have any soldiers trained to fight on horseback, and if so, how many?

If it is truly a mindless undead mob with no necromancer guiding it (yet...might be nice to thin out those numbers before one shows up), an entirely viable strategy might be to send out a small mounted warband to go make noise, harry the enemy a bit, retreat, come back, harry them again, etc. If these are standard dumb 'braiiiiiiins' style zombies without direction, a small mounted group could ride around getting a bunch of their attention and then basically leading them off on random goose chases all over the place, maybe picking off some here and there when they can be isolated. This potentially accomplishes two things:

One, if part of the hoard detaches to chase after meat that isn't behind a wall, that takes some momentary pressure off the defenders and might allow them to muster enough manpower to counterstrike and thin out the enemy a little.

Two, it lets them know they're not alone, which in terms of keeping up fighting spirits is likely worth quite a lot.

I was mistaken; I thought Greymalkin had a different speed than a warhorse,
but both him and a warhorse have speed 60'. He tires a little less easily

Ida's garrison is split into two "patrols" - the Bridge Patrol (20
soldiers), who mostly stay close to town, watch the bridge, etc and are
neither equipped nor trained for cavalry actions, and the River Patrol (35
soldiers), led by Evelyn (the woman who was interacting with Greymalkin and
a horse person). The River Patrol is a mounted patrol, who often rides
south to respond to incidents in the badlands on the south side of the
river. They would be well-trained on horseback and equipped with a
reasonable number of horses to support say 2 patrols of 10-12 troops at a
time (so maybe 25-30 horses?).

This is before losses and folks left behind in Dunfry. So say something
like:

Original strength = 55 soldiers, 25 warhorses, 15 pack animals
Left in Dunfry = 10 soldiers
Died on the road = 15 soldiers, 5 horses, 5 pack animals
Now in town = 30 soldiers, 20 horses, 10 pack animals. Most of the soldiers
(25 of 30) would be trained mounted combatants as it was the Bridge Patrol,
mostly, that stayed behind.

Outside the army garrison, there are plenty of farm work animals around,
but these are mostly donkeys or draft horses, and drawing on that stock of
animals for cavalry mounts probably wouldn't be a good idea. Could be a
hundred work horses in the region though, or even a bit more. (I'm not
honestly sure what is realistic).

Beyond that, Essford Manor had a good number of warhorses - 10 or so - but
all but 4 (Rinault and Harold's personal horses, Wymar's horse - an aging
stallion, plus a spare) left with Rosalind. (Plus there is Noble, of course)
The other lords definitely have a few warhorses around: a half dozen in
Beury, a couple in Valit. Lord Valbert unfortunately took five good horses
with him when he left (although, I guess, if you wanted to try to hunt him
down to take back the horses, he's probably in the haunted bog past Beury
right now).
There are three good, fast riding horses stabled at the River's Blessing,
for use by the crown messenger service. But you might not want to try to
requisition these.

Beyond that, there are probably a couple dozen riding horses stabled in
various places in Cleenseau, and another dozen across the three smaller
villages - but these would be of varying training and not all able to
actually fight.

If you want to try to build up a larger calvary force you could ask Evelyn
(or if any of you are good at animal handling, do it yourself) to go try to
requisition some horses, but you are basically taking riding horses of the
wealthy merchants so you might be some pushback, especially if you were not
paying.

The horses for the various lords of the manor are more accessible, although
folks are going to want to keep a few horses for their own mobility.

Mike

Pending Mike's additional briefing, and need some time to look over the information sent about what resources we have, but I'm envisioning something like...

A group of somewhere between 8 and 12 volunteers (exact numbers pending) who understand this could get very dangerous but are willing to help. Outfitted as light cavalry for speed...enough supplies for the journey to and from plus about a week in the field.

Travel to Rinburg, get and give news about what's going on in the area. Deliver a message (to be penned later) proposing formal communications between Rinburg and Clenseau for the purposes of forming a coherent regional defense. Do not engage any foes on the road, circumvent if they can, return if they can't.

Travel to Fellburn, assess the situation. If the group commander thinks they can proceed, orchestrate a series distracting hit and run strikes on undead forces. The purpose is less to get kills and more to try and create distractions and draw clumps of the enemy away and take pressure off the defense, hopefully letting the defenders make some progress.

At least a couple of the group should be devoted purely to battlefield awareness and making sure the group doesn't surrounded or cut off. If there is skeletal cavalry or something on the field that looks like it could give serious chase, leave. If something nasty shows up (floating undead, spectral undead, undead riding a pile of bones, anything that even vaguely hints of a spell caster), leave. The number one job before anything else is, seriously, don't die.

If contact can be made with the cities defenders, exchange what news they can and let them know they're not forgotten.

When either supplies are close to expended or the situation becomes too dangerous to continue, return to Rhinburg, exchange information again, return home.

Daniel Walters

More briefing will come, but a few distances to keep in mind:

Cleenseau to Rinberg is 60 miles along a very good road.
Rinburg to Fellburn is 100 miles along the same very good road.

Your cavalry unit, equipped with 2.5x the number of horses as people and
changing horses as they ride can cover ~60 miles/day for 2-3 days as long
as they are riding along the very good road.
Without the excess horses, they can cover more like ~30 miles/day.

So that's ~4.5 days to Fellburn, or ~3 days with sufficient horses.
Or ~1 day to Rinburg with fast horses, ~2 days without.
