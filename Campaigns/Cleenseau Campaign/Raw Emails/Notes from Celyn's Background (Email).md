Since Celyn's opinions about how a village economy works are turning out to be relevant, this is the set of assumptions I’ve been working from to form them.  About the Clawyn economy (with the assistance of my medren major friend who gave me reference links and a first pass for sense):




I assume that the Nefyn-and-outlying-villages system is as a whole food-and-basic-goods-sufficient (because that’s how it works in a medieval landscape) but the individual outlying villages may be short on some necessities that are produced either in Nefyn or one of the other villages, so market town is a major thing.

Nefyn has heavy grain production and also some cattle because it’s in the flat, which means it is also the source for plow oxen on the occasions that one needs a new plow ox.  It also has all of the small industries that benefit from centralization or aren’t supportable in the outlying villages.  (The dyer, an apothecary, and several inns I have mentioned in stories.)

Clawyn itself does not produce grain because of the hilly terrain, so flour and such are all obtained from Nefyn.  (This is of course part of why Celyn doesn’t have a taste for beer; buying grain in surplus to needs when there’s perfectly good wine right here is economically silly.)

Craftsmen in Clawyn specifically mentioned: potter, smith, cooper, and weaver.  There is no glassblower because there is no local sand, and glass bottles for fancy wine exports are among the imports.  I have not specified a clay source; possibly a combination of a nearby deposit along a streambed and the Nefyn market.  Wood for the cooper is locally sourced, but the iron for the bands is imported.  I am assuming when they have goats for slaughter (whether because of age or young male culls) or an end-of-life ox they go to the outskirts of Nefyn and deal with a slaughterhouse and tanner there, trading a percentage of the meat/leather to the craftsmen there and taking back the rest.  I would expect some basic woodworking for utensils and the like, since they are up against the forest, but probably not for export.

Like in many medieval villages, anyone with idle hands knows how to spin; the fiber is mostly either from gathered nettles (which make an excellent cloth actually) or fiber from the goats that graze the nearby slopes that are not suited to planting grapevines due to steepness or soil quality.  The goats are something like angora so the cloth could be a fancy export in theory but in practice this is subsistence cloth production heavily supplemented with importation.  The amount of research I have done on cloth production to work on the unfinished story about Celyn’s strong opinions about textiles is immense.

Local food supplies are probably somewhat insufficient if they can’t get flour from Nefyn, but include: communal vegetable fields in the space that’s flat enough to be worth having a couple oxen for ploughing (and otherwise working hauling carts of barrels around and such), food trees in the span between the village and the fairy-infested forest proper (mostly fruit, maybe some nuts, I don’t think I’ve specified anything other than apples and rowan), berry bushes various places, goat meat and milk in season, supplemented with foraging and hunting that has to be mediated by the people with boundary training in fey diplomacy to make sure nobody fucks it up.  (This heavily informs Celyn’s opinions about food security in the absence of solid exports.)  There are probably beehives because why would there not be beehives.

Clawyn has no proper inn, but does have something of a meetinghall.  The family that is attached to this building maintains communal food supplies, the bakehouse, and the food storage, which means that there are more workers freed up to produce the communal food and work in the vineyards and winery because they can go to the meetinghall to get food afterwards.

I figure an impoverished level of sufficiency in food and clothing (on the food possibly where Asineau is now or a little worse) that is balanced in trade with Nefyn for grain and occasional beef/other cow-based products and cloth imports to supplement the locally produced cloth and leather.  (I figure any village tries to at least scrape by on food and clothing as most essential supplies, just from historical knowledge.)

Exports from Clawyn potentially include: the wine, of course; a very little bit of wood, not a full-on timbering operation because any cutting is a diplomatic negotiation but enough to handle local needs and a bit down to Nefyn (doing research for this suggests they may have a saw pit in the village to split planks for local use and sale of leftovers rather than dragging logs further), foraged goods and kindling gathered from the forest.

Imports: wine bottles, supplies for the blacksmith, grain/flour and thatching straw from Nefyn.  More optional imports are on the order of magnitude of “more food variety” and “more cloth” and practical tools/utensils, not luxuries.  Salt.

This all makes sense to me. One note, I forget how close Nefyn is, but
grain would typically be stored unmilled rather than as flour. Not sure if
it would be that there is a miller in Clawyn who imports grain from Nefyn,
or if people just cart sacks up flour every month or so.

As a note, here is my thinking for the Cleenseau region economy, which
maybe you could also run by your friend.

There are three villages (Beury, Valit, Asineau), one major town
(Cleenseau), and two hamlets (Tavoise and Auloutte).

At a regional level, the major imports are: iron and other metals, mostly
from Maseau; ceramics from Rinburg; glass (not sure where from yet), plus
luxuries (foodstuffs, alcohol, fine cloth/clothes, furniture)
The major exports are: weapons and armor and other finished metal goods
from Cleenseau; grain (mostly sold west to the army), plus some charcoal to
the villages just to the east (Dallit, Champimont) with poorer wood supplies

The region is self-sufficient in food---

Grains are wheat (probably mostly winter wheat, actually), barley (main
crop), and millet (summer crop, grown on poor soils). Millet greens are
sometimes eaten in early summer. Hay is common and there is generally
sufficient pasture if not always of very high quality.
Livestock is mostly pigs and sheep, with some small cattle herds mostly for
dairy, plus chickens and geese.
Other protein sources include significant amounts of fish (shad and trout
in spring and fall, eels in late fall/early winter), snails and crayfish,
and a small amount of game (grouse/pheasant/turkey like ground birds,
mostly, in the lands west of Cleenseau)
Other vegetable foods include peas and lentils, cabbage, turnips, leeks,
onions, daikon radish, and Napa cabbage, nuts (chestnut and walnut,
mostly), fruits (apples, plums), and berries (blackberries and raspberries,
mostly)
Plus honey and mushrooms are both well-known in the region.

The lizardfolk additionally eat lotus root and marsh millet, which grow
wild along the river, particularly near Ganboa.

For clothing, the major source of fiber is wool from sheep. There is some
small cultivation of hemp mostly for rope and net-making, and reeds and
bulrushes are collected along the riverbanks especially for basketry and
thatch. In parts of the region basswood is grown as well and the inner bark
harvested for fiber.

Wood and charcoal are widely available, mostly from the edges of the
Cleenseau Wood, but also there is a substantial woodlot between Asineau and
Valit.
Beer is brewed widely and is the main alcoholic drink, alongside some
milard (a fictional millet-based clear alcohol similar to Chinese Baijiu,
which is a strongly flavored clear liquor)

The central channels of the Enst, although swift flowing, have good, fine,
sandy bottoms, but the sand is not currently exploited as retrieval is a
bit of a challenge. Clay is available in small quantities along the river
in places, and is used for wattle and daub houses and some small local
ceramic production, but most pottery is imported from Rinburg.

Good stone is not widely available although there is a small industry in
Beury collecting hearthstones and bricks from the haunted villages in the
bog, and also a good stone quarry on the south side of the Enst that is
controlled by Rosalind and her family, although it is not active constantly.

Amongst the various villages, this breaks down as:

*Beury*
Beury has the best soils outside of Cleenseau itself, and grows substantial
wheat and barley. The western part of the manor is also good pasture, and
there is a cattle herd and sheep. The northern part of the manor has patchy
woods and is a good source of berries. This is also where most of the
basswood grows which makes a good, coarse fiber, used for cloaks, rope, and
baskets, some of which are locally exported. Basswood is a flowering tree
and a good source of nectar for bees as well, and Beury has substantial
beehives.. Beury also collects mushrooms from the edges of the bog, as well
as snails, both local delicacies. Beury also derives income from guarding
caravans through the bog, and from foraging building materials (mostly
bricks and good stone) from the ruined villages in the bog.

Beury has: a smith, a carpenter/mason, a miller, two weavers/basketmakers,
a candlemaker, a leatherworker, a cooper, and a brewer. There is also a
mediocre inn but with a substantial stable and caravan yard. The weekly
market attracts buyers for honey, candles, snails, mushrooms, baskets, and
cloaks and similar heavy-duty fiber goods. The locals buy ceramics, glass,
iron and other metals, reeds and rushes, some fruits and nuts, charcoal and
some lumber, pork, and fish.

*Valit*
Valit has a small section of good soils (on the west side of the brook) and
substantial farmland that is a bit rocky or hilly and is farmed more in
scraps and pieces. There is more millet grown here than in Beury, but wheat
and barley are also grown. There are few cattle and sheep, but a
substantial number of pigs who forage in the eaves of the woods. There is a
significant charcoal industry here, and wood is plentiful. There are bees
kept here, although mostly for local consumption. Fruit and nut trees are
plentiful, and in the fall game is fairly easy to find. Valit derives
income from the baronial land registry in town, which draws travelers and
supports the work of a scribe and a small papermill along the brook, from
which paper is exported to the local region. There are also three other
mills along the river that grind grain for Cleenseau and Tavoise.

Valit has: a scribe, a papermaker, three millers, several charcoal makers,
a brewer, a blacksmith, and a carpenter.

*Tavoise*
A tiny hamlet. Mostly supports itself with the charcoal trade. Also a
source of tree nuts and apples and plums for Cleenseau, and there are
several pig farmers.

I haven't done the same writeup for Asineau but I envision it as the
poorest of the three villages. I think my other emails are largely accurate
although there is room for refinement given the above (i.e. there is
probably hemp cultivation and a larger rope/basket industry than one lone
ropemaker, there might be some additional cottage industries, etc)

Mike
