[2024-07-30 03:22 PM] Lilairen: Sylvan is an inflected language, not a word-order language; it has to be for the script to be functional as described.

[2024-07-30 03:23 PM] Lilairen: English is a word order language: "Man bites dog" and "Dog bites man" are completely different expressions.  Latin, on the other hand, is inflected.     The actual Greek, transliterated:  Andra moi, ennepe, Mousa, polutropon hos mala polla

[2024-07-30 03:27 PM] Lilairen: Each word is tagged with its role in the sentence, so the arrangement of the words is not loadbearing on meaning but instead allows for emphatic nuance.  So the Sylvan script, with its central glyph and surrounding subsidiary glyphs to elaborate on that concept, cannot be read in a particular order - there's no linear direction to it.  Instead the central glyph is like "andra" there in the Greek: the key concept around which the rest of the sentence orbits, and then the other things spin out from there.

[2024-07-30 03:30 PM] Lilairen: Presumably there are conventions about how to read the orbital glyphs.  Sunwise/clockwise is probably the most common for writing because of its affiliation with the natural flow of things, though there are probably ways of indicating that it ought to be read widdershins/counterclockwise which are relevant to specific artistic effects or anchoring particular spell effects for a type-three enchantment inscription.

[2024-07-30 03:32 PM] Lilairen: Meanwhile there is the question of how many layers of orbital glyphs are common.  I would suspect the default is probably three (as a stock fey number) (with the central glyph being the sun of the sentence, the primary orbitals being like planets, and then moons to modify those) but really fussy or intricate writing might go to seven layers because seven is _also_ a fairy number.  Then of course you get artistic schools that use nonstandard numbers of layers just to be weird, because artists are like that.

[2024-07-30 03:34 PM] Lilairen: And of course the enchantment layer would provide information about sequencing of read as well.

[2024-07-30 03:52 PM] Lilairen: If I were conlanging this I would probably do the writing with the central glyph, orbital glyphs *typically* read from the top and then clockwise (with their moons similarly) and also slightly signposted with size (so the top orbital glyph slightly larger than others, as second-most-important concept in the sentence), and convention having a lot of threes and sevens in number of glyphs permissible in writing.  And then additional things available for further nuance and enchantment anchors: is this read counterclockwise rather than sunwise?  Is a different glyph than default to be read second when reading it out loud (marked by size)? Are the moon glyphs all being read in the same direction or is one of the orbits reversed?  What is the significance of this one having different numbers of glyphs than normal/conventional?  Etc.  All of which does fun things for a medium primarily designed as an artistic one.
