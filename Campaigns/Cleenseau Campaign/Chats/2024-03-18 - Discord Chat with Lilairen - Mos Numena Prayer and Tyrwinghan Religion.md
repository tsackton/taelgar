[2024-03-18 01:46 PM] Lilairen: Next worldbuilding question, theological style!  People tend to do "I'll ask my favorite god to pass a message to the most relevant god" in this world, right?

[2024-03-18 02:30 PM] Deciusmus: Ah, I have many many pages about theological style... 
    
    Mos Numena (the religion of the eight divines that is most prevalent in Sembara and Tyrwingha) has an ancient belief that the gods cannot hear you very well if you are talking to them all at once. 
    In Sembara, this developed into an "intercessionary" style, where each town or community basically picks a favorite god, and then only *talks* to that specific god but expects that god to pass along the message to the more relevant god, so to speak.
    
    (In reality, few Sembarans would envision literal message passing, as the gods as seen as these big personifications not a Greek-style human family in the sky. So a Sembaran would more imagine their "community" god as the conduit for the prayer to get to the right personification)

[2024-03-18 02:31 PM] Deciusmus: In other places in the world though (Chardon, for example) this idea of intercession and having a community god never took hold. Instead, temple architecture and temple design keeps the prayers separate (so temples have 8 separate altars, that are very clearly segragated, similar to say the various chapels along the side of a medieval cathedral)

[2024-03-18 02:33 PM] Deciusmus: (The reason for the specific style in Sembara has to do with the ancient gods, the `*`kestavo*, who are mostly gone now, but who were very distinctly personified messengers tied to specific locations. So the kestavo of a specific brook would be a small god, living in that brook, with limited power themselves, but the ability to bring messages and prayers to the "overgods", and get answers)

[2024-03-18 02:36 PM] Deciusmus: (Throughout Taelgar there is a sense of the "over gods", in Sembara called Arha and Malik, representing creation & destruction/duality/life/death/beginnings and endings, but also very distant, not really relevant to human affairs, aloof and far away. But they are seen as the source of the power of the 8 divines; and in the *kestavo* tradition, a similar figure, Ako, the one the small gods brought their prayers too)

[2024-03-18 02:37 PM] Deciusmus: So in Sembara, long long ago, the people had these small gods and a long tradition of the idea that the little god of their village or mountain or spring or tree or brook would bring their prayer to Ako

[2024-03-18 02:37 PM] Deciusmus: And when the ancient empire of Drankor came, with their new, powerful gods (Mos Numena), the kestavo faded away, but the tradition of intercession stayed

[2024-03-18 02:39 PM] Deciusmus: But actually I haven't decided firmly where Tyrwingha falls on this spectrum.
    
    It would be perfectly plausible for the intercessionary style to be widespread in Tyrwingha - the Tyrwinghans share the same basic ancient culture as the Sembarans. But the Tyrwinghans also more explicitly adopted Mos Numena at the request of Ethlenn, as part of a deal where she ceded (some) control to Drankor
