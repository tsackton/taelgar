[2024-07-14 04:44 PM] Lilairen: The thing about true alphabetic systems is that there's a cap on how complicated they can get, really.  There are only so many sounds in a language, which means even if you manage to do one-letter-per-sound, it'll still cap out around no more than ... call it fifty symbols, most more in the thirtyish range, and some far less than that.  (Hawai'ian has ... eight consonants?  ... and apparently either 5 or 25 vowels depending on your take on dipthongs, heh.)

[2024-07-14 04:44 PM] Lilairen: WHich means there's a pretty hard limit on how complicated it can get.

[2024-07-14 04:46 PM] Lilairen: Next step up in complexity is a syllabary, where you have one symbol per *syllable* used, which generally happens only in languages where there's a strict formation of what's allowed to be a syllable.  Like Japanese, where all syllables are in the form (optional consonant)vowel(optional nasalisation), and thus Japanese can be written with Hiragana.  Which has `[looks it up on Wikipedia]` 50 characters.

[2024-07-14 04:47 PM] Lilairen: THough that doesn't have the nasalisation option that I can see, but I haven't studied it at all.

[2024-07-14 04:55 PM] Lilairen: The fun thing about hieroglyphics, by the way, is the determinatives, which are basically non-pronounced ideographic characters at the end of words to tell you what kind of word they are.  So, like, a motion-verb might end in `[walking feet]` so you know how to read it, or a name might end `[figure of man]`, `[figure of god]`, or `[figure of dog]` so you knew if someone was talking about a guy named Anup (Anubis), the god Anubis, or a dog named Anubis.
