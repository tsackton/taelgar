# Playing a Human
*See also: [[Humans]]*
D&D Beyond Link: [Human](https://www.dndbeyond.com/species/1751441-human)

The clatter of carts through a city gate, where merchants bargain in three languages in a crowded market square. A shepherd’s fire in front of a tent stirring in the wind, horses grazing under a starry sky. Temple bells, a quiet shrine beside a forest path, chanted prayers for healing and hope. These are the diversity of human homes. [[Humans]] live across Taelgar, in great kingdoms and small villages, in traveling communities and crowded ports. Their languages, customs, and gods have changed through centuries of migration and intermingling, but they all share the [[Divine Spark]], allowing their belief itself to create and sustain divinities.

## Creating Your Human Character

To create your human character, consider your cultural and religious background. What culture do you identify with, and how has it shaped you? What gods and religious traditions did you grow up with, and what place do they have in your life now?

Work with your DM to establish these details in the context of the campaign’s setting. A simple starting point is to share the cultural background and familiar religious practices of a community near where your adventures begin.

### Human Names

Humans have many naming customs, with no single pattern shared by every culture. Human names in Taelgar often follow traditional patterns of language and custom, but this is not a strict rule and many people have unusual or unique names. If you would like to reflect the traditions of your culture in your name, work with your DM to identify a language and naming tradition that suits your character’s upbringing. ## Basic Traits

**Creature Type:** Humanoid  
**Size:** Medium (about 4–7 feet tall) or Small (about 2–4 feet tall), chosen when you select this species  
**Speed:** 30 feet
**Age**: Humans reach maturity in their teens, and often live into their 70s, sometimes longer. 
**Languages**: You may select four [[Languages]] during character creation, which likely include Common and the native language of your homeland. Like all characters, you may choose to replace one of these language proficiencies with a tool proficiency.
