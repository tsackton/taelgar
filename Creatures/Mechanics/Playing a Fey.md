# Playing a Fey
*See more: [[Fey]], [[Feywild]]*
D&D Beyond Links: see Fey Options, below

%% Draft for review, 2026-10-28. A unified fey character guide, following the structure of [[Playing a Plane-Touched]], with published mechanical options and shared Taelgar traits. %%

The fey are natives of the [[Feywild]], and their forms are as varied as the realms they inhabit. Some resemble humans or elves, marked by unusual eyes or an otherworldly grace. Others have wings, horns, fur, or feathers, or take entirely animal forms. Some can change their appearance at will. This diversity offers considerable freedom in imagining your character's appearance and magic, with the options below providing different ways to represent them.

The Feywild is a realm of magic and emotion, where time and distance do not follow mortal expectations. Its lands can lie in perpetual dawn, twilight, or darkness, and a brief visit can span years on the [[Material Plane]]. Its great powers, the archfey, shape their domains around their own natures: the light, weather, landscape, and dangers of each realm reflect the being at its heart. Your homeland and your relationship to the power that shapes it can be important parts of your story, whether you still live there or have made a life among mortals.

## Creating Your Fey Character

To create your fey character, start with the form and magic you want them to have. Are you nearly human in appearance, or unmistakably a visitor from another plane? Do you have an animal form, wings, or the ability to change your appearance? Use the options below to choose mechanics that suit your idea.

Then, consider why you are in Taelgar. Were you born here, perhaps to traveling parents? Did you leave your Feywild home due to danger, curiosity, or a quest? If you have a homeland in the Feywild, consider whether this is an important part of your character's story. If so, work with your DM to choose or develop the fey realm you came from and your relationship to it and its archfey. These details do not need to matter if you do not wish them to.

Consider also how comfortable you are among mortals. Do you pass as one of them, or make little effort to hide your nature? How do the gifts, promises, and bargains of your home shape the way you treat your companions?

A simple answer if engaging with fey lore is not of interest to you is that you left the Feywild to see the mortal world. You can begin with curiosity and a reason to join your companions, leaving the details of your homeland for later.

### Fey Names

For a name from the Feywild, use [[Languages#Sylvan|Sylvan]] as your starting point: a lilting, musical language whose names should sound lyrical. Many existing fey names draw on classical Greek; Polynesian or Hawaiian names can also provide inspiration. These are starting points for choosing a name, rather than a single naming pattern that every fey must follow. You can invent a name with the sound you want, or work with your DM to choose one that suits your character's home.

## Basic Traits

**Creature Type**: Fey
**Size**: As specified by your chosen species traits.
**Speed**: 30 feet.
**Age**: The fey have an unusual relationship to time. Many fey do not appear to age; those that do age may age at unpredictable rates, or may appear to grow old as if they are human. However, most typical fey cannot persist on the Material Plane for more than roughly a human lifespan.
**Languages**: Your connection to the Feywild grants you knowledge of Sylvan. You may also select four additional [[Languages]] during character creation, which likely include at least Common. Like all characters, you may choose to replace one of these language proficiencies with a tool proficiency.

## Fey Options

Consider one of the published species below to represent your fey character. Choose one set of species traits, using the creature type, speed, age, and languages above and the Shared Fey Traits below. In Taelgar, you are a fey; the published species' ancestry and cultural background need not apply to your character.

| Fey Form | Species Traits to Consider |
| --- | --- |
| Human-like or elf-like form | [Eladrin](https://www.dndbeyond.com/species/1026386-eladrin) or [Hobgoblin](https://www.dndbeyond.com/species/1026393-hobgoblin) |
| Shifting appearance | [Changeling](https://www.dndbeyond.com/species/1026382-changeling) |
| Small, winged form | [Fairy](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/motm/fantastical-races#Fairy) |
| Horns and goat legs | [Satyr](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/motm/fantastical-races-continued#Satyr) |
| Rabbit-like form | [Harengon](https://www.dndbeyond.com/species/814914-harengon) |
| Cat-like form | [Tabaxi](https://www.dndbeyond.com/species/1026403-tabaxi) or [Leonin](https://www.dndbeyond.com/species/410992-leonin) |
| Bear-like form or another large, strong predator | [Bugbear](https://www.dndbeyond.com/species/1026380-bugbear) |

For bird-like forms, or if none of these options captures the form and magic you envision, work with your DM to choose or develop suitable traits. One of the existing options may work with a different description of how its abilities manifest.

### Shared Fey Traits

All of these options share the following traits. Where your chosen species already grants Darkvision or Fey Ancestry, use the shared version here; you do not gain the same benefit twice. Use Fey Nature for sleep and rest, retaining any unrelated benefits of your chosen species traits.

%% Draft integration rule: the paragraph above proposes how to combine the existing shared traits with a published species. In particular, check any published Trance feature against Fey Nature when building a character. %%

#### Darkvision

You have Darkvision with a range of 60 feet.

#### Fey Ancestry

You have Advantage on saving throws to avoid or end the Charmed condition.

#### Fey Nature

You do not need to sleep, and magic cannot put you to sleep. To gain the benefits of a Long Rest, you must spend 6 hours in a dream-like state. In this state, you appear motionless, but you are not rendered Unconscious, and you can perceive as normal.

## Draft Review Notes

The Old section of Playing a Fae also proposed the following shared planar-origin rule. It is preserved in full here for review, without making it an operative rule in this draft:

> In addition, even if you were born on Taelgar, your native plane is considered to be the Feywild. If you die, your soul will return to the Feywild, perhaps to be reincarnated or reborn, perhaps to fade away. If you are subject to an effect that banishes you to your native plane, you will be returned to the Feywild, where you may be trapped if you are permanently banished.

Using one character guide does not settle whether different fey forms constitute distinct metaphysical species. That remains an open worldbuilding question.
