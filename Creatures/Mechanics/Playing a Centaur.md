# Playing a Centaur
*See more: [[Centaurs]]*
D&D Beyond Link: [Centaur](https://www.dndbeyond.com/species/2236579-centaur)

Centaurs are a migratory people, traveling on long routes that take years, even generations, to complete. These migrations often follow ancient routes, with bands passing over the same lands as their ancestors, though some centaur groups seek what is new in the world, moving away from well-trodden paths. Proud and independent, centaurs regard themselves as children of Taelgar itself, giving thanks to the world rather than worshiping gods. Their deep connection to the land, and ability to read the omens of the world, give them a reputation as guardians and prophets, sensitive to signs and portents that other peoples overlook, but sometimes gives means centaurs are seen as arrogant and aloof.

## Creating Your Centaur Character

When you create your centaur character, first consider the community you came from. Most centaurs grow up in wandering bands, either following a familiar and traditional migration route or seeking new lands and novelty. Was this your childhood? Or did you grow up in a settled community, perhaps in a small family group or among other peoples? In any case, what led you to leave your home community? Some young adult centaurs leave their natal herd and travel, seeking another community, often after a period of independent wandering. Others might leave on a particular mission, to follow an omen, or simply because they became curious about the world beyond their usual paths.

Consider also your relationship with the land of Taelgar. Most centaurs believe they are the children and guardians of the land. How does this belief express itself in your character? Are you drawn to signs and portents, believing they are the messages of Taelgar for your people? Does your connection find expression in a more natural pursuit, an understanding and appreciation for the land? Many centaurs are aloof toward the affairs of other peoples, but you can decide how much this outlook shapes your character and their friendships.

A simple answer to these questions, if engaging with centaur lore is not of interest to you, is that you grew up in a wandering band and left to see more of the world, intending to seek a new herd when you are ready to settle down. 

### Centaur Names

Centaur names are diverse, often drawing on the lyrical sound of Sylvan. Different bands may follow different naming traditions. No single pattern is universal, and centaur names need not all follow these examples. For inspiration, you might draw on classical Greek names, choose one of the examples below, or invent your own.

**Example Names:** Althea, Anthea, Damaris, Ianthe, Leander, Lysander, Melantha, Nereus, Phaedra, Theron

## Basic Traits

**Creature Type**: Humanoid
**Size**: You are Medium
**Speed**: 40 feet
**Age**: Centaurs mature and age at the same rate as humans. 
**Languages**: The wide wanderings of many centaurs have left them prone to learn languages quickly. You may select six [[Languages]] during character creation, which should usually include Common, Centaur, and often either Elven or Sylvan. Like all characters, you may choose to replace one of these language proficiencies with a tool proficiency.

#### Equine Build
You count as one size larger when determining your carrying capacity and the weight you can push or drag. In addition, any climb that requires hands and feet is especially difficult for you because of your equine legs. When you make such a climb, each foot of movement costs you 4 extra feet instead of the normal 1 extra foot.

#### Sure Footed
You have Advantage on d20 Tests you make to avoid or end the Grappled or Restrained conditions, and on saving throws you make to avoid forced movement or being knocked prone.

#### Migratory Instincts
You come from a long line of migratory ancestors. As long as you are on the Material Plane, you always know which way is north. You have Proficiency in the Survival skill. 

#### Twist of Fate
You are deeply attuned to the tides of fate and can manipulate it in small ways. When a creature you can see within 60 feet of yourself succeeds or fails on a d20 Test, you can use your Reaction to roll a d4 and add the number rolled to, or subtract it from, the total of the d20 Test, potentially turning failure into success or success into failure. You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Proficiency Bonus, and regain all expended uses when you complete a Long Rest.
