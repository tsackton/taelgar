# Fey

The fey are the natives of the [[Feywild]]. They are generally referred to as the "fey", sometimes spelled "fae", or the "faerie", but there are distinctions, both obvious and subtle, among these folk. The fey can be mischievous, thriving on a clever trick, and even malevolent, seeking to profit from misery and finding pleasure in the suffering of others. They can also be noble, thriving on the majesty of creation and taking joy from the vast beauty of their home.  

All fey honor bargains, and the feywild runs on gifts and promises. But whether these are offered genuinely, or in an attempt to deceive, can be difficult for mortal minds to decipher. And the risk of accepting a bargain you do not understand may well be servitude in an archfey's court. 

## The Many Forms of the Fey

The fey are a diverse folk, though more so in appearance than in nature. The common folk who inhabit the courts and gardens and wild paths of the Feywild, and sometimes find themselves on the Material Plane, do not divide themselves by form. Mortals on the Material Plane, however, typically distinguish these common forms.

- The fae, a folk generally human-like in appearance, but possessing an otherworldly elegance and an ethereal quality, are among the most common inhabitants of the Feywild. Though many of them could pass as human or elven to a casual glance, their strange grace and unusual eyes mark them as natives of the Feywild. These are the folk that dominate stories of fey courts, such as [[A Court of Whispers]], the [[Light of the Everglade]], and [[A Fool's Fortune]], and are often described in mortal works as lords and ladies, though whether this reflects mortal confusion or is a true representation of their status in the Feywild is unclear. These folk, usually simply called the fey by mortals, though often spelled fae, are who the mortals in many parts of Taelgar likely imagine, if they ever think on the Feywild. Some of these folk can change their appearance at will; those who can do this are often referred to as changelings in legends and poems from the mortal world. 
- Rarer on the Material Plane, but common in the Feywild are the sometimes mischievous, sometimes helpful, often winged, and always magical fairies. These folk are usually small, and include creatures like the pixies, sprites, and brownies. 
- Many kinds of beastfolk inhabit the Feywild. Most famous, and most often found on the Material Plane, are the horned satyrs, but many other diverse forms of human-like creatures with some animal features, or animal-like creatures with some human features, can be found across the Feywild. 

Though a traveler to the court of an archfey might find fae, bear-folk, pixies, and satyrs all mingling together, there are some fey that are often seen as different and set apart, even among the fey themselves.

- Throughout the Feywild, there are also innumerable [[Nymphs|dryads, nymphs, and other spirits of the land]]. These fey are typically strongly tied to a particular place, and rarely, if ever, leave the tree or river they call home, though a rare nymph may join the court of a particular archfey.
- [[Story about Hags|Hags]] are also fey, and often powerful ones, feared even by other fey. Many leave the Feywild to find easier prey for their schemes in the material plane. 
- Across the feywild can be found many strange, terrible, and wonderful creatures that serve the lord of a fey realm, an archfey. [[Duskhounds]], the terrifying hunters of the [[Lord Umbraeth|Gloomshaper]], are a particularly feared example of these types of creatures. 

## Language and Naming
_See more: [[Playing a Fey#Fey Names|Fey Names]]_

The fey speak [[Languages#Sylvan|Sylvan]]. This is a strange language, a language that carries some of the fraught beauty of the Feywild itself, a language of magic and artistry above all. Powerful fey, writing in [[Sylvan Writing|sylvan characters]], can make a story on a page change the emotions and even thoughts of the reader, not just through words alone but through the magical-emotional determinatives of the language. Few mortals learn the true depths of Sylvan; most visitors to the Feywild use the Elvish script to write the Sylvan tongue.

Fey names are as diverse as the fey themselves. 

## Gifts, Promises, and Bargains

Gifts, hospitality, and promises are central to the [[Feywild]] and to fey understanding of the world. They bind the fey to one another and to mortals. Accepting a gift can bring an obligation to offer something in return, even when the recipient does not understand what they have accepted. Among some fey, these exchanges sustain generous hospitality and lasting friendships; among others, they offer opportunities for deception and coercion. Bargains carry magical power, and the price of a seemingly simple favor may reach far beyond what a mortal expects.

## Realms, Courts, and Archfey

The strangest and most powerful of the fey are the Archfey. They are the great powers of the [[Feywild]], singular beings who rule the diverse realms of the Feywild, and whose realms take shape around their nature. An archfey’s domain is not merely ruled, but marked by them: its light, weather, paths, hungers, splendors, and dangers all echo the character of the power at its heart. Some realms become courts of beauty, revelry, and impossible grace; others darken into decay, fear, or ruin. Few mortals encounter an archfey directly, and those who do rarely return unchanged.

Most fey inhabit the realm of a particular archfey, and are shaped, temporarily or permanently, by the nature of that archfey. However, the relationship between the fey and the archfey is changeable, and some fey wander between realms, or dwell in interstitial spaces ruled by no single sovereign. 

## Time, Life, and Death

In the Feywild, the passage of time is complicated by the unchanging time of day at any point in space, and for most fey, age and appearance are unrelated. However, few fey linger on the Material Plane for longer than a normal human lifespan.  

Little is known about fey traditions around life and death. Fey can die on the mortal world, but it is rare and even in [[Tyrwingha]] there are no formal rites or rituals around the remains of deceased fey.  However, cremation is a common response, and [[Tyrwingha|Tyrwinghan]] folklore holds that the remains of powerful fey may carry curses, change form, or disappear.

## Details for DMs

### Fey Categories

This is older brainstorming, preserved but maybe not relevant if we just consider "fey" to be one species. Plausibly the nomenclature issue is still relevant. 

There are a handful of unresolved points about fey categorization, especially the number of Fey species, discussed in: [[Metaphysics of Creatures]] and in [[Creature Rules in Taelgar]]. In particular, the current semi-canonical assumption is that fae are one of the two major "feywild species" (or perhaps the only real feywild species), and that things like changelings, beastfolk, and maybe hags are functionally "variant Fae" metaphysically, even though they use different statblocks. This has never been established in game, so could change. 

Nomenclature: The term Fae is confusing as it is functionally a synonym for Fey. This could be fine to reflect in-world confusion, but it is possible that as many as two new terms could be invented:
- A name for the metaphysical species of which fae, beastfolk, and changelings, at least, are part of. This is the species that is basically the "humans of the Feywild" but because of Feywild handwaving they are much more morphologically diverse than humans. 
- A name for the classic "Fae" described in this note, the subset of the "humans of the Feywild" that are human-like / elf-like in appearance (as opposed to the beastfolk, and changelings, and possibly hags). 

### Religion and Metaphysics

- **Mortal treatment of fey remains:** Mike’s [[Aftermaths - Fey Bodies]] distinguishes the lack of formal human rites from ways of honoring a particular fey. Celyn would favor returning a friend’s body to their home realm, or burial near a fey site if that were impossible; returning an honorable enemy to their own realm is also appropriate. Burning is common for dangerous remains. This supports the mortal customs described above, but does not establish the fey’s own funeral traditions or afterlife.

### Fey in Play

- **Courts and allegiance:** A fey encountered on the Material Plane need not serve an archfey, and may instead have personal claims on a grove, stream, or other place. Ethlenn’s involvement across Tyrwingha is exceptional. See [[2024-11-19 - Discord Chat with Lilairen - Fey Sovereignty Hidden Rules and Bargain Magic]].
