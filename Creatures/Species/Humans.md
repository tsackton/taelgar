# Humans

> [!image|hero]
> ![[humans-tollen-quays.webp]]
> *A conversation on the quays of [[Tollen]].*

From the shores of the [[Green Sea]] to the slopes of the [[Sentinel Range|Sentinels]], from the arid scrublands of the [[Hara Basin]] to the fertile riverlands of the [[Sembara|Sembaran heartlands]], humans have settled nearly everywhere that people can live across Taelgar. They are by far the most numerous sapient species in Taelgar, widespread and culturally diverse, speaking many languages and worshipping many gods. Humans were created by the [[Divine Presence]] on [[Hkar]], their ancient homeland, and spread across the world in a series of migrations large and small, culminating in the [[The Downfall|Downfall]], when [[Hkar]] was destroyed and humans fled their homeland forever. Endowed with the [[Divine Spark]], humans carry the power to bring gods into being through their belief. These gods wax and wane with the faith of their worshippers, tying divine power to the changeable fates of the human communities that worship them, and creating a diversity of religious traditions across the world. Across Taelgar, the descendants of Hkar have built a multitude of cultures, carrying languages, customs, and gods into new homelands and reshaping them along the way.

## Appearance

Humans are varied and diverse in appearance, with a wide range of complexions, hair, eyes, stature and build. Clothing and typical styles are shaped by local cultures, which are diverse and variable.

## Language and Naming
_See also: [[Playing a Human#Human Names|Human Names]], [[Languages]]_

Humans speak a [[Languages|diversity of languages]], arising from the species’ history of migration and cultural change. Though the original humans on [[Hkar]] spoke one common tongue, over the millennia since the first migrations, human languages have evolved and changed. Drankorian and Chardonian scholars describe three major language families: the Northros languages, spoken by the earliest migrants from Hkar, and represented by Deno'qai and Old Zimkovan, among others, in the modern day; the Eastros languages, spoken by the mariner culture that left Hkar before the Downfall and settled the Green Sea, and represented by Urskan, Skaegish, and Tollish, among others, in the modern day; and the Drankorian language family, spoken by the refugees that fled the Downfall and settled Drankor, and represented by Chardonian and Cymean, among others, in the modern day. Other languages, such as Dunmari, do not clearly fit into one of the major language families. 

Humans also commonly speak trade tongues that unite diverse peoples. The language known as Common, frequently spoken across the world, survives from the ancestral human tongue and remains innately easy for humans to pick up. Many non-humans learn Common as well. The language known as Eastros is a trade pidgin common across the Green Sea, particularly in the eastern regions.

Human naming customs vary considerably by culture and upbringing, with no shared species-wide tendencies. 

## Lifecycle

Humans reach physical maturity in their teens, and live into their seventies or beyond, though few live past a hundred. 

## Settlements and Homelands

Human settlements range from crowded ports and bustling agricultural market towns to isolated forest villages and nomadic camps. More than any other sapient species of Taelgar, humans reflect many different ways of organizing land and communal life, across many different scales. 

Many human societies are ruled as parts of larger, powerful realms: on the western coast is the [[Chardonian Empire]], with its powerful legions and magical institutions supported by [[Chalyte|chalyte]]; in the fertile river valleys of the [[Western Gulf]] the [[Sembara|Sembaran]] monarchy commands the loyalty of barons and dukes; in the arid central deserts of the [[Hara Basin]] the Samraat of [[Dunmar]] rules with divine blessing over a pastoral people who move their herds with the monsoon rains; in the cold northern forests of [[Ursk]] a mysterious circle of wizards rules and protects the land; from the [[Tollen|Free City of Tollen]] the powerful Tollish navy rules over coastal colonies across the [[Western Gulf]].

Other human societies find different ways to order their relations: the [[Deno'qai]] of the western forests organize their communities into tribes and villages without a single overarching government; the [[Skaer]] of the [[Western Green Sea]] live in small island communities organized around communal villages and fisheries; and the [[Istabor Alliance|Isinguese]] communities of the [[Refounded Alliance of Aurbez]] live in towns and villages organized around mutual obligations of defense and protection. 

Human settlements also frequently provide homes for other species. Dwarven quarters and halfling-kept inns are familiar features of many human towns and cities. Lizardfolk, while maintaining their own communal authority and customs, often settle near human towns. In cosmopolitan ports such as Tollen, members of many species live alongside one another, from the familiar to the exotic. 

## Cultural Traits

The diverse and adaptable human cultures across the world share few common cultural traits, but two things do unite them: all humans are descended from peoples who left their ancestral homeland of Hkar and settled elsewhere in the world, as part of one of the great human migrations, and all humans are gifted the divine spark, the mystical power that allows their beliefs to create and sustain divinities. 

### Migrations and Cultures

Humanity was created by the [[Divine Presence]] on [[Hkar]] long ago. All human cultures on Taelgar ultimately descend from a series of migrations and departures from [[Hkar]]. 

#### Migrations from Hkar

The earliest migrants from Hkar left in scattered groups, settling around what is now the [[Apporia|Apporian Peninsula]], the [[Emerald Bay]], and the [[Coastlands|Coastlands of the Chardonian Empire]]. These people, later called the [[Northerners]], settled among the great forests and mountain slopes of the [[Sentinel Range|Sentinels]], eventually crossing the mountains to the highlands of [[Vostok]] and [[Sembara]], carrying a religious tradition focusing on the worship of small gods, and speaking Northros languages. They were followed by another wave of departures, settling in the south and east, whose descendants today live in the [[Great Desert]] and other distant places. 

As a highly magical urban culture began to develop on [[Hkar]], another wave of migrants left the island. The longest and most populous of these was the exodus of the [[Mariners|seafaring people]] who settled first on the eastern edges of the [[Green Sea]], and slowly moved west, settling shores and islands, worshipping diverse and powerful pantheonic gods. But other smaller migrations brought other cultures, including the [[Dunmar|Dunmari]], away from Hkar to settle on the mainland. 

The last people to leave Hkar were the refugees of the [[The Downfall|Downfall]], the survivors of the destruction of [[Hkar]], and the inheritors of the legacy of the urban magical culture that had developed on Hkar, in part through the aid and support of the [[Peronar]]. These people -- the ones who survived the wars -- were the founders of [[Drankorian Empire|Drankor]] and the builders of the [[Drankorian Empire]]. 

#### Divergence and Intermingling

Centuries of separation and divergence away from Hkar produced a variety of distinct languages, customs, and religious traditions among human cultures. The northerners, for example, spread across the interior highlands, leading to diverse peoples such as the [[Deno'qai]] and the [[Zimka]], who share worship of small gods, but who speak distinct languages and express their beliefs in unique ways. 

Trade, conquest, and resettlement, in turn, brought human and nonhuman cultures into contact and produced new cultural combinations, shaping the diversity of human cultures in Taelgar. In Sembara, for example, the [[Mariners]], [[Northerners]], and [[Drankorian Empire|Drankorians]] all met and from their interactions produced the unique cultures of the fertile river valleys of [[Sembara]] today. 

The complex history of human movements and migrations means that language, religion, political identity, and cultural history can all follow unique paths. In parts of the [[Highlands|Sembaran Highlands]], for example, the [[Zimka]] speak an Eastros language, worship Northerner gods, and owe allegiance to kingdoms deeply shaped by the Drankorian political order. While ancient migrations have left their mark on the world, the years of change and cultural evolution mean they are not the same as modern day cultures.

### Religion and Belief

Humanity is unique among the sapient species of the world in possessing the [[Divine Spark]], which allows human belief to sustain divinities. This has led to a diversity of human religions worshipping many [[Incorporeal Gods]]. 

#### The Divine Spark

Humanity, created by the [[Divine Presence]], is gifted the [[Divine Spark]], the name given to the uniquely human ability to create and sustain [[Incorporeal Gods|divinities]] through the power of their belief. The power of these divinities, unlike that of the [[Divine Presence]] or the [[Embodied Gods|embodied Firstborn]] who created other species, depends on their worshippers’ belief and can wax and wane with the strength of the religious communities that sustain them.

#### Religious History and Traditions

Though the [[Divine Presence]] created humanity, unlike the creators of other species, the Divine Presence does not ordinarily answer prayers directly or grant any special magic to humans. From the earliest days of Hkar, humanity began to worship small local gods and intercessory powers, minor divinities who could provide a conduit to the magic of the [[Divine Presence]]. These religious traditions grew into the [[Northern Folk Religions]]; their followers approach the divine through particular places, offerings, and rites intended to draw a god’s attention and secure its intercession.

> [!image|figure standard]
> ![[humans-kestavo-forest-spring.webp]]
> *Tending a [[Kestavo|kestavo]] shrine at a forest spring in [[Zimkova]].*

On Hkar, early intercessory cults gave way to pantheonic traditions, worshipping a diversity of gods, which were carried by later migrants from humanity's birthplace. Those who left, the [[mariners]] and the [[Dunmar|Dunmari]] and others, brought their gods with them, developing into new religious traditions across the world. The divine family of the [[Dunmari Religion|Dunmari faith]] and the worship of [[Kaikkea]], the ancient ocean goddess of the Skaer, grew out of these emigrations. 

In the years before the [[The Downfall|Downfall]], the Divine Way, called [[Mos Numena]] in Drankorian, grew and spread and came to dominate the magical urban culture of Hkar. The [[Mos Numena Pantheon|Eight Divines]], powerful religious archetypes, saved the people of Hkar from disaster, helping them to establish [[Drankor]]. As the [[Drankorian Empire]] spread, so too did [[Mos Numena]] and the worship of the [[Mos Numena Pantheon|Eight Divines]]. 

In the modern day, humanity follows diverse religious traditions descended from these strands and shaped by their intermingling. 

## Historical Perspective

For much of the early history of humanity, humans lived largely on [[Hkar]]. The [[The Downfall|Downfall]] ended humanity’s long history on [[Hkar]], destroying its ancestral homeland and reshaping much of Taelgar. Refugees from Hkar founded [[Drankor]], which grew into an empire stretching from the [[Endless Ocean]] to the [[Western Green Sea]]. For roughly a thousand years, its armies, magical institutions, and imperial government shaped the lives of many human peoples. A thousand years after its founding, the Drankorian Empire fell as the [[First Plague]] devastated the imperial heartland. 

Out of this destruction, former provinces became independent realms and the world changed and grew. Centuries later, the [[Great War]] united human and nonhuman realms against [[Cha'mutte]], whose armies threatened the gods themselves. His defeat in DR 1545 ended the war, but left kingdoms destroyed, populations scattered, and entire landscapes transformed. The [[Plaguelands]] consumed much of the former [[Istabor Alliance]], while upheavals in Dunmar altered the land and climate. For many years after the fall of Cha'mutte, wars against hobgoblins and others shaped the histories of human realms.

Now, the world is a place shaped by these catastrophes, and by human resilience and adaptation in the face of them.
