# Stoneborn

High above the clouds, on the tallest peak of a mountain range, a Stoneborn sits quietly, watching the sunrise. On the steep slopes below, a small village comes to life with the sounds of bells, morning prayer, and martial practice. Scattered throughout the inhospitable high places of the world, Stoneborn live reclusively, rarely gracing the lowlands with their presence.

> [!image|hero]
> ![[stoneborn-above-the-clouds.webp]]
> *Dawn above a Stoneborn village.*

## Appearance

Stoneborn are imposing, powerfully built humanoids who appear to be shaped from stone. They usually stand between 6 and 8 feet tall and weigh between 300 and 500 pounds. Their skin reflects the varied colors and textures of stone, often marked with mottling, veins, or swirling patterns. Many are shades of gray, but their appearance is as diverse as the stone of their mountain homes.

Stoneborn are typically bald and hairless, with striking eyes that resemble gems and minerals, from bright colors to dark obsidian. As they age, they become increasingly stone-like; the eldest stoneborn can seem like statues, rarely moving or speaking.

Made of the stuff of mountains, Stoneborn do not feel cold as other species do, and are often seen walking high mountain paths in simple clothes or even little more than a few pieces of hide and fur. They prize fine craftwork in metal or stone, and many wear intricate jewelry.

## Language and Naming
_See more: [[Playing a Stoneborn#Stoneborn Names|Stoneborn Names]]_

Stoneborn speak [[Languages#Stoneborn|Stoneborn]], a language with no known dialects or variants. It has its own alphabet, related to Giant, although examples of writing are rare. Outsiders who spend time in Stoneborn communities sometimes learn the language.

Stoneborn names are a living, changing part of their identity. Stoneborn typically have a single given name. Some also use a community name reflecting the community they belong to. While in many cases this is the community they were born into, Stoneborn who join a new community will often change their name when they feel they are fully a part of their new settlement. Names are not gendered in Stoneborn society. 

A Stoneborn's training and discipline are as much a part of their identity as their family and community, and some Stoneborn take a use name reflecting their skill or the lineage of the master they trained under. Use names typically either reflect a skill, or the name of a master. For example, a skilled stonemason may take the use name Stonebreaker or Stonecarver; a skilled warrior may take the use name Quickblade or Strongaxe; or a skilled teacher may take the use name Loremaster or Mindshaper. Alternatively, a use name may reflect the lineage of the master a Stoneborn trained under. For example, a student of the master Maumak Ironforger may take the use name Maumakandi or Maumakundi (student of Maumak). It is also common to adopt the use name of your master, so if your master is Maumak Ironforger, you may also take the Ironforger use name. 

In normal usage, Stoneborn either go by their use name or their birth name. Like [[Dwarves|dwarves]], Stoneborn ascribe meaning to their use names and therefore often introduce themselves with use names in the trade tongues. But some may not follow this custom. And some may invent new names for themselves when they travel outside their homeland.

## Lifecycle

Stoneborn are born from stone, and mature somewhat more slowly than humans, reaching physical maturity in their 20s and usually settling into fully adult roles in their mid to late 30s. Young Stoneborn typically spend their 20s and early 30s apprenticed to masters, spending years developing the skills and disciplines that will shape their adult lives.

From around 150 years of age, most Stoneborn begin a long, gradual withdrawal from active life, passing their daily responsibilities to younger members of the community and turning increasingly toward contemplation. As the centuries pass, a Stoneborn’s metabolism slows, their need for food and other resources diminishes, and their periods of stillness grow longer. An elder of 250 might bestir themselves only once a week, speaking with visitors or attending to some small task before returning to quiet contemplation, and by 300 most elders have become as statues, returning to the stone from which they came. 

Some exceptional Stoneborn, sustained by dedication to a particular task or purpose, remain active much longer.

## Settlements and Homelands

Rarely do Stoneborn venture down from the summits of the high places of the world, preferring to live in isolated communities of their own kind. In their mountain strongholds, they are rumored to be able to mold stone with prayers to Entamba, to cut villages from mountainsides, and warm the high meadows to create lush fields with days of meditation.

Many Stoneborn communities lie in the [[Sentinel Range]], which they call the [[Sentinel Range|Indalas]]. Others make their homes in the [[Svinjo Mountains]], far to the north. In the northern Svinjo, rumors say that some Stoneborn live in the steadings of the frost giants of [[Kaldhalla]], exchanging labor for protection.

Not all Stoneborn live in isolated mountain communities. For example, in [[Apporia]], survivors of the [[War of the Dark Rift]] have settled among humans or established small coastal villages on cliffs and hilltops, especially in [[Cedrano]].

## Cultural Traits

Stoneborn culture is shaped by the pursuit of mastery, through sustained concentration, discipline, and years of practice. The teachings passed from master to student connect these deeply personal pursuits across generations, preserving knowledge while leaving each student a lifetime to make it their own.
### Mastery and Discipline

Stoneborn place a high value on expertise and training. Concentration, single-minded focus, and years of disciplined practice are central to their pursuit of mastery. Young Stoneborn apprentice themselves to masters in a skill or trade, and by years of training and practice become masters themselves. Stoneborn honor the training and dedication necessary to become true masters of any skill, whether in combat, crafting, teaching, tending the young, or healing.

> [!image|figure standard]
> ![[stoneborn-solitary-staff-mastery.webp]]
> *A Stoneborn practices with a staff on a mountain terrace.*

Individual Stoneborn tend to be proud and confident in their mastery of their discipline. A craft, field of knowledge, or particular pursuit can shape decades of a Stoneborn’s life, becoming a defining part of their identity. They greatly respect skill, and honor demonstrated masters even if they are not Stoneborn. While the ambition to be the best at something is one that many Stoneborn feel, for many the skill is reward enough.

The relationship between master and student gives this dedication a continuity beyond an individual life. Some Stoneborn take a use name reflecting the lineage of the master they trained under, or adopt their master’s use name as their own. Finding a student willing to dedicate themselves to the work can be deeply important to a master who wishes their knowledge to endure. Stoneborn masters sometimes accept members of other species who meet the rigorous standards they set for themselves for training and apprenticeship, carrying their teachings into new hands.

### Entamba, the Elder Mountain

Stoneborn are the embodiment of and draw magical power from their Firstborn creator, [[Entamba]], the Elder Mountain, whom they venerate as the soul of the mountains. Entamba is the first Stoneborn, born in the slow uplift of ground into mountains or the fiery blast of a volcano, and the last Stoneborn, the dust of mountains slowly worn away over eons and scattered into the sea and air.

Through their connection to Entamba, Stoneborn are capable of feats of concentration, single-minded focus, and supernatural mastery over their bodies and the stone of their mountain homes.

### Community and Commitment

Stoneborn live in small communities, typically in hard to reach places high in the mountains. Most villages are centered around one or a few gymnasiums or academies, where Stoneborn practice their craft, and where, in some villages, a renowned master may ply their trade. Their remote settlements offer places to pursue mastery, learning from one another and sharing the work that sustains their lives.

Stoneborn society has little organization beyond the village or local community. Stoneborn have no royalty, and no leaders dictate the decisions of the community. When decisions must be made, the community will gather, meet, and discuss in one of the many sites sacred to Entamba. A consensus is reached, and all are bound by it. Stoneborn act as one, or not at all. Elders remain valued teachers and advisors, sought out for their memories, insight, and accumulated mastery, but the work and decisions of the community pass to those still actively engaged in its affairs.

Those individuals who cannot abide by the consensus of their community often find themselves pushed outside Stoneborn society, departing to seek their way alone in the world or to find a new community. As a consequence, individual Stoneborn villages tend towards sameness, as dissident voices drift away and find like-minded people elsewhere. 

Stoneborn villages sometimes have members of other species living among them, generally while apprenticed to a master. 

## Historical Perspective

Like many long-lived species, Stoneborn perceive the flow of time differently than humans. While they tend to prefer the slow pace of mountain life, where change comes rarely as the slow movement of a glacier, they are also the people of avalanches and volcanoes. Stoneborn see the decadal crawl of a glacier and the thunderous destruction of an avalanche as equal parts of the ebb and flow of the world. 

Stoneborn often remain on the edges of the affairs of larger kingdoms, absorbed in their own homes and pursuits, seemingly leaving little impact on history. Yet respect for another’s skill, courage, or dedication can become the foundation of a lasting commitment. During the [[Fire War]], Stoneborn helped relieve the siege of the [[Lakan Monastery]] and, impressed by the monks’ valor and knowledge, promised further aid. In the [[Three Kin's War]], they fought alongside humans and lizardfolk to drive hobgoblins from the [[Aurbez Plateau]]. Such ties can draw Stoneborn far beyond their mountain homes.
