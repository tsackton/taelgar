Lizardfolk dwell where water and land meet, most commonly in swamps, river basins, and tidal marshes. Their villages are well‑kept places of fish weirs and reedcraft, hidden boardwalks and raised hearths, vibrant and teeming with life. Elders guide the growth of the community, so the land is never overburdened, and omens from dreams and ancestors steer important decisions in lizardfolk life. Though often living in isolated villages, when they settle near other species they usually build ties of trade and friendship, though rarely do lizardfolk depend on the trade of outsiders to sustain their way of life. 

## Appearance

Lizardfolk are humanoid reptiles: upright, bipedal, hairless, with distinctly reptilian heads. They are typically powerfully built, averaging about six feet tall with a slightly forward-canted posture, especially among those with particularly large tails. They are covered in close-fitting scales, rather than skin. Lizardfolk coloration varies widely, though generally juveniles and adults are more likely to be brightly colored than elders, whose scales often fade to light blue-greens or other pastel colors, and lose the wet sheen. In moments of deep focus elders may seem to sparkle, and very rarely appear faintly translucent under direct sunlight.

## Language and Naming
_See more: [[Playing a Lizardfolk#Lizardfolk Names|Lizardfolk Names]]_

Lizardfolk speak their own tongue, called Lizardling or sometimes Lizardfolk by outsiders, but also speak easily in trade pidgins when they settle near other species, and often learn the languages of their neighbors as well. Lizardfolk usually have a single given name, often chosen to honor a favored ancestor, though names tend not to repeat within a generation. Names are often passed down in families, and frequently, but not always, have some meaning. Lizardfolk will add home‑place or descriptor when needed abroad, but tend not to use family names in their home communities. 

## Lifecycle

Lizardfolk mature at roughly the same rate as humans, reaching adulthood in their late teens or early twenties. Once they mature, however, they age at an an increasingly slower rate compared to humans. A lizardfolk at a hundred is as hale and hearty as a human half their age, and lizardfolk elder years can last for a century or more, though vanishingly few lizardfolk live into their fourth century. Because of this extended old age, many generations of lizardfolk typically live together, and young lizardfolk commonly know their great-grandparents and great-great-grandparents. These long-lived elders hold an important place in community life, preserving knowledge of the land, family histories, and ancestral traditions.

Lizardfolk communities consciously shape their population growth to remain in balance with what their homelands can support. Bringing children into the community is a collective decision, guided by the elders and the ancestors and the needs of the community. In many lizardfolk communities, children are rare: many grow up supported by large families of grandparents and elders, but with few age-mates.

## Settlements and Homelands

Lizardfolk communities are almost invariably found near water, typically in naturally occurring swamps and marshes, though some lizardfolk, such as the salt lizardfolk of the [[Mawakel Peninsula]], settle in and around salt marshes, and others settle in river basins or near lakes which they frequently shape to create productive wetlands. Most lizardfolk villages are small, often tree-screened or otherwise hidden by the natural environment, nestled among weirs, fish ponds, and other modifications that create highly productive ecosystems. 

> [!image|figure standard]
> ![[lizardfolk-village-reflection.webp]]
> *Lantern light reflected in the still waters of a lizardfolk village.*

While some lizardfolk communities remain isolated from other species, they frequently settle near the towns and cities of humans and others, and often trade fish, herbs, and other goods, though usually maintain their own laws and society. Thus, while lizardfolk are often found near humans, they rarely are found living in human settlements. 

Some of the largest lizardfolk communities can be found in the [[Ozabal]] and [[Latazaro]] swamps near the [[Western Gulf]]; the [[Yeraad Marshes]] south of [[Chardon]]; the salt marshes of the [[Mawakel Peninsula]]; and the rivers and wetlands of [[Portalia]] and [[Apporia]]. However, many small lizardfolk communities are scattered widely throughout the world, with villages as diverse as [[Ganboa]], the tiny community on the [[Enst]] downriver from [[Cleenseau]]; the substantial lizardfolk towns on the outskirts of [[Adderfell]] and [[Darba]]; and the scattered villages of the [[Aursen Marshes]], just upriver from [[Aursenbourg]].

## Cultural Traits

Lizardfolk cultures are shaped by a pair of deep connections: their enduring ties to their homelands, and the spiritual guidance of elders and ancestor. While diverse lizardfolk communities differ in specific customs, these shared connections give even widely separated lizardfolk settlements underlying commonalities. 

### Lizardfolk Homelands

Lizardfolk shape their surroundings with considerable care and skill, maintaining a deliberate balance with the ecosystems in which they settle and live. Fish ponds, weirs, cultivated plants, and carefully directed waterways make their homelands richly productive, although to an unfamiliar visitor these places may look wild and unsettled. To keep these ecosystems productive and sustainable, lizardfolk keep their populations in balance with the land on which they dwell: reproduction and child-bearing is carefully controlled by elders and ancestors in proportion to what can be healthily sustained.

The natural abundance that arises from these arrangements supports ample leisure among lizardfolk, and allows the adult populations of lizardfolk villages to support the large numbers of elders typical of lizardfolk communities. This also leaves time for spiritual pursuits, studying dreams, and uncovering and understanding the nature of everyday magic. Many lizardfolk value knowledge of plants, animals, and the movements of water highly; herbalists and healers are respected in many communities. Regional lizardfolk traditions emphasize local connections to the magic of the land: among the salt lizardfolk of the [[Mawakel Peninsula]], for example, controlled ceremonies using [[Gatza|gatza]] allow communion with the spirits of the dead.

Lizardfolk communities are typically self-sufficient and often somewhat insular. However, they commonly settle near the cities and towns of other species, and frequently maintain warm relations with their neighbors. Trade with humans and others supplements what a community can provide for itself. 

### Elders, Ancestors, and the Divine

> [!image|right standard]
> ![[lizardfolk-elder-and-child.webp]]
> *An elder shares knowledge of wetland plants with a child.*

Elders hold a central place in lizardfolk communities. In many respects, most lizardfolk villages function as gerontocracies, with decisions made communally but guided and shaped by the wisdom of the elders, and the channeled spiritual guidance of the ancestors. Elders also preserve knowledge of the local land, interpret omens and dreams, and are generally seen as the conduits between the people of the community and the divine realm. 

Elders shape lizardfolk family life as well. Several generations often live alongside one another, and family relationships connect young lizardfolk to people whose memories reach far beyond their own experience. Decisions about the growth of a community, including bringing children into it, are collective concerns, typically shaped by the guidance of elders and ancestors.

Elders also serve as the conduit between lizardfolk communities as their Firstborn creators, the divine [[Tharzen Anzinakoa]], worshipped both collectively and through their four faces: [[Negu]], [[Hazkunde]], [[Bero]], and [[Udazkena]]. All lizardfolk share a religious tradition that is built on dreams and prophecies and omens that connect the past and future, believed to be associated with the idea that the divine is a repeating cycle. Lizardfolk religion envisions the Tharzen Anzinakoa as the divine representation of this spiritual cycle, marking life from birth and renewal through growth, elderhood, and death. 

The precise way in which these are represented and connected to the Tharzen Anzinakoa varies widely. In the tradition associated with Greater Sembara and the various lizardfolk realms that were, in ancient days, part of a vast wetlands known as [[Urtzabala]], the four faces of the [[Tharzen Anzinakoa]] are typically described as representing the seasons. [[Hazkunde]] is associated with birth and renewal, and depicted as the coming of spring; [[Bero]] is associated with growth and the fullness of life, and depicted as the height of summer; [[Udazkena]] is associated with elderhood and responsibility, and depicted as the harvest of autumn; and [[Negu]] is associated with ancestors and death, and is depicted as the hibernation of winter. But, in other places, these associations vary, with local cultures.

## Historical Perspective

Lizardfolk communities are typically led by elders, with a strong connection to their ancestors. This perspective informs lizardfolk views of history: they often have deep ties to the local history of the land they live on, and many lizardfolk settlements claim to have lived in the same place since time immemorial. Indeed, many current lizardfolk communities are the remnants of [[Urtzabala|vast ancient wetlands]] from before the [[The Downfall|Downfall]] reshaped coasts and rivers, and many ancient wetlands shrank or shifted.

### Open Questions and Further Work
- Subspecies: “salt” lizardfolk appear in coastal villages (e.g., Eskorola). Are there consistent physiological or cultural differences, is “salt” a local label specifically for Mawar, are there other "salt lizardfolk" communities?
- Afterlife mechanics: lots of variation and uncertainty about role of direct rebirth from ancestral spirits and generally how this connects to the  model of gifted soulstuff returning to divine realms. 
- There is some question about ancient lizardfolk kingdoms. Some older brainstorming proposes a fraction of the Green Sea was lizardfolk swamps; when these dried, lizardfolk largely migrated south. This is possible and gives space for a big tropical lizardfolk empire far away but remains completely open.
### Where You’ll Find Them
- Lizardfolk tend to be found in their own communities, often near human settlements, and invariably associated with water, usually with wetlands. 
- Lone lizardfolk travelers are plausible almost anywhere, but typically would need a reason for traveling. 
- Lizardfolk almost never settle embedded within human communities. A lone lizardfolk, a small group, or a lizardfolk enclave living in the city of another species needs a special explanation. A lone lizardfolk living alone in the wilderness would be less surprising and could have any number of reasons. 
- Invented regions with high numbers of lizardfolk include:
	- Greater Sembara: Major community in the [[Ozabal]] swamps (Enst–Wistel confluence) and the [[Latazaro]] (east of [[Tyrwingha]]), and scattered, potentially densely scattered, communities along the [[Enst]] (cf. [[Ganboa]]) and almost certainly many other places.  
	- Western Coast: Salt‑marsh communities in coastal villages like [[Itzal|Eskorola]] on the [[Mawakel Peninsula]]; fairly dense settlement in [[Apporia]] as well. 
	- Upper Istaros and Aurbez: Marsh hamlets along the [[Aursen Marshes]]
	- Far South jungles: The lizardfolk realm of [[Orekatu]] with settlements like [[Bedez]] near the [[Azta Lekua|Footprint of the Gods]].

### Lizardfolk Ki from Orekatu
*these notes might reflect general lizardfolk metaphysics / vibes, or might reflect something specific to the jungle lizardfolk of Orekatu*

The lizardfolk call ki arima, and at its most basic level, consider it the concentrated energy of the [[Plane of Souls]], which any living being on Taelgar can, in principle, learn to channel. However, the lizardfolk spirit guides and prophets speak of many other forms of ki, which are the echoes and resonances of the higher spiritual planes, and which are often difficult or impossible to channel without an appropriate focus. While the lizardfolk think of this ki as “spiritual harmonics”, these echoes of other planes in the fabric of Taelgar have other names in other places. Chalyte, for example, is a stone that gathers the resonances of the [[Plane of Magic]].

Kenzo learned about the following plants and the kinds of ki they can channel: 

Amezza: Amezza is the name for a common jungle tree, generally short and shrubby, that grows in openings and clearings. The name, roughly translated, means “dreamberry”; the aromatic berries are dried and traded as a spice. When the tree grows in a place rich in connections to the [[Dreamworld]], the berries collect amezza, also known as dream ki. But care must be taken, because Amezza can also absorb the energy of nightmares and not everything in the Dreamworld is friendly. 

Hutsu: Hutsu is the name for a flowering vine that is common across the tropical regions of Taelgar, which grows up the trunks of large trees. When it grows near places of peaceful death, the leaves occasionally turn white, giving it its translated name in Common, “whiteleaf vine”. When hutsu vines grow in places with a strong connection to the [[Land of the Dead]], the white leaves collect hutsu, also known as spirit ki.

Ohana: Ohana is the name for the largest of the jungle trees, a species that is typically dominant among the emergent trees that grow above the rainforest canopy. These trees grow in places rich in connection to the outer planes of primal power, particularly [[Arborea]] but also any other spiritual plane that is connected to primal forces of nature, growth, abundance, wildness. When they grow in such places, they will often grow small branches that drop off naturally, the bark of which is infused with ohana, also known as life ki or lushness ki. The lizardfolk believe that Ohana trees have a special connection to Zerua, the home of the Tharzen Anzinakoa. %%name Zerua is not canonical and could be changed %%

Urtaro: Urtaro is the name for a lilypad that grows in still water and swamps, frequently cultivated by lizardfolk communities for its beauty and also as food (the tubers are highly nutritious and are made into a starch used to make a variety of things). When grown in places of great peace and beauty, especially places cultivated and tended, urtaro flowers sometimes develop a connection to planes of order and beauty. The flowers become infused with urtaro, also known as solid ki or permanence ki, thought of as the energy of solid things, the bones, the mountains, the earth, the permanent structures on which life can grow and thrive. 

### Ecology, Craft, Settlement, and Trade
- Lizardfolk are exceptionally good at settlement engineering: they make fish weirs and ponds; raised boardwalks and stilted huts; nesting rafts; and generally coax extreme productivity from the lands they settle, usually without obvious, intensive agriculture (no fields of wheat or anything). 
- In many games, lizardfolk have been associated with herbalism, medicinal herb lore, and general plant lore; in Mawar, [[Trok]] is a tattoo warrior, with tattoos associated with the salt lizardfolk, but unclear if this is general. 
- Lizardfolk often trade with human communities (cf [[Ganboa]]). 
- Lizardfolk villages and communities are typically led by an elder or council of elders, who often look to ancestors for advice; in some fashion, ancestors and/or elders control settlement size, keeping lizardfolk communities from growing beyond the carrying capacity of the land.
