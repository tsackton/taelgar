# Kobolds

In abandoned mines, concealed hillside warrens, and the shadows of dragon lairs, kobolds make their homes in a world of creatures often larger and stronger than themselves, creatures who often see them as little more than thieves, bandits, or servants. Ingenious builders and clever bargainers, kobold communities often turn to a powerful patron for protection; those that eke out an independent life survive through hidden passages, clever contraptions, and guile. Kobold communities accumulate languages, skills, and even religious traditions from the peoples around them, making borrowed things their own. Some find purpose in the service of dragons; others fiercely guard their independence. Behind their varied ways of life lies a shared uncertainty: kobolds have many stories about who made them, but no certain knowledge of their beginnings.

## Appearance

Most kobolds are scaled humanoids with a distinctly draconic appearance. They come in all colors and sizes. While many are small, often no more than 4 feet tall, some are much bigger, and a rare few have wings. Some communities consider resemblance to dragons to be a source of pride and respect, though this is far from universal. 

## Language and Naming
_See also: [[Playing a Kobold#Kobold Names|Kobold Names]]_

Kobolds speak the languages of their homes and neighbors. There is no unique kobold tongue shared by all kobolds. Common and Draconic are widespread, with Draconic commonly spoken among communities that serve or worship dragons, although the languages heard in any particular warren reflect its particular history. 

Naming customs are similarly varied. Names may reflect the languages spoken within a community or those of the peoples among whom a kobold grew up. Descriptive bynames can commemorate a talent, an ambition, or a memorable experience.

## Lifecycle

Kobolds hatch from reptilian eggs, and grow very quickly in their first few years, but otherwise mature and age at roughly the same rate as humans. Some communities maintain communal hatcheries, tending their eggs together within the shelter of the warren.

## Settlements and Homelands

Kobold communities can be found scattered across the world, though their settlements are usually tucked into overlooked places, such as caves, abandoned mines, or narrow tunnels beneath rough hills. Their tendency to hide can make kobolds seem less common than they really are. 

Some kobold settlements lie within the domains of dragons or other powerful patrons, whose protection shapes their communities and lives. Independent warrens are more likely to rely on secrecy for protection: their warrens can be intricate places, relying on concealed entrances, traps, and complex escape routes for defense. Individuals and small groups also make homes among other peoples, although the suspicion with which kobolds are sometimes treated may make such settlement difficult.

## Cultural Traits

Kobolds readily adopt the skills, customs, and beliefs of those around them, whether they live in servitude under a powerful patron or independently in a hidden warren on the edges of a human realm. The diversity of attitudes and beliefs gives their scattered communities widely differing ways of life.

### Ingenuity and Opportunity

Kobolds have a reputation for cleverness and trickery, in part from the practical demands of frequently living among larger and stronger neighbors. A well-placed trap or a concealed entrance can keep their homes safe in the face of threats, but their inventiveness also extends to craftwork, mechanical contraptions, and even experiments with magic. Kobold communities regularly pick up skills, languages, and customs from the peoples around them. This flexibility gives kobold life much of its variety. 

### Patrons and Independence

Many kobolds live under the protection of a powerful patron, often a dragon, exchanging service for safety. Though for some kobolds, serving a dragon may provide a sense of belonging to something magnificent, and even religious meaning, others may feel stifled by the demands of obedience. Questions of loyalty and independence can divide a community deeply.

Independent kobolds cultivate other means of survival. Some turn to raiding and banditry, earning the hostility of their neighbors. Others build lasting ties through trade and even friendship. Across much of the world, the reputation of kobolds as raiders, thieves, and servants of dragons colors the perception of individuals who may be none of those things. 

### Belief and Worship

Kobold religious life is shaped by a readiness to find sacred meaning in what the world offers. Though all kobolds agree their species was created long ago, in the ages of myths and legends before the world took form and shape during the [[Riving]], kobolds tell many diverse and conflicting tales of how they were created. With no universally accepted tale of their creation, or universal creator deity all kobolds worship, their communities tend to gather prayers, stories, and traditions from many sources. A dragon, a place of extraordinary magic, or a god worshipped by neighboring peoples may become the center of a warren’s religious life. In some communities, these practices grow into deep and enduring devotion, where a borrowed god may become the beloved protector of a community; in others, borrowed traditions are cast off as each generation seeks new powers and practices.

## Historical Perspective

Kobolds preserve many accounts of their beginnings. Some tell of ancient dragons who created them as servants, spies, and messengers, giving them a place beside the greatest creatures in the world. Others describe an accidental creation, life emerging from something a dragon cast away. No one story is agreed by all kobolds; indeed, it is often said there are as many stories of kobold origins as there are kobolds. 

These stories shape how communities understand their lives. Those who believe themselves created for dragons may see service as a sacred inheritance, or claim a kinship that deserves recognition. Those who tell of accidental beginnings may find pride in having survived and made a place for themselves without their creator’s care. For kobolds, the unanswered question of their origin leaves considerable room to decide what their future should be.

## Details for DMs

### Metaphysics and Creation

Kobolds were created before the [[Riving]]. Their creator, the process of their creation, and any original purpose remain unspecified. Kobolds do not know their own origins, and none of their surviving traditions is established as the true account. 

Their death and afterlife metaphysics remain open, as does the explanation for wings and other unusual draconic traits. 

### Kobolds in Play

#### Addermarch Campaign

[[Zibzig Sparkscale]] featured one adventure. He was explicitly basically a run-away from home, somewhere undetermined. No details exist outside the linked note, really, but basically he was comic relief and roleplaying opportunity in a dungeon without many others. Some details on his "lair" in [[Adventure 4 - Into the Underdark]]. 

#### Cleenseau Campaign

[[Cleenseau - Session 29]] and [[Cleenseau - Session 30]] establish competing factions among Krauzeth's followers, including kobolds who wanted to escape him. Drubgrak and Vikka's group helped the party arrange the ambush and fought hostile kobolds during the battle. Drubgrak's insistence that flight conferred authority led the group to offer tribute to [[Greymalkin]] after he flew. The [[cleenseau-blog-826614 - Original|original recap for Session 31]] records their settlement in Asineau, friendship between one kobold and the lizardfolk youths, and service as scouts during the royal escort.

[[2025-11-11 - Species and Cultures]] records that the fleeing kobolds spoke Common because they feared Draconic might attract Krauzeth's attention. That fear was part of their portrayal, not an established magical property of the language.

#### Dunmar Campaign

Kobolds have only come up really in connection to some stuff that Kadmos and friends ended up dealing with, rumors of kobold bandits on the Chardon-Dunmar road west of Songara. Note that this is implied to be a long-standing "problem" based on [[Kirian]], who was wounded in DR 1720s fighting kobolds in this area. 

Some more detail in a non-canonical planning note, brainstorming in case you ended up chasing kobold rumors. The basic vibe is likely true; certainly kobold bandits in the Myraeni Gap are canonical: 

*The land north and west of [[Songara]] is known as the Myraeni Gap, an expanse of rough, broken terrain, full of cliffs and gullies, as the land tumbles down from the high plateaus of Dunmar to wide lowlands stretching to Chardon and the sea. During the years before the Great War, the area was patrolled by a strange people who protected this the wild broken lands between Chardon and Dunmar, keeping the trade safe, but never allowing outsiders into their hill forts near the Sentinel Range. Since the chaos of the Blood Years, they have been rarely seen; in their absence kobolds have become a nuisance, exacting heavy tolls on trade and often turning to banditry. While many Dunmari are wary of inviting the Chardonian empire to their borders, some in [[Songara]] now council that the Empire may be the only option for keeping the roads safe and trade possible.*

Note that the vibe is not necessarily "war parties", and might involve more "contraptions that block the road unless you pay up." But none of this was canonically developed anyway. 

The one canon piece is that Marcella hid in kobold tunnels from Kadmos for awhile, at least implying a complicated underground lair. 

#### Great Library Campaign

Kobolds came up four times in the Great Library campaign.

- Originally in the first adventure as the occupants of a very simple lair, which was destroyed by party; portrayed basically as trying to hide from anyone. Worshipped a relic of elemental fire. See [[Session 1 - Fire Scroll - DM Notes]]
- Once as basically bandits, but bandits who were willing to ransom a hostage they had taken for coin. See [[Kobold Lair - DM Notes]]
- Once as kind of frenemies who could be bought off to help; they were more-or-less hired as guides to lead the party to a roc egg being held captive by a dao. See [[Roc Quest - DM Notes]]
- Once as servants (but vibe is kind of corrupted slaves) of a beholder - these were canonically the same group, or at least knew of, the original kobolds. Some were scared and basically slaves, others mind-controlled/corrupted: see [[Goldpeak Mines - DM Notes]]. 

In this campaign tended to play them as either cunning / clever but susceptible to bribery and greedy, or as a little naive/foolish and susceptible to browbeating or clever ruses. 

The bandits + dao scouts both had extensive lairs with traps. The beholder slaves and the fire elemental worshippers did not.
