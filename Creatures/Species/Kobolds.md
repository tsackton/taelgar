# Kobolds

In abandoned mines, concealed hillside warrens, and the shadows of dragon lairs, kobolds make homes in a world of creatures larger and often stronger than themselves. Ingenious builders and eager bargainers, they survive through hidden passages, clever contraptions, useful friendships, and powerful protectors. Their communities gather languages, skills, and religious traditions from the peoples around them, making borrowed things their own. Some find purpose in the service of dragons; others fiercely guard their independence. Behind their varied ways of life lies a shared uncertainty: kobolds have many stories about who made them, but no certain knowledge of their beginnings.

## Appearance

Most kobolds are small, scaled humanoids with a distinctly draconic appearance. Some are larger than their kin, with more pronounced draconic features, and a few have wings. Such traits can be a source of considerable pride, especially in communities that look to dragons as patrons or models of greatness.

## Language and Naming

_See more: [[Playing a Kobold#Kobold Names|Kobold Names]]_

Kobolds speak the languages of their homes and neighbors, with no separate tongue shared by the species. Common and Draconic are widespread, although the languages heard in any particular warren reflect its friendships, patrons, and history. Draconic carries particular weight among those who serve or worship dragons.

Naming customs are similarly varied. Names may reflect the languages spoken within a community or those of the peoples among whom a kobold grew up. Descriptive bynames can commemorate a talent, an ambition, or a memorable experience.

## Lifecycle

Kobolds lay eggs and mature and age at roughly the same rate as humans. Some communities maintain communal hatcheries, tending their eggs together within the shelter of the warren.

## Settlements and Homelands

Kobold settlements are often tucked into places that larger peoples overlook: caves, abandoned workings, and narrow tunnels beneath rough hills. Their warrens can be intricate places, with concealed entrances, workshops, food stores, and passages connecting chambers that seem isolated to an unfamiliar visitor. Mines and mushroom farms support some underground communities. Traps and escape routes are important defenses, allowing inhabitants to retreat, divide intruders, and defend their homes without meeting an enemy in open battle.

Kobolds are scattered across many regions, including the [[Fiatara Mountains]], the [[Myraeni Gap]], the [[Plaguelands]], and the caves of [[Addermarch]]. Some settlements lie within the domains of dragons or other powerful patrons, whose protection shapes the lives of everyone beneath it. Independent warrens rely on secrecy, defensible terrain, and arrangements with their neighbors. Individuals and small groups also make homes among other peoples, although suspicion often makes such settlement difficult.

## Cultural Traits

### Ingenuity and Opportunity

Kobold ingenuity grows from the practical demands of living among stronger neighbors. A concealed entrance, a well-placed trap, or a timely bargain can accomplish what strength alone cannot. Their craftwork and architecture often reflect this attention to possibilities: how a passage might serve as an escape, how a mechanism might deter an intruder, or how knowledge of a place might become something worth trading.

This inventiveness also finds expression in magical experimentation and personal ambition. Kobold craftspeople and spellcasters may take great pride in their creations, seeking recognition for a clever device or an unfamiliar spell. The wish to be taken seriously can be powerful among a people so often dismissed by outsiders.

### Patrons and Independence

Powerful patrons occupy an important place in many kobold communities. A dragon can offer protection, wealth, and a sense of belonging to something magnificent. Service may carry religious meaning, and devoted followers can regard their work as a rightful place in the world.

Such relationships also demand much. Tribute, obedience, and dangerous service can bind a warren to the fortunes of its protector. Questions of loyalty and independence can divide a community deeply: some see security in an enduring allegiance, while others see a future in choosing their own friends and purposes.

Independent kobolds cultivate other means of survival. Bargains, local knowledge, and alliances help sustain their communities. Some turn to raiding, ransom, and toll-taking, earning the hostility of their neighbors. Others build lasting ties through trade, shared work, and friendship. Across much of the world, the reputation of kobold raiders precedes travelers who have never taken part in such acts.

### Belief and Worship

Kobold religious life is shaped by a readiness to find sacred meaning in what the world offers. With no known creator to whom all owe allegiance, their communities gather prayers, stories, and observances from many sources. A dragon, a place of extraordinary magic, or a god worshipped by neighboring peoples may become the center of a warren’s religious life.

Kobolds often borrow freely, adding unfamiliar rites to inherited traditions or adopting a god whose protection they hope to earn. A traveler may bring home a prayer learned abroad; a community may embrace the faith of its neighbors while preserving older stories of draconic creation. Different warrens can develop very different answers to whom they should worship and why.

These practices can grow into deep and enduring devotion. A borrowed god becomes the beloved protector of a community, and an improvised observance becomes a tradition passed through generations. Other communities remain willing to seek new powers, change their practices, or abandon patrons who have failed them.

## Historical Perspective

Kobolds preserve many accounts of their beginnings. Some tell of ancient dragons who created them as servants, spies, and messengers, giving them a place beside the greatest creatures in the world. Others describe an accidental creation, life emerging from something a dragon cast away. No account commands the agreement of all kobolds.

These stories shape how communities understand their lives. Those who believe themselves created for dragons may see service as a sacred inheritance, or claim a kinship that deserves recognition. Those who tell of accidental beginnings may find pride in having survived and made a place for themselves without their creator’s care. For kobolds, the unanswered question of their origin leaves considerable room to decide what their future should be.

## Metaphysics

Kobolds accrete [[Soulstuff]] independently. Their continued existence does not depend on a creator supplying their souls, and adopting the religion of another people does not by itself change the source of their soulstuff. Its precise planar composition remains unspecified; no unusual ability to accrete soulstuff from multiple planes is established.

Their death and afterlife metaphysics remain open, as does the explanation for wings and other unusual draconic traits. These traits do not establish a particular planar affinity.

## Creation Story

Kobolds were created before the [[Riving]]. Their creator, the process of their creation, and any original purpose remain unspecified. Kobolds do not know their own origins, and none of their surviving traditions is established as the true account. Whether their pre-Riving antiquity is known in-world remains undecided.

These decisions were adopted on October 31, 2026. [[Metaphysics of Creatures#Unknown]] still leaves kobold soulstuff undecided, and the older draft in [[Playing a Kobold]] presents creation by dragons as fact; those accounts have not yet been reconciled with these decisions. [[Drubgrak]] also retains name metadata identifying her name's language as "Kobold", despite the decision that there is no separate kobold language; its actual language remains to be determined.

## Additional Campaign Context

### Cleenseau Campaign

[[Cleenseau - Session 29]] and [[Cleenseau - Session 30]] establish competing factions among Krauzeth's followers, including kobolds who wanted to escape him. Drubgrak and Vikka's group helped the party arrange the ambush and fought hostile kobolds during the battle. Drubgrak's insistence that flight conferred authority led the group to offer tribute to [[Greymalkin]] after he flew. The [[cleenseau-blog-826614 - Original|original recap for Session 31]] records their settlement in Asineau, friendship between one kobold and the lizardfolk youths, and service as scouts during the royal escort.

[[2025-11-11 - Species and Cultures]] records that the fleeing kobolds spoke Common because they feared Draconic might attract Krauzeth's attention. That fear was part of their portrayal, not an established magical property of the language.

### Plaguelands Campaign

[[Plaguelands - Part 1 - DM Notes]] preserves a brief recollection of an opening dungeon in which hostages were rescued from kobolds. The detailed notes are lost, limiting what can be inferred about their portrayal.

%%^Campaign:none%%

Kobolds in Taelgar are the children of great dragons of an age long past, created originally to be the servants, spies, and messengers of dragons. When the world was young, and the power of magic strong, they lived in the shadow of their creators and masters. Now, they live across the world, often in caves and tunnels. Renowned for their craftiness and trickery, some kobolds prey on other species, luring them into devious ambushes. Others worship their draconic creatures as gods, hoping to return to their rightful place at the side of the great wyrms of old. Still others forge their own way in the world, seeking their own path. 

Some vibes-based details of how they have been used in play. 

### Addermarch Campaign

[[Zibzig Sparkscale]] featured one adventure. He was explicitly basically a run-away from home, somewhere undetermined. No details exist outside the linked note, really, but basically he was comic relief and roleplaying opportunity in a dungeon without many others. Some details on his "lair" in [[Adventure 4 - Into the Underdark]]. 

### Great Library Campaign

Kobolds came up four times in the Great Library campaign.

- Originally in the first adventure as the occupants of a very simple lair, which was destroyed by party; portrayed basically as trying to hide from anyone. Worshipped a relic of elemental fire. See [[Session 1 - Fire Scroll - DM Notes]]
- Once as basically bandits, but bandits who were willing to ransom a hostage they had taken for coin. See [[Kobold Lair - DM Notes]]
- Once as kind of frenemies who could be bought off to help; they were more-or-less hired as guides to lead the party to a roc egg being held captive by a dao. See [[Roc Quest - DM Notes]]
- Once as servants (but vibe is kind of corrupted slaves) of a beholder - these were canonically the same group, or at least knew of, the original kobolds. Some were scared and basically slaves, others mind-controlled/corrupted: see [[Goldpeak Mines - DM Notes]]. 

In this campaign tended to play them as either cunning / clever but susceptible to bribery and greedy, or as a little naive/foolish and susceptible to browbeating or clever ruses. 

The bandits + dao scouts both had extensive lairs with traps. The beholder slaves and the fire elemental worshippers did not. 

### Dunmar Campaign

Kobolds have only come up really in connection to some stuff that Kadmos and friends ended up dealing with, rumors of kobold bandits on the Chardon-Dunmar road west of Songara. Note that this is implied to be a long-standing "problem" based on [[Kirian]], who was wounded in DR 1720s fighting kobolds in this area. 

Some more detail in a non-canonical planning note, brainstorming in case you ended up chasing kobold rumors. The basic vibe is likely true; certainly kobold bandits in the Myraeni Gap are canonical: 

*The land north and west of [[Songara]] is known as the Myraeni Gap, an expanse of rough, broken terrain, full of cliffs and gullies, as the land tumbles down from the high plateaus of Dunmar to wide lowlands stretching to Chardon and the sea. During the years before the Great War, the area was patrolled by a strange people who protected this the wild broken lands between Chardon and Dunmar, keeping the trade safe, but never allowing outsiders into their hill forts near the Sentinel Range. Since the chaos of the Blood Years, they have been rarely seen; in their absence kobolds have become a nuisance, exacting heavy tolls on trade and often turning to banditry. While many Dunmari are wary of inviting the Chardonian empire to their borders, some in [[Songara]] now council that the Empire may be the only option for keeping the roads safe and trade possible.*

Note that the vibe is not necessarily "war parties", and might involve more "contraptions that block the road unless you pay up." But none of this was canonically developed anyway. 

The one canon piece is that Marcella hid in kobold tunnels from Kadmos for awhile, at least implying a complicated underground lair.
