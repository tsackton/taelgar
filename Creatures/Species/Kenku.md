# Kenku

*(KEHN-koo)*

Kenku, the birdfolk of Taelgar, favor high places and steady winds. Clifftop towns perch above sheltered harbors in the [[Eastern Green Sea]], while bands in the far north fly the summer thermals where the [[Sentinel Range|Sentinels]] meet the plains. Kenku are enigmatic and private, and most kenku settlements are in places unreachable by those who cannot fly. Nonetheless, kenku travelers and wanderers are not an uncommon sight around the Green Sea, and across Taelgar many common folk know of kenku from garbled stories and rumors of the [[Great War]].

## Appearance

Kenku are humanoid birds with diverse appearances. Many kenku who settled in the far north resemble ravens, hawks, owls, or eagles, with plumage, eyes, and beaks matching their avian kin. But rumors and reports of other kenku settlements across the Green Sea speak of individuals resembling gulls, terns, and songbirds as well. All kenku are feathered, with feathered wings that sprout from their backs and support true flight. 

## Language and Naming
_See also: [[Playing a Kenku#Kenku Names|Kenku Names]]_

Kenku speak their own tongue and trade broadly in Common and Eastros across the Green Sea. They typically take a single name, though various bynames are used when needed to distinguish individuals. Naming traditions vary widely across diverse kenku populations. 

## Lifecycle

Kenku mature and age at roughly the same rate as humans. Like the birds they resemble, kenku hatch from eggs, though they are very secretive about their family life and only rumors reach outsiders. 

## Settlements and Homelands

Many kenku settled in the northern plains after the [[Great War]], east of the [[Sentinel Range|Sentinels]] and north of [[Vostok]]. These northern settlements are rarely visited by outsiders, and the exact extent of kenku communities in the north is a subject of much rumor and mystery. Rumors also speak of large populations of islander kenku, living in the [[Eastern Green Sea]]. 

In much of the Green Sea, kenku travelers are more common than settlements, and a wandering kenku making their way across coastal Vostok or the villages of the Teft is not an uncommon sight. Nonetheless, small communities do live in isolation, or sometimes among other species, in a broad area across the [[Green Sea]], with notable populations in [[Tollen]] and the [[Zimkova|Sembaran highlands]]. 

Kenku are rarely seen, though not unheard of, west of the [[Sentinel Range]] and south of the [[Green Sea]].

## Cultural Traits

Though individual kenku vary as all people do, the kenku as a species are a reclusive people. They build settlements around flight, making them largely inaccessible to those who cannot fly, and perhaps because of their traumatic experiences in and before the [[Great War]], tend to avoid speaking too much of their history and culture to outsiders. 

### Aerie Homes

Like the birds they resemble, kenku build settlements that tend to occupy high places, with no roads and a vertical orientation. They frequently take advantage of natural landscapes, so many kenku villages are built on cliffs or mountains, prioritizing defensibility and open vistas over accessibility to outsiders who cannot fly. A typical kenku town might be arrayed in caves and structures carved into the sides of a massive cliff wall, with larger clusters of buildings at high points where houses can group together, or perhaps constructed as a series of towers and perches with no interior stairs at all. In some places, such as the Eastern Green Sea, where kenku frequently trade with other species, they may maintain some infrastructure accessible to those without wings, but rarely do kenku settlements sprawl across the landscape and kenku rarely settle in places where they cannot occupy high ground. 

### A Private People

It is not just their aerie homes that keep kenku mysterious. Many kenku still feel the scars of their treatment by [[Cha'mutte]], and are reluctant to show themselves to the wider world, keeping their history, culture, religion, and even the names of their settlements and homes secret. It is said that some kenku can shift their form, and a traveler enjoying an ale at a tavern in Tollen may, in fact, be a kenku in disguise, appearing to the world as a human. Some say kenku can even hide their thoughts from prying magic. 

Not all kenku share these attitudes, and as the [[Great War]] fades to a distant memory, kenku are increasingly settling among other peoples. A small but growing kenku community in Tollen, for example, shows the changing nature of kenku culture. 

## Historical Perspective

Though kenku rarely speak of their history, it is well-known that many kenku were subjugated by [[Cha'mutte]] before and during the [[Great War]], living in squalor in [[Pandar]] and forced to work to support [[Cha'mutte]]'s war effort. Freed kenku warriors were among the fiercest opponents of [[Cha'mutte]] in the [[Great War]] and many died in the fighting. After the [[Great War]], many kenku retreated from settled lands to make their own way in the north, finding places where they could live in isolation and peace away from the traumas of the world. 

Some people, especially traders on the Green Sea, speak of kenku living in the eastern Green Sea who were never under Cha'mutte's thumb, and may preserve a memory of the pre-War history of the species. But if they do, they do not speak of it.

## DM Notes

**Origins and the Firstborn.** Kenku existed before Cha'mutte's rise. [[Aerin]] became their Firstborn through time travel during the Great War campaign; their creation belongs in the [[Riving]], as clarified in [[2026-09-28 - Riving History and Linter Review]]. Aerin's worship of his own divine self is the central paradox. The precise metaphysics, their place in the species taxonomy, and how kenku themselves understand this relationship remain open, as does what outsiders know.

**Divine name.** Older sources call the god Vorshitaal or Vortishaal. The name likely derives from Votishal of Lankhmar/Nehwon; [[1998-03 - Emails E090-E099|email E093]] already flags its forgotten provenance. See the [AD&D Votishal entry](https://1eonline.info/4ddg/votishal.htm). The divine name remains undecided.

**Divine appearances.** The [[dunmar-frontier-139-transcript|Dunmari Frontier epilogue]] (u1315–u1378) reveals Aerin in god-of-the-kenku form as both the Salty Squid bartender and [[Kecha]], listening to the party's private conversations while traveling in disguise. His subsequent journey to the eastern islanders confirms that some kenku were never enslaved by Cha'mutte.

**Regional portrayal.** Preserve the kenku's broad Green Sea focus as further communities are developed. [[Session 83 (DuFr)]] establishes northern withdrawal after liberation and younger kenku seeking wider contact. [[Wahacha]] provides a trading community with an accessible harbor beneath its aerial homes; [[Kemeko Monastery]] preserves Aerin's martial traditions. These communities offer distinct models for kenku life. Wellby's encounters with [[Makha]], [[Nahto]], [[Skoda]], and [[Rufus]] appear in [[Session 60 (DuFr)]], [[Session 62 (DuFr)]], and [[Session 66 (DuFr)]].
