# Orcs

> [!image|hero]
> ![[orcs-war-without-end.webp]]
> *An orc warband on the march.*

> [!quote] The Blood Binding, excerpt
>_O Thark, whose chains no hand can break, by fire and blood this soul we bind._  
>_No peace. No rest. Let hunger burn. Let strife be all this soul shall find._

Scattered across Taelgar, small orc bands roam the margins of settled lands, raiding and pillaging in the name of [[Thark]], the God of War Without End. Orcs are Thark's creation, bound to his will and driven to ceaseless conflict, fighting as much among themselves as against other peoples. The burning fire of his curse turns their strength against neighboring peoples and one another alike. When a strong leader blessed by Thark arises, they can force warring bands to fight as one in a ravaging horde. These hordes, emerging from marginal lands and sweeping across the land, are what give orcs their fearsome reputation.

## Appearance and Lifecycle

Orcs are robust and imposing, often standing over six feet tall, with tusks protruding from their lower lips. Their skin ranges from green to dull gray, and their eyes are typically black or red. 

Orcs mature much faster than humans, reaching adulthood at about ten years of age. Their natural lifespan is similar to that of humans, but most die in battle long before old age. Despite high birth rates, the constant warfare between orc bands usually keeps their populations small.

## Warbands and Hordes

> [!image|right standard]
> ![[orcs-horde-leader.webp]]
> *An orc horde leader in battle gear.*

Orc communities are generally small, and scattered widely across Taelgar, often in marginal lands on the edges of settled realms. They lack centralized governance, and an orc band often extends no further than a large family. Compelled to violence by [[Thark]], these orc groups frequently fight each other, although they raid settled areas when they can. These raids against neighboring settlements provide food, weapons, and treasure, as well as captives who may be held for ransom, forced to labor, or killed.

At times, a leader blessed by [[Thark]] may unite many small bands, turning their violence towards a common enemy, and forming a horde that can number in the hundreds or thousands. These large hordes can emerge out of marginal lands unexpectedly, marching under the banner of a strong leader, and threatening the cities and kingdoms of other peoples.

## Thark's Chains

Thark's will dominates and controls the lives of those bound to him. Orcish religion, such as it is, glorifies strength, victory, and the idea that might makes right. Thark drives his followers to fight one another, blessing the victors with strength. War serves his will even when nothing is gained from it: his curse demands violence without purpose, war for the sake of war.

The bond to Thark is imposed through a ritual performed on newborn orcs, a ritual that all orcs bound by Thark feel compelled to carry out. Few outsiders understand its workings, and orcs do not allow others to observe it, but it is rumored that this ritual infuses a newborn orc with the fire and strength of Thark's will through a cleansing fire that binds them to a life of conflict. Infants who cannot or will not accept the curse are said not to survive the rite.

Rumors circulate throughout Taelgar of unchained orcs, free orcs that have broken the bonds of [[Thark]]'s curse, and live without the burning fire of [[Thark]]'s divine will driving them to ceaseless violence.

## Free Orcs

> [!image|right standard]
> ![[orcs-farmer-at-rest.webp]]
> *A free orc farmer rests beside his fields after a hot day's work.*

Thark's chains can be escaped. Orcs who have never undergone the binding ritual grow up free of his compulsion, and free to live their lives for themselves. Others have had their bonds severed through powerful magic. Once tied to [[Thark]], breaking the connection is very hard, so most free orcs are those who were born free, usually to other free orcs who, naturally, refuse to carry out the binding ritual.

For those once bound, freedom can be difficult. Some orcs freed from bondage describe the fire of Thark's chains still burning at times, long after liberation. Among the [[People of the Rainbow]], the [[Hezguli]] offer support and fellowship to orcs learning to live with these scars.

Orcs born free, having never endured the chains of Thark themselves, do not suffer the scars of Thark. But all free orcs face danger from outside. None are more hated by Thark's followers than those who have broken free: any community that turns from [[Thark]]'s way is quickly subject to unceasing attacks until they are defeated and subjugated, unless they can find a way to hide from [[Thark]]'s probing eyes. Among other peoples, free orcs must also contend with the fearsome reputation of Thark's warbands, and often struggle to distinguish themselves from their [[Thark]]-cursed brethren.

## The People of the Rainbow

The [[People of the Rainbow]] are a community of free orcs living in the deserts east of [[Dunmar]]. They trace their beginnings to orc children raised in [[Amberglow]], under the protection of the [[Cloudspinner]], who kept them from Thark's influence, and wove the [[Cloak of Rainbows]] for them. The cloak protected them from Thark and allowed them to venture into Taelgar and free others. But it was not to last: the cloak was stolen by [[Apollyon]] and remained lost for centuries after the [[Fall of Drankor]]. The People of the Rainbow survived for centuries of darkness, in scattered, hidden communities.

After the [[Great War]], the [[Cloak of Rainbows]] was found by [[Uzgash]], who settled [[Xurkhaz]] as the homeland of the People of the Rainbow in the deserts east of [[Dunmar]]. In Xurkhaz, irrigated fields and cattle pastures support villages and towns decorated with colorful paintings and carvings. The rulers of Xurkhaz maintain the cloak's protection by walking the kingdom's borders, while soldiers defend its settlements. Freeing others from Thark remains an enduring hope of the People of the Rainbow, alongside and as part of the work of sustaining their own families and communities.

> [!image|figure standard]
> ![[orcs-life-in-xurkhaz.webp]]
> *Village life beside the irrigated fields of Xurkhaz.*

Among the People of the Rainbow it is believed that a life well lived becomes poison to Thark. They believe that happiness, good deeds, and the defense of others strengthen a soul for a final blow against him after death.

## DM Notes

### Orcs in Play

- **Thark's compulsion:** the central framing of orcs is violence imposed by Thark, metaphorically represented by Thark's chains. 
- **Warbands:** lots of examples including [[Grumella]], [[Ravager of Winter]]. Note that warbands need not be led by orcs: [[Grash]], [[Agata]], and [[Wakog]] all led orc hordes.
- **Free communities:** Though Xurkhaz and the People of the Rainbow the only existing free orc culture, they should be seen as just a single example, not a template. Other free orc communities likely exist. 

### Binding, Freedom, and the Cloak

- **Infancy matters:** in Session 76, Lubash says that the binding ritual works only on infants. Orcs raised without it remain free, and free parents can raise free children. Capture by a hostile warband therefore need not mean an adult can simply be ritually bound for the first time.
- **Suppression and liberation:** Lubash describes bound orcs becoming unable to fight effectively when Thark's voice falls silent within Xurkhaz. Murook describes both the quiet she felt near the cloak and a subsequent severing of her chains. The exact metaphysics of the cloak are undeveloped but it can also free bound orcs, or at least some bound orcs. 
- **Crown of Purity**: In DR 1750, Riswynn is on a quest to challenge Thark, or at least make it easier for free orcs to exist, using the Crown of Purity. The exact outcome of this is undecided. 

### Open Questions and Ideas to Reuse

- **Other free communities:** There are presumably others, but haven't been developed. Since any free orc community would have a reason to hide, they can be dropped almost anywhere.
- **Family life — brainstorming:** the November 2023 discussion suggests some free orcs avoid children because they fear Thark might claim them, while others have large families because they trust their freedom. This could give individuals contrasting relationships to parenthood.
- **Religion and gratitude — brainstorming:** the same discussion explores distrust of gods, gratitude toward ancestors who earned the Cloudspinner's gift, and gratitude toward rulers who do the difficult work of maintaining Xurkhaz's protection. Riswynn offers a reason for some individuals to reconsider their attitudes toward divine powers.
