# Centaurs

> [!image|hero]
> ![[centaurs-migration-stream-crossing.webp]]

>[!quote] Centaur creation myth
*Long ago, before the form of the world was fixed, we walked the ever-changing land, mindless and soulless, empty shells. Then Yestare, the Beginning, and Mettare, the Ending, stepped into this chaos, and spoke words of separation, words of renewal, words of power to fix the form of the world. In this timeless moment of sundering, the World reached out, and chose us to be its guardians.*
>
*Taelgar itself gave us life, a purpose, freedom and souls, an act of need to ensure the land was not forgotten, an act not of the gods, even Yestare and Mettare, but of a higher purpose. In that moment, we awoke to our new selves. Our hoofbeats were the first to stride across the new world, fixed in form. Our souls were the first to be made, not by any god or power but by the need of the land itself.*

Striding across the open plains of the south, their hoofbeats ringing, a herd of centaurs turns north, following the winds and weather. The gentle strum of a harp as voices raise in song, in praise of the earth, around a campfire. The cycle of migrations, following the seasons across Taelgar. These are the rhythms of the centaur life.

## Appearance

With the torso, arms, and head of a human, and the four legs and body of a horse, centaurs are a blend of strength and grace. Typically standing 6 or 7 feet tall, with their equine bodies reaching 4 feet at the withers, centaurs are an imposing presence. They can run quickly, for miles, carrying a heavy load, and without tiring. While all centaurs share the same form, their appearance is otherwise as varied as the natural world they love.

## Language and Naming
_See more: [[Playing a Centaur#Centaur Names|Centaur Names]]_

Centaurs speak their own language, rarely, but not never, taught to outsiders. Most centaurs speak Elvish, Sylvan, or both in addition to their own tongue, and most outsiders who must speak with them do so in one of those tongues.

Centaur names are diverse, often sharing the lyrical sound of Sylvan. Different bands may follow different naming traditions, and there is no single naming pattern shared by all centaurs.

## Lifecycle

Centaurs mature and age at broadly the same rate as humans. Some young adult centaurs leave their home bands to travel independently or seek another community, but this is not a universal custom.

## Settlements and Homelands

Centaur societies are almost exclusively migratory, traveling on long migration routes that can take years to complete, sometimes even generations. Some centaur societies shape their travels to seek new marvels of the natural world, while others follow the time-honored paths of generations. While they will sometimes spend a season or a year in one place, building temporary camps and tending the local land, few centaurs build cities or other permanent structures.

Some more isolated bands have little contact with humans or others, preferring to remain far from civilization. Centaurs who settle down, turning from the regular migrations of their people, typically live alone or with a family, rather than in villages, though some choose to settle in the cities and towns of other species. 

## Cultural Traits

Centaurs tend to be reclusive, staying on the outskirts of the societies of other peoples. Their connection to Taelgar shapes both their pride in their independence and their concern for the health of the land, though it often makes centaurs appear aloof or arrogance to outsiders.

### Pride and Independence

Many centaurs regard themselves as the first people of Taelgar, proud of their belief that they came into being independently, without a creator god, the first to run across the land of Taelgar. They place great value on their freedom and can be headstrong and difficult to shake from a course of action they think is best.

This pride often comes with an aloofness toward the affairs of other peoples. The disputes of human kingdoms and the concerns of their gods can seem distant from a centaur band's own responsibilities to the land.

### Guardians of Taelgar

Centaurs usually do not worship gods, giving thanks and prayers to the world of Taelgar itself instead. Many centaurs view themselves as the protectors and guardians of the natural world. Never numerous, they have little power to stop human cities or empires from spreading, and don’t try. Instead, they try to heal what they can when they see the world is damaged.

Their veneration of Taelgar also manifests itself in other ways. Many centaurs feel uneasy when they are not in contact with the ground, so second stories, ships, and even buildings with stone floors may make them uncomfortable.

Centaurs have an uncanny connection to the winds of fate, and many will speak of how they hear, and feel in their hooves, the resonances of the world of Taelgar. Being able to read these signs and echoes and portents in the world, they say, connects them to fate and the future: other species often seek out centaurs for their skill in prophecy and divination.

Their major form of religious practice, such as it is, celebrates moments of change in the cycle of the seasons, although the exact details vary depending on time and place. Even the same tribe may change its religious rituals over the course of years, as they move into new lands. For example, in Dunmar centaurs would celebrate the coming of the rains, and the end of the monsoon season, whereas in northern Chardon, they would celebrate spring and fall. Wanting no afterlife in some distant plane away from Taelgar, they allow their souls to fade, and believe that they are returned to Taelgar itself. Their bodies are also returned to the earth. Most commonly they are buried, and allowed to decompose, although some are cremated and the ashes scattered. Many graves are not marked, especially those that die of natural causes, but sometimes they will mark the graves of those that fall in battle, especially those that fall in battle defending Taelgar from its enemies, as a memorial and a reminder. ### Livelihoods and Trade

Centaurs tend to have a very light touch on the land, generally building no buildings and plowing no fields. Centaurs gather food from the land, trusting the bounty of the world to sustain them on their long migrations, supplemented with hunting and trade.

They trade animal products—fur, bone, and meat—as well as omens and prophecy, carvings and statuary made of bone, wood, or clay, weaving, and leatherwork. In some bands, a family or a handful of families takes responsibility for knowing outsider languages and organizing trade. These smaller groups may leave the main band for a time and return with goods.

## Details for DMs

### Origins and Open Questions

- The “children of Taelgar” identity is established, but the mechanism of centaur creation remains undecided, though it clearly has sometime to do with the Riving or the aftermath of the Riving, and is intentionally intended to not quite fit established metaphysics. 
- Death customs and the fate of centaur souls remain undeveloped. The older proposals about returning to Taelgar and carrying memorial carvings are tentative.
- Seasonal religious observances remain proposals rather than adopted general customs - see note above. 
- Metaphysics of souls are pretty unclear. It is possible that they are kind of like the equivalent of elementals but for the material plane. 

### Centaurs in Play

- Centaur prophecy emphasizes reading signs and symbols in the physical world: watching for portents in the sky, for example, rather than entering the Dreamworld or speaking with ancestors.
- [[Tharandros]] provides an example of concern for the land taking precedence over human politics, but his particular attitudes should not define every centaur.
- Some young adults travel independently or seek another band. This is a possible adventuring background, not a required stage of centaur life.
- [[Omphale]] provides an established example of a centaur prophet with a settled home.

### Old Notes for Vibes

These are vibes and color from Tim's DM Notes; shouldn't be taken as strict canon
- Often seeking new marvels of the natural world, in a semi-religious sense.
- centaurs that settle outside of the migrations usually would be solitary or perhaps a family, not large villages. but of course there are exceptions to everything. hidden centaur cities could certainly exist in the right place if there was a player need or a game reason to invent such a thing.
- Hunter-gatherers, living in small independent bands. Young adults often will leave to seek a new tribe; most lone centaurs “adventuring” will be these young adults who have left their home tribe and have not yet joined a new one.
- Centaurs are deeply involving in reading the signs and echos and portents in the world, and can sense these because of their connection to Taelgar. This is less of a “Dreamworld-based” prophecy, as lizardfolk, and more of a directly reading the signs and symbols in the physical world. E.g., watching for portents in the sky vs going into a trance and speaking with the ancestors. More Aragon than mystic. 
- The basic creation story of the centaurs is that they were created or perhaps freed from an empty existence or perhaps fixed in their form from a more chaotic ancestry by the force of the Riving. Typically in centaur stories this is interpreted to mean that they were created by the Material Plane itself, by the world of Taelgar, and they consider themselves the true "firsrtborn".
- Really do see themselves as the true first species, and also proud of their belief they are the only species that does not have a creator god, that came to be independently. This makes them a bit arrogant, headstrong, and stubborn about their free will, and hard to shake from a course of action they think is best.

Relationships with other species. Not necessarily canon; some of this was [[Tharandros]]' attitude but not necessarily the entire species:
- They do not particularly have a problem with orcs or many other “evil” races, especially those who simply attack humans, raid, and fade away, although they will certainly defend people who are in imminent danger of being slaughtered. But they are unlikely to, for example, hunt down an orc horde passing across their land. This should read not as selfish, but as “our concerns are larger than an individual village”. 
- Likely - but don't definitely - have poor-ish relationships with natives of other planes. This is not developed in play but could be used to cover older "anti-fey" attitudes.
