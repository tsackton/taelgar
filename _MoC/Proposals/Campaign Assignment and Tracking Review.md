# Campaign assignment and tracking review

**20 November 2026 · Design discussion and handoff.** The next decision is how to determine which campaign produced an ordinary document. Directory-based ownership is agreed for numbered session bundles. Extending that rule to campaign documents is a proposal for discussion.

## Human summary

Taelgar has one ID per game. The keys in `_sessions/campaign-registry.json` identify 22 campaigns and point to editable notes in `Campaigns/_Index`. The transition feels unfinished because different readers still decide for themselves what ties a particular note, person, encounter, or passage to a campaign. Renaming fields alone would leave those differences in place. See [Campaign Registry](<../Campaign Registry.md>) for the adopted conventions and [[Campaign ID Transition]] for the migration history.

**Document ownership is the best place to simplify first.** In a scan of 2,907 tracked world-reference and campaign notes with frontmatter, all 416 explicit `campaign` declarations agreed with the registered directory. All 241 notes tagged `session-note` had an explicit campaign. Yet removing the field from a correctly filed session today could preserve its website backlink ownership while dropping it from the campaign timeline and latest-session selection. Outside those folders there are genuine uses for an explicit declaration, including Dunmar PC reference documents under `assets` and a Chasm index under `_dm_notes`.

**Several campaign relationships are worth keeping.** [[Ghemdorn]] is played by Sasha, belongs to Oskar’s Companions, is known to the Dunmar party, and has a recorded meeting with Riswynn. The Dunmar marker around the meeting in the header determines when the line appears. These facts answer different questions. This page is a useful test case for any proposed consolidation.

**Proposed next discussion.** A campaign record in a registered campaign directory could inherit its owner. An out-of-folder record could declare `campaign` explicitly. If both sources exist and disagree, the tools could report the conflict. First compare reader behavior with all existing declarations present. Decide later whether removing repeated declarations is worth the edits. This proposal concerns records produced by a campaign, such as session notes, handouts, stories, and meta pages. A reference article about a person or place has separate knowledge and encounter relationships.

## Where readers diverge

**Document ownership.** The website backlink resolver checks a declared campaign first. In the absence of a declaration it uses the longest matching registered directory. Name Explorer checks directories before the field. An older CustomJS interaction view tries the configured session folder, parentheses in the filename, and finally the field. The validator treats a session without a declared campaign as an error, recommends a declaration on other campaign records in a registered folder, and diagnoses directory disagreement. The Dataview campaign timeline and website context selector both depend on declared session metadata. An inherited owner should not automatically turn an in-world entity in a campaign folder into a campaign document.

**Knowledge and interaction displays.** The website’s person-page renderer shows the intersection of selected campaigns and registered `knownTo` entries. A known person may have no recorded encounters. Conversely, an encounter can be present in the compiled evidence while the campaign section is absent from the page. The Obsidian interaction component can also work on non-person notes with `knownTo` and uses the author’s local material. An older CustomJS helper named `isKnownToParty()` checks `campaignInfo`, optional tags, and optional incoming links; it never reads `knownTo`. Classify remaining callers by what they need: confirmed knowledge, evidence to review, or a rendered history. The [[Metadata Specification#KnownTo Framework|KnownTo Framework]] already defines the first of these.

**Latest played and latest published.** The tracker is meant to give the DM the latest session actually played, including unpublished games, historical estimates, and dates recorded outside the vault. The website ranks published session notes primarily by real-world date, then session number, and takes the selected note’s full `DR_end` or `DR`. Great Library Arc 9 contains sessions 64–68 under headings in a single note. A page, a played session, and a prepared bundle therefore have different counts. Keep the distinction visible when choosing the website’s context date or backfilling the register.

**Names in code.** Core and Obsidian project the registry key into `code`; some callers also call it `slug`. They expose `campaignDirectory` as `campaignRoot` and derive `sessionRoot` by removing the leading `_sessions/`. The Core source search found one reader of that shortened root, in a recap fallback. By contrast, computing a session-note folder from the directory and note pattern serves a real purpose. Change producers and consumers together when retiring an adapter. Website bookmark aliases and these in-memory field names are separate issues.
