# Calibration cases

These are acceptance cases for editorial judgment, not prose to copy. Resolve
the named session's polished source through its YAML and reread the source before
extracting. UID references locate whole original turn groups. No generated recap
or prior chat excerpt is the authority.

## Current calibration: focused interactions

The October 31 trial of sessions 55, 79, 86, and 99 was too broad and often too
long. The user requested maps first, selected stars, and minimal introductory
context. The later [importance calibration](../../check-session-entities/references/importance.md)
distinguishes session prominence from passage focus and requires much tighter
cameo cuts. The earlier cases below describe source material to inspect, not a
checklist of everything to retain. Apply the focal test to every stretch.

These cases govern selection and cuts, not the retired per-entity Markdown
layout. In interactions v1, each selected focal entity has local `role: star`,
even when its session importance is supporting or cameo. Supporting/cameo entries
describe other mapped entities represented in retained text; their inclusion
does not broaden the focal selection. Store a shared exchange once, in source
order, with original text and marked cuts in `lines`. Mirror-examination flags
remain in the bound entity map rather than being copied into interaction JSON.

### Dunmari Frontier 79: Valius and the Cloak of Rainbows

The focused tests cut too deeply into continuous narration. Restore Valius's
army background, unsuccessful treasure hunting, and early paid expedition as
parts of the story he tells, not disposable setup. Follow his account through
its natural endpoint before the next line of questioning; the threat to his
brother is not the end of the narration. Dice rolls, the table's crown-identification
aside, and the interruption about Evelina's death can still be snipped.

Preserve the cloak's continuous mirror vision, including the silver-scaled man's
argument with Apollyon, the threat, and the transitions between images. Keep
clarifications about what can be understood and the uncertain identification.
These are parts of the vision being shown about the cloak, even when someone
else acts or speaks. Later table speculation can still be cut.

The general rule is to select coherent narrative passages rather than isolated
facts about an entity. Importance governs breadth and surrounding context, not
permission to break up a selected story. This feedback does not classify either
entity's session importance or approve every other cut.

### Items: minimal action, full original lore

The user's tighter item rule applies to use/acquisition even when an item merits
inclusion or a higher importance tier. The Immovable Rod's brief rowing exchange
and the ring of displacement's short loan illustrate the desired economy. Keep
only enough original wording to understand the action and its result or necessary
limitation; do not retain surrounding planning or rules discussion by default.

Original setting history and lore are the exception and should usually be
extracted fully. The Cloak of Rainbows' mirror vision is such an account: its
restored narrative stays intact. Flag the cloak as specifically examined in the
Mirror of the Past in session 79; the mirror itself and other items seen within
that vision do not acquire the flag merely from their presence. Ordinary rules
descriptions do not become original lore simply because they are narrated by the DM.

## Boundary cases

- One substantive sentence about an absent entity can be complete. Do not pad it.
- Mere presence, a passing comparison, or a name in a place label is insufficient.
- An identity occurrence does not prove the whole encounter was read. A mapped
  place/time span need not all be retained.
- A vivid cut description must not replace the target's important action or speech.
- Limited-scene requests need not trigger whole-session processing. Representative
  occurrences alone cannot establish exhaustive coverage.
