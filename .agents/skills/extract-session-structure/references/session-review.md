# Build the session review

Use the six verified extractions for the same session and source revision. The review and its time-watch data are derived from those files. A review-only request starts here. If an extraction is missing, partial, or obsolete, identify the affected section and follow the owning skill when regeneration is in scope. Read the marker evidence, YAML assessment, location readings, entity decisions, interaction roles, classification qualifications, and combat records. Reopen the registered polished transcript for material ambiguities. This is a review of existing extractions. Keep any suspected error visible with its source reference.

## Group in-world time

Work in fictional chronology. Start each actor or group from the best supported day and watch. Carry elapsed play forward cumulatively, using dawn 02–06, morning 06–10, midday 10–14, afternoon 14–18, dusk 18–22, and midnight 22–02. Use those boundaries for clock readings and allow a rough estimate for narrated scenes. Midmorning plus an hour usually stays in morning. Dawn plus a few hours usually reaches morning. Show the resulting watch and the evidence behind a meaningful advance. A later direct anchor or correction can revise an earlier estimate. Avoid invented clock times.

Count a duration accumulated “in all” once. Rejoin parallel clocks by their reunion or other shared anchor. Track nested activities, retroactive rest credit, a return from a flashback, remote or unchanging light, and open-ended rests at their actual scope. Elapsed time without a usable starting point still belongs in the evidence. Leave undated activity out of the table, including planning, resolving a whole day of rumors with a check, and the temptation to list it under an “unknown” watch. A dawn opening and a played lunch produce just those two anchors. Use the civil date at each supported endpoint, keeping separate midnight watches on opposite ends of one day; label relative days when absolute dates are unavailable. Group repeated markers by date and watch, keeping separate rows only for meaningful track, night, or frame differences. Where travel spans multiple watches, use the two endpoints in a single interval row. Assign each accepted marker exactly once. For each row give the strongest clock anchor or elapsed-time calculation in one short line (usually 8–18 words, maximum 28). Name an event only to identify the moment, such as “Lookout corrected to early afternoon. Roadside meeting about 4 p.m.” Keep the remaining markers as references; several can support a row without appearing individually in its prose. Supplementary transcript ranges belong in the watch JSON. No empty watch rows.

## Prepare the review

From the vault root, take a snapshot before drafting. Use a temporary path outside the session bundle for `DRAFT_JSON`.

```sh
python3 .agents/skills/extract-session-structure/scripts/render_review.py SESSION_YAML --prepare > DRAFT_JSON
```

Read any existing review and watch data. Carry forward human decisions and open questions, and revisit those affected by changed evidence. Fill the following keys in the draft. Keep its `inputs` and `previous` snapshots intact.

- `watches` — list of objects with `day` (readable date/relative frame), `startWatch`, `endWatch` (each one of the six lowercase names or null; two equal names mean a single watch), `basis` (stated, inferred, mixed, or open, describing the watch), `summary` (short evidence and whose clock), and `markers` (list of t IDs). Use `clock` (adventure by default, local for a separate local time or social cycle) so a campaign timeline can filter remote readings. Keep stationary lighting in the evidence. It supplies no civil watch. A community’s dinner and sleep signal may suggest local dusk without synchronizing its clock to the party’s world. Cite optional `sourceRanges` as pairs of whole transcript UIDs when using additional source evidence to infer a watch. Optional `scope` identifies only a necessary track, local frame, or flashback; optional `startDate` / `endDate` are ISO civil dates when supported, including YAML-derived dates clearly identified in the text. Only use a watch range when it communicates something specific. Preserve open endpoints in prose.
- `dates` — one sentence distinguishing spoken, inferred, and YAML-only dates.
- `flags` — list of short review questions or precise unresolved findings with source IDs or UIDs; cover material ambiguity in time, location, entity identities, combat, and segment boundaries. Ordinary approximations can stay in the rows. Distinguish a fact established by play from a character's belief or a pending proposal.
- `locations` — location-first rows. Each takes a `kind` (presence, approach, observation), `places` (ordered p IDs), `actors` (exact names or the party label from the cited location markers), and `markers` (l IDs). Choose the major physical settings and the few subgroup positions that distinguish them. A presence row can trace a short route shared by those actors; start a separate row when they split. Use approach for an underway destination. For an observation, use the observer's physical `from` place ID when known and optionally a short `via` label such as “via bat”, “Clairvoyance”, or “dream”. The renderer gives observations their own table. Leave proposals out. Repeated movements in combat rarely merit their own row.
- The location cells contain only place names from the dictionary. Optional `placeLabels` maps a p ID to a shorter, source-supported place name for display (e.g. “Branching chamber”). Retain enough geography to identify the place. Actions, reasons for going, and outcomes stay in the extraction. The Present and Observer columns take named participants or the established party label. For a split use names, such as Seeker, Delwath, Kenzo, and Faldrak.
- `entities` — list of objects with `ids` (e IDs) and `summary` (one session-specific line). Include every star and group supporting entities where it helps. The renderer counts all cameos; omit the inventory of remaining names from the input. Select an individual cameo only when its role matters to this review; surface material ambiguity even for a cameo.
- `combats` — one object per encounter with `id`, `opening` if present, `rounds` (one short string per numbered round, in order), and `ending`. Summarize turning points in resolved order, preserving consequential reactions, conditions and the actual outcome. Omit routine roll-by-roll detail.

## Render and verify

```sh
python3 .agents/skills/extract-session-structure/scripts/render_review.py SESSION_YAML --editorial DRAFT_JSON
```

The renderer reads the six ordinary `cleaned/<stem>-*.json` files. It writes only `structured-data-review.md` and `time-watches.json` beside them. Speaking time adds timestamped utterance durations within each of the five segment classes, counting each source UID once. Gaps are excluded. Overlapping speech counts for each utterance. If timing is unavailable, the review flags the limitation.

For an isolated comparison, pass the same `--input-dir DIRECTORY` (if the six extractions live together elsewhere), `--time-markers PATH` (if only time comes from another run), and separate `--output-dir DIRECTORY` as applicable to both commands. Input files must retain their vault-relative provenance and component bindings. Do not redirect comparison results into an ordinary bundle’s cleaned folder.

Check the rendered page against its linked inputs. Spot-check the first watch reached by elapsed time on each track, physical arrivals against the cited actors, and observed places against the observer’s position. A route place can be passed or bypassed; include it in a physical route only when the cited reading supports that placement. Review each combat round and the reported speaking-time denominator. Return the page, watch JSON, and any material uncertainties. An interrupted write can be retried with the same draft. If inputs or existing outputs changed during drafting, inspect the differences and start a fresh snapshot before applying the affected revisions.
