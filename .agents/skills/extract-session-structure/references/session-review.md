# Build the session review

Use the six verified extractions for the same session and source revision. The review and its time-watch data are derived from those files. A review-only request starts here. If an extraction is missing, partial, or obsolete, identify the affected section and follow the owning skill when regeneration is in scope. Read the marker evidence, YAML assessment, location readings, entity decisions, retained interaction text and roles, classification qualifications, and combat records. Resolve summarization choices from that JSON evidence first; reopen relevant registered polished transcript passages for material ambiguities that it cannot settle. A complete transcript reread is not required. Keep qualifications in the relevant summary; raise a review flag only when a human has a specific action or correction to make.

## Group in-world time

Work in fictional chronology. Start each actor or group from the best supported day and watch. Carry elapsed play forward cumulatively, using dawn 02–06, morning 06–10, midday 10–14, afternoon 14–18, dusk 18–22, and midnight 22–02. Use those boundaries for clock readings and allow a rough estimate for narrated scenes. Midmorning plus an hour usually stays in morning. Dawn plus a few hours usually reaches morning. Show the resulting watch and the evidence behind a meaningful advance. A later direct anchor or correction can revise an earlier estimate. Avoid invented clock times.

**Feywild time.** A rest or awarded rest benefits mean time passed: one hour for a short rest and eight hours for a long rest unless the JSON establishes otherwise. The sun does not move, so a long rest can start in the afternoon and end in the afternoon while eight hours pass. Rely on rests and explicitly narrated elapsed time, not sunlight or the landscape's appearance. Preserve strange or nonsensical time points rather than forcing a conventional chronology.

Count a duration accumulated “in all” once. Rejoin parallel clocks by their reunion or other shared anchor. Track nested activities, retroactive rest credit, a return from a flashback, remote or unchanging light, and open-ended rests at their actual scope. Elapsed time without a usable starting point still belongs in the evidence. Leave undated activity out of the table, including planning, resolving a whole day of rumors with a check, and the temptation to list it under an “unknown” watch. A dawn opening and a played lunch produce just those two anchors. Use the civil date at each supported endpoint, keeping separate midnight watches on opposite ends of one day; label relative days when absolute dates are unavailable. Group repeated markers by date and watch, keeping separate rows only for meaningful track, night, or frame differences. Where travel spans multiple watches, use the two endpoints in a single interval row. Assign each accepted marker exactly once. For each row give the strongest clock anchor or elapsed-time calculation in one short line (usually 8–18 words, maximum 28). Name an event only to identify the moment, such as “Lookout corrected to early afternoon. Roadside meeting about 4 p.m.” Keep the remaining markers as references; several can support a row without appearing individually in its prose. Supplementary transcript ranges belong in the watch JSON. No empty watch rows.

## Prepare the review

From the vault root, take a snapshot before drafting. Use a temporary path outside the session bundle for `DRAFT_JSON`.

```sh
python3 .agents/skills/extract-session-structure/scripts/render_review.py SESSION_YAML --prepare > DRAFT_JSON
```

Read any existing review and watch data. Preserve human decisions and actionable open questions, and revisit those affected by changed evidence. Reassess old flags under the action requirement below rather than copying every observation. Fill the following keys in the draft. Keep its `inputs` and `previous` snapshots intact.

- `watches` — list of objects with `day` (readable date/relative frame), `startWatch`, `endWatch` (each one of the six lowercase names or null; two equal names mean a single watch), `basis` (stated, inferred, mixed, or open, describing the watch), `summary` (short evidence and whose clock), and `markers` (list of t IDs). Use `clock` (adventure by default, local for a separate local time or social cycle) so a campaign timeline can filter remote readings. Keep stationary lighting in the evidence. It supplies no civil watch. A community’s dinner and sleep signal may suggest local dusk without synchronizing its clock to the party’s world. Cite optional `sourceRanges` as pairs of whole transcript UIDs when using additional source evidence to infer a watch. Optional `scope` identifies only a necessary track, local frame, or flashback; optional `startDate` / `endDate` are ISO civil dates when supported, including YAML-derived dates clearly identified in the text. Only use a watch range when it communicates something specific. Preserve open endpoints in prose.
- `dates` — one sentence distinguishing spoken, inferred, and YAML-only dates.
- `flags` — only obvious, specific actions or corrections a human should take. State what to check, decide, or correct, the affected field or claim, and the source IDs or UIDs supporting that request. Preserve genuine actionable uncertainty and existing human decisions. Do not flag observations, explanations, ordinary approximations, or uncertainty the JSON already resolves. There is no requirement to produce flags for every topic; use an empty list when no human action is needed. Distinguish established facts, beliefs, and proposals in their relevant summary rows.
- `locations` — location-first rows. Each takes a `kind` (presence, approach, observation), `places` (ordered p IDs), `actors` (exact names or the party label from the cited location markers), and `markers` (l IDs). Choose the major physical settings and the few subgroup positions that distinguish them, following **Played settings and travel routes** below. A presence row with multiple places renders as an arrow chain and requires a nonempty `routeBasis`: a short JSON-backed explanation of why these places form travel rather than separate substantive played stops. This drafting rationale is not rendered. Start a separate row when actors split. Use approach for an underway destination. For an observation, use the observer's physical `from` place ID when known and optionally a short `via` label such as “via bat”, “Clairvoyance”, or “dream”. The renderer gives observations their own table. Leave proposals out. Repeated movements in combat rarely merit their own row.
- The location cells contain only place names from the dictionary. Optional `placeLabels` maps a p ID to a shorter, source-supported place name for display (e.g. “Branching chamber”). Retain enough geography to identify the place. Actions, reasons for going, and outcomes stay in the extraction. The Present and Observer columns take named participants or the established party label. For a split use names, such as Seeker, Delwath, Kenzo, and Faldrak.
- `entities` — list of objects with `ids` (e IDs) and `summary` (one session-specific line). Include every star and group supporting entities where it helps. The renderer counts all cameos; omit the inventory of remaining names from the input. Select an individual cameo only when its role matters to this review; surface material ambiguity even for a cameo.
- `combats` — one object per encounter with `id`, `opening` if present, `rounds` (one short string per numbered round, in order), and `ending`. Summarize turning points in resolved order, preserving consequential reactions, conditions and the actual outcome. Omit routine roll-by-roll detail.

When reusing an older editorial draft, reassess each multi-place presence row before rendering: split substantive played stops or add `routeBasis` for genuine travel. Do not add a generic rationale merely to make an old itinerary pass validation.

For example, “t01 is stationary Feywild light; t04's rate difference is hypothetical; the sheet reset establishes no additional rest” requests no correction and earns no review flag. Keep any useful qualification with the time evidence. “Decide whether e028 is Count Vashan before merging the identities; the JSON retains only a medium-confidence match at u1515–u1520” is actionable while that identity decision remains open. Once resolved, preserve the decision without continuing to flag it. Source limitations and missing timing belong in their own sections; promote one to a review flag only when there is a specific human task to request.

### Played settings and travel routes

Give each substantive played stop its own presence row with one place. A substantial conversation, investigation, encounter, consequential decision, or other independently useful activity can make a place a played setting even when the extraction represents arrival only with a movement marker. Do not require a presence marker or a segment boundary. Conversely, a presence marker, arrival, place mention, or long UID span alone does not prove substantive play.

Use location marker readings, evidence, actors, context, completion, and route descriptions to establish physical placement. Read the interaction JSON's retained `lines` and `reviewContext` for what happens there; use its `source` UIDs to align passages with spatial evidence. Those ranges are citations, not occupancy intervals: an exchange can span a departure, and a conversation can continue while walking. Entity roles and titles alone do not establish a stop. Combat JSON can establish a played encounter setting. Segment classification describes activity, not scenes. Missing interactions do not prove that a place is transient; use the other retained evidence and flag a material uncertainty rather than inventing a stop or compressing it away.

Keep arrow chains for actual travel through transient waypoints, including accepted compressed narration, brief crossings, and incidental stops with no substantive independent play. Shared actors or consecutive arrivals alone do not make several played settings one route. A route may span multiple movement markers; it need not be split by distance, time, or classification changes. Preserve the distinction between passing, bypassing, approaching, observing, and entering. Do not add every geographical mention as a visit. Keep substantive stops separate from any useful travel row; do not repeat the whole itinerary merely to connect them. Cite the same arrival marker in separate setting rows when it supports both; no extraction changes are needed.

## Render and verify

```sh
python3 .agents/skills/extract-session-structure/scripts/render_review.py SESSION_YAML --editorial DRAFT_JSON
```

The renderer reads the six ordinary `cleaned/<stem>-*.json` files. It writes only `structured-data-review.md` and `time-watches.json` beside them. Recording time comes from the YAML-registered cleaned UID transcript, falling back to the prepared UID transcript when cleaned is not registered. Its headers contain ranges such as `[u0001 | 00:00:08.710-00:00:10.210 | DM]`. Polished Markdown markers contain character offsets, not recording timestamps. Do not use speaker-stat totals as recording length.

The recording-length estimate is the timestamp extent, `latest end − earliest start`, across all UIDs, not necessarily the final UUID's end. Display the endpoints too; silence before the first or after the last timestamp and the complete audio-file duration remain unknown. For each segment JSON record, elapsed time spans its earliest UID start through its latest UID end (normally its first start and last end), including internal gaps and pauses. Sum those blocks by class; do not span intervening blocks of other classes or merge separate encounters. Speaking time covers only the UID timestamp intervals. Count overlapping milliseconds once in each measure, assigning overlaps to the earliest source block or utterance so class totals remain additive. Elapsed and speaking shares each use their own total. Report gaps between block spans separately from both totals.

Keep missing, invalid, or backwards timestamps visibly unavailable or ambiguous rather than treating them as zero or interpreting polished offsets as times. Timestamp starts running backwards may indicate a clock reset or reordered source; do not sum those timelines as if they shared a reliable recording clock. Source-fingerprint or segment-coverage failures still stop rendering. Timing limitations are explanatory recording-time notes, not automatic review flags.

For an isolated comparison, pass the same `--input-dir DIRECTORY` (if the six extractions live together elsewhere), `--time-markers PATH` (if only time comes from another run), and separate `--output-dir DIRECTORY` as applicable to both commands. Input files must retain their vault-relative provenance and component bindings. Do not redirect comparison results into an ordinary bundle’s cleaned folder.

Check the rendered page against its linked inputs. Spot-check the first watch reached by elapsed time on each track, physical arrivals against the cited actors, and observed places against the observer’s position. For every arrow chain, check the JSON evidence for substantive played stops hidden inside it; split those into setting rows. A route place can be passed or bypassed; include it in a physical route only when the cited reading supports that placement. In Feywild rows, check that lighting or landscape impressions have supplied no inferred watch or day transition. The renderer checks references and requires a route rationale; substantive stops and actionable flags remain editorial decisions. Review each combat round, recording endpoints, elapsed blocks, and both time denominators. For every flag, identify the concrete human action; otherwise move useful context to its relevant summary or omit it. Return the page, watch JSON, and any actionable uncertainties. An interrupted write can be retried with the same draft. If inputs or existing outputs changed during drafting, inspect the differences and start a fresh snapshot before applying the affected revisions.
