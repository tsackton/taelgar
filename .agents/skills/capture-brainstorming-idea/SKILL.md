# Capture Brainstorming Idea

Save a casual idea with useful fuel for later. Select for contribution, not length. Keep research and links in the note and chat to brief confirmations.

## Capture and destination

- A request to capture ideas, including invoking this skill, authorizes writing the monthly log and continuing to capture subsequent ideas in that conversation without repeated approval. Follow-ups about an idea belong with its existing entry. Workflow discussion and unrelated conversation are not new captures.
- Use `Worldbuilding/Research and Mechanics/Idea Capture/YYYY-MM.md`, one note per calendar month across topics and inspiration sources. Resolve the month and entry date using the user's timezone; preserve the original capture date when importing older ideas. Reuse the existing monthly note; do not create separate topic or source notes or an index.
- Record the inspiration in a short line for each entry: documentary or episode, book, conversation, place, original thought, or another source. Infer it from chat history when clear, including an ongoing source across successive ideas. Ask a concise question if the source is missing, confused, or uncertain; do not invent an attribution. Capture the idea and continue independent research while waiting.
- Preserve the user's idea verbatim, including wording, spelling, punctuation, capitalization, and paragraph/line breaks. A blockquote can distinguish it from your additions. Do not silently correct the quoted idea; put a useful factual correction in the research beside the relevant point.
- Append a short dated heading, the inspiration line, the original idea, and useful additions. Extend the relevant entry for follow-ups while retaining the original capture. Read the monthly note and inspect local changes before writing, following root Git and preservation rules.

Monthly capture notes have **no YAML frontmatter, status tags, Obsidian comments, or generated metadata**. This is the workflow's format exception to the ordinary content-note metadata/status/comment rules. The directory already identifies these as brainstorming; omit canonical-status and adoption reminders. Writing is limited to the monthly log, without changing reference notes or promoting ideas into canon.

## Research that adds something

Search the web for the real-world inspiration and the vault for useful connections. Use a focused first pass; follow promising leads when they contribute something new. Reuse research already established in the conversation. There is no source-count quota or requirement to fill empty sections.

- Explain the useful finding and attach a direct source link to it. Prefer first-hand accounts, scholarly work, and cultural or local sources when available. Summarize findings rather than listing sources or describing the verification process.
- Retain concrete color: preparation, materials, roles, sensory details, social practice, or an interesting mechanism. Ritual roots cleaned and pounded on stone by assigned participants at a ceremony leader's direction, then squeezed through hibiscus bark, can be worth a whole sentence or short paragraph.
- Use no word target or length cap. Keep details that add a new fact, vivid color, a useful distinction, or a connection worth returning to. Give each enough explanation to make sense later without repeating the research. Cut text because it contributes nothing: repetitive explanation, source biographies, bibliography padding, generic disclaimers, and accounts of what could not be verified. Concision means removing filler, not compressing useful material into keywords.
- Maintain accuracy through precise positive wording and attribution. Put an actual correction where it helps, such as identifying sakau as a psychoactive kava-root drink. If a central claim remains unresolved, state that briefly beside the claim when needed for accuracy; skip qualification inventories and speculation about everything a source might not establish.

## Taelgar connections

Search relevant filenames, then names/aliases and passages, and read enough of each source to substantiate the connection. Choose a few useful connections and explain each casually, with enough context to show why it matters. A sentence or two per connection is often useful; a bare list of names and keywords usually loses too much. For example: `[[Gatza]] began as a lizardfolk ritual drug for speaking with the dead. Its controlled communal use and dangerous solitary use offer two different ways to build customs around a substance.` Keep existing lore distinct from a suggested connection through the wording itself.

Never explain what a note does not say. Omit status/lint metadata, backtracking, and repeated warnings about adoption. Respect source privacy: local `_DM_` material stays out of the shared monthly log.

## Entry shape and finish

Use this light shape, adjusting research into a paragraph or bullets as useful:

```markdown
## October 26 — Short idea title

*Inspiration: Source title and episode or context, when known.*

> User's exact idea.

**Research:** A useful finding with its source link; concrete color where worthwhile.

**Taelgar connections:** Explain the useful association with each [[Existing Note]], using a paragraph or bullets as needed.
```

Verify that the original words and earlier entries survived, sources support the added findings, and wikilinks resolve. Review the scoped changes; run a scoped whitespace check, retaining intentional whitespace in verbatim captures. Confirm completion briefly in chat, with the monthly-note link when helpful; keep research and source links in the note.
