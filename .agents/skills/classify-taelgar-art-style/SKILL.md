# Classify Taelgar art style

The catalog is at `~/Documents/RPGs/taelgar-art-catalog/`. Open `index.html` to browse, or use the script below for lookup. The catalog holds observations about art already in the collection. The user chooses any future art direction separately.

**Would the reason for grouping two images still hold if they depicted completely different things?** Look for the way an edge is made, how two colors meet, or how marks turn a flat shape into a volume. An inn and a castle can have very little in common visually. A tree and a face may share the same treatment.

## Review an image

Check the format first. Put framed tokens and battlemaps directly into the set-aside inventory and stop the style review for those assets. A framed token uses an enclosing ring or square frame as a playing piece. A square portrait, circular vignette, or painted picture frame alone is insufficient. A battlemap depicts a space for tactical play at encounter scale, with or without a grid. Regional maps and settlement overviews can enter style discovery. When a token has an unframed source, review that source independently.

1. View the pixels. Use a labeled sheet to orient yourself, then open individual previews or originals wherever marks, outlines, a border or lettering are hard to resolve. A prompt in a filename is evidence about naming, never evidence of what the finished image looks like. Allow uncertainty about physical media and provenance.
2. Write a short, specific paragraph before assigning a group. Follow the most diagnostic features through two or three visible parts of the image. Consider line weight, lost or found edges, stroke direction, exposed ground, modeling, surface, palette, light, scale of detail and depth. Describe relationships. “Pale paper shows between the irregular brown lines of the ribs and through the amber wash” locates a technique; “warm, detailed fantasy illustration” could fit dozens of treatments.
3. Record broad subjects independently. Include a named person or place where a filename, caption, labeled map or unambiguous note identifies the depiction. Give the evidence. An embedding note can use a generic illustration or depict someone other than its title character.
4. Compare the image with actual members of the nearest treatments. Describe what they share and where they diverge. Use an existing treatment, propose a new candidate (a single example is sufficient), or record an uncertain match with alternatives in the basis. Name the treatment by the rendering. Use the catalog families in `config.json`. The five illustration families are Photographic realism, Painterly illustration, Drawn illustration, Stylized 3D illustration, and Sketches, prints and decorative art. Maps and Documents and diagrams have their own treatments. Use Unassigned for open illustration-family decisions. Each treatment belongs to one family. When a treatment appears to span families, compare the images with treatments in their assigned family and reapply the fitting treatment. Preserve detailed observations and choose the broad family by the overall look. Proposed review-sheet groups become treatments only after human review. Keep quality opinions, guessed generator versions and preferred future styles out of the discovery record.

## Run a batch

The coordinator owns assignment, synthesis and publication. Read `config.json`, `styles.json`, `corrections.json` and recent batch reviews. Use `scripts/catalog.py` with Python 3 and Pillow (on this Mac, `~/.local/miniforge3/envs/taelgar/bin/python`). Run `--help` for commands.

- Refresh the path inventory with `inventory`. Use `inventory --match-copies` to identify exact copies among paths with the same byte size as reviewed assets. `plan --count 100 --output /some/scratch/manifest.json` samples across both roots and folders, skips reviewed content, and assigns IDs. Keep one active coordinator; resume an existing manifest after an interruption. Hashing and rendering only selected images limits cloud hydration. A planner entry with an unsupported format needs a compatible viewer. For an SVG, view it with a vector-capable renderer, supply its viewed dimensions and put a rendered JPEG preview in `thumbnails/<sha256>.jpg` before building. Confirm the pixels before marking it reviewed.
- Give parallel reviewers disjoint subsets of the same manifest, the same style index snapshot, and the review instructions above. Include a couple of shared examples for calibration outside the count. Compare independently observed marks and resolve disagreements before merging. Reviewers write separate scratch files. For a set-aside asset, supply only `id, title, battlemap, framedToken`. Its source metadata comes from the manifest. Exclude it from treatment counts, comparisons, reference examples and uncertain assignments. For a style review, a worker row contains `id, title, subjects[], depicts[{role,name,evidence}], battlemap, framedToken, family, treatment, fit, statement, basis`, and optionally `format`. Allowed fit values are `existing`, `candidate` and `uncertain`.
- Compare candidate sets across workers side by side, including similar names and the closest pilot examples. Reconcile synonyms when the marks support it. For painted likenesses, compare how the cheek turns, where individual marks remain visible and how face, hair and ground meet. Compare the current painted portrait treatments side by side. Keep singleton candidates and mixed cases visible. Review obvious matches as well. Save independent reviews if a change in grouping needs an audit trail. Preserve the original observation when moving an image between groups. Related variants are useful examples of variation but do not establish independent recurrence.
- In a staging copy, use `assemble --manifest manifest.json --reviews worker-a.json worker-b.json --output batches/002.json` to join reconciled reviews to their manifest by ID. Use completed batch files for the exact schema. Add a batch JSON array under `batches/` and update `styles.json` with the labels, descriptions, examples, neighbor comparisons and status. Put user-directed changes in `corrections.json`, keyed by stable image ID, as patches to the appropriate fields. Those patches take precedence during lookup, validation and gallery generation. Only record a human correction when the user gives one.
- Run `build` to validate the effective records, cache missing previews and rebuild gallery and inventory. Run `verify` for path, thumbnail and gallery checks. Publish validated new files together and preserve the rest of the catalog. Include a visual review under `reviews/` at roughly 100 new images, with new sets, neighboring or ambiguous examples, possible splits and a sample of existing matches. Tell the user which proposals need their eye.

## Repair original-file links after duplicate review

Use the vault's `_scripts/plan_art_cleanup.py` with the exported decisions and duplicate manifest to preview keeper paths, vault references, catalog repairs and session provenance. The catalog's optional `source-replacements.json` maps stable review IDs to their surviving files. Several reviews of different encodings can point to the same image. Batch observations and thumbnail hashes retain the identity originally reviewed. Run the catalog verifier after publishing approved repairs. Session provenance records the original directory and filename under `moved` or `deleted`, with `file` pointing to the current keeper. After a set-aside transfer, moved files become the review originals. Register incoming copies of vault files in the inventory with the original review ID. Collections listed in `linkedCollections` contribute only those registered paths and review originals to a refresh.

## Look up existing examples

Run `lookup 'Ink and watercolor illustration'` or search by subject or name in the gallery. Return the diagnostic description, a few representative originals across subjects where available, and the nearest alternative with its visual distinction. Open the originals before using them in a new brief. The bank records styles encountered and user corrections; approval to restyle an image is a separate task.
